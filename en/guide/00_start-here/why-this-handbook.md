# Why we made this handbook

> This page explains why the project exists. It is our own view, not research advice, so it has no footnotes.

## Where it started: we work on auto research

We work on auto research: getting large language model agents to do research on their own.

As these models get better, we lean on them more and think less for ourselves. Yet again and again they turn out to be bad at things, and we end up going back and forth with them, arguing over the result.

As mechanical work, and work that needs only simple judgment, moves to agents, what is left for people is the part that most needs clear thinking. That raises the bar for our own thinking. Many things we used to do on autopilot now make us stop and ask what is really going on.

Research is one of them.

## A question we couldn't answer

To get an agent to do research, you first have to answer: how is research actually done? What are the methods, and what are the tricks?

A PhD student starts as a complete beginner and, step by step, becomes a researcher who can stand on their own: they write papers faster, know what is worth working on, and can set a clear direction. Along the way they build up a great deal of experience and knowledge, much of it without noticing.

Yet when we asked ourselves how research is done, we couldn't put it into words. Few people seem to have written it down systematically; most researchers work out their own approach through practice. Debugging is similar: it is hard to explain, and you get good at it by doing it, over and over.

## What does an agent need?

If we want agents to do research, what do they need? A research handbook to start from, or experience they build up through practice? And even learning from experience, doesn't it need someone to explain how to learn?

We don't have the answers yet. But we think the first step is to take this unspoken know-how and write it down as clearly as we can.

## So we wrote this handbook

This handbook tries to write down how research is done, from start to finish: choosing a topic, designing a study, moving a project forward, writing and submitting, and the conventions of each field.

We hold ourselves to two rules:

- Every piece of advice can be traced to its original source; anything that is our own judgment carries no footnote, so you can always tell the two apart.
- Plain language, so that someone new to research can follow it.

It is written first for people: for anyone starting out in research who hasn't yet found their own way of working. It is also the groundwork for our auto research. Explaining research clearly helps people and agents alike.
