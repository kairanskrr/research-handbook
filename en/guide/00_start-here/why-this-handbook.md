# Why we made this handbook

We work on auto research: getting large language model agents to do research on their own. To get an agent to do research, you first have to answer a question: how is research actually done? When we asked ourselves, we couldn't put it into words.

A PhD student starts as a complete beginner and, step by step, becomes a researcher who can stand on their own. Along the way they build up a great deal of experience, much of it without noticing. Few people write this down systematically; most researchers work out their own approach through practice. So we set out to write it down.

## What this handbook is for

We judge this handbook by one question: **could someone new to research follow it to get started?**

So it is written for people starting out in research, and it covers how research is done from start to finish: choosing a topic, designing a study, moving a project forward, and the conventions of each field. Every piece of advice can be traced to its original source, and we keep the language plain.

## Explaining it, and practicing it

This handbook tries to explain how research is done as clearly as we can, including things many people believe you can only learn by doing, such as judging whether a direction is worth pursuing or what to change when you are stuck.

But having it explained is not the same as being able to do it. Knowing which questions to ask does not mean you will answer them well the first time; judgment is built project by project, in real work. What the handbook can do is give your practice a direction and spare you some detours.

People also differ: in their circumstances, and in the ways of working that suit them and that they are good at. A piece of advice may need adjusting before it works for you, and the adjustment that suits you best is something you find by trying.

We are still working on explaining all of this. If, in practice, you find something here that is wrong or doesn't fit, please [let us know](../../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Written for people only

This handbook is written purely for people, not for agents.

We are taking things in order: first, organize the material clearly for people; only then explore how to turn it into knowledge that agents can use effectively. That is the next step, and it is not part of this handbook.
