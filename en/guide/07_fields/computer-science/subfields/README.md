# Areas of computer science

The areas within computer science differ a lot: where people publish, what reviewers look for, and how much evidence is expected are all different. For why you should look at your own area, see [Computer science field overview: Conventions differ across research areas](../README.md#conventions-differ-across-research-areas).

This section covers only **practices specific to each area**. Everything else lives elsewhere:

- Practices common to all fields (choosing a problem, planning, writing, peer review) are in the chapters of Parts 1–4; the general logic and standards of evidence for each kind of research are in [Research strategies](../../../05_research-strategies/README.md).
- Conventions shared across computer science (conferences as the main venue, double-blind review and preprints, artifact evaluation) are in the [computer science field overview](../README.md).
- Concrete practices specific to computer science (engineering research, benchmarking, mining software repositories, and so on) are in [Research practices specific to computer science](../practices/README.md).

## How to use this section

`[Our analysis]`

- **Read the computer science field overview first, then your area's page.** The area pages assume you already know how conferences, double-blind review, and artifact evaluation work.
- **If your work spans areas, read both pages.** For example, if you use machine learning for program analysis, you have to meet the expectations of reviewers from both sides; in the end, go by the area of the conference you are submitting to.
- **The conference rules on the area pages are each from one conference in one year.** They are examples, not rules for the whole area; always read the call for papers of your target conference for the current year before you submit.
