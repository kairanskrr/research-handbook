# Research Handbook

From choosing a topic to publishing: how to do a piece of research properly.

This is the English edition. The handbook also has a [Chinese edition](../README.md) (中文版); the web reader has a switch for it.

**Web reader**: <https://kairanskrr.github.io/research-handbook/>. Browse by table of contents, search the full text, and click a footnote to read the original source in place. It comes in a concise edition and a full edition.

Written for people starting out in research: how to choose a topic, read the literature, design a study, move a project forward, write and submit, and what conventions each field has of its own. The content comes from the experience of senior researchers and from widely accepted methodological standards in each field, and every piece of advice can be traced back to its original source.

## Where to start

| Your situation | Start here |
|---|---|
| This is your first research project and you want to know how to read this handbook | [How to use this handbook](guide/00_start-here/how-to-use.md), then [The research journey at a glance](guide/00_start-here/research-journey.md) |
| You are at a particular stage: you have just picked an area, you are designing the study, running experiments, or getting ready to submit | The matching chapter in "The four Parts" below |
| You have a specific problem: the experiment won't work, the paper was rejected, your advisor has no time… | [FAQ: quick answers](guide/faq.md) |
| You have written down the sentence you want to prove, and want to know what kind of research this is and what evidence it needs | [Research strategies](guide/05_research-strategies/README.md) |
| You need a particular research method: controlled experiment, interviews, questionnaire, review… | [Research methods](guide/06_methods/README.md), or the practices specific to your field in Fields |
| You are entering a new field and want to learn its conventions | Your field in [Fields](guide/07_fields/README.md) |

## How to check the sources

Every piece of advice has a footnote. Open the footnote to see which source it comes from, where in the original it appears, and what the original says. Where something is our own judgment with no source behind it, it is tagged `[Our analysis]`. Where sources disagree, the different views are set out side by side.

All original sources are listed in the [source list](sources/README.md), and the quoted excerpts from each source are in [`sources/notes/`](sources/notes/README.md).

```
    computer-science/practices/     research practices specific to computer science
    <field>/practices/              research practices specific to other fields
  08_throughout/                  Throughout the project: planning, materials, advisors and collaboration, integrity, mindset, reviewing
  faq.md                          FAQ: quick answers
sources/                        original sources
  README.md                       source list: papers, standards, blogs, repositories
  notes/                          evidence notes: the original text and its location for each source
papers/                         BibTeX for the papers (references.bib) and an index
repos/                          GitHub repositories included as reference material (git submodules)
templates/                      document templates
scripts/                        paper download script, web reader build script
site/                           web reader pages
```

To clone this repository:

```
git clone --recursive https://github.com/kairanskrr/research-handbook.git
```

If you have already cloned it, run `git submodule update --init` to fetch the submodules under `repos/`.
