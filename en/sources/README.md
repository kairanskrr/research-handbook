## Quality standards and reporting guidelines

Widely accepted quality standards and reporting guidelines for different kinds of research.

- [ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards](https://www2.sigsoft.org/EmpiricalStandards/) - Ralph et al., 2020, continuously updated. For each of 19 research methods, lists essential, desirable, and extraordinary attributes, plus antipatterns, acceptable deviations, invalid criticisms, and exemplar papers. Its General Standard applies to every type of study. Released under CC0. 📦 🆓 `ralph2020empirical`
- [Guidelines for Empirical Studies in Software Engineering involving Large Language Models](https://llm-guidelines.org/) - Baltes et al., Empirical Software Engineering 2026. Eight guidelines: declare large language model use, report model versions and configurations, publish prompts and interaction logs, validate model outputs with humans, use an open model as a baseline, and more. 🆓 `baltes2026guidelines`
- [REFORMS](https://reforms.cs.princeton.edu/) - Kapoor et al., Science Advances 2024. A 32-item checklist in 8 sections for machine-learning-based science. `kapoor2024reforms`
- [PRISMA 2020](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71) - Page et al., BMJ 2021. The reporting guideline for systematic reviews: 27 items in 7 sections, plus flow diagram templates. Note that it governs how you report a review, not how you conduct it. 🆓 `page2021prisma`
- [PRISMA 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000097) - Moher et al., PLoS Medicine 2009. The previous version of the reporting guideline, still cited by many software engineering reviews. 🆓 `moher2009prisma`

## Research methods guides

### Systematic literature reviews and mapping studies

- [Guidelines for performing Systematic Literature Reviews in Software Engineering](https://ebse.webspace.durham.ac.uk/ebse-bibliography/guidelines-for-performing-systematic-literature-reviews-in-software-engineering/) - Kitchenham & Charters, technical report EBSE-2007-01, 2007. The foundational guideline for systematic literature reviews in software engineering: the planning, conducting, and reporting phases; what goes into the review protocol; study selection, quality assessment, data extraction, and synthesis; with quality assessment checklists for quantitative and qualitative studies. 🆓 `kitchenham2007guidelines`
- [Systematic Mapping Studies in Software Engineering](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~feldt/publications/petersen_ease08_sysmap_studies_in_se.pdf) - Petersen et al., EASE 2008. The original guideline for mapping studies: a five-step process, building a classification scheme by keywording, and how mapping studies differ from systematic reviews. 🆓 `petersen2008systematic`
- [Guidelines for conducting systematic mapping studies in software engineering: An update](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2015.03.007) - Petersen et al., IST 2015. The updated version of the mapping study guidelines. 💰 `petersen2015guidelines`
- [Guidelines for snowballing in systematic literature studies and a replication in software engineering](https://www.wohlin.eu/ease14.pdf) - Wohlin, EASE 2014. The original guideline for snowballing: choosing the start set, backward and forward snowballing, and when to stop; it also redoes a published systematic literature review by snowballing for comparison. 🆓 `wohlin2014guidelines`
- [Successful Combination of Database Search and Snowballing for Identification of Primary Studies in Systematic Literature Studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02612) - Wohlin et al., IST 2022. A hybrid strategy combining database search with snowballing, which finds about 30% more primary studies than database search alone. 🆓 `wohlin2022successful`
- [Paving the Way for Mature Secondary Research: The Seven Types of Literature Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01163) - Ralph & Baltes, ESEC/FSE 2022. Sorts literature reviews into seven types and recommends that software engineering do more meta-analyses, case surveys, and meta-syntheses, and fewer scoping reviews and rapid reviews. 🆓 `ralph2022paving`
- [ACM Computing Surveys: Author Guidelines](https://dl.acm.org/journal/csur/author-guidelines) - ACM. The submission requirements of the main survey journal in computing: a survey should classify the existing literature, develop a perspective on the field, and evaluate trends; it also distinguishes surveys from tutorials. 🆓 `acm2026csurguidelines`

### Replication studies

- [Understanding replication of experiments in software engineering: A classification](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2014.04.004) - Gómez, Juristo, Vegas, IST 2014. A classification of replication studies. 💰 `gomez2014understanding`
- [The role of replications in Empirical Software Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-008-9060-1) - Shull et al., EMSE 2008. The role of replication studies in empirical software engineering. 💰 `shull2008role`
- [Towards Reporting Guidelines for Experimental Replications: A Proposal](https://carver.cs.ua.edu/ReplicationGuidelines.htm) - Carver, RESER 2010. Reporting guidelines for replication studies. 🆓 `carver2010towards`
- [ACM Artifact Review and Badging](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/artifact-review-and-badging-current) - ACM, v1.1, 2020. ACM's policy on artifact review and badges, distinguishing levels such as "Available," "Evaluated," and "Results Reproduced/Replicated." 🆓 `acm2020artifact`

### Mining studies

- [The promises and perils of mining git](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/bird2009ppm.pdf) - Bird et al., MSR 2009. Pitfalls in mining git history data. 🆓 `bird2009promises`
- [Fair and Balanced? Bias in Bug-Fix Datasets](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/bird2009fbb.pdf) - Bird et al., ESEC/FSE 2009. In bug datasets, the bug fixes that can be linked to commits are a systematically biased subset, and this bias affects the conclusions of hypothesis tests and defect prediction. 🆓 `bird2009fair`
- [The promises and perils of mining GitHub](https://kblincoe.github.io/publications/2014_MSR_Promises_Perils.pdf) - Kalliamvakou et al., MSR 2014. Common pitfalls in mining GitHub data. 🆓 `kalliamvakou2014promises`
- [An in-depth study of the promises and perils of mining GitHub](https://kblincoe.github.io/publications/2015_EMSE_GitHubPerils.pdf) - Kalliamvakou et al., EMSE 2016. The extended journal version of the previous paper. 🆓 `kalliamvakou2016indepth`
- [It's not a bug, it's a feature: how misclassification impacts bug prediction](https://www.st.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/files/herzig-icse-2013.pdf) - Herzig, Just, Zeller, ICSE 2013. Misclassification in issue data and how it affects research conclusions. 🆓 `herzig2013bug`

### Evaluating tools and models

- [Evaluating Fuzz Testing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09700) - Klees et al., CCS 2018. Surveys the evaluation methods of 32 fuzzing papers, points out common problems, and gives recommendations. 🆓 `klees2018evaluating`
- [SoK: Benchmarking Flaws in Systems Security](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/vanderKouwe_HABG_19.pdf) - van der Kouwe et al., EuroS&P 2019. Catalogs 22 kinds of benchmarking misuse in systems security papers, based on 50 papers. 🆓 `vanderkouwe2019sok`
- [Dos and Don'ts of Machine Learning in Computer Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09470) - Arp et al., USENIX Security 2022. Based on 30 papers from top security conferences, summarizes common pitfalls in the design, implementation, and evaluation of machine learning in security research. 🆓 `arp2022dos`
- [A practical guide for using statistical tests to assess randomized algorithms in software engineering](https://doi.org/10.1145/1985793.1985795) - Arcuri & Briand, ICSE 2011. How to use statistical tests to compare randomized algorithms or tools. `arcuri2011practical`
- [A Hitchhiker's guide to statistical tests for assessing randomized algorithms in software engineering](https://doi.org/10.1002/stvr.1486) - Arcuri & Briand, STVR 2014. The extended journal version of the previous paper. [Author version](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/1071/1/paper_stvr_icse_2012.pdf). 🆓 `arcuri2014hitchhiker`
- [SIGPLAN Empirical Evaluation Guidelines](https://www.sigplan.org/Resources/EmpiricalEvaluation/) - Blackburn, Berger, Hauswirth, Hicks et al., ACM SIGPLAN 2018. Seven categories of checks: clearly stated claims, suitable benchmarks, data analysis, workloads, statistical rigor, mitigating threats, and reproducibility. Works well as a checklist for tool evaluations. 🆓 `blackburn2018sigplan`
- [Gernot's List of Systems Benchmarking Crimes](https://gernot-heiser.org/benchmarking-crimes.html) - Gernot Heiser (UNSW), continuously updated. Common benchmarking "crimes" in systems papers: selective use of benchmarks, improper comparisons, missing key information, and more. 🆓 `heiserbenchmarking`
- [The Devil's Guide to Incremental Research](https://andreas-zeller.info/2021/04/15/the-devils-guide-to-incremental-research.html) - Andreas Zeller, 2021. A tongue-in-cheek list of 12 tricks for padding research (such as cherry-picking favorable results), to help authors and reviewers spot them. 🆓 `zeller2021devil`
- ["Bad smells" in software analytics papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05518) - Menzies & Shepperd, IST 2019. A list of "bad smells" in software analytics papers for authors and reviewers to check against. 🆓 `menzies2019bad`
- [How to avoid machine learning pitfalls: a guide for academic researchers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02497) - Michael Lones, arXiv 2021 (continuously updated). Lists common pitfalls in academic machine learning research, from modeling and evaluation to comparison and reporting, and how to avoid them. 🆓 `lones2021avoid`
- [On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06705) - Carlini et al., arXiv 2019. Methods and a checklist for evaluating defenses for adversarial robustness. 🆓 `carlini2019evaluating`
- [Successful language model evals](https://jasonwei.net/blog/evals) - Jason Wei, 2024. What makes a good large language model evaluation benchmark: enough examples, scores that are clear and easy to interpret, use in major papers, and so on. 🆓 `wei2024evals`
- [NLP Evaluation in trouble: On the Need to Measure LLM Data Contamination for each Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18018) - Sainz et al., Findings of EMNLP 2023. Data contamination in large language model evaluation: test data leaking into training data inflates model performance; proposes measuring contamination separately for each benchmark. 🆓 `sainz2023nlp`
- [Producing wrong data without doing anything obviously wrong!](https://doi.org/10.1145/1508244.1508275) - Mytkowicz et al., ASPLOS 2009. Seemingly irrelevant factors such as link order and environment variable size cause measurement bias large enough to overturn experimental conclusions. `mytkowicz2009producing`
- [Statistically rigorous Java performance evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1145/1297027.1297033) - Georges et al., OOPSLA 2007. Performance measurements should use statistical methods such as confidence intervals rather than reporting a single or best run. [Co-author's homepage](https://dri.es/files/oopsla07-georges.pdf). 🆓 `georges2007statistically`
- [Rigorous benchmarking in reasonable time](https://doi.org/10.1145/2464157.2464160) - Kalibera & Jones, ISMM 2013. Designing multi-level repeated experiments within limited time to get reliable performance results. [University of Kent repository (author accepted manuscript)](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/33611/). 🆓 `kalibera2013rigorous`
- [Wake up and smell the coffee: evaluation methodology for the 21st century](https://doi.org/10.1145/1378704.1378723) - Blackburn et al., CACM 2008. Points out flaws in performance evaluation methodology and suggests improvements. `blackburn2008wake`
- [Scientific benchmarking of parallel computing systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/2807591.2807644) - Hoefler & Belli, SC 2015. Twelve rules for reporting parallel computing performance. [Author's homepage](https://htor.inf.ethz.ch/publications/img/hoefler-scientific-benchmarking.pdf). 🆓 `hoefler2015scientific`


### Empirical research in general

- [Experimentation in Software Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-69306-3) - Wohlin et al., Springer, 2nd edition 2024. The standard textbook on experiments; the classic treatment of threats to validity comes from here. 💰 `wohlin2024experimentation`
- [Guidelines for conducting and reporting case study research in software engineering](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-008-9102-8) - Runeson & Höst, EMSE 2009. Case study guidelines; their discussion of validity is a useful reference. Open access (CC BY-NC). 🆓 `runeson2009guidelines`
- [Contemporary Empirical Methods in Software Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-32489-6) - Felderer & Travassos (eds.), Springer 2020. A recent overview of many empirical methods; only Chapter 17 (Open Science) is open access. 💰 `felderer2020contemporary`
- [Sampling in software engineering research: a critical review and guidelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07764) - Baltes & Ralph, EMSE 2022. A critical review of sampling in software engineering research, with guidelines. 🆓 `baltes2022sampling`
- [Views on Internal and External Validity in Empirical Software Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2015.24) - Siegmund, Siegmund, Apel, ICSE 2015. Surveys how the software engineering community views the trade-off between internal and external validity. `siegmund2015views`
- [SoK: Science, Security and the Elusive Goal of Security as a Scientific Pursuit](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2017.38) - Herley & van Oorschot, IEEE S&P 2017. Uses the philosophy of science to examine how scientific security research is, and criticizes unfalsifiable claims. `herley2017sok`
- [The Menlo Report](https://www.caida.org/publications/papers/2012/menlo_report/menlo_report.pdf) - Bailey, Dittrich, Kenneally, Maughan, IEEE Security & Privacy 2012. Introduces the Menlo Report, funded by the US Department of Homeland Security: it adds "respect for law and public interest" to the three Belmont Report principles and is a common basis for ethics in security and network research. The report itself is on [SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2445102). 🆓 `bailey2012menlo`
- [The European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity (2023 revised edition)](https://doi.org/10.26356/ECOC) - ALLEA (All European Academies), 2023. A research integrity code for all disciplines: four basic principles, good research practices, and definitions of research misconduct. CC BY 4.0. 🆓 `allea2023european`
- [Ethical considerations in network measurement papers](https://doi.org/10.1145/2896816) - Partridge & Allman, CACM 2016. How measurement papers should discuss and handle ethical issues. `partridge2016ethical`


### Methods sources for computing research types

Methodology sources cited by the pages on research types in the computing field section (added 2026-10).

- [Ten Signs a Claimed Mathematical Breakthrough is Wrong](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=304) - Aaronson, Shtetl-Optimized (blog) 2008. A blog post by theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson listing ten signs that a claimed "major mathematical breakthrough" is probably wrong; he stresses these are only heuristics. 🆓 `aaronson2008ten`
- [Output Data Analysis for Simulations](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc02papers/012.pdf) - Alexopoulos et al., Proceedings of the 2002 Winter Simulation Conference 2002. A tutorial on the statistical analysis of simulation output: terminating versus steady-state simulations, independent replications, initialization bias and warm-up truncation (including Welch's graphical method), the replication/deletion approach, and more. 🆓 `alexopoulos2002output`
- [Using thematic analysis in psychology](https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa) - Braun et al., Qualitative Research in Psychology 2006. The classic guide to thematic analysis: what it is, inductive versus deductive approaches, the six phases of analysis, common pitfalls, and a 15-point checklist. 🆓 `braun2006using`
- [Simulation Based Studies in Software Engineering: A Matter of Validity](https://www.clei.org/cleiej/index.php/cleiej/article/view/54) - de França et al., CLEI Electronic Journal 2015. De França & Travassos identify 28 threats to validity in software engineering simulation studies, map them to verification and validation methods, and give 7 recommendations for planning simulation studies. 🆓 `defranca2015simulation`
- [Selecting Empirical Methods for Software Engineering Research](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-84800-044-5_11) - Easterbrook et al., Guide to Advanced Empirical Software Engineering 2008. The classic introduction to choosing empirical methods in software engineering: types of research questions, philosophical stances, and when each method fits (experiments, case studies, surveys, ethnography, action research); §5.6 introduces three mixed-methods designs. 🆓 `easterbrook2008selecting`
- [Empirical Software Engineering Experts on the Use of Students and Professionals in Experiments](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/14575) - Falessi et al., Empirical Software Engineering 2018. Seven empirical software engineering researchers led by Falessi, drawing on an ISERN 2014 focus group and a follow-up expert survey, discuss whether to use students or professionals as participants in experiments; they propose 16 statements and the "real, relevant, recent experience" (R3) characterization scheme. 🆓 `falessi2018empirical`
- [Psychometrics in Behavioral Software Engineering: A Methodological Introduction with Guidelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09959) - Graziotin et al., ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology 2021. Graziotin, Lenberg, Feldt, and Wagner introduce psychometrics to software engineering researchers: how to develop and evaluate scales that measure psychological constructs (item analysis, factor analysis, reliability, validity, fairness), with R examples. 🆓 `graziotin2021psychometrics`
- [State of the Art: Reproducibility in Artificial Intelligence](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11503) - Gundersen et al., Proceedings of the Thirty-Second AAAI Conference on Artifici 2018. Surveys 400 papers from IJCAI and AAAI for how much of the information needed for reproducibility they document; proposes three degrees of reproducibility (R1 same implementation and data, R2 different implementation and same data, R3 different implementation and different data). 🆓 `gundersen2018state`
- [Systematic methodological review: developing a framework for a qualitative semi-structured interview guide](https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.13031) - Kallio et al., Journal of Advanced Nursing 2016. A systematic review of how semi-structured interview guides are designed, distilled into five phases: identifying the prerequisites, using previous knowledge, formulating a preliminary guide, pilot testing, and presenting the complete guide; it is concrete about main themes, follow-up questions, and three ways of pilot testing. 🆓 `kallio2016systematic`
- [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804) - Kapoor et al., Patterns 2023. Surveys reproducibility failures caused by data leakage across 17 fields (294 papers), proposes a taxonomy of 8 types of leakage and "model info sheets," and reproduces civil war prediction studies, finding that once leakage is fixed, complex models do no better than logistic regression. 🆓 `kapoor2023leakage`
- [Quantitative Longitudinal Research: A Review of IS Literature, and a Set of Methodological Guidelines](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2015_cr/94) - Kehr et al., Proceedings of the 23rd European Conference on Information S 2015. Reviews quantitative longitudinal research in information systems and proposes four planning principles: time measurements to the phenomenon's natural rhythm, choose the right number of measurement waves, watch for threats to internal validity (including attrition), and use analysis methods suited to dependent data. 🆓 `kehr2015quantitative`
- [A Practical Guide to Controlled Experiments of Software Engineering Tools with Human Participants](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Ko2013SEToolEvaluations.pdf) - Ko et al., Empirical Software Engineering 2015. A methods paper by Ko, LaToza, and Burnett in Empirical Software Engineering that, drawing on a systematic review of 345 tool evaluations with human participants from 2001 to 2011, walks through the options and trade-offs for recruitment, informed consent, group assignment, training, task design, outcome measurement, debriefing, and pilot studies. 🆓 `ko2015practical`
- [How to Write a Proof](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/lamport-how-to-write.pdf) - Lamport, The American Mathematical Monthly 1995. Lamport's first article proposing structured proofs: how to write them, how to expand levels of detail, and his experience of finding errors in textbook proofs. 🆓 `lamport1995how`
- [How to Write a 21st Century Proof](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/proof.pdf) - Lamport, Journal of Fixed Point Theory and Applications 2012. Leslie Lamport on writing structured (hierarchically numbered) proofs, with twenty years of experience using them and an example proof machine-checked with TLAPS. 🆓 `lamport2012how`
- [Reliability and Inter-rater Reliability in Qualitative Research: Norms and Guidelines for CSCW and HCI Practice](https://doi.org/10.1145/3359174) - McDonald et al., Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 2019. Analyzes how qualitative papers at CSCW and CHI from 2016 to 2018 report reliability, and advises when you should and should not compute inter-rater reliability. 🆓 `mcdonald2019reliability`
- [Case Survey Studies in Software Engineering Research](https://doi.org/10.1145/3382494.3410683) - Melegati et al., Proceedings of the 14th ACM / IEEE International Symposium o 2020. Reviews the classic definitions and steps of the case survey method (Lucas, Yin, Larsson, and others) and uses a mapping study of 12 software engineering papers that claim to use case surveys, finding that most do not follow the classic definition. 🆓 `melegati2020case`
- [An Empirically Evaluated Checklist for Surveys in Software Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09850) - Molléri et al., Information and Software Technology 2020. Molléri, Petersen, and Mendes compile 14 methodological sources on surveys in software engineering into a checklist for survey research, then use it to assess 24 survey papers and ask the original authors for feedback. 🆓 `molleri2020empirically`
- [Improving Reproducibility in Machine Learning Research (A Report from the NeurIPS 2019 Reproducibility Program)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-303.html) - Pineau et al., Journal of Machine Learning Research 2021. Report on the NeurIPS 2019 reproducibility program: the code submission policy, a community reproducibility challenge, and the machine learning reproducibility checklist; it explains why replication is hard in machine learning and distinguishes several terms. 🆓 `pineau2021improving`
- [Action Research Can Swing the Balance in Experimental Software Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2414) - dos Santos et al., Advances in Computers 2011. A chapter in *Advances in Computers* volume 83. 🆓 `santos2011action`
- [Verification and Validation of Simulation Models](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc10papers/016.pdf) - Sargent, Proceedings of the 2010 Winter Simulation Conference 2010. Sargent's classic tutorial on verification and validation of simulation models (WSC 2010 version): definitions of verification and validation, four kinds of checks (conceptual model validation, computerized model verification, operational validation, data validity), common validation techniques, and a recommended minimal validation procedure. 🆓 `sargent2010verification`
- [The Role of Ethnographic Studies in Empirical Software Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2016.2519887) - Sharp et al., IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering 2016. Introduces ethnography to software engineering researchers: four main features, five dimensions of study design, four roles in empirical software engineering, with eight examples of software engineering ethnographies. 🆓 `sharp2016role`
- [Inferencing into the Void: Problems with Implicit Populations. Comments on ``Empirical Software Engineering Experts on the Use of Students and Professionals in Experiments''](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.07392) - Shepperd, 2018. Shepperd's commentary on Falessi et al. 2018 (later included in the "four commentaries" by Feldt et al. 2018 in Empirical Software Engineering); it stresses defining the target population explicitly and considering the representativeness of participants, tasks, and settings together, and argues for using professionals whenever professionals are the target. 🆓 `shepperd2018inferencing`
- [Guidelines for Conducting Action Research Studies in Software Engineering](https://www.e-informatyka.pl/EISEJ/papers/2025/1/5/) - Staron, e-Informatica Software Engineering Journal 2025. Action research guidelines in e-Informatica by Staron (author of the 2020 Springer book *Action Research in Software Engineering*); it consolidates existing guidelines by Davison, Baskerville, and others into 20 recommendations covering project preparation, intervention, reporting, and ethics. 🆓 `staron2025guidelines`
- [Grounded Theory in Software Engineering Research: A Critical Review and Guidelines](https://hdl.handle.net/10344/5396) - Stol et al., Proceedings of the 38th International Conference on Software 2016. An ICSE 2016 paper. 🆓 `stol2016grounded`
- [The who, what, how of software engineering research: a socio-technical framework](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-020-09858-z) - Storey et al., Empirical Software Engineering 2020. Proposes a socio-technical framework of who benefits, what the contribution is, and how it is studied, and applies it to 151 ICSE and EMSE papers from 2017, finding that most studies claiming to benefit people did not study people directly, and that data-centric papers rarely triangulate with other research strategies. 🆓 `storey2020who`
- [Career Advice](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/career-advice/) - Tao, Whats new (blog). A series of short career-advice posts on Terence Tao's blog. 🆓 `taocareer`
- [On Writing](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/advice-on-writing-papers/) - Tao, Whats new (blog). A series of short posts on Tao's blog with advice on writing papers. 🆓 `taowriting`
- [Lessons Learnt in Conducting Survey Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05744) - Torchiano et al., Proceedings of the 5th IEEE/ACM International Workshop on Co 2017. Torchiano, Méndez Fernández, Travassos, and de Mello summarize lessons from running surveys in industry: setting research goals, defining the population and unit of analysis, the sampling frame, question wording and answer-option design, recruitment and reminders, with many good and bad example questions. 🆓 `torchiano2017lessons`
- [Challenges in Survey Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05899) - Wagner et al., Contemporary Empirical Methods in Software Engineering 2020. The main text uses it to state that sample size is counted in groups; that recommended group sizes mostly fall between 4 and 12 people; that 2 to 5 groups per participant category is only a rule of thumb; that the median number of groups was 5 and only 17% of studies explained their number of groups; and that many studies claiming saturation did not analyze data while collecting it. 🆓 `carlsen2011what`

The main text uses it to state that four groups reached code saturation while meaning saturation needed more; that one group per stratum can find the issues and two give a fuller understanding; that the first group produced 60% of new codes; and that you should write down a range for the number of groups in advance. 🆓 `hennink2019what`

The main text uses it to state that the researcher is a moderator, not an interrogator; that more than 12 people is hard to manage and you can over-recruit by 10% to 25%; that you need a moderator and an assistant; that focus groups are a poor fit when you need numbers or participants are uncomfortable; the dominance effect, halo effect, and groupthink; and that reporting gaps are serious. 🆓 `nyumba2018use`

### Research-type exemplar papers (smart contract security)

- [Characterizing Code Clones in the Ethereum Smart Contract Ecosystem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00272) - He et al., FC 2020. A large-scale empirical study of code clones in Ethereum smart contracts: it collects nearly 10 million contracts, detects clones with fuzzy hashing and checks accuracy against 1,100 manually labeled pairs, and finds that over 96% of contracts have duplicates, about 9.7% of similar contract pairs share the same vulnerabilities, and 73 decentralized applications are copies. 🆓 `he2020characterizing`

A model smart contract benchmark paper: it builds one dataset of 69 contracts with 115 manually labeled vulnerabilities and another of 47,518 on-chain contracts, and evaluates 9 analysis tools with the SmartBugs framework. Data sources, labeling method, and deduplication steps are all clearly described. 🆓 `durieux2020empirical`

- [A Survival Guide to a PhD](http://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) - Andrej Karpathy, 2016. Whether to do a PhD, choosing an advisor, developing taste in problems, one core contribution per paper, giving talks, and attending conferences. 🆓 `karpathy2016survival`
- [How to Be a Successful PhD Student (in Computer Science (in NLP/ML))](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~mdredze/publications/HowtoBeaSuccessfulPhDStudent.1_2.pdf) - Mark Dredze & Hanna Wallach, 2012 (v1.2 updated 2022). For NLP/ML PhD students: working and meeting with your advisor, day-to-day productivity, reading papers and choosing problems, running the simplest experiment that could prove you wrong first, analyzing failure cases, and submitting on schedule. 🆓 `dredze2012successful`
- [Prof Halim's PhD Tips](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~stevenha/myresearch/phd_tips.html) - Steven Halim (NUS), continuously updated. Pros and cons of PhD life, technical writing conventions, presentation skills, reviewing and responding to reviews, the defense, and teaching. 🆓 `halimtips`
- [Five tips for starting (and continuing) a PhD](https://blogs.ed.ac.uk/ellie-wood/2020/09/04/five-tips-for-starting-and-continuing-a-phd/) - Ellie Wood (doctoral researcher, University of Edinburgh), 2020. Keep systematic reading notes, read a few recent theses, start a thesis outline document early, and talk often with peers and your advisor. 🆓 `wood2020five`
- [A PhD So Good It Can't Be Ignored: Passion, Practice, and Control](https://ahappyphd.org/posts/phd-so-good-1/) - Luis P. Prieto (A Happy PhD), 2025. Borrows Cal Newport's view: don't just follow your passion; first build rare skills through deliberate practice, then seek autonomy. 🆓 `prieto2025so`
- [How to be a successful PhD student?](https://medium.com/data-science/how-to-be-a-successful-phd-student-3f3a4eb48be8) - Dorien Herremans (SUTD), 2020. A list of lab best practices: don't show your advisor a draft the day before the deadline, pair up with a postdoc, open-source your code and post preprints, keep a regular schedule, and take ownership of your project. 🆓 `herremans2020successful`
- [10 pieces of advice I wish my PhD advisor had given me](https://www.sigcommedu.web.illinois.edu/advice/kurose_wish_advisor.pdf) - Jim Kurose (UMass), slides, year to be confirmed. Ten pieces of advice: learn broadly (especially mathematics), choose problems carefully, write top-down, take the reader's point of view, and give good writing time. 🆓 `kuroseadvice`
- [The illustrated guide to a Ph.D.](https://matt.might.net/articles/phd-school-in-pictures/) - Matt Might, 2010. A series of circle diagrams showing that a PhD means pushing a small dent outward in the boundary of human knowledge. See also [3 qualities of successful Ph.D. students](https://matt.might.net/articles/successful-phd-students/). 🆓 `might2010illustrated` `might2010successful`
- [A graduate school survival guide: "So long, and thanks for the Ph.D!"](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~azuma/hitch4.html) - Ronald Azuma, 1997 (revised 2003). The resilience and skills a PhD requires, and job hunting after graduation. 🆓 `azuma2003survival`
- [Expectation Setting for a PhD](https://www.eugenevinitsky.com/posts/expectation-setting) - Eugene Vinitsky, year to be confirmed. How a PhD student and advisor set mutual expectations. 🆓 `vinitskyexpectation`
- [How to meet with your advisor?](https://twitter.com/jbhuang0604/status/1653614931202195458) - Jia-Bin Huang, tweet thread 2023. Share results asynchronously before the meeting, prepare an agenda, give context before details, propose next steps yourself, and write notes right after the meeting. 🆓 `huang2023advisor`
- [How to cope with paper rejection?](https://twitter.com/jbhuang0604/status/1443248831102136333) - Jia-Bin Huang, tweet thread 2021. Rejection is the norm; treat reviews as a list of improvements, and consider keeping a "CV of failures." 🆓 `huang2021rejection`
- [Behind the Scenes: The Struggle for Each Paper](https://jeffhuang.com/struggle_for_each_paper/) - Jeff Huang (Brown), 2021. Goes paper by paper through the rejections, timing, and collaborations behind 30 of his own papers, showing that publishing is never smooth. 🆓 `huang2021struggle`
- [AI research is a max-performance domain](https://jasonwei.net/blog/ai-research-is-a-max-performance-domain) - Jason Wei, 2025. AI research is judged by peak performance: being outstanding at one skill can make up for weaknesses elsewhere. See also [Dopamine cycles in AI research](https://jasonwei.net/blog/e2jwko63n64ot4kkvqdd7nlejd2m3x). 🆓 `wei2025maxperf`
- [Career Advice](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/career-advice/) - Terence Tao (University of California, Los Angeles), continuously updated. Career advice for mathematical research, organized by stage (undergraduate, graduate, postdoc), such as attending many talks and accepting partial progress. 🆓
- [Applying to Ph.D. Programs in Computer Science](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf) - Mor Harchol-Balter (Carnegie Mellon University), 2014. A guide to applying for computer science PhD programs: what admissions committees look for, recommendation letters and research experience, and how to choose schools and advisors. 🆓 `harcholbalter2014applying`
- [How to Choose Your Grad School](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/) - Tim Dettmers, 2022. Choosing a graduate school by advisor, peers, location, funding, and more. - [AI research: the unreasonably narrow path and how not to be miserable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0blQp0_9NwY) - Rosanne Liu, talk, 2020. ## Research skills (to be evaluated)

> These repositories turn the research process into concrete step-by-step procedures (most are published as skills for AI tools such as Claude Code). They are another kind of best-practice source: different people break the same process down differently. For most of them there is no public evidence that following them improves research quality, so evaluate them against experience-based sources and methodological standards before adopting them.

- [Orchestra-Research/AI-Research-SKILLs](https://github.com/Orchestra-Research/AI-Research-SKILLs) - 98 skills, mostly machine learning engineering (fine-tuning, distributed training, inference deployment, and so on). MIT. 📦
- [K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills) - Formerly claude-scientific-skills. 177 skills for bioinformatics, cheminformatics, clinical research, materials science, and other fields. MIT. 📦
- [Imbad0202/academic-research-skills](https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills) - A Claude Code plugin with deep research, paper writing, reviewing, and a 10-stage pipeline with human checkpoints. **CC BY-NC 4.0, no commercial use.** 📦
- [brycewang-stanford/Awesome-Agent-Skills-for-Empirical-Research](https://github.com/brycewang-stanford/Awesome-Agent-Skills-for-Empirical-Research) - Agent skills for empirical research (AERS), leaning toward economics and statistical methods. Now renamed Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills (the old address redirects). CC BY-SA 4.0. 📦
- [InternScience/Awesome-Scientific-Skills](https://github.com/InternScience/Awesome-Scientific-Skills) - An index of agent skills for research. MIT. 📦

### Empirical software engineering research

The following come from the software engineering group at Heidelberg University (se-uhd). They have few stars, but the authors are authoritative and the repositories were still being updated in September and October 2026. None has a public evaluation of its effectiveness.

- [se-uhd/llm-guidelines-skill](https://github.com/se-uhd/llm-guidelines-skill) - Turns the 8 guidelines of `baltes2026guidelines` into a skill: explore mode for discussing the guidelines and planning a study, review mode for checking a paper draft against the guidelines and producing a report. Own content CC BY 4.0. 📦
- [se-uhd/llm-guidelines-paper](https://github.com/se-uhd/llm-guidelines-paper) - LaTeX source of the guidelines above and the source of the content on llm-guidelines.org. CC BY 4.0. 📦
- [se-uhd/cea-skill](https://github.com/se-uhd/cea-skill) - Claim-Evidence Alignment: extracts quantitative claims and their chains of evidence from a paper PDF and checks each one against the paper and the artifact; Claude drafts, a human verifies. Useful for replication and reviewing. MIT. 📦
- [se-uhd/hallucite](https://github.com/se-uhd/hallucite) - Detects fabricated (hallucinated) references in paper PDFs, with a scoring script; its reported numbers are to be confirmed. MIT. 📦
- [se-uhd/ai-slop-skill](https://github.com/se-uhd/ai-slop-skill) - Detects and revises "AI-sounding" prose in manuscripts, aimed at empirical software engineering papers. Own content CC BY 4.0. 📦
- [keemanxp/slr-prisma](https://github.com/keemanxp/slr-prisma) - Guides you through writing a systematic review in six stages, following the 27-item PRISMA 2020 checklist. Author background unclear; aimed at general disciplines rather than the Kitchenham approach used in software engineering. MIT. 📦

- [SamuelSchmidgall/AgentLaboratory](https://github.com/SamuelSchmidgall/AgentLaboratory) - An end-to-end research agent for literature review, experiments, and report writing, with role prompts for each stage; the paper (arXiv:2501.04227) includes a human evaluation. MIT. 📦
