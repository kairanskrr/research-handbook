# American Historical Association 2023: Statement on Standards of Professional Conduct

- Source: `aha2023standards`, [original (Internet Archive snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/20240604095630/https://www.historians.org/jobs-and-professional-development/statements-standards-and-guidelines-of-the-discipline/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct)
- Version: statement of the American Historical Association (AHA), comprehensively revised in 2005 and most recently revised in January 2023. The website returned 403 to scripted access, so these excerpts come from the Internet Archive snapshot of 2024-06-04 (old URL `historians.org/jobs-and-professional-development/statements-standards-and-guidelines-of-the-discipline/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct`). Locations give section numbers and titles.

### E1

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "By “documents,” historians typically mean all forms of evidence—not just written texts, but artifacts, images, video, statistics, oral recollections, the built and natural environment, and many other things—that have survived as records of former times."
- Key point: By "documents," historians mean all surviving records of the past: not just written texts, but artifacts, images, video, statistics, oral recollections, the built and natural environment, and more.

### E2

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "This distinction between primary and secondary sources is among the most fundamental that historians make. Drawing the boundary between them is a good deal more complicated than it might seem, since determining whether a document is primary or secondary largely depends on the questions one asks of it."
- Key point: The distinction between primary and secondary sources is among the most fundamental in history; but the boundary is not simple, and whether a document counts as primary or secondary depends largely on the question you ask of it.

### E3

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "It enables other historians to retrace the steps in an argument to make sure those steps are justified by the sources."
- Key point: The scholarly apparatus of notes and bibliography lets other historians retrace each step of an argument and check that it is justified by the sources.

### E4

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "The sloppier their apparatus, the harder it is for other historians to trust their work."
- Key point: The sloppier the apparatus, the harder it is for other historians to trust the work.

### E5

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "Doing justice to those views means to some extent trying (never wholly successfully) to see their worlds through their eyes."
- Key point: Doing justice to the views of people in the past means trying (never wholly successfully) to see their worlds through their eyes.

### E6

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "They should report their findings as accurately as possible and not omit evidence that runs counter to their own interpretation."
- Key point: Report findings as accurately as possible and do not omit evidence that runs counter to your own interpretation.

### E7

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Historians should acknowledge the receipt of any financial support, sponsorship, or unique privileges (including special access to research material) related to their research, especially when such privileges could bias their research findings."
- Key point: Acknowledge any financial support, sponsorship, or unique privileges (including special access to research material), especially when they could bias the conclusions.

### E8

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Certain kinds of research, certain forms of employment, and certain techniques (for instance, in conducting oral history interviews) sometimes entail promises about what a historian will and will not do with the resulting knowledge. Historians should honor all such promises."
- Key point: Some research (for example, oral history interviews) involves promises about how material will be used; historians must honor those promises.

### E9

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "They should define any confidentiality requirements before their research begins, and give public notice of any conditions or rules that may affect the content of their work."
- Key point: Define confidentiality requirements before research begins, and give public notice of any conditions or rules that may affect the content of the research.

### E10

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "They should be careful to avoid any actions that might prejudice access for future historians."
- Key point: Avoid any action that might prejudice access to sources for future historians.

### E11

- Location: §4 Plagiarism
- Quote: "Borrowing unexamined primary source references from a secondary work without citing that work is likewise inappropriate."
- Key point: Borrowing primary source references you have not examined from a secondary work, without citing that work, is likewise inappropriate.

### E12

- Location: §4 Plagiarism
- Quote: "A basic rule of good note-taking requires every researcher to distinguish scrupulously between exact quotation and paraphrase."
- Key point: A basic rule of note-taking: distinguish scrupulously between exact quotation and paraphrase.

### E13

- Location: §8 Reputation and Trust
- Quote: "“Forthcoming”: a completed manuscript has been accepted by a press or journal. “Under contract to . . .”: a press and an author have signed a contract for a book in progress, but the final manuscript has not yet been submitted."
- Key point: The AHA's suggested terms for work in progress: "forthcoming" means a completed manuscript has been accepted by a press or journal; "under contract" means the book is still being written and a contract has been signed with a press, but the final manuscript has not been delivered.

### E14

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "Because the record of the past is so fragmentary, absolute historical knowledge is denied us."
- Key point: Because the record of the past is fragmentary, absolute historical knowledge is impossible.

### E15

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Historians should document their findings and be prepared to make available their sources, evidence, and data, including any documentation they develop through interviews."
- Key point: Document your findings and be prepared to make your sources, evidence, and data available, including records produced through interviews.

### E16

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "We honor the historical record, but understand that its interpretation constantly evolves as historians analyze primary documents in light of the ever-expanding body of secondary literature that places those documents in a larger context."
- Key point: Interpretation of the historical record constantly evolves, as historians analyze primary documents in the light of an ever-expanding body of secondary scholarship.

### E17

- Location: §2 Shared Values of Historians
- Quote: "They do not fabricate evidence."
- Key point: Historians do not fabricate evidence.

### E18

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "They should always acknowledge assistance received from colleagues, students, research assistants, and others, and give due credit to collaborators."
- Key point: Always acknowledge help from colleagues, students, research assistants, and others, and give collaborators due credit.

### E19

- Location: §4 Plagiarism
- Quote: "The plagiarist’s standard defense—that he or she was misled by hastily taken and imperfect notes—is plausible only in the context of a wider tolerance of shoddy work."
- Key point: Plagiarists often claim they were misled by hastily taken notes; this defense is plausible only where shoddy work is widely tolerated.

### E20

- Location: §8 Reputation and Trust
- Quote: "Historians should not list among the completed achievements on their resumes degrees or honors they have never earned, jobs they have never held, articles or books they have never written or published, or any comparable misrepresentations of their creative or professional work."
- Key point: Do not list on your resume degrees or honors not earned, jobs not held, or articles or books not written or published as completed achievements.

### E21

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Although they recognize the legitimacy of restricting access to some sources for national security, proprietary, and privacy reasons, they have a professional interest in opposing unnecessary restrictions whenever appropriate."
- Key point: Historians recognize that restricting access to some sources for national security, proprietary, and privacy reasons is legitimate, but should oppose unnecessary restrictions where appropriate.

### E22

- Location: §2 Professional Integrity
- Quote: "Any changes to a primary source or published secondary work, whether digital or print, should be noted."
- Key point: Any changes to a primary source or published work, digital or print, should be noted.

### E23

- Location: §2 Professional Integrity
- Quote: "The ease with which digitally preserved records are reproduced and accessed facilitates this practice but makes accurate citation even more vital."
- Key point: Digitized records are easy to reproduce and access, which makes accurate citation even more vital.

### E24

- Location: §2 Professional Integrity
- Quote: "contributing in a fair-minded way to ongoing scholarly and public debates over what those sources tell us about the past and also what they fail to illuminate."
- Key point: Professional historical work contributes fair-mindedly to debates over what sources tell us about the past, and also what they fail to illuminate.

### E25

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Historians should not misrepresent their sources. They should report their findings as accurately as possible and not omit evidence that runs counter to their own interpretation."
- Key point: Do not misrepresent sources; report findings as accurately as possible and do not omit evidence that runs counter to your interpretation.

### E26

- Location: §3 Scholarship
- Quote: "Historians sometimes appropriately agree to restrictive conditions about the use of particular sources."
- Key point: Historians sometimes appropriately agree to restrictive conditions on the use of particular sources.
