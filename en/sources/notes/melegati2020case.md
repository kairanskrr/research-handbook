# Melegati & Wang 2020: Case Survey Studies in Software Engineering Research

- Source: `melegati2020case`, [arXiv:2007.13592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13592)
- Version: arXiv v1 (2020-07-27), the authors' accepted manuscript (postprint); published version in ESEM 2020.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "It is based on existing case studies reported in the literature and intends to harness the generalizability of survey and the depth of case study."
- Key point: The case survey method builds on existing case studies reported in the literature and aims to combine the generalizability of surveys with the depth of case studies.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "we performed a systematic mapping study and analyzed 12 Software Engineering studies that used the case survey method" / "They have not applied the case survey method consistently as defined in the seminal methodological papers."
- Key point: Through a mapping study the authors found 12 software engineering papers that said they used a case survey, and found that they did not apply the method consistently with its definition in the seminal methodology papers.

### E3

- Location: §2 The Case Survey Method
- Quote: "Larsson [19] described the method in four steps: i) select a group of existing case studies, ii) design a coding scheme to convert case descriptions into quantitative variables, iii) code the cases using multiple raters, and iv) analyze the results statistically."
- Key point: Larsson summarized the case survey in four steps: select a group of existing case studies; design a coding scheme that converts case descriptions into quantitative variables; have multiple raters code the cases; analyze statistically.

### E4

- Location: §2, Larsson's 12 steps: "initial research question" and "case selection criteria"
- Quote: "Larsson [19] argued that case surveys could be used to test hypotheses or explore but stressed the importance of theory to select cases properly." / "It should be based on how much data is reported rather than “how, when, or where” it was published. Besides that, a large number of cases is needed for statistical hypothesis testing."
- Key point: Case surveys can test hypotheses or explore, but case selection needs a theoretical basis; selection criteria should depend on how much data a case reports, not on where, when, or how it was published; and statistical testing needs many cases.

### E5

- Location: §2, summary of the three key steps
- Quote: "For instance, Larsson mentioned his study on mergers and acquisitions, where he employed a minimum requirement of two pages describing the business and human issues."
- Key point: Example: in his study of mergers and acquisitions, Larsson required at least two pages describing the business and people issues before a case could be included.

### E6

- Location: §2, "Creation of the code scheme"
- Quote: "It is determined by a trade-off between “resource-saving, reliable simplicity, and information-rich complexity.”"
- Key point: The coding scheme is set by a trade-off between "resource-saving, reliable simplicity, and information-rich complexity."

### E7

- Location: §2, "Coding the cases with multiple-raters" and "Measure of inter-rater reliability"
- Quote: "Several authors [15, 19] suggested that at least two, preferably three raters, are employed." / "this aspect is a crucial measure of coding quality, but focusing too deep on it may be counter-productive"
- Key point: Use at least two, preferably three, raters; interrater agreement is a crucial measure of coding quality, but obsessing over it can be counterproductive.

### E8

- Location: §2, "Participation of original authors"
- Quote: "Larsson [19] suggested that original case study authors could be employed as the thirdrater for their studies. Since they had contact with primary data, they may have other insights since part of the data may have been left out of the report because of space constraints."
- Key point: The authors of the original case studies can serve as the third rater: they had contact with the primary data, some of which may have been left out of the report for space reasons.

### E9

- Location: §2, "Statistical analysis of the impact of specific case characteristics"
- Quote: "evaluation if case study aspects such as research design, publication, and time influenced the coding."
- Key point: Check whether features of the original case studies, such as research design, publication, and time, influenced the coding.

### E10

- Location: §2.1 Differences to other research methods
- Quote: "can achieve [cross-case pattern analysis], but the resource-consuming, intensive research they require typically limits case sets to smaller sizes than are needed to benefit from advanced statistical cross-case analysis." / "are performed by the same person, with the same purpose, perspective, method, and theoretical framework."
- Key point: Multiple-case studies can also do cross-case pattern analysis, but the effort they require usually keeps the number of cases below what statistical analysis needs; and a multiple-case study is done by the same people with the same purpose, perspective, methods, and theoretical framework (unlike the original cases in a case survey).

### E11

- Location: §2.1
- Quote: "a case survey study is similar to an SLR since its goal is to synthesize evidence rather than structure a research area as in an SMS. Nevertheless, case surveys are constrained to analyze studies describing cases using quantitative techniques on data obtained through a defined coding scheme."
- Key point: Like a systematic review, a case survey aims to synthesize evidence rather than map the structure of a research area as a mapping study does; but a case survey analyzes only studies that describe cases, and analyzes the data produced by its coding scheme quantitatively.

### E12

- Location: §6 Conclusions
- Quote: "The most common approach labeled as case survey is actually based on primary data collected through questionnaires or online resources such as blog posts. The other authors used the term case survey to describe multiple-case studies with a large number of cases."
- Key point: Most studies labeled "case surveys" are actually based on primary data collected through questionnaires or online sources such as blog posts; the rest are multiple-case studies with many cases.

### E13

- Location: §5 Discussion
- Quote: "But, the depth and breadth of the data about that case, generally a questionnaire answer or a blog post from only one person, weakens this claim." / "using only one source to describe a case jeopardizes one key element for case studies: triangulation."
- Key point: Treating a single questionnaire answer or blog post as a "case" does not hold up: the data lack depth and breadth and come from a single source, losing a key element of case study research, triangulation (corroboration across multiple sources).

### E14

- Location: §5 Discussion
- Quote: "When the use deviates from the canonical approach, without proper reflection on the consequences, the validity of the research findings may be put into question."
- Key point: When a study deviates from the canonical approach without proper reflection on the consequences, the validity of its findings may be put in question.
