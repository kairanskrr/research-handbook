### E1

- Location: Basic Information About CADDIS, What is CADDIS?
- Quote: "CADDIS, or the Causal Analysis/Diagnosis Decision Information System, is an on-line application designed to help users conduct causal assessments, primarily in stream" / "It provides a logical, step-by-step framework for stressor identification"
- Key point: CADDIS is an EPA online application that helps users conduct causal assessments, mainly in stream ecosystems, with a step-by-step framework for identifying the "stressors" (causes of biological impairment).

### E2

- Location: About Causal Assessment, A Philosophical System
- Quote: "For example, we may determine that an effluent is the likely cause of an impairment using abduction. We then can deduce that eliminating the effluent would result in biotic recovery. Environmental monitoring could then be used inductively to infer that effluent was indeed the cause."
- Key point: Example: first infer by abduction that an effluent is the likely cause of an impairment; then deduce that eliminating the effluent should let the biota recover; then confirm through environmental monitoring that it really was the cause.

### E3

- Location: About Causal Assessment, The Issue
- Quote: "these methods might evaluate if silt can decrease caddisflies, instead of whether silt caused decreased caddisflies at a specific site. CADDIS addresses causation for specific, individual cases."
- Key point: Most causal analysis methods answer general questions (can silt decrease caddisflies?); CADDIS addresses specific cases (did silt cause the decrease in caddisflies at this site?).

### E4

- Location: About Causal Assessment, Weighing of Evidence
- Quote: "We believe that all relevant evidence should be considered." / "Common sources include site observations, regional monitoring studies, environmental manipulations, laboratory experiments, and general scientific knowledge."
- Key point: All relevant evidence should be considered; common sources are site observations, regional monitoring studies, environmental manipulation experiments, laboratory experiments, and general scientific knowledge.

### E5

- Location: About Causal Assessment, Rejection
- Quote: "we recognize that one can more confidently eliminate than accept a causal hypothesis. For example, suppose an effect occurs downstream of a source. This provides weak supporting evidence for emissions from that source as a cause. However, if the effect occurs upstream of the source, rejection with confidence is possible." / "Therefore, we begin weighing evidence by rejecting as many causes as possible."
- Key point: You can eliminate a causal hypothesis more confidently than you can accept one. An effect occurring downstream of a source is only weak evidence for it; an effect occurring upstream of the source lets you confidently eliminate it. So first eliminate as many candidate causes as you can.

### E6

- Location: Step 5. Identify Probable Causes, Overview
- Quote: "This is why several candidate causes are listed at the beginning of the causal analysis process." / "Then, each candidate cause is compared to every other candidate cause to determine which causes led to the specific effects."
- Key point: The causal analysis lists several candidate causes at the outset, and at the end compares each candidate cause with every other one.

### E7

- Location: Step 5. Identify Probable Causes, Overview
- Quote: "In some situations, no cause is identified or the confidence in conclusions will be too low to support management action." / "A classification of each candidate cause as refuted, diagnosed, probable, unlikely or uncertain"
- Key point: Sometimes no cause is identified, or confidence in the conclusions is too low to support management action; the output of Step 5 includes a classification of each candidate cause as eliminated, diagnosed, probable, unlikely, or uncertain.

### E8

- Location: About Scoring the Evidence, Overview
- Quote: "the highest scores are given to the types of evidence: That use data from the site, That are based on more than one association, That closely link the proximate cause and the effect."
- Key point: The highest scores go to evidence that uses data from the site, is based on more than one association, and closely links the proximate cause to the effect.

### E9

- Location: Step 5. Identify Probable Causes, Evaluating the Quantity and Quality of Evidence
- Quote: "Increasing the number of types or pieces of evidence decreases the likelihood that any one faulty study or data set will mislead you."
- Key point: More types and pieces of evidence lower the chance that any one faulty study or data set will mislead you.
