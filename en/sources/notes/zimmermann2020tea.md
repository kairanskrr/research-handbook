# Zimmermann et al. 2020: Techno-Economic Assessment Guidelines for CO2 Utilization

- Source: `zimmermann2020tea`, [original](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2020.00005/full), DOI: 10.3389/fenrg.2020.00005
- Version: Frontiers in Energy Research, vol. 8, article 5, January 31, 2020, open access under a Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) license. It is a summary of the techno-economic assessment part of the 2018 "Techno-Economic Assessment & Life Cycle Assessment Guidelines for CO2 Utilization" published by the Global CO2 Initiative (University of Michigan, USA) and other institutions. It applies to Carbon Capture and Utilization (CCU) technologies. The web page has no page numbers, so locations give the subheading. Accessed 2026-10-06.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "No generally accepted techno-economic assessment (TEA) standard has evolved, and assessment studies often result in “apples vs. oranges” comparisons, a lack of transparency and a lack of comparability to other studies."
- Key point: In carbon capture and utilization, there is no generally accepted standard for techno-economic assessment (TEA); assessment studies often make "apples vs. oranges" comparisons, lack transparency, and cannot be compared with other studies.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "For CCU, TEA is reported to be commonly used in industrial companies following internal standards—however usually remains unpublished."
- Key point: Companies are reported to commonly run techno-economic assessments of carbon capture and utilization following internal standards, but they usually do not publish them.

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Most academic TEAs in CCU follow chemical engineering text books such as Peters et al. (2003); Sinnott and Towler (2009), and Turton et al. (2012)."
- Key point: Most academic techno-economic assessments in carbon capture and utilization follow chemical engineering textbooks.

### E4

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "the feedback of more than 50 experts from industry, academia and policy"
- Key point: The guidelines incorporate feedback from more than 50 experts from industry, academia, and policy.

### E5

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Goal › Perspectives and Principles of Assessment Goals
- Quote: "First and foremost, all assessments need to be based on process concepts that are technologically plausible; for example, proposed concepts do not violate the laws of thermodynamics. Before the assessment, a “sanity check,” for example, checking kinetics as well as mass and energy balances, needs to be conducted"
- Key point: Every assessment must rest on a technologically plausible process concept, for example one that does not violate the laws of thermodynamics; before the assessment, run a "sanity check" of the kinetics and the mass and energy balances.

### E6

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Goal › Perspectives and Principles of Assessment Goals
- Quote: "The study context, especially comparison to what, location, time horizon, scale and partners"
- Key point: The goal of the assessment should spell out the study context, especially what it is compared to, location, time horizon, scale, and partners.

### E7

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Goal › Perspectives and Principles of Assessment Goals
- Quote: "Commissioners and authors of the study (e.g., funding organization, university, company, individual)"
- Key point: The goal of the assessment should state who commissioned and who carried out the study (funding organization, university, company, individual).

### E8

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Scope › Including or excluding CCU upstream processes in system boundaries
- Quote: "Therefore, such exclusion cannot result in zero cost input flows, as it is unlikely that suppliers provide CO2 or H2 or electricity without charge."
- Key point: Excluding upstream steps from the system boundary does not mean those inputs can be costed at zero; suppliers will not provide CO2, hydrogen, or electricity free of charge.

### E9

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Data Quality
- Quote: "If practitioners cannot improve data quality to a satisfactory level, they might not be able to answer the questions posed in the goal. Thus, they should either adjust goal and scope according to data availability or discontinue the study."
- Key point: If data quality cannot be raised to a satisfactory level, the questions posed in the goal cannot be answered; then either adjust the goal and scope to the data available or discontinue the study.

### E10

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Data Quality
- Quote: "Practitioners shall state any problems with the acquisition of confidential data clearly."
- Key point: Any problems with acquiring confidential data must be stated clearly.

### E11

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Collecting Data
- Quote: "When collecting data from different sources, practitioners should carry out harmonization, which means keeping uniformity and aligning assumptions (e.g., adapting data to the same year, continuous use of lower heating value)."
- Key point: When collecting data from different sources, harmonize them and align assumptions, for example by adapting all data to the same year and consistently using lower heating values.

### E12

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Collecting Data
- Quote: "If the technology maturity is low, experts have to include learning curves and improvements for the system elements, for both reference product systems as well as the benchmark systems"
- Key point: When technology maturity is low, include learning curves and improvements, for both the system being assessed and the benchmark systems.

### E13

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Other Key CCU Inputs › Hydrogen as an input
- Quote: "Assuming an optimistic future hydrogen production technology or a larger than currently feasible production scale in the base case scenario"
- Key point: One common pitfall: assuming an optimistic future hydrogen production technology, or a larger production scale than is currently feasible, in the base case scenario.

### E14

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Other Key CCU Inputs › Electricity as an input
- Quote: "Assuming free or negative electricity prices in the base case scenario."
- Key point: One common pitfall: assuming free or negative electricity prices in the base case scenario.

### E15

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Inventory › Deriving a CO2 Price
- Quote: "One typical pitfall when deriving a CO2 price from literature is mixing up the cost of CO2 captured, and the cost of CO2 avoided."
- Key point: A typical pitfall when taking a CO2 price from the literature is mixing up the cost of CO2 captured and the cost of CO2 avoided.

### E16

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Calculation › Best Practices
- Quote: "practitioners shall organize indicator calculation separately from inventory (in a different file or sheet) but link to it allowing indicators to update following changes of the inventory."
- Key point: Keep the indicator calculation separate from the inventory (in a different file or sheet) but linked to it, so that indicators update automatically when the inventory changes.

### E17

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Interpretation › Uncertainty and Sensitivity Analysis
- Quote: "Practitioners shall provide conclusions, limitations, and a basis for recommendations in the report—which actively build on the analysis of uncertainty and sensitivity."
- Key point: The conclusions, limitations, and recommendations in the report must build on the uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.

### E18

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Interpretation › Interpretation of Indicators
- Quote: "Indicators, in particular the ones without an inherent comparison, shall be interpreted by comparing the indicator value of the product system in focus to one or multiple benchmark values."
- Key point: Indicators (especially those without an inherent comparison) should be interpreted by comparing them to one or more benchmark values.

### E19

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Reporting › Content
- Quote: "In particular, the report shall present all assumptions, data, methods, results, recommendations, and limitations transparently and as detailed as possible given the audience and style of the report."
- Key point: The report must present all assumptions, data, methods, results, recommendations, and limitations transparently and in as much detail as possible.

### E20

- Location: TEA Guidelines › Reporting › Reporting Styles
- Quote: "Finally, the report should list the names and backgrounds of the practitioners that carried out the study and include a description of whether and how a review was conducted."
- Key point: The report should list the names and backgrounds of the practitioners who carried out the assessment and say whether and how a review was conducted.
