# US FDA 2024: Quality Management System Regulation final rule (Federal Register notice, including preamble)

- Source: `fda2024qmsr`, [original (govinfo, Federal Register 89 FR 7496)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-02/html/2024-01709.htm)
- Version: Final rule of the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR), published by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in the Federal Register on February 2, 2024, effective February 2, 2026; preceded by point-by-point responses to public comments (the preamble). Plain-text version read from govinfo on 2026-10-11. Locations give the comment number and page. The preamble does not directly discuss concept development or feasibility studies, nor say whether the 1996 explanation still applies.

### E1

- Location: response to comment 45, 89 FR 7511
- Quote: "The QMSR, as proposed, retains the scope of the previous Sec.  820.30(a) of the QS regulation and does not modify which devices are subject to these requirements."
- Key point: The new regulation keeps the scope of the old §820.30(a) and does not change which devices are subject to the design and development requirements.

### E2

- Location: response to comment 45, 89 FR 7511
- Quote: "For all devices to which design and development requirements apply, FDA does not expect manufacturers to maintain records of all changes proposed during the very early stages of the design process. However, a successful QMS requires a manufacturer to document design changes made after the initial design inputs have been approved, and/or any changes made to correct design deficiencies once the design has been released to production."
- Key point: FDA does not expect manufacturers to keep records of all changes proposed very early in the design process; but design changes made after the initial design inputs are approved, and changes made after release to production to correct design deficiencies, must be documented.

### E3

- Location: response to comment 76, 89 FR 7517
- Quote: "Because we consider the QS regulation and the QMSR to be substantially similar, we expect to update guidance documents for consistency but do not expect there to be many differences in interpretation of these regulations or application of relevant policies."
- Key point: FDA considers the old and new regulations substantially similar and does not expect many differences in how they are interpreted or how related policies apply.
