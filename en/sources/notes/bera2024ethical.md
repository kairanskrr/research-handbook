# British Educational Research Association 2024: Ethical Guidelines for Educational Research (5th ed.)

- Source: `bera2024ethical`, [original PDF](https://www.bera.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Ethical-Guidelines-for-Educational-Research-5th-edition.pdf)
- Version: Ethical Guidelines for Educational Research, 5th edition, British Educational Research Association (BERA), April 2024, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, free to download. Locations give the guidelines' paragraph numbers (paras. 1-83).

### E1

- Location: Fundamentals, p. 7
- Quote: "The intended audience for these guidelines is anyone undertaking educational research – whether their job description includes research, or they conduct research within the field (for example, while studying for a qualification or with the intention of improving practice). This includes both independent researchers and practitioners based in educational institutions of any kind."
- Key point: The guidelines are for anyone doing educational research, including people researching for a qualification or to improve their own practice, and practitioners in all kinds of educational institutions.

### E2

- Location: para. 11 (Consent)
- Quote: "Researchers should think about whether they should approach gatekeepers before directly approaching participants, and whether they should adopt an institution's own ethical approval and safeguarding procedures; this is usually a requirement."
- Key point: Before approaching participants directly, consider whether to approach gatekeepers first (e.g., school leaders); following the institution's own ethical approval and safeguarding procedures is usually a requirement.

### E3

- Location: para. 18
- Quote: "Institutional leaders may agree to take part, acting as gatekeepers on behalf of members (such as teachers and students in schools). In order to ensure that all participants are as fully informed as possible about the benefits and potential costs of the study, researchers should offer both information and support."
- Key point: In large-scale studies, institutional leaders may agree to take part on behalf of teachers and students; researchers should still offer every participant information and support so they understand the study's benefits and costs as fully as possible.

### E4

- Location: para. 23
- Quote: "Principles of consent apply to children and young people as well as to adults. This is termed assent for those under the age of legal consent [...] in the setting/culture."
- Key point: Consent principles apply to children and young people too; for those under the age of legal consent, this is called assent.

### E5

- Location: para. 23
- Quote: "Information sheets and consent forms should be appropriately designed for participants who may differ in such factors as age, reading ability and attention span."
- Key point: Information sheets and consent forms should be designed for participants' age, reading ability, and attention span.

### E6

- Location: para. 24
- Quote: "This may involve gaining the consent of those responsible for children, such as parents, guardians or others in loco parentis."
- Key point: It may also be necessary to obtain consent from those responsible for children, such as parents or guardians.

### E7

- Location: para. 26
- Quote: "Generally, opt-in procedures for gaining consent are preferred. However, opt-out procedures are acceptable in some circumstances. Researchers have a responsibility to consult local legislation and consider local practice if opt-out procedures are envisaged."
- Key point: Opt-in consent is generally preferred; opt-out procedures are acceptable in some circumstances, but check local legislation and practice.

### E8

- Location: para. 16
- Quote: "In circumstances in which some members of a group (such as students in a class or their parents/guardians) have not given consent to participate, for example in class observation, researchers should decide whether this was an active refusal of consent, in which case they would need to respect this and find a practical solution."
- Key point: For example, if some students or parents in a class being observed have not consented, researchers must decide whether this is an active refusal; if so, they must respect it and find a workable arrangement.

### E9

- Location: para. 17
- Quote: "If the research aims to understand the roles of individuals within the group, then these non-consenting members must not be included in the research. However, it may still be feasible and ethically permissible to record consenting individuals' interactions with anonymised non-consenting individuals."
- Key point: If the research aims to understand individuals' roles within a group, non-consenting members must not be included; but recording consenting individuals' interactions with anonymized non-consenting individuals may still be feasible.

### E10

- Location: para. 19
- Quote: "An important consideration is the extent to which a researcher's reflective research into their own practice impinges upon others – for example, in the case of power relationships arising from the dual roles of teacher/ lecturer/manager and researcher, and their impact on students and colleagues."
- Key point: When researching your own practice, consider the power relationships created by the dual role of teacher (or manager) and researcher, and the impact on students and colleagues.

### E11

- Location: para. 19
- Quote: "These may be addressed appropriately by, for example, making the researcher role very explicit, involving an independent third party in the research process and seeking agreement for politically"
- Key point: Ways to handle the dual role include making the researcher role very explicit and involving an independent third party in the research process.

### E12

- Location: para. 20
- Quote: "This can occur, for example, when the researcher and potential participant are family members, or if the researcher is the participant's teacher/lecturer. Researchers need to consider carefully how to deal with such situations and, if possible, should reassure potential participants that non-participation is entirely acceptable."
- Key point: When the researcher is the participant's teacher, consent may not be truly voluntary; researchers must handle this carefully and, where possible, reassure people that not taking part is entirely fine.

### E13

- Location: para. 35
- Quote: "For example, in an experimental design (including a randomised controlled study), the intervention made available to one group, while being unavailable to the control or comparison group, may be viewed as desirable. In mitigation, for example, an intervention found to be effective can typically be offered to control groups after the end of a trial."
- Key point: In an experiment, offering the intervention to only one group may be seen as unfair; one mitigation is to offer an intervention found effective to the control group after the trial.

### E14

- Location: para. 48
- Quote: "In some cases, such as revelations of abuse or proposed acts of terrorism, researchers may be under statutory duty to disclose confidential information to relevant authorities."
- Key point: In some cases, such as revelations of abuse, researchers may have a statutory duty to disclose confidential information to the relevant authorities.

### E15

- Location: para. 48
- Quote: "There are circumstances in which confidentiality may need to be broken, and information sheets and consent forms should state this."
- Key point: There are circumstances in which confidentiality may need to be broken, and information sheets and consent forms should say so in advance.

### E16

- Location: para. 69
- Quote: "All results of interventions and evaluations, including those that are negative, should be reported. Evaluations should preferably be registered beforehand with an official body that maintains a platform for this purpose (indeed, many sponsors require this)."
- Key point: All results of interventions and evaluations, including negative ones, should be reported; evaluations should preferably be registered beforehand on an official platform, as many sponsors require.

### E17

- Location: para. 67
- Quote: "Ideally, researchers will make shareable anonymised versions of data available for secondary analysis."
- Key point: Ideally, researchers make shareable anonymized versions of their data available for secondary analysis.

### E18

- Location: para. 76 (Authorship)
- Quote: "Where research has involved collaboration across different roles or professions – for example, between education researchers who are academics and those who are teachers or other practitioners – then anyone who has made a substantive contribution should be credited as a co-author."
- Key point: In research involving collaboration between academics and practitioners such as teachers, anyone who made a substantive contribution should be credited as a co-author.

### E19

- Location: para. 77
- Quote: "Academic status or any other indicator of seniority does not determine first authorship. Rather, the order of authorship should reflect relative leadership and contributions made. Alternatively, co-authors may agree to a simple alphabetic listing of their names."
- Key point: Academic status or seniority does not determine first authorship; author order should reflect leadership and contribution, or co-authors may agree on alphabetical order.

### E20

- Location: Aspirations of educational researchers, p. 9
- Quote: "Concepts such as 'data', 'reliability', 'validity', 'credibility', 'trustworthiness', 'subjectivity' and 'objectivity' may therefore be understood and legitimately applied in different ways."
- Key point: Because educational researchers come from many disciplines and use diverse methods, concepts such as "data," "reliability," "validity," and "credibility" can be understood and legitimately applied in different ways.

### E21

- Location: para. 56 (Methods)
- Quote: "These guidelines should not be interpreted as privileging particular research methods over others: the Association respects the diverse range of possible methods."
- Key point: The guidelines do not privilege any research method; BERA respects the diversity of methods.

### E22

- Location: About BERA, p. 4
- Quote: "We run a major international conference each year alongside a diverse and engaging series of events, and publish high quality research in our peer-reviewed journals, reports, book series and the groundbreaking BERA Blog."
- Key point: BERA runs a major international conference each year and publishes peer-reviewed journals, reports, book series, and a blog.

### E23

- Location: para. 37
- Quote: "Researchers should consider the impact of their research on the lives and workloads of participants, particularly when researching vulnerable or over-researched populations."
- Key point: Consider the burden your research places on participants' lives and workloads, especially for vulnerable or over-researched populations.

### E24

- Location: para. 52
- Quote: "For example, a group of teachers engaging in a process of action research as part of curriculum renewal should inform the school management of their intentions."
- Key point: For example, teachers doing action research as part of curriculum renewal should inform school management.

### E25

- Location: Fundamentals, p. 8
- Quote: "It is recognised that educational researchers whose work is conducted under the auspices of an educational institution will be required to seek ethical review and clearance from that institution."
- Key point: Researchers working under the auspices of an educational institution need ethical review and clearance from that institution.
