# Hussey & Hughes 2020: Hidden Invalidity Among 15 Commonly Used Measures in Social and Personality Psychology

- Source: `hussey2020hidden`, [preprint (PsyArXiv)](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/7rbfp); [published version (open access)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245919882903)
- Version: PsyArXiv preprint PDF; the published version appeared in Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science 3(2):166-184 (2020). Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "When the scales were assessed using the modal practice of considering only internal consistency, 89% of them appeared to possess good validity. Yet when validity was assessed comprehensively (via internal consistency, immediate and delayed test-retest reliability, factor structure, and measurement invariance for age and gender groups), only 4% demonstrated good validity." / "We comprehensively assessed structural validity in a uniquely large and varied data set (N = 144,496 experimental sessions) to investigate the psychometric properties of some of the most widely used self-report measures (k = 15 questionnaires, 26 scales)"
- Key point: Using data from more than 140,000 experimental sessions, the authors tested 15 questionnaires (26 subscales) common in social and personality psychology: judged on internal consistency alone, 89% appeared to have good validity; assessed comprehensively (internal consistency, test-retest reliability, factor structure, and measurement invariance across age and gender), only 4% did.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "The first, the substantive phase, involves identifying and defining a construct" / "Quantitative analyses (e.g., item and factor analyses; assessments of consistency, stability, and measurement invariance) are used to determine the psychometric properties of the measure. The third phase, the external phase, involves examining if the measure appropriately represents the construct via checks for convergent and discriminant validity with other measures"
- Key point: Construct validation has three phases: the substantive phase (defining the construct, writing items), the structural phase (item analysis, factor analysis, consistency, stability, measurement invariance), and the external phase (convergent and discriminant validity with other measures, etc.).

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "they found an overreliance on Cronbach's α as the sole source of evidence for structural validity"
- Key point: Flake et al.'s 2017 review found an overreliance on Cronbach's alpha as the sole evidence for structural validity.

### E4

- Location: Purpose of the Present Study
- Quote: "the most common test (α) relies on numerous assumptions that can be assessed only by less commonly applied analyses (e.g., within a CFA context)"
- Key point: The most common test, alpha, relies on many assumptions that can be checked only by less commonly applied analyses (such as confirmatory factor analysis).

### E5

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "if evidence for measurement invariance is not obtained--which is typically the case--then it is difficult to determine if a given measure reflects the same construct across samples, contexts, and conditions"
- Key point: Without evidence of measurement invariance (which is typically missing), it is hard to tell whether a scale measures the same construct across samples and contexts.

### E6

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "In short, replicability does not equal validity."
- Key point: Replicability does not equal validity.

### E7

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "we refer to the first practice as v-hacking: selectively choosing and reporting a combination of metrics, including their implementations and cutoffs, and taking advantage of other degrees of experimenter freedom so as to improve the apparent validity of measures."
- Key point: "V-hacking": selectively choosing and reporting metrics, implementations, and cutoffs to make a scale look more valid.

### E8

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "We are not advocating for the introduction of some set of universally applied metrics or cutoff values for those metrics."
- Key point: The authors do not advocate a universal set of metrics and cutoffs for everyone.

### E9

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "Researcher degrees of freedom could be greatly constrained by expanding the practice of preregistration to also include choices concerning the assessment of structural validity (e.g., metrics, cutoffs, measurement models, and decision-making strategies)."
- Key point: Extending preregistration to cover the assessment of structural validity (metrics, cutoffs, measurement model, decision rules) could greatly constrain researcher degrees of freedom.

### E10

- Location: Purpose of the Present Study
- Quote: "many self-report scales are being used in contexts, and with samples, that differ from those in which they were originally validated."
- Key point: Many self-report scales are used in contexts and with samples that differ from those in which they were originally validated.
