# American Educational Research Association 2006: Standards for Reporting on Empirical Social Science Research in AERA Publications

- Source: `aera2006standards`, [original PDF](https://www.aera.net/Portals/38/docs/12ERv35n6_Standard4Report%20.pdf)
- Version: adopted by the AERA Council in June 2006, published in Educational Researcher 35(6): 33–40; the AERA website provides the PDF free of charge. Locations give section and item numbers.

### E1

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "They cover, but are not limited to, what are commonly called qualitative and quantitative methods."
- Key point: The standards cover, but are not limited to, what are commonly called qualitative and quantitative methods.

### E2

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "[...] acceptability cannot be determined by using a checklist. In a given case, there may be a sound professional reason why a particular standard is inapplicable."
- Key point: Whether a report is acceptable cannot be determined with a checklist; in a given study, there may be a sound professional reason why a particular standard does not apply.

### E3

- Location: Preamble to Standards
- Quote: "Two overarching principles underlie the development of these reporting standards: the sufficiency of the warrants and the transparency of the report."
- Key point: Two overarching principles underlie the standards: sufficiency of warrants for conclusions, and transparency of the report.

### E4

- Location: 2. Design and Logic
- Quote: "Some studies are concerned with drawing causal inferences regarding intended treatments, such as estimating the effects of reducing class size on student achievement. Other studies are concerned with describing particular occurrences and the meanings people give them in a single setting, such as how early literacy is taught and understood in a particular preschool."
- Key point: Education research designs are diverse: some studies draw causal inferences (such as the effect of reducing class size on achievement), while others describe what happens in a particular setting and how people understand it (such as how one kindergarten teaches early literacy).

### E5

- Location: 3.1.b
- Quote: "Descriptions should include how access, selection, and consent of participants were addressed; how rapport was established; what roles were taken by the researcher (e.g., interviewer, observer) and participants (e.g., respondent, informant) in the data collection; and what significant changes, if any, in relationships and roles of researcher and participants occurred over time."
- Key point: Reports should describe how access, selection, and consent of participants were handled, how rapport was established, what roles the researcher and participants took, and whether these relationships and roles changed significantly over time.

### E6

- Location: 3.1.d
- Quote: "When an intervention or treatment is implemented, the intervention or treatment should be described in sufficient detail so that its key features can be identified and used to account for results, and be compared with related interventions or treatments. Similarly, features of control or comparison groups should be described"
- Key point: When an intervention is implemented, describe it in sufficient detail; also describe the control or comparison condition.

### E7

- Location: 5.8
- Quote: "Any considerations that arose in the data collection and processing (e.g., attrition, missing data, ceiling or floor effects, deviations from standard administration of instruments, suspected cheating) that might compromise the validity of the statistical analysis or inferences should be reported."
- Key point: Report anything that arose in data collection and processing that could affect the conclusions, such as attrition, missing data, ceiling or floor effects, deviations from standard administration, or suspected cheating.

### E8

- Location: 5.10
- Quote: "An index of the quantitative relation between variables (an effect size of some kind such as a treatment effect, a regression coefficient, or an odds ratio)"
- Key point: Report an effect size for each key statistical result (such as a treatment effect, a regression coefficient, or an odds ratio).

### E9

- Location: 5.10
- Quote: "An indication of the uncertainty of that index of effect (such as a standard error or a confidence interval)."
- Key point: Also report the uncertainty of the effect size, such as a standard error or confidence interval.

### E10

- Location: 5.10
- Quote: "A qualitative interpretation of the index of the effect that describes its meaningfulness in terms of the questions the study was intended to answer."
- Key point: Also give a qualitative interpretation of what the effect size means for the questions the study set out to answer.

### E11

- Location: 5.12
- Quote: "The evidence that serves as a warrant for each claim should be presented. The sources of evidence and the strength and variety of evidence supporting each claim should be described. Qualifications and conditions should be specified; significant counter-examples should be reported."
- Key point: (Qualitative research) Present the evidence that warrants each claim, with the sources, strength, and variety of that evidence; state qualifications and report important disconfirming evidence.

### E12

- Location: 5.12
- Quote: "Speculations that go beyond the available evidence should be clearly represented as such."
- Key point: Speculations that go beyond the available evidence should be clearly marked as such.

### E13

- Location: 6.2
- Quote: "When generalization is intended, the author should make clear the intended scope of generalization of the findings of the study."
- Key point: When generalization is intended, make clear the intended scope of generalization.

### E14

- Location: 7.1
- Quote: "Study approval in accordance with an Institutional Review Board (IRB) should be stated."
- Key point: The paper should state that the study was approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB).

### E15

- Location: 7.2
- Quote: "Even when direct identifying information is not used, inadvertent disclosure of research participants can happen indirectly."
- Key point: Even when direct identifying information is not used, participants' identities can be disclosed indirectly.

### E16

- Location: 3. Sources of Evidence
- Quote: "Since the role of the researcher and the relationship between the researcher and the participants can influence the data collected, this relationship is addressed in descriptions of sources of evidence."
- Key point: Because the researcher's role and the researcher–participant relationship can influence the data collected, this relationship should be addressed when describing the sources of evidence.
