# Roettger 2019: Researcher degrees of freedom in phonetic research

- Source: `roettger2019researcher`, [original](https://doi.org/10.5334/labphon.147)
- Version: Laboratory Phonology 10(1), open access; the publisher's site blocks scripts, so the text was taken from an Internet Archive snapshot

### E1

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "It is argued that phonetic research faces a large number of researcher degrees of freedom due to its scientific object"
- Key point: Phonetic research has many "researcher degrees of freedom," that is, choices you can make during analysis.

### E2

- Location: §5.2
- Quote: "We should explore our data. Yes. Yet we should not pretend we are testing concrete hypotheses when doing so."
- Key point: Explore your data, but do not present exploration as testing hypotheses set in advance.

### E3

- Location: §5.3
- Quote: "A preregistration is a time-stamped document in which researchers specify exactly how they plan to collect their data and how they plan to conduct their confirmatory analyses."
- Key point: A preregistration is a time-stamped document that specifies in advance how you will collect data and run the confirmatory analyses.

### E4

- Location: §5.4
- Quote: "if we have measured and analyzed ten phonetic parameters to establish whether the final syllable of a word is more prominent than the prefinal syllable, we should provide this information"
- Key point: If you measured ten phonetic parameters, report that honestly.

### E5

- Location: §2
- Quote: "there are good reasons to believe that they are, more often than not, unintentional."
- Key point: Most of the time these problems are not intentional.

### E6

- Location: notes
- Quote: "Note that there are only a few journals on quantitative linguistics that have adopted registered reports."
- Key point: (As of 2019) only a few quantitative linguistics journals had adopted Registered Reports.
