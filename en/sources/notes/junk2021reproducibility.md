# Junk & Lyons 2021: Reproducibility and Replication of Experimental Particle Physics Results

- Source: `junk2021reproducibility`, [original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06864)
- Version: Published in Harvard Data Science Review, CC BY 4.0; this excerpt follows arXiv:2009.06864v4 (2021-05-05). The authors are from Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory, Imperial College London, and the University of Oxford.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Large collaborations produce and check the results, and many papers are signed by more than three thousand authors."
- Key point: Large collaborations produce and check the results, and many papers are signed by more than three thousand authors.

### E2

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "The ATLAS and CMS collaborations at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) comprise more than 3000 physicists each. Large collaboration size is not limited to collider physics – neutrino experiments often have collaborations consisting of several hundreds to more than a thousand members."
- Key point: The ATLAS and CMS collaborations at the Large Hadron Collider each have more than 3,000 physicists; neutrino experiments also often have collaborations of several hundred to more than a thousand people.

### E3

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Particle physics experiments take years to design, build and run, and they require large financial resources, equipment, and effort."
- Key point: Particle physics experiments take years to design, build, and run, and require large financial resources, equipment, and effort.

### E4

- Location: §3, Using the p Value as a Tool for Discovery
- Quote: "The cut-off on p0 is conventionally taken as 2.87 × 10−7, corresponding to a z-score of 5.0."
- Key point: The p-value cut-off for claiming a discovery is conventionally 2.87×10⁻⁷, corresponding to 5 standard deviations (5σ).

### E5

- Location: §3, reasons for 5σ
- Quote: "Past experience shows that effects with z-scores of 3 and 4 have often not been replicated when more data are collected"
- Key point: Historically, 3σ and 4σ effects have often failed to replicate when more data were collected.

### E6

- Location: §3, after the reasons for 5σ
- Quote: "The choice of z ≥5.0 as the requirement for claiming a discovery is arbitrary"
- Key point: Choosing 5σ as the threshold is itself an arbitrary convention.

### E7

- Location: §3, reasons for 5σ
- Quote: "a systematic error that is not covered by an appropriate uncertainty can result in a false discovery of an effect with an arbitrarily high significance."
- Key point: A systematic error not covered by an appropriate uncertainty can produce a false discovery with arbitrarily high significance; 5σ does not protect against this.

### E8

- Location: §6.6 Blind Analyses
- Quote: "Blind analysis methods are designed to prevent experimenters from consciously or unconsciously manipulating their data analyses in order to obtain desired results."
- Key point: Blind analysis is meant to prevent experimenters from consciously or unconsciously tuning their analysis to obtain desired results.

### E9

- Location: §6.6 Blind Analyses
- Quote: "One simple method is to optimize the analysis using simulated data and reserve the input of data from the experimental apparatus until the procedures have been decided upon, including the data selection, classification, and statistical procedures."
- Key point: One simple blinding method: optimize the analysis on simulated data, fixing the event selection, classification, and statistical procedures, before feeding in the real data.

### E10

- Location: §6.6 Blind Analyses
- Quote: "The collaboration must then agree to accept the result of the analysis after the data are input to the analysis (“unblinding”), without change to any step of the analysis, or the procedure is not fully blind."
- Key point: After unblinding, the collaboration must accept the result without changing any step of the analysis; otherwise the procedure is not fully blind.

### E11

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results, Preliminary Results
- Quote: "Results in preparation must pass through a lengthy, formal approval process before they can be presented outside of the collaboration."
- Key point: Results must pass a lengthy, formal approval process before they can be presented outside the collaboration.

### E12

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results, Publication
- Quote: "a committee of collaborators who have worked on similar topics but who are not directly involved in the particular result is set up to review the paper draft."
- Key point: The collaboration sets up a committee to review the paper draft, made up of members who have worked on similar topics but were not directly involved in this result.

### E13

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results, Publication
- Quote: "This process often uncovers even tiny flaws and it can take several months or even years to complete."
- Key point: Internal review often uncovers even tiny flaws and can take months or even years.

### E14

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results, Publication
- Quote: "In these cases, dissenting collaborators can request their names to be removed from the author list of that paper."
- Key point: In the rare cases where consensus cannot be reached, dissenting members can ask to have their names removed from that paper's author list.

### E15

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results, Publication
- Quote: "Sometimes a collaboration may fall into the trap of “group-think,” having repeated the same arguments to itself over and over again, so an independent check has as much value in EPP as in other fields."
- Key point: Collaborations can fall into "group-think," so independent journal peer review is as valuable in particle physics as elsewhere.

### E16

- Location: §6.9, Statistics Committees
- Quote: "It is important for physicists who are embarking on a new analysis to consult with the collaboration’s statistics committee, so that work is not steered in a direction that is only later found to be flawed under collaboration review."
- Key point: Before starting a new analysis, consult the collaboration's statistics committee, so you do not head in a direction that internal review later finds flawed.

### E17

- Location: §4.1 Ensuring Reproducibility as the Results are Made
- Quote: "The first exercise for the new student however is to reproduce the earlier result performed by the previous student using the same data and software."
- Key point: A new student taking over an earlier analysis first reproduces the previous result with the same data and software.

### E18

- Location: §4.1
- Quote: "Reproducibility only tests the integrity of the computational steps, not the correctness of the assumptions entering the analysis or the quality of the input data."
- Key point: Reproducibility only tests that the computational steps are sound, not that the analysis assumptions are correct or the input data are good.

### E19

- Location: §6.2 Software Version Control
- Quote: "Not only must the source code be under strict version control, but so too must the installed environments, which include auxiliary files and databases."
- Key point: Not only the source code but also the installed environments (including auxiliary files and databases) must be under strict version control.

### E20

- Location: §4.1
- Quote: "Running the programs on computers of different architectures and comparing the results has also long been a tradition in EPP."
- Key point: Running the same programs on computers of different architectures and comparing results is a long tradition in experimental particle physics.

### E21

- Location: §7 Special Features of Particle Physics Results
- Quote: "Another peculiarity of EPP is the specialization of practitioners into theoretical and experimental categories."
- Key point: One peculiarity of experimental particle physics is that practitioners specialize as either theorists or experimentalists.

### E22

- Location: §7
- Quote: "The second benefit is that experimentalists almost never test theories that they themselves invented, helping to reduce possible effects of confirmation bias."
- Key point: One benefit of this division: experimentalists almost never test theories they invented themselves, which helps reduce confirmation bias.

### E23

- Location: §7
- Quote: "Models to be tested generally are published in theory or phenomenology papers before the experimental studies are conducted."
- Key point: Models to be tested are generally published in theory or phenomenology papers before the experiments are done.

### E24

- Location: §10 Conclusions
- Quote: "Experimenters do not hesitate to publish null results."
- Key point: Experimenters do not hesitate to publish null results (results that do not find the expected signal).

### E25

- Location: §7
- Quote: "Null results are therefore typically presented as upper limits on signal strengths."
- Key point: Null results are typically presented as upper limits on signal strength.

### E26

- Location: §4.2, Strategies for Publishing and Preserving Analyses
- Quote: "A common failure of published physics results is the lack of communication of correlated uncertainties for the predictions in each bin of each histogram, which precludes the exact reproduction of the interpretation"
- Key point: A common failure of published physics results is not reporting the correlated uncertainties across histogram bins, which prevents others from reproducing the interpretation exactly.

### E27

- Location: §10 Conclusions
- Quote: "every new student is given examples of how well-meaning researchers can come to wrong conclusions because they misled themselves and therefore others."
- Key point: Every new student is given examples of well-meaning researchers who reached wrong conclusions because they misled themselves and therefore others.

### E28

- Location: §3, reasons for 5σ
- Quote: "It is important to have a pre-registered threshold for discovery that is shared by competing collaborations. Otherwise, credit for discoveries will go to those with the lowest standards."
- Key point: Competing collaborations need a shared, pre-registered threshold for discovery; otherwise credit for discoveries goes to those with the lowest standards.

### E29

- Location: §7
- Quote: "the z ≥5.0 criterion for discovery makes more sense in EPP than it does in medical trials, for example."
- Key point: The 5σ threshold makes more sense in particle physics than in fields such as medical trials.

### E30

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results
- Quote: "All collaborators on the author list are given the opportunity to review each result that is published."
- Key point: Every member on the author list has the opportunity to review each result before it is published.

### E31

- Location: Media Summary
- Quote: "p values must be less than 2.87×10−7, not 0.05 as is common in some fields of study."
- Key point: Particle physics requires p-values below 2.87×10⁻⁷ to claim a discovery, not the 0.05 common in some fields.

### E32

- Location: §10 Conclusions
- Quote: "Many analyses are blind. Stringent multi-level review procedures must be followed before results are published."
- Key point: Many analyses in experimental particle physics are blind, and results must pass stringent multi-level review before publication.

### E33

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results
- Quote: "Below, we describe the review procedures used by CDF, a typical hadron collider collaboration."
- Key point: The paper uses CDF, a typical hadron collider collaboration, to illustrate the review process.

### E34

- Location: §3.3 Systematic Uncertainties
- Quote: "Systematic effects are not easy to detect, and in general much more effort is needed to evaluate the corresponding uncertainties."
- Key point: Systematic effects are hard to detect, and evaluating their uncertainties generally takes much more effort.

### E35

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "Measurements nearly always have some bias, and the question is by how much are they biased. Results are corrected for known biases, and the uncertainties in these corrections contribute to the total uncertainties."
- Key point: Measurements nearly always have some bias; the question is how much. Known biases are corrected, and the uncertainties of those corrections count toward the total uncertainty.

### E36

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "They can be detected by the fact that, if the experiment is repeated several times, the measured physical quantity will vary." / "Systematic effects, however, can cause the result to be shifted from its true value, but in a way that does not necessarily change from measurement to measurement."
- Key point: Statistical uncertainty shows up when an experiment is repeated (the result varies); systematic effects can shift the result away from the true value without changing from one measurement to the next, so repetition cannot reveal them.

### E37

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "“Uncertainties” are numerical estimates of the possible values of the errors" / "Particle physicists often use the word “error” when they mean “uncertainty.”"
- Key point: An "error" is the difference between an estimate and the unknown true value; an "uncertainty" is a numerical estimate of the possible values of the error. Particle physicists often say "error" when they mean "uncertainty."

### E38

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "The separation of the two uncertainties conveys to the reader both the claimed accuracy of the result and the claimed precision." / "the reader may judge whether it makes sense to run the experiment longer in order to improve the total uncertainty, or if the result is “systematics limited.”"
- Key point: Reporting statistical and systematic uncertainties separately lets readers judge whether more data would improve the total uncertainty, or whether the result is already "systematics-limited."

### E39

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "In the bulk of a paper, the numerical effects on the total systematic uncertainty are quoted separately for each source."
- Key point: In the body of the paper, list each source's contribution to the total systematic uncertainty separately.

### E40

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "Detailed breakdowns of systematic uncertainties by their source and full explanations of how they were estimated assists in this process. The most problematic sources of systematic error are those that are neglected or incorrectly dismissed."
- Key point: Breaking down systematic uncertainties by source and fully explaining how each was estimated makes later correction and reuse easier; the most problematic sources of systematic error are those neglected or wrongly dismissed.

### E41

- Location: §3.3
- Quote: "If systematic uncertainties remain difficult to control, a solution can be to perform a measurement of a quantity that can be more unambiguously defined, such as the ratio of two production rates."
- Key point: If systematic uncertainties remain hard to control, you can measure a more unambiguously defined quantity instead, such as the ratio of two production rates.

### E42

- Location: §6.1 Monitoring and Recording Experimental Conditions
- Quote: "Environmental variables such as ambient temperature, pressure and humidity are also included in these records."
- Key point: Running conditions, including environmental variables such as ambient temperature, pressure, and humidity, are continuously monitored and recorded for use in the analysis.

### E43

- Location: §6.1
- Quote: "It is up to the shift crew to identify those conditions, notify experts who may be able to repair the errant condition, and mark the data appropriately so they do not bias physics results."
- Key point: The shift crew must identify abnormal conditions, notify experts who can fix them, and mark the affected data so they do not bias physics results.

### E44

- Location: §6.3 Reporting the Expected Sensitivity
- Quote: "A common and necessary practice in EPP is to compute the expected sensitivity of an analysis before the data are collected, or at least before they are analyzed."
- Key point: A common and necessary practice in experimental particle physics is to compute the expected sensitivity of an analysis before the data are collected, or at least before they are analyzed.

### E45

- Location: §6.3
- Quote: "Even if a result fails to be significant, if the sensitivity of the test is high, then the result is worth publishing."
- Key point: Even if a result is not significant, it is worth publishing if the test's sensitivity is high.

### E46

- Location: §6.4 Standard Candles and Control Samples
- Quote: "To show that a known signal is found in the same analysis with the expected strength and properties gives confidence that the whole chain is working as desired."
- Key point: Finding a known signal (a "standard candle") with the expected strength and properties in the same analysis gives confidence that the whole chain works as intended.

### E47

- Location: §6.4
- Quote: "Disagreements in the predictions of background rates and properties from different control samples are often contributions to the systematic uncertainty estimations used in the signal sample."
- Key point: Disagreements between background predictions from different control samples (data known to contain no signal) are often counted toward the systematic uncertainty.

### E48

- Location: §6.5 Dedicated Calibration Working Groups
- Quote: "One avenue that subconscious bias can affect an analysis is in the calibration stage."
- Key point: Subconscious bias can enter the analysis at the calibration stage.

### E49

- Location: §6.6
- Quote: "Yet another blinding procedure which applies to precision measurement of physical quantities is to encode an arbitrary, fixed offset in the final step of parameter inference in software, and to hide the value of this offset from researchers performing the analysis work." / "This method is not used for significance tests."
- Key point: A blinding method for precision measurements: encode an arbitrary, fixed offset in the final parameter inference step of the software and hide its value from the analysts; this method is not used for significance tests.

### E50

- Location: §6.6
- Quote: "Sometimes surprises are found in the control samples – previously unappreciated sources of background events or miscalibrations can show up at this stage."
- Key point: In partially blind analyses, looking at control samples first sometimes reveals surprises: previously unappreciated background sources or miscalibrations.

### E51

- Location: §6.9 Review of Results
- Quote: "There are negative consequences to overestimating systematic uncertainties."
- Key point: Overestimating systematic uncertainties also has costs (e.g., when several measurements are combined, the chi-square is too small, and the uncertainty of the combined result may actually be underestimated).

### E52

- Location: §5 Replication of Experimental Particle Physics Results
- Quote: "In order to tell if a second experimental result successfully replicates the first, shared and independent sources of error must be carefully taken into account."
- Key point: To tell whether a second experiment successfully replicates the first, you must carefully account for the sources of error they share and those that are independent.

### E53

- Location: §5
- Quote: "A result with a very large systematic uncertainty is consistent with more possible true values of the parameter(s) of interest and thus passes replication tests more easily than one with a smaller systematic uncertainty."
- Key point: A result with a large systematic uncertainty is consistent with more possible true values, so it "passes" replication tests more easily.

### E54

- Location: §5
- Quote: "A replication attempt that is not an identical repetition the original experiment, but which rather changes some aspect of it, can be more informative than an exact repetition"
- Key point: A replication that changes some aspect of the original experiment can be more informative than an exact repetition.
