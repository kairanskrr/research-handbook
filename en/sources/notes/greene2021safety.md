# Greene et al. 2021: Safety and Belonging in the Field: A Checklist for Educators

- Source: `greene2021safety`, [Original](https://go-outdoors.caltech.edu/documents/20636/greene-et-al_2021.pdf)
- Version: EarthArXiv preprint (2021), not peer reviewed; the authors are staff and students in geography, earth, and environmental sciences at UK universities. Read from the preprint PDF reposted on the Caltech website. The checklist targets **teaching field trips**; some items also apply to research fieldwork. Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §1 Author Positionality Statement
- Quote: "The authors comprise current and former staff and students in UK Higher Education spanning a variety of GEES disciplines."
- Key point: The authors are current and former staff and students at UK universities in geography, earth, and environmental sciences (GEES).

### E2

- Location: §4.2.2 Permission/Ethics
- Quote: "Meeting legal requirements is a minimum standard. Check whether any of the sites you plan to visit are protected (this includes biology and environment, not just rocks) and whether your intended activities are permissible."
- Key point: Meeting legal requirements is the minimum standard: check whether the sites you plan to visit are protected (biology and environment too, not just rocks) and whether your intended activities are permitted.

### E3

- Location: §4.2.2 Permission/Ethics
- Quote: "Obtain relevant permissions (e.g., site access). Obtain sampling permission and export/import permits, if applicable."
- Key point: Obtain site access permission, sampling permission, and export/import permits where applicable.

### E4

- Location: §4.2.2 Permission/Ethics
- Quote: "Beyond legality, the fieldwork must also be ethical." / "Whose land is it, are locals involved in your work, and are they mutually benefitting (or at minimum not being harmed) by your visit"
- Key point: Beyond legality, fieldwork must be ethical: whose land is it, are locals involved, and do they benefit from (or at minimum not get harmed by) your visit?

### E5

- Location: §4.2.6 Health, Safety, Hazards, and Risks
- Quote: "Hazard and risk assessment needs to be checked off by a third party and communicated to all participants prior to the activity commencing so that everyone is familiar with the risks and control measures"
- Key point: The hazard and risk assessment must be checked by a third party and shared with all participants before the activity starts.

### E6

- Location: §4.2.6 Health, Safety, Hazards, and Risks
- Quote: "Investigate local emergency services. Whom do you call in a medical emergency? Where is the nearest hospital?"
- Key point: Find out about local emergency services in advance: whom to call in a medical emergency and where the nearest hospital is.

### E7

- Location: §4.2.6 Health, Safety, Hazards, and Risks
- Quote: "Review previous years' incident and near miss forms"
- Key point: Review previous years' incident and near-miss records.

### E8

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "To guarantee safety and belonging, participants' identities (backgrounds and protected characteristics) must be considered as a part of fieldwork planning and implementation."
- Key point: To guarantee everyone's safety and belonging, fieldwork planning must take participants' identities and backgrounds into account.

### E9

- Location: §4.2.5 Scheduling
- Quote: "In addition to factors like seasonal weather and tides, consider religious calendars when scheduling your field dates."
- Key point: When scheduling field dates, consider religious calendars as well as seasonal weather and tides.

### E10

- Location: §4.1.1 Training
- Quote: "Physical first aid training for staff (and sometimes students) is standard, but is also insufficient to ensure the health and safety of all participants."
- Key point: First aid training is standard but on its own is not enough to ensure the health and safety of all participants.

### E11

- Location: §4.2.2 Permission/Ethics
- Quote: "Whose land is it, are locals involved in your work, and are they mutually benefitting (or at minimum not being harmed) by your visit"
- Key point: Whose land is it, are locals involved in your work, and do they benefit from (or at minimum not get harmed by) your visit?
