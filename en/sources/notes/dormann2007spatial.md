# Dormann et al. 2007: Methods to account for spatial autocorrelation in the analysis of species distributional data: a review

- Source: `dormann2007spatial`, [Original](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2007.0906-7590.05171.x)
- Version: Ecography 30: 609–628, 2007. We read the PDF in the University of Lausanne repository (iris.unil.ch), marked by Unpaywall as the submitted version. A methods review of spatial autocorrelation in species distribution data, with 17 authors. Locations give paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, paragraph 2
- Quote: "Spatial autocorrelation (SAC) occurs when the values of variables sampled at nearby locations are not independent from each other"
- Key point: Spatial autocorrelation means that values of a variable sampled at nearby locations are not independent of each other.

### E2

- Location: Introduction, paragraph 2
- Quote: "3) the statistical model fails to account for an important environmental determinant that in itself is spatially structured and thus causes spatial structuring in the response"
- Key point: One common cause of spatial autocorrelation: the model leaves out an important environmental factor that is itself spatially structured, so the response shows spatial structure too.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The violation of the assumption of i.i.d. residuals may bias parameter estimates and can increase type I error rates (falsely rejecting the null hypothesis of no effect)."
- Key point: Non-independent residuals can bias parameter estimates and raise the type I error rate (finding an effect where there is none).

### E4

- Location: Methods, paragraph on checking for spatial autocorrelation
- Quote: "In all three cases a measure of similarity (Moran's I, Geary's c) or variance (variogram) of data points (i and j) is plotted as a function of the distance between them"
- Key point: The three common plots for checking spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I correlogram, Geary's c correlogram, and the variogram) all plot the similarity or variance of pairs of data points against the distance between them.
