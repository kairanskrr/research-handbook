# Peyton Jones 2013: How to Write a Great Research Paper

- Source: `peytonjones2013write`, [talk page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/write-great-research-paper/), [slides PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf)
- Version: Slides PDF on the Microsoft Research page. The slides have no page numbers; locations give slide titles.

### E1

- Location: Slide "Writing papers: model 2"
- Quote: "Writing papers is a primary mechanism for doing research (not just for reporting it)"
- Key point: Writing papers is a primary mechanism for doing research, not just for reporting it afterward.

### E2

- Location: Slide "The idea"
- Quote: "Your paper should have just one “ping”: one clear, sharp idea"
- Key point: A paper should have just one clear, sharp idea (one "ping"); if you have several ideas, write several papers.

### E3

- Location: Slide "The idea"
- Quote: "You may not know exactly what the ping is when you start writing; but you must know when you finish"
- Key point: You may not know exactly what your core idea is when you start writing, but you must know when you finish.

### E4

- Location: Slide "State your contributions"
- Quote: "The list of contributions drives the entire paper: the paper substantiates the claims you have made"
- Key point: Write the list of contributions first; the whole paper exists to substantiate those claims.

### E5

- Location: Slide "Contributions should be refutable"
- Quote: "We prove that the type system is sound, and that type checking is decidable (Section 4)"
- Key point: State contributions as specific, refutable claims with the section where each is supported (the quote is the slide's "good" example), not empty statements like "we describe a system, and it is cool" or "we study its properties."

### E6

- Location: Slide "Evidence"
- Quote: "Check each claim in the introduction, identify the evidence, and forwardreference it from the claim"
- Key point: For every claim in the introduction, identify the evidence in the body and forward-reference it from the claim. Evidence can be analysis and comparison, theorems, measurements, or case studies.

### E7

- Location: Slide "Putting the reader first"
- Quote: "Do not recapitulate your personal journey of discovery. This route may be soaked with your blood, but that is not interesting to the reader."
- Key point: Do not recount your personal journey of discovery; readers don't care. Take the most direct route to explaining the idea.

### E8

- Location: Slide "Related work"
- Quote: "Problem 2: describing alternative approaches gets between the reader and your idea"
- Key point: Peyton Jones argues for putting related work later: describing other approaches first gets between the reader and your idea, and readers who don't yet understand the problem cannot follow the comparisons.

### E9

- Location: Slide "Presenting the idea"
- Quote: "Conveying the intuition is primary, not secondary"
- Key point: Convey the intuition first, then the details; with the intuition, readers can follow the details, but not the other way around.

### E10

- Location: Slide "Getting help"
- Quote: "Each reader can only read your paper for the first time once! So use them carefully"
- Key point: Each reader can read your paper for the first time only once, so use them carefully; ask them to point out where they got lost, not typos.

### E11

- Location: Slide "Credit"
- Quote: "Giving credit to others does not diminish the credit you get from your paper"
- Key point: Giving credit to others generously and acknowledging your approach's weaknesses does not diminish your own credit.

### E12

- Location: Slide "Do not be intimidated"
- Quote: "Writing the paper is how you develop the idea in the first place"
- Key point: Don't wait for a "great idea" before writing; writing the paper is how you develop the idea.

### E13

- Location: Slide "Describe the problem"
- Quote: "Use an example to introduce the problem"
- Key point: Introduce the problem with a concrete example rather than general claims that it is important.
