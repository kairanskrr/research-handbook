# Ruder 2020: 10 Tips for Research and a PhD

- Source: `ruder2020tips`, [original](https://www.ruder.io/10-tips-for-research-and-a-phd/)
- Version: Author's blog, 2020, read 2026-10-05.

### E1

- Location: Work on two things
- Quote: "Instead, I've found it useful to work on two projects at once to keep my sanity in check. If you hit a wall on one project, you can spend some time working on the other."
- Key point: Work on two projects at once; when you hit a wall on one, you can switch to the other, and the change of view may even crack the problem.

### E2

- Location: Work on two things
- Quote: "To minimise context-switching, I generally try to work on one project each day."
- Key point: To minimize context switching, work on only one of the projects each day.

### E3

- Location: 4) Collaborate
- Quote: "On the contrary, being able to collaborate is an important skill that you will need later on."
- Key point: A PhD is not a solo effort; collaboration is an important skill you will need later.

### E4

- Location: 4) Collaborate
- Quote: "In collaborations, particularly if they are remote, it is important to communicate clearly and to set expectations."
- Key point: In collaborations (especially remote ones), communicate clearly and set expectations.

### E5

- Location: 4) Collaborate
- Quote: "If you are working on two projects, make one of them a collaboration. Collaborating with someone different from your advisor introduces you to a new perspective and will allow you to learn more than working on your own."
- Key point: Collaborating with someone other than your advisor brings a new perspective and teaches you more than working alone.

### E6

- Location: 10) Play the long game
- Quote: "Be generous in giving praise and attribution."
- Key point: Be generous with praise and attribution for others' contributions.

### E7

- Location: 1) Read broadly.
- Quote: "In order to have a rich repertoire to draw on for inspiration, try to cultivate diverse interests. Look beyond your immediate horizon."
- Key point: To have a rich repertoire of inspiration, cultivate diverse interests and look beyond your own subfield.

### E8

- Location: 2) Work on two things.
- Quote: "If the project is not going well, your motivation and well-being may suffer. If you hit a roadblock, you can do nothing but grind until you resolve it."
- Key point: The risk of a single project: if it goes badly, your motivation and well-being suffer, and when you hit a roadblock all you can do is grind.

### E9

- Location: 3) Be ambitious.
- Quote: "You can work on a relatively safe project and one that is high-risk but also may be more impactful. The safe work ensures that you will graduate."
- Key point: Pair a relatively safe project with a high-risk, high-reward one; the safe one ensures you graduate.

### E10

- Location: 3) Be ambitious.
- Quote: "A good indication of whether something you are working on is impactful is whether you'd be excited if it was published by someone else."
- Key point: One test of whether your work is impactful: would you be excited if someone else published it?

### E11

- Location: 5) Be proactive. (opening)
- Quote: "This is probably the most important piece of advice. Don't restrict yourself to the people in your immediate circle."
- Key point: What the author considers the most important advice: don't restrict yourself to the people around you; reach out to more researchers.

### E12

- Location: 7) Keep a source of positive energy.
- Quote: "External rewards such as paper acceptances are sparse, so leveraging intrinsic rewards is often necessary."
- Key point: External rewards such as paper acceptances are sparse, so you often have to rely on intrinsic motivation.

### E13

- Location: 7) Keep a source of positive energy.
- Quote: "So it is important to build a support network that you can rely on." / "find an activity that you can fall back on to give you positive energy when things don't go as planned"
- Key point: Build a support network you can rely on, and find an activity that gives you positive energy when things go wrong.

### E14

- Location: 7) Keep a source of positive energy.
- Quote: "In the end, the most important resource is not the amount of compute you have, but your personal well-being."
- Key point: The most important resource is not compute but your own well-being.

### E15

- Location: 8) Play to your strengths.
- Quote: "Your ideal research topic sits at the intersection of work that is impactful, work that you are passionate about, and work that you are uniquely suited for."
- Key point: The ideal topic sits at the intersection of work that is impactful, work you are passionate about, and work you are uniquely suited for.

### E16

- Location: 10) Play the long game.
- Quote: "Take care of yourself. Work hard but get enough sleep and exercise." / "the more important outcome of a PhD is a better version of yourself."
- Key point: Take care of yourself: work hard, but get enough sleep and exercise; the more important outcome of a PhD is a better version of yourself.

### E17

- Location: 7) Keep a source of positive energy.
- Quote: "The most natural way to stay positive and energised in research is to work on something that excites you and to follow your curiosity." / "In those cases, try to find a particular angle that excites you."
- Key point: The most natural way to stay positive is to work on something that excites you and follow your curiosity; when you cannot choose your topic, find an angle within it that excites you.
