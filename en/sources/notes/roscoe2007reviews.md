# Roscoe 2007: Writing reviews for systems conferences

- Source: `roscoe2007reviews`, [original PDF](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/troscoe/pubs/review-writing.pdf)
- Version: Lecture notes, March 2007. Page numbers are PDF page numbers.

### E1

- Location: §2, p. 1
- Quote: "A review provides feedback to the authors on how to improve the paper or otherwise proceed with their work, regardless of whether it’s accepted or not."
- Key point: Accepted or not, a review should give the authors feedback on how to improve.

### E2

- Location: §2, p. 2
- Quote: "People who have written down a review of a paper are generally better qualified to assess it than those who have merely read the paper."
- Key point: Writing a review down forces you to clarify your judgment; people who have written one are better qualified to assess a paper than those who have only read it.

### E3

- Location: §4, p. 3
- Quote: "First of all, summarize the paper. Give a neutral description of what you think the paper is about, where the authors are coming from, why they view the problem as important, and what they’ve done."
- Key point: First step of a review: neutrally summarize what the paper is about, why it matters, and what the authors have done.

### E4

- Location: §4, p. 3
- Quote: "Second, state what you think the contributions are."
- Key point: Second step: state what you think the contributions are.

### E5

- Location: §4, p. 4
- Quote: "Are there any apparent technical flaws? If you think there are, it’s better to express these concerns as questions than flat assertions"
- Key point: If you suspect a technical flaw, express it as a question rather than a flat assertion.

### E6

- Location: §4, p. 4
- Quote: "Was there anything you thought was really cool about the paper? It’s always good to mention any moments of delight you had reading it."
- Key point: Also mention anything in the paper that delighted you.

### E7

- Location: §4, p. 4
- Quote: "the important thing is to give a brief recommendation for the paper and your reasons for it."
- Key point: End with a clear recommendation and your reasons for it.

### E8

- Location: §5, p. 4
- Quote: "Reviews are almost always anonymous, so the reviewer is in a position of considerable power and privilege, without much means of being held to account"
- Key point: Reviews are usually anonymous, so reviewers hold considerable power with little accountability, and must not abuse it.

### E9

- Location: §5, p. 5
- Quote: "Make the review constructive. With a bit of thought, it’s very easy to transform every negative comment into a constructive suggestion"
- Key point: Turn every negative comment into a constructive suggestion, for example change "the system does not handle X" to "the paper would be more convincing if it discussed how the system handles X."

### E10

- Location: §5, p. 5
- Quote: "Also bear in mind that, in all cases, there is a still a remote possibility that you’ve misunderstood the paper."
- Key point: Always remember you may have misunderstood the paper, so leave room in your wording.

### E11

- Location: §5, p. 5
- Quote: "it’s OK to be humorous in a review, but try to stay emotionally mature, and never ridicule the paper."
- Key point: Humor is fine, but never ridicule the paper.

### E12

- Location: §4, p. 4
- Quote: "Also, in theory at least conference papers report on complete, mature work whereas workshop papers present early work or argue a position."
- Key point: In computer science, conference papers should report complete, mature work, while workshop papers can present early work or argue a position.

### E13

- Location: §5, p. 4
- Quote: "Most review forms have an entry of “reviewer confidence”, and it’s OK to put a low score here if you’re not sure you’re right."
- Key point: If unsure, you can give a low score in the "reviewer confidence" field.

### E14

- Location: §6, p. 5
- Quote: "Remember that the purpose of the PC meeting is to select the best possible program, not necessarily to pass judgement on each paper."
- Key point: The purpose of a program committee meeting is to select the best possible program, not to pass judgment on each paper.

### E15

- Location: §5, p. 5
- Quote: "I would find the paper more persuasive if it stated what the authors do over and above Multics."
- Key point: When pointing to similar prior work, do not say "this has all been done"; ask the authors to state what they do beyond it.
