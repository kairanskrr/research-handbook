# Historic England 2015: Management of Research Projects in the Historic Environment (MoRPHE) Project Managers' Guide

- Source: `historicengland2015morphe`, [official page](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/morphe-project-managers-guide/) (if the official site shows an access check, use the Internet Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/2022/https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/morphe-project-managers-guide/heag024-morphe-managers-guide/)
- Version: Published by Historic England on 30 April 2015, written by Edmund Lee. The guide states that research projects funded by Historic England must follow it, and others may use it as good practice. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "Research and R&D projects funded by Historic England will be required as a condition of grant or contract to follow this guidance."
- Key point: Research projects funded by Historic England must follow this guidance (a condition of grant or contract).

### E2

- Location: 1.1 The MoRPHE procedural model
- Quote: "Initiation is the project's design Stage." / "Uncertainties, potential problems and opportunities are documented in a Risk Log (see Glossary)."
- Key point: The "Initiation" stage is the project's design stage; uncertainties, potential problems, and opportunities are recorded in a Risk Log.

### E3

- Location: 1.1 The MoRPHE procedural model
- Quote: "Review Point R3 is a review, conducted at the conclusion of each Execution Stage, of the project's progress against its Aims and Objectives. The review may generate an Updated Project Design, to be applied to subsequent Execution Stages."
- Key point: At the end of each Execution Stage, progress is reviewed against the Aims and Objectives; the review may produce an Updated Project Design for later stages.

### E4

- Location: 2.5 Execution
- Quote: "This flexible approach is consistent with the unpredictable nature of historic-environment research."
- Key point: This flexible approach fits the unpredictable nature of historic-environment research (e.g., new discoveries).

### E5

- Location: 2.5.3 Archive preparation
- Quote: "Archive should be selected for inclusion on this basis, and actively compiled and indexed as it is acquired, throughout the project's life cycle."
- Key point: The archive should be selected around the research questions and compiled and indexed as material is acquired, throughout the project.

### E6

- Location: 2.5.3 Archive preparation
- Quote: "The archive is considered complete only at project Closure. It should be capable of independent third-party interrogation, via specific mechanisms as set out explicitly in the Project Design."
- Key point: The archive is complete only at project closure; it should allow independent third-party interrogation.

### E7

- Location: 2.5.4 Dissemination
- Quote: "Dissemination of results is fundamental to the success of any historic-environment research project."
- Key point: Disseminating results is fundamental to the success of any historic-environment research project.
