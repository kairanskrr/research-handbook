# Patterson 2001: How to Have a Bad Career in Research/Academia

- Source: `patterson2001bad`, [original](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pattrsn/talks/BadCareer.pdf)
- Version: Talk slides, November 2001. Text extraction from the PDF lost apostrophes and quotation marks (e.g., "can't" became "can t"); quotes below follow the extracted text as is.

### E1

- Location: p. 15, Bad Career Move #4: Use the Computer Scientific Method
- Quote: "Obsolete Scientific Method * Hypothesis * Sequence of experiments * Change 1 parameter/exp. * Prove/Disprove Hypothesis * Document for others to reproduce results"
- Key point: Ironic slide: the author calls "hypothesis, sequence of experiments, change one parameter per experiment, prove or disprove, document for others to reproduce" the "obsolete scientific method," meaning that this is in fact what you should do.

### E4

- Location: p. 30, 1) Selecting a Problem
- Quote: "Make sure you are excited enough to work on it for 3-5 years"
- Key point: Make sure you are excited enough about the topic to work on it for 3-5 years.

### E5

- Location: p. 11, Bad Career Move #1: Be THE leading expert
- Quote: "Never define success"
- Key point: Ironic slide: "never define success" is one way to ruin a research career; in other words, state your goals clearly.

- Location: p. 42, Conclusion: Alternatives to a Bad Career
- Quote: "Taste is critical in selecting research problems, solutions, experiments, & communicating results; acquired by feedback and completing projects"

### E8

### E9

- Location: "5) Evaluating Quantitatively"
- Quote: "If you can t be proven wrong, then you can t prove you re right" / "Report in sufficient detail for others to reproduce results"
- Key point: If you can't be proven wrong, you can't prove you're right; report in enough detail for others to reproduce results. (Apostrophes are missing in the extracted PDF text.)

### E10

- Location: "Summary Advice of Alternative to Bad Career in Graduate School"
- Quote: "Show Initiative!" / "Ask questions!"
- Key point: Advice for graduate school: show initiative and ask questions.

### E11

- Location: "4) Finishing a project"
- Quote: "People count projects you finish, not the ones you start" / "Successful projects go through an unglamorous, hard phase" / "Finishing a project is how people acquire taste in selecting good problems, finding simple solutions"
- Key point: People count the projects you finish, not the ones you start; successful projects go through an unglamorous, hard phase; finishing projects is how you acquire taste in selecting good problems and finding simple solutions.

### E12

- Location: "3) Running a project"
- Quote: "Periodic Project Reviews with Outsiders"
- Key point: Hold periodic project reviews with outsiders to get feedback.
