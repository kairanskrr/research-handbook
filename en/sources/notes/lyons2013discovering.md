# Lyons 2013: Discovering the Significance of 5σ

- Source: `lyons2013discovering`, [full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1284)
- Version: arXiv:1310.1284v1, 2013. The author's listed affiliations are Imperial College London and the Department of Particle Physics at the University of Oxford.

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "It has become the convention in Particle Physics that in order to claim a discovery of some form of New Physics, the chance of just the background having a statistical fluctuation at least as large as the observed effect is equivalent to the area beyond 5σ in one tail of a normalised Gaussian distribution, or smaller"
- Key point: The convention in particle physics for claiming a discovery of new physics: the probability that background fluctuations alone produce an effect this large must not exceed the one-sided tail area beyond 5σ of a standard normal distribution.

### E2

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Indeed, journals are very reluctant to allow the word ‘discovery’ to appear in the title, abstract or conclusions of a paper, unless the p-value is that small."
- Key point: Unless the p-value is that small, journals are very reluctant to allow the word "discovery" in a paper's title, abstract, or conclusions.

### E3

- Location: §2, Look Elsewhere Effect
- Quote: "If the background fluctuation is required to be at the observed mass, this probability is called the local p-value. If, however, the restriction on the location of the background fluctuation is removed, this is instead the global p-value, and is larger than the local one because of the LEE."
- Key point: The probability of a fluctuation at the observed location is the local p-value; allowing it to occur anywhere gives the global p-value, which is larger. This is the Look Elsewhere Effect (LEE).

### E4

- Location: §2, Look Elsewhere Effect
- Quote: "It is clearly important to decide in advance of the analysis what procedure will be used; and to specify in any publication exactly how the global p-values are defined."
- Key point: Decide the procedure in advance of the analysis, and state in the paper exactly how the global p-value was defined.

### E5

- Location: §2 Why 5σ?
- Quote: "Statisticians who hear about the 5σ criterion are very skeptical about it being sensible."
- Key point: Statisticians who hear about the 5σ criterion are very skeptical that it is sensible.

### E6

- Location: §4 Conclusions
- Quote: "It would be very useful if we could distance ourselves from the attitude of `Require 5σ for all discovery claims'."
- Key point: The author argues against requiring 5σ for all discovery claims across the board.

### E7

- Location: §3 Why not 5σ?
- Quote: "There are several reasons why it is not sensible to use a uniform criterion of 5σ for all searches for new physics."
- Key point: A uniform 5σ criterion for all searches for new physics is not sensible, because the look-elsewhere effect, prior plausibility, and the impact of systematic errors differ from experiment to experiment.
