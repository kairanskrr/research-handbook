# Schäfer, Lückemeier & Glorius 2024: Improving reproducibility through condition-based sensitivity assessments

- Source: `schafer2024sensitivity`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4sc03017f) ([PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11382186/))
- Version: Chemical Science 2024, 15, 14548-14555, perspective article, CC BY 3.0 open access. From the Glorius group at the University of Münster, Germany. Text taken from the Europe PMC full-text XML.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, para. 3
- Quote: "While the general outcome of chemical reactions tends to align with existing literature, there is often variability in quantitative results such as yield, selectivity, and purity." / "Even with detailed protocols provided in ESI, reaction parameters may differ between labs with different chemists conducting the experiments"
- Key point: The general outcome of a reaction usually matches the literature, but numbers such as yield, selectivity, and purity often vary; even with detailed supporting information, conditions can differ between labs and between people.

### E2

- Location: Introduction (design of the sensitivity screen)
- Quote: "To do so, single reaction parameters are varied in both positive and negative directions, while all other parameters are kept constant. The resulting impact on the yield provides a valuable clue regarding which parameters to prioritize when troubleshooting reproducibility issues."
- Key point: How a sensitivity screen works: vary one reaction parameter at a time, both up and down, keep all others constant, and see how the yield changes; this tells you which parameter to check first when troubleshooting.

### E3

- Location: General parameters
- Quote: "Frequently neglected parameters in published laboratory procedures encompass stirring rate, reaction duration (including "overnight" reactions), and the purity of substrates and reagents."
- Key point: Parameters often neglected in published procedures: stirring rate, reaction time (including "overnight"), and the purity of substrates and reagents.

### E4

- Location: General parameters
- Quote: "They repurified their carboxylic acid starting material, enhancing its purity from 98.9% to 99.9%. This small adjustment, as revealed by their sensitivity screen, led to a notable +23% increase in yield"
- Key point: An example described in the paper: Bull and colleagues repurified their carboxylic acid starting material from 98.9% to 99.9% purity, which raised the yield by 23%.

### E5

- Location: Homogeneous catalysis and cross-coupling reactions
- Quote: "Gooßen and colleagues investigated the impact of different commercial palladium precatalyst batches and found considerable yield discrepancies among batches"
- Key point: Described in the paper: Gooßen and colleagues found considerable yield differences between batches of a commercial palladium precatalyst.

### E6

- Location: Introduction (revision of the screening range)
- Quote: "we acknowledge that our proposed range of ±10% concentration was frequently insufficient for detecting substantial yield changes beyond the detection limit of the analytical method employed. The widespread adoption of at least a ±50% concentration variation by many groups"
- Key point: The authors acknowledge that their originally proposed ±10% change in concentration was often insufficient, and many groups now use at least ±50%.

### E7

- Location: Conclusion and outlook
- Quote: "for an in-depth evaluation of reactions it should be followed up by more detailed approaches such as Design of experiments (DoE), mechanistic analysis, and advanced process optimisation."
- Key point: A sensitivity screen is only a first tool; in-depth evaluation needs follow-up methods such as design of experiments (DoE) and mechanistic studies.

### E8

- Location: Introduction, para. 4
- Quote: "Conducting Design of Experiment (DoE) studies to explore every aspect of a reaction reveals its sensitivity, but this approach is likely the most time-consuming and costly."
- Key point: Exploring every aspect of a reaction with design of experiments (DoE) reveals its sensitivity best, but is likely the most time-consuming and costly approach.

### E9

- Location: Introduction (origins of the sensitivity screen)
- Quote: "In response to this challenge, the sensitivity screen was developed as a general tool to tackle the aforementioned reproducibility problems." / "Our innovation was embraced by the scientific community and has since been widely adopted for applications beyond the originally suggested ones."
- Key point: The sensitivity screen was developed by the authors' group as a general tool for reproducibility problems and has since been widely adopted.
