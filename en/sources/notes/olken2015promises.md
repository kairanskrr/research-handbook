# Olken 2015: Promises and Perils of Pre-analysis Plans

- Source: `olken2015promises`, [AEA article page (with abstract)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.29.3.61)
- Version: Journal of Economic Perspectives 29(3). The full-text PDF was blocked by the website; these excerpts rely only on the public abstract on the AEA article page.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "A pre-analysis plan pre-specifies in a precise way the analysis to be run before examining the data. A researcher can specify variables, data cleaning procedures, regression specifications, and so on."
- Key point: A pre-analysis plan specifies precisely, before you examine the data, the analysis you will run: which variables, data cleaning procedures, regression specifications, and so on.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "If the regressions are pre-specified in advance and researchers are required to report all the results they pre-specify, data-mining problems are greatly reduced."
- Key point: If regressions are pre-specified and researchers must report all pre-specified results, data-mining problems are greatly reduced.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "I also explore some of the limitations and costs of such plans. I then review a few pieces of evidence that suggest that, in many contexts, the benefits of using pre-specified analysis plans may not be as high as one might have expected initially."
- Key point: Pre-analysis plans have limitations and costs too; the author argues that in many contexts their benefits may be smaller than first thought.

### E4

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "For the most part, I will focus on the relatively narrow issue of pre-analysis for randomized controlled trials."
- Key point: The article focuses mainly on pre-analysis plans for randomized controlled trials.
