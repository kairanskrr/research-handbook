# Karhulahti 2024: Positionality statements in science

- Source: `karhulahti2024positionality`, [full text on PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11200055/), doi:10.12688/openreseurope.17058.2
- Version: Open Research Europe 4:62 (2024, version 2), open access (CC BY). The author is an academic at the University of Jyväskylä in Finland; this short essay discusses when positionality statements suit different disciplines. Full text read via Europe PMC. Locations give sections.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "positionality statements, which refer to disclosing the scientist’s position in relation to the conducted research."
- Key point: A positionality statement discloses the researcher's position in relation to the research they conduct.

### E2

- Location: What positionality does
- Quote: "When the scientist itself is part of the toolset that is used to generate primary research data—for instance, ethnographic fieldnotes—the details of that “tool” are relevant to acknowledge"
- Key point: When researchers themselves are part of the toolset that generates primary data (e.g., ethnographic fieldnotes), the details of that "tool" should be acknowledged.

### E3

- Location: What positionality does
- Quote: "Because engaging in fieldwork is a process where only a small part of interactions and observations can be documented, it matters who engaged and selected the fieldnote data."
- Key point: Fieldwork can document only a small part of interactions and observations, so it matters who took part and who selected what went into the fieldnotes.

### E4

- Location: Two common misunderstandings
- Quote: "A common misunderstanding is that scientific positionality statements must pick a set of group identities, as from a ‘shopping list’ [...] Each positionality statement should fit the methods and topics of a specific study"
- Key point: A common misunderstanding is that a positionality statement must list group identities like a "shopping list"; in fact each statement should fit the methods and topic of the specific study.

### E5

- Location: What positionality does
- Quote: "As in preregistration more generally [...] stating pre-study assumptions can help making useful analytic calibrations before it is too late."
- Key point: As with preregistration, stating your assumptions before the study helps you make useful analytic adjustments before it is too late.

### E6

- Location: Two common misunderstandings
- Quote: "journals should not mandate positionality statements but treat them like other research tools: encourage them to be used wisely when fitting with the methodology and philosophy of a study."
- Key point: The author argues that journals should not mandate positionality statements, but encourage their wise use when they fit a study's methodology and philosophy.
