# Thompson, Ellison, Wood 2002: Harmonized guidelines for single-laboratory validation of methods of analysis (IUPAC Technical Report)

- Source: `thompson2002harmonized`, [original PDF](https://publications.iupac.org/pac/2002/pdf/7405x0835.pdf)
- Version: Pure and Applied Chemistry 74(5): 835-855, 2002. A technical report of the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC), drafted by an IUPAC working party with ISO and AOAC International taking part in the consultations. The PDF on the IUPAC website returns a verification page to scripts, so the text is taken from the same PDF saved by the Internet Archive; the report states that reproduction is permitted with acknowledgment of the source. Locations give sections. It concerns method validation in analytical laboratories, not journal review criteria.

### E1

- Location: 3. Method validation, uncertainty, and quality assurance
- Quote: "Method validation makes use of a set of tests that both test any assumptions on which the analytical method is based and establish and document the performance characteristics of a method, thereby demonstrating whether the method is fit for a particular analytical purpose."
- Key point: Method validation uses a set of tests that both check the assumptions on which an analytical method rests and establish and document the method's performance characteristics, thereby showing whether the method is fit for a particular analytical purpose.

### E2

- Location: 3. Method validation, uncertainty, and quality assurance
- Quote: "Typical performance characteristics of analytical methods are: applicability, selectivity, calibration, trueness, precision, recovery, operating range, limit of quantification, limit of detection, sensitivity, and ruggedness. To these can be added measurement uncertainty and fitness-for-purpose."
- Key point: Typical performance characteristics are applicability, selectivity, calibration, trueness, precision, recovery, operating range, limit of quantification, limit of detection, sensitivity, and ruggedness, to which measurement uncertainty and "fitness for purpose" can be added.

### E3

- Location: Appendix A12. Fitness for purpose
- Quote: "Fitness for purpose is the extent to which the performance of a method matches the criteria, agreed between the analyst and the end-user of the data, that describe the end-user's needs."
- Key point: "Fitness for purpose" is the extent to which a method's performance meets criteria, agreed in advance between the analyst and the data user, that describe the user's needs.

### E4

- Location: 1.1 Background
- Quote: "“Full” validation for an analytical method is usually taken to comprise an examination of the characteristics of the method in an interlaboratory method performance study (also known as a collaborative study or collaborative trial)."
- Key point: "Full" validation usually means examining a method's characteristics in a method performance study (collaborative study) involving several laboratories.

### E5

- Location: 1.1 Background
- Quote: "However, it is not always practical or necessary to provide full validation of analytical methods. In such circumstances a “single-laboratory method validation” may be appropriate."
- Key point: Full validation is not always practicable or necessary, in which case single-laboratory method validation can be done.

### E6

- Location: Appendix A11. Ruggedness
- Quote: "The ruggedness of a method is tested by deliberately introducing small changes to the procedure and examining the effect on the results."
- Key point: To test ruggedness, deliberately introduce small variations into the procedure and see how much the results are affected.
