# Sytsma, Reuter & Willemsen 2026: Experimental Philosophy (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

- Source: `sytsma2026experimental`, [original](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/experimental-philosophy/)
- Version: A new entry in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (free online) published April 8, 2026, by Justin Sytsma, Kevin Reuter, and Pascale Willemsen, replacing an earlier entry of the same name by other authors. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Experimental philosophy (or “x-phi”) is a methodological naturalist approach to philosophy that is distinguished by practitioners employing empirical methods for philosophical purposes."
- Key point: Experimental philosophy (x-phi) is characterized by using empirical methods for philosophical ends.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Most notably, this includes that experimentalists have often aimed to study “intuitions” concerning concepts of philosophical interest, usually by investigating the judgments of laypeople (or “the folk”)."
- Key point: The most prominent theme is the study of people's "intuitions" about philosophical concepts, usually by surveying the judgments of ordinary people.

### E3

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "In line with this target, there is also a correspondingly prominent method in x-phi—the use of questionnaires soliciting judgments about short stories (or “vignettes”) based on philosophical thought experiments."
- Key point: The corresponding main method is the questionnaire: participants are shown short stories (vignettes) adapted from philosophical thought experiments and asked to make judgments.

### E4

- Location: §2 Methods in Experimental Philosophy
- Quote: "This will focus on the two approaches most common in the sub-discipline—questionnaire methods (§2.2) and corpus-linguistic methods (§2.3)—before concluding with a brief survey of the broader range found in the literature (§2.4)."
- Key point: The two most common kinds of method in this branch are questionnaire methods and corpus linguistic methods, alongside many others.

### E5

- Location: §2.1 Basics of Experimental-Philosophical Research
- Quote: "As such, it (ideally) begins with formulating arguments that engage with the surrounding philosophical literature."
- Key point: Experimental philosophy is essentially doing philosophy, and ideally starts from an argument that engages with the existing philosophical literature.

### E6

- Location: §2.1 Basics of Experimental-Philosophical Research
- Quote: "Empirical premises in those arguments—premises involving claims about how our world is—then form the entry point to standard descriptions of the quantitative empirical research process, such as one would find in discussions of methods in experimental psychology."
- Key point: The empirical premises of an argument (claims about how the world actually is) are the entry point into a quantitative empirical research process, the same one described in the methodology of experimental psychology.

### E7

- Location: §2.1 Basics of Experimental-Philosophical Research
- Quote: "Typically, the interpretation step will involve connecting the empirical results back to the philosophical arguments generating the research questions. And this will require that researchers consider the limitations of their studies in the context of the broader arguments they make."
- Key point: When interpreting results, connect the empirical findings back to the philosophical argument that raised the research question, and consider the study's limitations within the larger argument.

### E8

- Location: §1.2 Systematic Empirical Observation
- Quote: "For instance, typical sample sizes have increased and statistical reporting has improved as experimental philosophy has developed, with some early studies falling short of contemporary standards in these regards."
- Key point: As experimental philosophy has developed, sample sizes have grown and statistical reporting has improved; some early studies do not meet today's standards.

### E9

- Location: §1.2 Systematic Empirical Observation
- Quote: "Open science came about in part in response to the replication crisis in psychology (Open Science Collaboration 2015) and involves preregistering study designs, hypotheses, and predictions, as well as making raw data and code for statistical analyses available online."
- Key point: In recent years experimental philosophy has moved increasingly toward open science: preregistering study designs, hypotheses, and predictions, and sharing raw data and analysis code online; open science arose in part from psychology's replication crisis.

### E10

- Location: §1.2 Systematic Empirical Observation
- Quote: "The key point is that in comparison to the types of informal observation Jackson suggests, the methods that practitioners of experimental philosophy adopt are designed to minimize the risk of drawing conclusions based on idiosyncratic or biased responses."
- Key point: Compared with informally polling students, the methods of experimental philosophy aim to avoid drawing conclusions from isolated or biased responses.

### E11

- Location: §1.2 Systematic Empirical Observation
- Quote: "This might include taking steps to help ensure that students are answering independently from one another, such as by using individual questionnaires rather than a show of hands;"
- Key point: For example, even when handing out questionnaires in class, have students answer independently (individual questionnaires rather than a show of hands).

### E12

- Location: §4.1.3 Amateur psychology?
- Quote: "Following Machery (2017: §5.1), this can be referred to as the amateur psychology objection, echoing Williamson (2010) who disparagingly refers to practitioners as “amateur experimentalists” and experimental philosophy as “imitation psychology”."
- Key point: One criticism of experimental philosophy is that it is "amateur psychology": critics consider it crudely done.

### E13

- Location: §4.1.3 Amateur psychology?
- Quote: "Indeed, in recent years, experimental philosophers have called on empirical tools to investigate key methodological concerns for the practice, such as the adequacy of statistical reporting (Colombo et al. 2018), the occurrence of p-hacking (Stuart et al. 2019), and the replicability of findings (Cova, Strickland, et al. 2021). On each of these metrics, experimental philosophy fares rather better than the critical remarks would suggest."
- Key point: Experimental philosophers have used empirical methods to examine statistical reporting, p-hacking, and replicability in their own field; the entry's authors judge the results to be better than critics claim.

### E14

- Location: §4.2 Divisions within Experimental Philosophy
- Quote: "Thus, research in the positive program aims to bolster the practice of calling on intuitions as evidence in philosophy, most often by putting the surveying of intuitions on a firmer empirical footing."
- Key point: The "positive program" aims to strengthen the practice of using intuitions as philosophical evidence, mainly by giving the study of intuitions a firmer empirical basis.

### E15

- Location: §4.2 Divisions within Experimental Philosophy
- Quote: "By contrast, research in the negative program aims to undermine the standard practice, often by targeting underlying assumptions for the method of cases."
- Key point: The "negative program" aims to undermine this standard practice, often targeting the assumptions behind the "method of cases."

### E16

- Location: §4.3 The Restrictionist Challenge
- Quote: "The restrictionist challenge is driven by a body of empirical results that indicate that some of the case judgments called on in the armchair literature vary along dimensions that are not philosophically relevant to those judgments."
- Key point: The "restrictionist challenge" rests on a body of empirical results: some case judgments used in the armchair philosophy literature vary with factors that have nothing to do with philosophy.

### E17

- Location: §4.2 Divisions within Experimental Philosophy
- Quote: "Explanatory experimental philosophy plausibly covers a notable majority of the work that has been done in the field (Knobe 2016)."
- Key point: Explanatory experimental philosophy (studying intuitions themselves and explaining the effects found) may make up the bulk of existing work.

### E18

- Location: §2.2 Questionnaires Involving Vignettes
- Quote: "Thus, the method of cases is often treated as a central component of analytic philosophy. Vignette studies can be seen as the experimentalists’ analog of this method, allowing researchers to investigate case judgments in a systematic way"
- Key point: The "method of cases" is often seen as a core component of analytic philosophy; vignette studies can be seen as experimental philosophers' counterpart to this method, used to study people's judgments about cases systematically.

### E19

- Location: §1.1 Empirical Claims in Philosophy
- Quote: "A prominent part of experimental philosophy involves drawing out and testing assumptions made in philosophical theorizing."
- Key point: A large part of experimental philosophy's work is identifying and testing assumptions in philosophical theories.

### E20

- Location: §1.1 Empirical Claims in Philosophy
- Quote: "This is clearly an empirical assumption, as it concerns how people actually conceptualize free will."
- Key point: (Example: "most ordinary people are natural incompatibilists") This is an empirical hypothesis, because it is about how people actually understand free will.
