# UK Oral History Society: Looking after yourself (self-care for interviewers)

- Source: `ohs2026wellbeing`, [original](https://ohs.org.uk/looking-after-yourself/)
- Version: Web page of the Oral History Society (OHS, UK), undated, read 2026-10-06.

### E1

- Location: Main text, on scheduling
- Quote: "try to avoid doing more than one interview a day"
- Key point: Try not to do more than one interview a day.

### E2

- Location: Main text, on personal safety
- Quote: "it is crucially important to tell someone who you are going to see, where and when, and at what sort of time you expect to be back from an interview"
- Key point: Before going to an interview, always tell someone who you are seeing, where and when, and roughly when you expect to be back.

### E3

- Location: Main text, on emotional support
- Quote: "If you anticipate that an interview may be distressing in the nature of the interview topic, it can be helpful to talk to someone in advance about how they have dealt with a similar situation"
- Key point: If you expect the topic to be distressing, talk beforehand to someone who has handled a similar situation about how they coped.

### E4

- Location: Main text, on emotional support
- Quote: "Can you also identify someone who is willing for you to talk to them about your emotions and reactions after the interview, if you have been upset by what you have heard"
- Key point: Identify in advance someone you can talk to after the interview in case what you heard upsets you.

### E5

- Location: Main text, on people beyond the interviewer
- Quote: "People archiving and digitising material will listen to sections of the recordings, and those cataloguing interviews will also be listening in detail, as will transcribers." / "think carefully in advance if there is there a way to build in breaks away from material (especially if the content is traumatic)"
- Key point: People who archive, digitize, catalog, and transcribe also listen closely to the recordings and can be affected too; plan breaks away from the material in advance, especially when the content involves trauma.
