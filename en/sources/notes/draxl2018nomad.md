# Draxl & Scheffler 2018: NOMAD: The FAIR Concept for Big-Data-Driven Materials Science

- Source: `draxl2018nomad`, [Original (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05039)
- Version: arXiv:1805.05039 preprint; published in MRS Bulletin 43, 676–682 (2018). It describes NOMAD's practice as of 2018; the NOMAD website is authoritative for current rules. The preprint has no numbered sections, so locations give paragraph headings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Having a closer look, however, one realizes that such studies are inefficiently exploited, so far, as only a tiny amount of the information that is contained in all the computed data is being used."
- Key point: High-throughput screening computes many thousands of structures, yet only a tiny fraction of the information in the computed data is used.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Unfortunately, besides a few numbers, tables or graphs that appear in the resulting publication, the wealth of other information contained in the full research work is typically disregarded or even deleted."
- Key point: Apart from the few numbers, tables, and graphs that appear in the paper, most other information produced in the research is typically disregarded or even deleted. This applies to both theoretical and experimental work.

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "throughput screening initiatives (for example, Refs. 1,2,3,4,5,6), significantly boosted by the Materials Genome Initiative7,8 of the White House, meet this issue by computing the properties of many thousands of structures."
- Key point: High-throughput screening initiatives (boosted by the US Materials Genome Initiative) look for new materials by computing the properties of many thousands of structures.

### E4

- Location: The NOMAD Repository
- Quote: "NOMAD keeps scientific data for at least 10 years for free. That way, NOMAD also facilitates research groups to organize their data for their own groups and to share and exchange their results between two or more groups, and to recall what was actually done some years ago."
- Key point: As described in 2018, NOMAD keeps scientific data free for at least 10 years, which also helps groups organize their own data, exchange results with other groups, and recall what exactly they did years earlier.

### E5

- Location: The NOMAD Repository
- Quote: "Results are accepted in their raw format as produced by the underlying code. The only condition is that the list of authors is provided, and code and code version can be retrieved from the uploaded files, and the input and output files have to be complete."
- Key point: NOMAD accepts results in the raw format produced by the code, provided the authors are listed, the code and code version can be retrieved from the files, and the input and output files are complete.

### E6

- Location: The NOMAD Repository
- Quote: "Data can be restricted to the owner or made available to other people (selected by the owner). After a maximum period of three years though, all data become open access."
- Key point: Data can first be restricted to the owner or to people the owner selects, but all data become open access after at most three years.

### E7

- Location: Summary and outlook
- Quote: "Another crucial issue is the quality of experimental samples, which is often not sufficiently known or characterized. In fact, the sample's quality depends very much on its history."
- Key point: Another key issue for experimental data is that sample quality is often not well known or characterized; it depends heavily on the sample's history.

### E8

- Location: Summary and outlook
- Quote: "Metadata about the experiment, the sample and the data must be defined if the experimental data is re-used or combined with other data."
- Key point: For experimental data to be reused or combined with other data, metadata about the experiment, the sample, and the data must be defined.
