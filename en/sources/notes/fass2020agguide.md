# ADSA, ASAS, PSA 2020: Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching (4th edition)

- Source: `fass2020agguide`, [original PDF](https://www.asas.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/agguide_4th.pdf)
- Version: 4th edition, 2020, jointly published by the American Dairy Science Association (ADSA), the American Society of Animal Science (ASAS), and the Poultry Science Association (PSA) under a CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license, free to download. Commonly called the "Ag Guide". Earlier editions were published by FASS (Federation of Animal Science Societies), hence the common name used here. Locations give the chapter and subheading.

### E1

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "This is the fourth edition of the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching, commonly known as the Ag Guide." / "The Ag Guide is published jointly by the American Dairy Science Association (ADSA), the American Society of Animal Science (ASAS), and the Poultry Science Association (PSA)."
- Key point: This is the fourth edition of the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching, commonly known as the Ag Guide, jointly published by the American Dairy Science Association, the American Society of Animal Science, and the Poultry Science Association.

### E2

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "Must indicates that the animal care and use must be as stated; should indicates that the animal care and use ought to be as indicated unless otherwise justified; recommend indicates an appropriate way of doing things but leaves room for other approaches that achieve the same result."
- Key point: In the guide, "must" means it has to be done as stated; "should" means it ought to be done unless otherwise justified; "recommend" means an appropriate practice, but other approaches achieving the same outcome are acceptable.

### E3

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "It is essential that IACUCs and others who oversee animal care practices exercise professional judgment to encourage appropriate animal welfare outcomes to be achieved, rather than merely seeking to apply specific housing and management requirements."
- Key point: Committees overseeing animal care must exercise professional judgment and aim for actual welfare outcomes, not just apply specific husbandry and management provisions mechanically.

### E4

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "Although many of the species chapters have standards for such things as floor space, feeder space, and drinker allocations because these metrics have been verified by scientific study"
- Key point: Many species chapters give standards for floor space, feeder space, drinker allocation, and the like, because these metrics have been verified by scientific research.

### E5

- Location: Chapter 1: Institutional Policies (opening)
- Quote: "The use of animals for research and teaching, including production research, is governed by numerous federal and local laws, regulations, and standards. In the United States, the Animal Welfare Act (AWA, 1990) and Title 9 Part 1A of the Code of Federal Regulations (USDA, 1985) apply, whereas in Europe, the European Convention for the Protection of Vertebrate Animals used for Experimental and Other Scientific Purposes (ETS123; Council of Europe, 1986) and subsequent amendments apply (Directive 2010/63/EU; European Union, 2010)."
- Key point: Animal use in research and teaching (including production research) is governed by many laws and regulations: in the United States, the Animal Welfare Act and Title 9 of the Code of Federal Regulations; in Europe, the Council of Europe convention on the protection of experimental animals, EU Directive 2010/63/EU, and others.

### E6

- Location: Chapter 1, Monitoring the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals
- Quote: "The chief executive officer, institutional official, or responsible administrative official of the institution should appoint a committee (known as the institutional animal care and use committee or IACUC) to monitor the care and use of agricultural animals in agricultural research and teaching activities within the institution."
- Key point: The institution should appoint an Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) to oversee the care and use of agricultural animals in its agricultural research and teaching.

### E7

- Location: Chapter 1, Monitoring the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals
- Quote: "a scientist who has experience in agricultural research or teaching involving agricultural animals;" / "a veterinarian who has training and experience in agricultural animal medicine and who is licensed or eligible to be licensed to practice veterinary medicine;"
- Key point: Committee members should include a scientist experienced in agricultural research or teaching with agricultural animals and a licensed veterinarian trained in agricultural animal medicine (plus a non-scientist member, a member from outside the institution, and others).

### E8

- Location: Chapter 1, Protocol Review
- Quote: "Animal use protocol(s) (AUP) describing these activities must be reviewed before the initiation of the research or teaching activity to determine whether the proposed care and use of animals is appropriate and humane."
- Key point: Animal use protocols must be reviewed before research or teaching begins, to determine whether the proposed care and use are appropriate and humane.

### E9

- Location: Chapter 1, Protocol Review
- Quote: "Availability or appropriateness of alternative procedures or models (e.g., less invasive procedures, cell or tissue culture, or computer simulations) for the proposed research or teaching activity." / "It should be noted, however, that hands-on training involving animals is an important component of agricultural research and teaching (Vemulapalli et al., 2017); additionally, there is no substitute of another animal or simulation for production research."
- Key point: Review must consider alternatives (e.g. cell culture, computer simulation); but the guide notes that hands-on work with animals is an important part of agricultural research and teaching, and production research has no substitute animals or simulations.

### E10

- Location: Chapter 1, Protocol Review
- Quote: "justification for the species and strain of animal used; justification for the number of animals used; description of procedures that may cause discomfort, distress, or pain"
- Key point: The protocol must justify the species and strain used and the number of animals, and describe procedures that may cause discomfort, distress, or pain.

### E11

- Location: Chapter 2: Agricultural Animal Health Care, Euthanasia
- Quote: "Protocols for euthanasia should follow the specifications for "acceptable" or "acceptable with conditions" techniques as described in the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (AVMA, 2020) and include the method for confirming that death has occurred." / "The current edition of the AVMA guidelines should be considered the primary standard for euthanasia."
- Key point: Euthanasia protocols should use techniques rated "acceptable" or "acceptable with conditions" in the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals and state how death will be confirmed; the current AVMA guidelines are the main standard for euthanasia.

### E12

- Location: Chapter 3: Husbandry, Housing, and Biosecurity (research with high-consequence pathogens)
- Quote: "Research or other activities with high-consequence livestock and poultry pathogens causing high morbidity and mortality (e.g., tuberculosis, foot and mouth disease) or the vectors carrying these pathogens (e.g., mosquitoes, ticks) must be conducted in biocontainment."
- Key point: Research with high-consequence livestock and poultry pathogens that cause high morbidity and mortality (e.g. tuberculosis, foot and mouth disease), or with their vectors, must be done in biocontainment facilities.

### E13

- Location: Chapter 1: Institutional Policies (opening)
- Quote: "Scientists, veterinarians, and others must assume responsibility for animal welfare and uphold the rigor and integrity of agricultural animal research and instruction."
- Key point: Scientists, veterinarians, and others must take responsibility for animal welfare and uphold the rigor and integrity of agricultural animal research and teaching.

### E14

- Location: Chapter 1, Special Considerations, Genetically Engineered, Gene Edited, and Cloned Animals
- Quote: "Guidelines for research involving genetically engineered (GE) and gene edited (GEd) animals do not differ materially from those that apply to conventional animals used in research except under special conditions. The published scientific literature has not established the need for unique guidelines."
- Key point: Except in special cases, guidelines for research on genetically engineered and gene-edited animals do not differ materially from those for conventional research animals; the published literature does not show a need for separate guidelines.

### E15

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "The Ag Guide comprises subject-oriented chapters covering subjects that pertain to all the agricultural animal species (chapters 1 to 5) and species chapters, which fall under the purview of the respective association(s) dedicated to the species (chapters 6 to 13)."
- Key point: Chapters 1 to 5 are subject chapters that apply to all agricultural animal species; chapters 6 to 13 are species chapters.
