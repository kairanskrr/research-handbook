# Freelon 2018: Computational Research in the Post-API Age

- Source: `freelon2018computational`, [Author's website version](http://dfreelon.org/publications/2018_Computational_research_in_the_postAPI_age.pdf)
- Version: Political Communication 35(4), 665–668, 2018, DOI 10.1080/10584609.2018.1477506; read from the accepted manuscript on the author's website (dated 2018-08-20, "Forthcoming in Political Communication"), 6 pages. Locations are given as subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "The closure of the Pages API eliminated all terms of service (TOS)-compliant access to Facebook content."
- Key point: After Facebook closed its Pages API in April 2018, there was no longer any terms-of-service-compliant way to access Facebook content.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "When companies can restrict or eliminate API access at any time, for any reason, and without any recourse, computational researchers and students need to seriously consider how to proceed."
- Key point: Companies can restrict or eliminate API access at any time, for any reason, with no recourse, so researchers need to think seriously about how to proceed.

### E3

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "First, they should learn how to scrape the web; and second, they should understand the potential consequences of violating platforms’ TOS by doing so."
- Key point: The author advises researchers to learn web scraping and to understand the potential consequences of violating platforms' terms of service by doing so.

### E4

- Location: Terms of service, Use authorized methods whenever possible
- Quote: "Before scraping a site that prohibits it, exhaust all possible means of procuring your data via officially-sanctioned means."
- Key point: Before scraping a site that prohibits it, exhaust every officially sanctioned way of getting the data.

### E5

- Location: Terms of service, Do not confuse TOS compliance with human subjects compliance or privacy protection
- Quote: "The purpose of TOS is to protect the companies that create them."
- Key point: Terms of service exist to protect the companies that write them.

### E6

- Location: Terms of service, Do not confuse TOS compliance with human subjects compliance or privacy protection
- Quote: "By employing TOS-compliant methods, you are respecting the business prerogatives of the company that created the platform you are studying, but you may or may not be respecting the dignity and privacy of the platform’s users."
- Key point: Complying with the terms of service respects the company's business prerogatives; it does not necessarily respect the dignity and privacy of the platform's users.

### E7

- Location: Terms of service, Use authorized methods whenever possible
- Quote: "Twitter’s API allows generous prospective collection of tweets but sharply limits retrospective collection. Therefore, researchers might set up a server-based data collection workflow that can quickly be initialized as events of theoretical interest occur."
- Key point: At the time, Twitter's API sharply limited retrospective collection but allowed generous prospective collection, so researchers could set up a collection workflow in advance and start it as soon as an event occurs.

### E8

- Location: Terms of service, Understand the risks of violating TOS
- Quote: "Until it is resolved, researchers should bear in mind the potential (if unlikely) consequences of even small-scale TOS violations."
- Key point: Until the relevant lawsuit is resolved, keep in mind the potential (if unlikely) consequences of even small-scale terms-of-service violations.

### E9

- Location: Terms of service, Understand the risks of violating TOS
- Quote: "the only data it allows to be shared are unique object IDs from which the (undeleted) original data can be reconstituted."
- Key point: At the time, Twitter allowed only tweet IDs to be shared, from which others could reconstitute the original data that had not been deleted.
