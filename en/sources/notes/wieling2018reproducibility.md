# Wieling, Rawee & van Noord 2018: Reproducibility in Computational Linguistics: Are We Willing to Share?

- Source: `wieling2018reproducibility`, [original](https://aclanthology.org/J18-4003/)
- Version: Computational Linguistics 44(4), 641-649 (2018), DOI 10.1162/coli_a_00330, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 open access; the authors are at the University of Groningen, the Netherlands. The papers surveyed are from the Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL).

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We surveyed all 395 full papers presented at the 2011 and 2016 ACL Annual Meetings, and identified whether links to data and code were provided." / "For a selection of ten papers, we attempted to reproduce the results using the provided data and code. We were able to reproduce the results approximately for six papers. For only a single paper did we obtain the exact same results."
- Key point: The authors surveyed all 395 full papers from the 2011 and 2016 ACL Annual Meetings for links to data and code; for ten selected papers they tried to reproduce the results with the authors' data and code, roughly reproducing about six, with only one matching exactly.

### E2

- Location: §1 (reporting Fokkens et al. 2013)
- Quote: "many details (including pre-processing, the experimental set-up, versioning, system output, and system variations) are important in reproducing the exact results of published research."
- Key point: Many details, such as preprocessing, experimental setup, and software versions, affect whether published results can be reproduced exactly.

### E3

- Location: §4 Discussion
- Quote: "Approaches such as providing a virtual (e.g., Docker) image with all software, source code, and data, or using CodaLab worksheets as done by Liang, Jordan, and Klein (2011) might prove to be worthwhile in order to ensure a more effortless reproduction."
- Key point: Practices such as providing a virtual image (e.g., Docker) with all the software, source code, and data may make reproduction easier.
