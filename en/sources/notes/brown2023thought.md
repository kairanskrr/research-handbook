# Brown & Fehige 2023: Thought Experiments (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

- Source: `brown2023thought`, [Original](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/thought-experiment/)
- Version: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry by James Robert Brown and Yiftach Fehige; first published 1996, substantively revised November 28, 2023. Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Thought experiments are basically devices of the imagination."
- Key point: Thought experiments are basically devices of the imagination.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Few would object to thought experiments that serve to illustrate complex states of affairs, or those that are used in educational contexts. The situation is different, however, with respect to the appropriation of imagined scenarios to investigate reality"
- Key point: Few object to thought experiments used to illustrate complex situations or for teaching; using imagined scenarios to investigate reality is contested.

### E3

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Secondly, they are used in many disciplines, including biology, economics, history, mathematics, philosophy, and physics (although, interestingly, not with the same frequency in each)."
- Key point: Thought experiments are used in many disciplines, including biology, economics, history, mathematics, philosophy, and physics, though not equally often in each.

### E4

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "Intuition pumps are often abused, though seldom deliberately"
- Key point: (The entry quotes Dennett 1985.) "Intuition pump" thought experiments are often abused, though seldom deliberately.

### E5

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "Wilkes wants philosophy "to use science fact rather than science fiction or fantasy" (Wilkes 1988, p. 1)"
- Key point: Wilkes wants philosophy to use science fact rather than science fiction or fantasy.

### E6

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "We also agree that the inferences drawn in thought experimenting are highly problematic if the hypothetical scenario "is inadequately described" (Wilkes 1988, p. 8)."
- Key point: The entry's authors agree that inferences drawn from a thought experiment are highly problematic if the hypothetical scenario is inadequately described.

### E7

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "That is to say that the more detailed the imaginary scenario in the relevant aspects is, the better the thought experiment"
- Key point: The more detailed the imagined scenario is in the relevant respects, the better the thought experiment.

### E8

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "her suggestion that thought experimental scenarios would have to satisfy current scientific knowledge about the relevant entities featured in a thought experiment is highly implausible."
- Key point: The entry's authors find Wilkes's demand that thought-experiment scenarios satisfy current scientific knowledge highly implausible.

### E9

- Location: §4.1 The Skeptical Objection
- Quote: "This is indeed problematic because intuitions can be highly misleading"
- Key point: Intuitions can be highly misleading.
