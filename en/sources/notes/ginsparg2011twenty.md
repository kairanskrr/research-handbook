# Ginsparg 2011: It was twenty years ago today ...

- Source: `ginsparg2011twenty`, [Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.2700)
- Version: arXiv:1108.2700v2, 2011. A 20th-anniversary retrospective by the founder of arXiv, adapted from his 2008 article in Physics World.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "To mark the 20th anniversary of the commencement of hep-th@xxx.lanl.gov (now arXiv.org)"
- Key point: arXiv began as an email preprint server for high-energy physics theory (hep-th).

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "hep-th@xxx.lanl.gov received its first email submission on 14 Aug 1991."
- Key point: The server received its first submission on August 14, 1991.

### E3

- Location: p. 2, the era of paper preprints
- Quote: "For purely practical reasons, authors at the time used to mail photocopies of their newly minted articles to only a small number of people."
- Key point: In the era of paper preprints, authors could mail photocopies of new papers to only a small number of people.

### E4

- Location: p. 2, the era of paper preprints
- Quote: "aspiring researchers at non-elite institutions were frequently out of the privileged loop entirely."
- Key point: Young researchers at non-elite institutions were often entirely out of the loop.

### E5

- Location: p. 7
- Quote: "the current metastable state in physics publications, of preprint servers happily coexisting with conventional online publications, the two playing different roles."
- Key point: Physics today has preprint servers coexisting with conventional journals, the two playing different roles.

### E6

- Location: p. 7
- Quote: "Physicists have been quick to adopt widespread pre-refereed distribution of scientific papers, but that has not been the case in other fields."
- Key point: Physicists quickly adopted widespread distribution of papers before peer review; other fields have not necessarily done so.

### E7

- Location: p. 7
- Quote: "In the biomedical and life sciences, for example, adoption of preprint servers may be impeded by a long-standing tradition of regarding only refereed journal publication as a legitimate intellectual priority claim"
- Key point: In the biomedical and life sciences, a long tradition of regarding only refereed journal publication as a legitimate priority claim may have held back preprints.
