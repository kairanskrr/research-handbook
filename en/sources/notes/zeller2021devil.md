# Zeller 2021: The Devil's Guide to Incremental Research

- Source: `zeller2021devil`, [original](https://andreas-zeller.info/2021/04/15/the-devils-guide-to-incremental-research.html)
- Version: The author's blog, 2021-04-15, originally a tweet thread. The whole piece is satire: it lists tricks for padding out papers as "the devil's advice," and readers should read it in reverse.

### E1

- Location: THE CHERRY SHUFFLE
- Quote: "Run it again and again until it once outperforms the competition. Report these “better” results. Save the seed for later reproduction, and quietly drop the results of the earlier experiments."
- Key point: (Satire) If your method is randomized, run it again and again until it once beats the competition, report only that run, and quietly drop the other results.

### E2

- Location: THE AUTOTUNE
- Quote: "Auto-tune them towards your benchmark until you are significantly “better” than the untuned competition."
- Key point: (Satire) Tune your own method's parameters toward the test benchmark, then compare against untuned competitors.

### E3

- Location: THE ACHILLES HEEL
- Quote: "Your achievements go into the title, limitations into a footnote."
- Key point: (Satire) Achievements go in the title, limitations in a footnote.

### E4

- Location: THE FISHING EXPEDITION
- Quote: "Use a hypothesis like “The more X in a file, the more Y in it, too.” Whether X and Y are comments, loops, bugs, or emojis - anything will work as a “novel insight”."
- Key point: (Satire) Correlate any two quantities and you will always find a publishable "novel insight."

### E5

> Withdrawn: duplicate of [E8](#e8) (its quote is fully contained in E8).

- Location: Third-to-last paragraph (Be efficient)
- Quote: "Do not register your study. Do not keep logs of experiments or decisions. Do not keep a history. Do not test your code. Do not validate your results. Do not share anything."
- Key point: (Satire) Don't register your study, don't keep experiment logs, don't test your code, don't validate your results, don't share anything. Read in reverse, it is a checklist of good practice.

### E6

- Location: THE RUBE GOLDBERG
- Quote: "take some simple technique and attach some huge machinery – say, a machine learner and a data corpus. Invariably, this will improve results somewhat, and you can claim you are “better”!"
- Key point: (Satire) Attach huge machinery to a simple technique, and results will always improve a little, so you can claim to be "better" without accounting for the cost.

### E7

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "patterns that maximize the number of publications despite limited significance"
- Key point: The author targets a class of "recipes" that maximize the number of publications despite limited research significance.

### E8

- Location: Third-to-last paragraph
- Quote: "Do not register your study. Do not keep logs of experiments or decisions. Do not keep a history. Do not test your code. Do not validate your results. Do not share anything. None of this is misconduct!"
- Key point: (Satire) Not preregistering, not keeping experiment logs, not keeping version history, not testing code, not validating results, and not sharing are "none of them misconduct," yet all of them undermine the credibility of research.
