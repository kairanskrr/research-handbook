# Sargeant et al. 2016: Methods and Processes of Developing the STROBE-Vet Statement

- Source: `sargeant2016strobevet`, [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5115188/)
- Version: Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine 30(6):1887-1895, 2016, open access (CC BY-NC). The same article was published simultaneously in four other journals. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract, Objective
- Quote: "To develop an extension of the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) statement that addresses unique reporting requirements for observational studies in veterinary medicine related to health, production, welfare, and food safety."
- Key point: STROBE-Vet is the veterinary extension of STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology), for observational studies in veterinary medicine on health, production, welfare, and food safety.

### E2

- Location: Introduction (first paragraph)
- Quote: "Thus, observational studies may be used to estimate the prevalence or incidence of a condition, to investigate the distribution of conditions in time and space, to explore risk factors and compare management options, to create explanatory models, or to evaluate diagnostic test accuracy."
- Key point: Veterinary observational studies can estimate prevalence or incidence, investigate the distribution of conditions in time and space, explore risk factors and compare management options, build explanatory models, and evaluate diagnostic test accuracy.

### E3

- Location: Introduction (first paragraph)
- Quote: "Observational studies are a common methodological approach in veterinary research"
- Key point: Observational studies are a common methodological approach in veterinary research.

### E4

- Location: Results (scope)
- Quote: "Studies involving human health outcomes related to animal exposure were considered outside the scope of this initiative. For these studies, the original STROBE statement would be the appropriate guideline to use."
- Key point: Studies of human health outcomes related to animal exposure are outside the scope of STROBE-Vet; use the original STROBE statement for them.

### E5

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "However, in veterinary medicine, many observational studies do not adhere strictly to one of these three classical designs, and large population cohort studies are rare."
- Key point: In veterinary medicine, many observational studies do not strictly follow one of the three classical designs (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional), and large population cohort studies are rare.

### E6

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "In veterinary medicine, there generally are two components to the concept of “participant:” the owner/manager of the animals included in the study population and the animals themselves." / "a footnote was added to note this point and to recommend that relevant information concerning both types of “participants” should be reported."
- Key point: In veterinary research, "participant" usually has two components: the owner or manager of the animals, and the animals themselves; a footnote was added to the checklist recommending that information on both be reported.

### E7

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "It is common in veterinary medicine, particularly in livestock and shelter medicine (where companion animals are kenneled), for animals to be housed or managed in groups." / "It is necessary to account for any non‐independence of the observational units in the design, sampling strategy, and statistical analysis to avoid violating the assumption of independence underlying many statistical procedures."
- Key point: Housing or managing animals in groups is common in veterinary medicine, especially in livestock and shelter medicine; design, sampling, and statistical analysis must account for non-independence between observational units.

### E8

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "The non‐independence of observational units may be hierarchical, for instance animals within pens, pens within barns, barns within same‐owner facilities. However, this is not always the case." / "non‐independence can also result from repeated samples taken over time from the same animal or facility."
- Key point: Non-independence may be hierarchical (animals within pens, pens within barns, barns within same-owner facilities), but not always; repeated sampling of the same animal or facility also creates non-independence.

### E9

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "An example of an addition is item 7 (variables), which now calls for the specification of the putative causal structure (with a causal diagram being highly encouraged) for all hypothesis‐driven studies."
- Key point: A new requirement: every hypothesis-driven study must specify the putative causal structure, and a causal diagram is highly encouraged.

### E10

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "With numbered subitems, the transparency item addresses sources of funding, conflicts of interest, authors' roles, ethical approval (animal, human, or data use, as applicable), and the use of any quality standards."
- Key point: The final item was expanded into "transparency": sources of funding, conflicts of interest, authors' roles, ethical approval (animal, human, or data use), and use of quality standards.

### E11

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "We strongly recommend that the STROBE‐Vet checklist be used in conjunction with the explanation and elaboration document for all observational studies related to animal health, production, welfare, or food safety outcomes."
- Key point: The authors strongly recommend that all observational studies with animal health, production, welfare, or food safety outcomes use the STROBE-Vet checklist together with the explanation and elaboration document.
