# ICMJE 2026: Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals

- Source: `icmje2026recommendations`, [original PDF](https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf)
- Version: International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE), updated January 2026. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §I.B Who Should Use the Recommendations?
- Quote: "Many non-ICMJE journals voluntarily use these recommendations. The ICMJE encourages that use but has no authority to monitor or enforce it. In all cases, authors should use these recommendations along with individual journals’ instructions to authors."
- Key point: Many non-ICMJE journals voluntarily use these recommendations, but ICMJE has no authority to enforce them; authors should use them together with each journal's instructions for authors.

### E2

- Location: §II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "1. Substantial contributions to the conception or design of the work; or the acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data for the work; AND 2. Drafting the work or reviewing it critically for important intellectual content; AND 3. Final approval of the version to be published; AND 4. Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work"
- Key point: The four authorship criteria: substantial contributions to conception or design, or to data acquisition, analysis, or interpretation; drafting or critically revising the work; approving the final version; and agreeing to be accountable for all aspects of the work. All four must be met ("AND").

### E3

- Location: §II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for authorship, and all who meet the four criteria should be identified as authors."
- Key point: Everyone listed as an author must meet all four criteria, and everyone who meets all four should be listed as an author.

### E4

- Location: §II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "The individuals who conduct the work are responsible for identifying who meets these criteria and ideally should do so when planning the work, making modifications as appropriate as the work progresses."
- Key point: The people who do the work decide who meets the criteria, ideally when planning the work, adjusting as it progresses.

### E5

- Location: §II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "If agreement cannot be reached about who qualifies for authorship, the institution(s) where the work was performed, not the journal editor, should be asked to investigate."
- Key point: If there is an authorship dispute, the institution where the work was done, not the journal editor, should investigate.

### E6

- Location: §II.A.2 Who Is an Author? (large multi-author groups)
- Quote: "When a large multi-author group has conducted the work, the group ideally should decide who will be an author before the work is started and confirm who is an author before submitting the manuscript for publication."
- Key point: Large multi-author groups should ideally decide who will be an author before the work starts and confirm it before submission.

### E7

- Location: §II.A.3 Non-Author Contributors
- Quote: "Examples of activities [...] that alone (without other contributions) do not qualify a contributor for authorship are acquisition of funding; general supervision of a research group or general administrative support; and writing assistance, technical editing, language editing, and proofreading."
- Key point: Acquiring funding, general supervision or administrative support, or writing assistance and language editing do not alone qualify someone for authorship.

### E8

- Location: §II.A.3 Non-Author Contributors
- Quote: "Those whose contributions do not justify authorship may be acknowledged individually or together as a group under a single heading (e.g., “Clinical Investigators” or “Participating Investigators”), and their contributions should be specified"
- Key point: Contributors who do not qualify for authorship can be acknowledged individually or as a group under a single heading such as "Clinical Investigators," with their contributions specified.

### E9

- Location: §II.B.1 Participants
- Quote: "The potential for conflict of interest and bias exists when professional judgment concerning a primary interest (such as patients' welfare or the validity of research) may be influenced by a secondary interest (such as financial gain). Perceptions of conflict of interest are as important as actual conflicts of interest."
- Key point: A conflict of interest exists when professional judgment about a primary interest (such as patients' welfare or the validity of research) may be influenced by a secondary interest (such as financial gain); the appearance of a conflict matters as much as an actual one.

### E10

- Location: §II.B.1 Participants
- Quote: "Financial relationships (such as employment, consultancies, stock ownership or options, honoraria, patents, and paid expert testimony) are the most easily identifiable, the ones most often judged to represent potential conflicts of interest"
- Key point: Financial relationships such as employment, consultancies, stock ownership, honoraria, patents, and paid expert testimony are the easiest to identify and the most often judged to be potential conflicts of interest.

### E11

- Location: §II.E Protection of Research Participants
- Quote: "All investigators should ensure that the planning, conduct, and reporting of human research are in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration as revised in 2024"
- Key point: Human research must be planned, conducted, and reported in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki as revised in 2024.

### E12

- Location: §III.B Scientific Misconduct
- Quote: "Some people consider failure to publish the results of clinical trials and other human studies a form of scientific misconduct."
- Key point: Some people consider failing to publish the results of clinical trials and other human studies a form of scientific misconduct.

### E13

- Location: §III.D.3 Preprints
- Quote: "Authors should become familiar with the policies of journals they wish to submit their work to prior to posting work on a preprint server."
- Key point: Before posting a preprint, learn the target journal's preprint policy.

### E14

- Location: §III.D.3.b Submitting Manuscripts That Are in Preprint Archives to a Peer-Reviewed Journal
- Quote: "Authors should inform a journal if the work submitted to the journal has been posted on a preprint server and provide a link to the preprint"
- Key point: When submitting, tell the journal the work has been posted as a preprint and give a link.

### E15

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "Briefly, the ICMJE recommends that all medical journal editors require, registration of clinical trials in a public trials registry at or before the time of first patient consent for enrollment as a condition of consideration for publication."
- Key point: ICMJE recommends that journals require registration in a public trials registry at or before the time of first patient consent for enrollment as a condition of considering a trial for publication.

### E16

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "The ICMJE defines a clinical trial as any research project that prospectively assigns people or a group of people to an intervention, with or without concurrent comparison or control groups, to study the relationship between a health-related intervention and a health outcome."
- Key point: The definition of a clinical trial: a study that prospectively assigns people to an intervention to study the relationship between a health-related intervention and a health outcome, with or without a control group.

### E17

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "Health-related interventions are those used to modify a biomedical or health-related outcome; examples include drugs, surgical procedures, devices, behavioral treatments, educational programs, dietary interventions, quality improvement interventions, and process-of-care changes."
- Key point: Interventions are defined broadly: drugs, surgical procedures, and devices, but also behavioral treatments, educational programs, dietary interventions, quality improvement, and process-of-care changes.

### E18

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "Approval to conduct a study from an independent local, regional, or national review body (e.g., ethics committee, institutional review board) does not fulfill the ICMJE requirement for prospective clinical trial registration."
- Key point: Approval by an ethics committee does not count as trial registration.

### E19

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "The purpose of clinical trial registration is to prevent selective publication and selective reporting of research outcomes, to prevent unnecessary duplication of research effort, to help patients and the public know what trials are planned or ongoing into which they might want to enroll"
- Key point: Registration prevents selective publication and selective outcome reporting, avoids unnecessary duplication of research, and lets patients and the public know which trials they could join.

### E20

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "Retrospective registration, for example at the time of manuscript submission, meets none of these purposes."
- Key point: Retrospective registration (for example, at the time of submission) meets none of these purposes.

### E21

- Location: §III.L.1 Registration
- Quote: "the ICMJE encourages registration of research with non-trial designs, but because the exposure or intervention in non-trial research is not dictated by the researchers, the ICMJE does not require it."
- Key point: For non-trial designs such as observational studies, ICMJE encourages but does not require registration.

### E22

- Location: §III.L.3 Data Sharing
- Quote: "1. As of 1 July 2018 manuscripts submitted to ICMJE journals that report the results of clinical trials must contain a data sharing statement as described below. 2. Clinical trials that begin enrolling participants on or after 1 January 2019 must include a data sharing plan in the trial’s registration."
- Key point: Since 1 July 2018, clinical trial manuscripts submitted to ICMJE journals must include a data sharing statement; trials that begin enrolling on or after 1 January 2019 must include a data sharing plan in their registration.

### E23

- Location: §III.L.3 Data Sharing
- Quote: "Data sharing statements must indicate the following: whether individual deidentified participant data (including data dictionaries) will be shared (“undecided” is not an acceptable answer); what data in particular will be shared; whether additional, related documents will be available (e.g., study protocol, statistical analysis plan, etc.); when the data will become available and for how long; by what access criteria data will be shared"
- Key point: A data sharing statement must say whether de-identified individual participant data will be shared ("undecided" is not acceptable), which data, whether documents such as the protocol and statistical analysis plan are available, when and for how long, and under what access conditions.

### E24

- Location: §IV.A.2 Reporting Guidelines
- Quote: "Journals are encouraged to ask authors to follow these guidelines because they help authors describe the study in enough detail for it to be evaluated by editors, reviewers, readers, and other [...] researchers evaluating the medical literature."
- Key point: Journals are encouraged to ask authors to follow reporting guidelines, because they help authors describe a study in enough detail for editors, reviewers, and readers to evaluate it.

### E25

- Location: §IV.A.2 Reporting Guidelines
- Quote: "examples include CONSORT (www.consort-spirit.org/) for randomized trials, STROBE for observational studies (http://strobe-statement.org/), PRISMA for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (http:// prisma-statement.org/), and STARD for studies of diagnostic accuracy"
- Key point: Common reporting guidelines: CONSORT for randomized trials, STROBE for observational studies, PRISMA for systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and STARD for diagnostic accuracy studies.

### E26

- Location: II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for authorship, and all who meet the four criteria should be identified as authors. Those who do not meet all four criteria should be acknowledged"
- Key point: Every author should meet all four criteria, everyone who meets all four should be listed as an author, and those who do not should be acknowledged.

### E27

- Location: II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "The criteria used to determine the order in which [...] authors are listed on the byline may vary, and are to be decided collectively by the author group and not by editors."
- Key point: The criteria for author order may vary and are decided collectively by the authors, not by editors.

### E28

- Location: II.A.2 Who Is an Author?
- Quote: "The corresponding author is the one individual who takes primary responsibility for communication with the journal during the manuscript submission, peer-review, and publication process."
- Key point: The corresponding author is the one person primarily responsible for communicating with the journal during submission, peer review, and publication.

### E29

- Location: II.A.4 Artificial Intelligence (AI)–Assisted Technology
- Quote: "Chatbots (such as ChatGPT) should not be listed as authors because they cannot be responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the work, and these responsibilities are required for authorship"
- Key point: Chatbots cannot be listed as authors, because they cannot be responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the work, which authorship requires.

### E30

- Location: II.A.4 Artificial Intelligence (AI)–Assisted Technology
- Quote: "For example, if AI was used for writing assistance, describe this in the acknowledgment section (see Section II.A.3). If AI was used for data collection, analysis, or figure generation, authors should describe this use in the methods"
- Key point: If AI was used for writing assistance, say so in the acknowledgments; if it was used for data collection, analysis, or figure generation, describe it in the Methods.

### E31

- Location: II.A.4 Artificial Intelligence (AI)–Assisted Technology
- Quote: "Authors should carefully review and edit the result because AI can generate authoritative-sounding output that can be incorrect, incomplete, or biased."
- Key point: AI output can sound authoritative yet be incorrect, incomplete, or biased, so authors must carefully review and edit it.

### E32

- Location: IV.A.3.d Methods
- Quote: "Authors who used AI technology to conduct the study should describe its use in the Methods section in sufficient detail to enable replication of the approach, including the tool used, version, and prompts where applicable."
- Key point: If AI was used to conduct the study, describe it in the Methods in enough detail for replication, including the tool, the version, and, where applicable, the prompts.

### E33

- Location: V.A Use of AI by Authors
- Quote: "Nondisclosure of AI use may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct in some circumstances"
- Key point: Failing to disclose AI use may require corrective action and in some circumstances may be treated as misconduct.

### E34

- Location: II.C.1.a Predatory or Pseudo-Journals
- Quote: "These journals (“predatory” or “pseudo-journals”) accept and publish almost all submissions and charge article processing (or publication) fees, often informing authors about this after a paper’s acceptance for publication."
- Key point: "Predatory" journals (pseudo-journals) accept and publish almost all submissions and charge publication fees, often telling authors about the fee only after acceptance.

### E35

- Location: II.C.1.a Predatory or Pseudo-Journals
- Quote: "They often claim to perform peer review but do not and may purposefully use names similar to well-established journals."
- Key point: They often claim to do peer review but do not, and may deliberately use names similar to well-established journals.

### E36

- Location: II.C.1.a Predatory or Pseudo-Journals
- Quote: "Authors have a responsibility to evaluate the integrity, history, practices, and reputation of the journals to which they submit manuscripts."
- Key point: Authors are responsible for evaluating the integrity, history, practices, and reputation of the journals they submit to.
