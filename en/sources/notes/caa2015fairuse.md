# College Art Association 2015: Code of Best Practices in Fair Use for the Visual Arts

- Source: `caa2015fairuse`, [Original PDF](https://www.collegeart.org/pdf/fair-use/best-practices-fair-use-visual-arts.pdf)
- Version: February 2015. Published by the College Art Association (CAA); project leads Patricia Aufderheide and Peter Jaszi (American University). Applies only to fair use under US copyright law. Letters split by drop caps in the source text (such as "T he") have been rejoined. Locations are given as subheadings and page numbers.

### E1

- Location: When the Code Does and Does Not Apply, pp. 6–7
- Quote: "Fair use is part of US copyright law, and the Code applies to the activities of members of the visual arts community who use copyrighted works in the US in furtherance of their professional goals. [...] The Code does not apply to reproductions, distributions, | CODE OF BEST PRACTICES performances, and other uses outside the United States"
- Key point: Fair use is part of US copyright law; the Code applies only to uses in the United States, not elsewhere.

### E2

- Location: One: Analytic Writing, Principle, p. 9
- Quote: "In their analytic writing about art, scholars and other writers (and, by extension, their publishers) may invoke fair use to quote, excerpt, or reproduce copyrighted works, subject to certain limitations:"
- Key point: In analytic writing, scholars (and their publishers) may invoke fair use to quote, excerpt, or reproduce copyrighted works, subject to certain limitations.

### E3

- Location: One: Analytic Writing, Limitations, p. 9
- Quote: "The writer’s use of the work, whether in part or in whole, should be justified by the analytic objective, and the user should be prepared to articulate that justification."
- Key point: The use (of part or all of a work) should be justified by the analytic objective, and the user should be ready to articulate that justification.

### E4

- Location: One: Analytic Writing, Limitations, p. 9
- Quote: "The writer’s analytic objective should predominate over that of merely representing the work or works used."
- Key point: The analytic objective should predominate over merely presenting the work.

### E5

- Location: One: Analytic Writing, Limitations, p. 9
- Quote: "the size and resolution of the published reproduction should not exceed that appropriate to the analytic objective."
- Key point: The size and resolution of images should not exceed what the analytic objective requires.

### E6

- Location: One: Analytic Writing, Limitations, p. 10
- Quote: "The writing should provide attribution of the original work as is customary in the field, to the extent possible."
- Key point: Attribute the original work as is customary in the field.

### E7

- Location: Why the Code?, p. 6
- Quote: "reported that the practices of many professionals in the visual arts are constrained due to the pervasive perception that permissions to use third-party materials are required even where a confident exercise of fair use would be appropriate."
- Key point: A CAA-commissioned report found that many visual arts professionals wrongly believe permission is always required, even where fair use could be confidently exercised, and that this constrains their work.

### E8

- Location: When the Code Does and Does Not Apply, p. 7
- Quote: "fair use cannot be invoked to avoid paying such fees."
- Key point: Fees charged by collections as the "sole source" of an image are not based on copyright, and fair use cannot be invoked to avoid them.

### E9

- Location: When the Code Does and Does Not Apply, p. 7
- Quote: "Fair use and, therefore, the Code, also do not apply to the extent that a license agreement or other contract controls reproduction or other use."
- Key point: Where a license agreement or contract governs reproduction and use, neither fair use nor the Code applies.

### E10

- Location: Three: Making Art, Limitations, p. 11
- Quote: "Artists should avoid uses of existing copyrighted material that do not generate new artistic meaning, being aware that a change of medium, without more, may not meet this standard."
- Key point: Artists using others' work should generate new artistic meaning; a mere change of medium may not be enough.

### E11

- Location: Three: Making Art, Limitations, p. 11
- Quote: "When copying another’s work, an artist should cite the source, whether in the new work or elsewhere (by means such as labeling or embedding), unless there is an articulable aesthetic basis for not doing so."
- Key point: When copying another's work, cite the source unless there is an articulable aesthetic reason not to.

### E12

- Location: Message from the CAA President, p. 2
- Quote: "surveying nearly 12,000 CAA members on their use of copyrighted materials, conducting 100 interviews"
- Key point: The Code is based on a survey of nearly 12,000 CAA members and 100 interviews.

### E13

- Location: When the Code Does and Does Not Apply, p. 7
- Quote: "Bridgeman, however, does not on its face apply to still photographs of three-dimensional works, such as sculpture, architecture, and performance art."
- Key point: The US Bridgeman ruling (faithful photographs of two-dimensional works are not protected by copyright) does not on its face apply to photographs of sculpture, architecture, or performance art.

### E14

- Location: When the Code Does and Does Not Apply, p. 7
- Quote: "Similarly, the Code is not a guide to—and, again, is not needed for—the use of material that is in the public domain."
- Key point: Public domain material does not need the Code.
