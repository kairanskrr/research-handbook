# American Fisheries Society et al. 2014: Guidelines for the Use of Fishes in Research

- Source: `afs2014fishes`, [original](https://fisheries.org/policy-media/science-guidelines/guidelines-for-the-use-of-fishes-in-research/)
- Version: 2014 edition, prepared by a joint committee of the American Fisheries Society (AFS), the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, and the American Institute of Fishery Research Biologists. The website returned 403 to scripted access, so the text is taken from the Internet Archive snapshot of 2025-05-18 (the web page is the full guideline). Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: 2.3 Statistical Design
- Quote: "A true “replicate” is the smallest experimental unit to which a treatment can be applied independently. Pseudoreplication can result from wrongly treating multiple samples from one experimental unit as multiple experimental units or from using experimental units that are not statistically independent"
- Key point: A true "replicate" is the smallest experimental unit to which a treatment can be applied independently. Treating multiple samples from one experimental unit as multiple units, or using experimental units that are not statistically independent, leads to pseudoreplication.

### E2

- Location: 2.3 Statistical Design
- Quote: "In all cases, studies should be designed to use the fewest animals necessary to reliably answer the questions posed."
- Key point: In all cases, studies should be designed to use the fewest fish necessary to reliably answer the question.

### E3

- Location: 1. Introduction
- Quote: "All United States institutions that use vertebrate animals for research, teaching, research training, and biological testing are required to create an IACUC to oversee and evaluate all aspects of the institution’s animal care and use program."
- Key point: In the United States, every institution using vertebrate animals for research, teaching, or testing must set up an Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) to oversee animal care and use. Fish are vertebrates.

### E4

- Location: 3. Statutory Requirements and Regulatory Bodies
- Quote: "The investigator must have knowledge of all regulations pertaining to the animals under study, as well as to biosecurity issues, and must obtain all permits necessary for carrying out proposed studies"
- Key point: Investigators must know all regulations on the animals under study and on biosecurity, and obtain all permits needed for the research.

### E5

- Location: 3.1 International Regulations and Guidelines
- Quote: "Investigators working outside of the United States should comply with all wildlife regulations of the country in which the research is being performed."
- Key point: Investigators working outside the United States should comply with the wildlife regulations of the country where the research is done.

### E6

- Location: Final Disposition of Experimental Animals
- Quote: "Fish collected in the wild and brought into the laboratory for experimental or teaching purposes should never be returned to the environment according to the general consensus among agencies granting scientific collecting permits."
- Key point: According to the general consensus of agencies issuing scientific collecting permits, wild-caught fish brought into the laboratory for experiments or teaching should never be returned to the environment.
