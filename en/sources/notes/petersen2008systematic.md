# Petersen et al. 2008: Systematic Mapping Studies in Software Engineering

- Source: `petersen2008systematic`, [original PDF (author's homepage)](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~feldt/publications/petersen_ease08_sysmap_studies_in_se.pdf)
- Version: Paper at the Evaluation and Assessment in Software Engineering conference (EASE 2008). Page numbers are the page order of the PDF file.

### E1

- Location: §2, PDF p. 2
- Quote: "The essential process steps of our systematic mapping study are definition of research questions, conducting the search for relevant papers, screening of papers, keywording of abstracts and data extraction and mapping"
- Key point: A systematic mapping study has five steps: define research questions, search, screen papers, keyword the abstracts, and extract data and build the map.

### E2

- Location: §2.4, PDF p. 4
- Quote: "Keywording is done in two steps. First, the reviewers read abstracts and look for keywords and concepts that reflects the contribution of the paper. [...] When this is done, the set of keywords from different papers are combined together to develop a high level understanding about the nature and contribution of the research. [...] When abstracts are of too poor quality to allow meaningful keywords to be chosen, reviewers can choose to study also the introduction or conclusion sections of the paper."
- Key point: The classification scheme is built by keywording: first read abstracts and pick out keywords reflecting each paper's contribution, then combine and cluster the keywords into categories. If the abstract is not informative enough, read the introduction or conclusion.

### E3

- Location: §3.2, PDF p. 7
- Quote: "a systematic review aims at establishing the state of evidence [...] the systematic reviews we have found focus on identifying best practices based on empirical evidence [...]. This is not a goal for systematic maps"
- Key point: The goals differ: a systematic review aims to establish the state of evidence and identify practices with empirical support; a mapping study does not.

### E4

- Location: §3.2, PDF p. 7
- Quote: "In maps, the articles are not evaluated regarding their quality as the main goal is not to establish the state of evidence." / "In a systematic mapping study, more articles can be considered as they don't have to be evaluated in such detail."
- Key point: A mapping study generally does not assess the quality of individual papers, so it can cover more papers and a broader area; a systematic review assesses quality and is usually narrower and more work.

### E5

- Location: §3.2, PDF p. 8
- Quote: "a systematic review focusing on papers using some particular method might introduce a bias when it comes to presenting the overall research area."
- Key point: Including only papers that use a particular research method, then using them to describe the whole field, introduces bias.

### E6

- Location: §4, PDF p. 9
- Quote: "A mapping study that goes deeper into the papers can become more like a systematic review. The two type of studies can be considered as different points on a continuum."
- Key point: The deeper a mapping study goes into the papers, the more it resembles a systematic review; the two are points on a continuum.

### E7

- Location: Abstract (PDF p. 1); §1 (PDF p. 2)
- Quote: "Systematic maps and reviews are different in terms of goals, breadth, validity issues and implications. Thus, they should be used complementarily and require different methods (e.g., for analysis)." / "In this paper we clarify and expand upon the differences between systematic review and systematic mapping studies and argue for a broader set of situations where the latter is appropriate."
- Key point: Mapping studies and systematic reviews differ in goals, breadth, validity issues, and implications and should be used to complement each other; the authors argue mapping studies are applicable more widely than previously thought.

### E8

- Location: §2.5, PDF p. 5
- Quote: "In our study, we used a bubble plot to report the frequencies, shown in Figure 3."
- Key point: Mapping results can be shown as a bubble plot of the number of papers at each intersection of categories.
