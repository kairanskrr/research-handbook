# Taylor and Kuyatt 1994: NIST Technical Note 1297, Guidelines for Evaluating and Expressing the Uncertainty of NIST Measurement Results

- Source: `taylor1994nist`, [original](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote1297.pdf)
- Version: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Technical Note 1297, 1994 edition, a US government publication available free on the NIST website. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: 6.5 (citing NIST policy)
- Quote: "To be consistent with current international practice, the value of k to be used at NIST for calculating U is, by convention, k = 2."
- Key point: NIST's own rule: when calculating the expanded uncertainty U, use a coverage factor of k = 2 by convention, consistent with international practice at the time.

### E2

- Location: D.1.1.1, TN 1297 Comments item 2
- Quote: "Because “accuracy” is a qualitative concept, one should not use it quantitatively, that is, associate numbers with it; numbers should be associated with measures of uncertainty instead."
- Key point: "Accuracy" is a qualitative concept and should not be given a number; numbers should be attached to uncertainty.

### E3

- Location: D.1.2
- Quote: "Nevertheless, precision is often taken to mean simply repeatability."
- Key point: The word "precision" is often used simply to mean "repeatability."
