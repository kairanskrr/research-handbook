# Haddaway et al. 2018: ROSES RepOrting standards for Systematic Evidence Syntheses

- Source: `haddaway2018roses`, [Original](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13750-018-0121-7) (Environmental Evidence 7:7, open access, CC BY)
- Version: Journal article published in March 2018. Excerpts are from the publisher Springer's open-access full-text PDF (https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13750-018-0121-7.pdf, downloaded 2026-10-10); E1 and E2 were first taken from the article's abstract as indexed in DOAJ, which matches the abstract in the full text.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "However, due to its development for systematic reviews in healthcare, PRISMA has limited applicability for reviews in conservation and environmental management. We highlight 12 key problems with the application of PRISMA to this field, including an overemphasis on meta-analysis and no consideration for other synthesis methods."
- Key point: PRISMA was developed for systematic reviews in healthcare and has limited applicability to reviews in conservation and environmental management; the authors list 12 problems, such as an overemphasis on meta-analysis and no consideration of other synthesis methods.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We introduce ROSES (RepOrting standards for Systematic Evidence Syntheses), a pro forma and flow diagram designed specifically for systematic reviews and systematic maps in the field of conservation and environmental management."
- Key point: ROSES is a reporting pro forma and flow diagram designed specifically for systematic reviews and systematic maps in conservation and environmental management.

### E3

- Location: Key differences between ROSES and PRISMA, p. 4
- Quote: "Our proposed pro forma for systematic review and map protocols and reports can be found in Additional files 1, 2, respectively, along with the template flow diagram in Additional file 3."
- Key point: ROSES has two pro formas, one for systematic review and map protocols and one for final reports, plus a template flow diagram.

### E4

- Location: Key differences between ROSES and PRISMA, p. 5
- Quote: "Thirdly, we have departed from the format of PRISMA by not only providing reporting guidance in a checklist form, but also including requirements to record summary information (i.e. meta-data) that describes the key steps in the conduct of reviews."
- Key point: Unlike PRISMA, ROSES not only gives a checklist but also requires authors to record summary information (meta-data) describing the key steps of the review.

### E5

- Location: Background, p. 4
- Quote: "reviewers could also record and present in a standard form important summary details, such as the number of search results obtained before duplicate removal."
- Key point: An example of summary information: the number of search results obtained before duplicate removal.

### E6

- Location: Benefits of ROSES, p. 6
- Quote: "ROSES focuses on the earlier and middle stages of the review process, i.e. searching, screening, data extraction and critical appraisal, whilst there is limited detail regarding synthesis."
- Key point: ROSES focuses on the earlier and middle stages (searching, screening, data extraction, and critical appraisal) and gives limited detail on synthesis.

### E7

- Location: Benefits of ROSES, p. 6
- Quote: "However, ROSES is considerably longer than the current PRISMA forms and it will, therefore, undoubtedly take more time for authors to complete." / "We strongly recommend that authors begin to complete ROSES pro forma as early as possible."
- Key point: ROSES is considerably longer than PRISMA and takes more time to complete; the authors strongly recommend starting it as early as possible.
