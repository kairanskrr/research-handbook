# Vaux et al. 2012: Replicates and repeats—what is the difference and is it significant?

- Source: `vaux2012replicates`, [original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3321166/)
- Version: EMBO Reports 13(4): 291-296, 2012, by Vaux, Fidler, and Cumming, open full text on PubMed Central, accessed 2026-10-06. Locations describe the paragraph content or give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "As biological experiments can be complicated, replicate measurements are often taken to monitor the performance of the experiment, but such replicates are not independent tests of the hypothesis, and so they cannot provide evidence of the reproducibility of the main results." / "data from replicates cannot be used to draw inferences about the validity of a hypothesis, and therefore should not be used to calculate CIs or P values"
- Key point: Because biological experiments are complicated, replicate measurements are often taken to monitor how the experiment performs; but such replicates are not independent tests of the hypothesis, cannot show that the main result is reproducible, and should not be used to compute confidence intervals (CIs) or P values.

### E2

- Location: The bone marrow colony assay example
- Quote: "We have made just one comparison, so n = 1, no matter how many replicate plates we count. To make an inference that can be generalized to all WT mice and Bdl−/− mice, we need to repeat our experiments a number of times, making several independent comparisons using several mice of each type."
- Key point: When all cells come from one wild-type mouse and one knockout mouse, n = 1 no matter how many plates you count; to generalize to all such mice, you must make several independent comparisons with several mice.

### E3

- Location: Sidebar A, Fundamental principle 3
- Quote: "A conclusion can only apply to the population from which you took the random sample of independent measurements"
- Key point: A conclusion applies only to the population from which you took independent samples.

### E4

- Location: Why bother with replicates at all?
- Quote: "Replicates serve as internal quality checks on how the experiment was performed. If, for example, in the experiment described in Table 1 and Fig 1, one of the replicate plates with saline-treated WT bone marrow contained 100 colonies, you would immediately suspect that something was wrong. You could check the plate to see if it had been mislabelled. You might look at the colonies using a microscope and discover that they are actually contaminating colonies of yeast."
- Key point: Replicates serve as internal quality checks on how the experiment was performed. For example, if a control plate unexpectedly grew 100 colonies, you would immediately suspect a problem and check for mislabeling or contaminating yeast.

### E5

- Location: The quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) example
- Quote: "The three 10 μl aliquots each came from the same, single, mRNA preparation, so we can only make inferences about the contents of that particular tube."
- Key point: The three aliquots all came from the same mRNA preparation, so you can only draw conclusions about the contents of that one tube.

### E6

- Location: Sidebar A, Fundamental principle 2
- Quote: "Experimental design, at its simplest, is the art of varying one factor at a time while controlling others"
- Key point: Experimental design, at its simplest, means varying one factor at a time while controlling the others.

### E7

- Location: So, what is wrong?
- Quote: "Terms such as ‘required’, ‘essential’ and ‘obligatory’ are not relative, yet are still often incorrectly used when partial effects are seen."
- Key point: Words such as "required" and "essential" are not relative, yet people still often misuse them when only partial effects are seen.

### E8

- Location: Sidebar A, Fundamental principle 4
- Quote: "Values from an outlying replicate can be omitted if a convincing explanation is found, although repeating part or all of the experiment is a safer strategy. Results from an independent sample, however, can only be left out in exceptional circumstances"
- Key point: An outlying replicate can be omitted if a convincing explanation is found, though repeating part or all of the experiment is safer; results from an independent sample can be left out only in exceptional circumstances.
