# Open Science Collaboration 2015: Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science

- Source: `osc2015estimating`, [original PDF (author accepted manuscript, University of Dundee repository)](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/7385883/RPP_SCIENCE_2015.pdf)
- Version: Published in Science 349(6251), aac4716. This is the peer-reviewed accepted manuscript; page numbers follow the manuscript header "Reproducibility Project N."

### E1

- Location: Abstract, p. 2
- Quote: "We conducted replications of 100 experimental and correlational studies published in three psychology journals using high-powered designs and original materials when available. Replication effects were half the magnitude of original effects, representing a substantial decline."
- Key point: The project replicated 100 experimental and correlational studies from three psychology journals, using high-powered designs and original materials where available. Replication effect sizes were on average half those of the original studies.

### E2

- Location: Abstract, p. 2
- Quote: "Ninety-seven percent of original studies had significant results. Thirty-six percent of replications had significant results"
- Key point: 97% of original studies had significant results; only 36% of replications did.

### E3

- Location: Introduction, p. 3
- Quote: "A direct replication may not obtain the original result for a variety of reasons: Known or unknown differences between the replication and original study may moderate the size of an observed effect, the original result could have been a false positive, or the replication could produce a false negative."
- Key point: A replication can fail to reproduce the original result for three reasons: differences between the two studies, a false positive in the original, or a false negative in the replication. So one failed replication does not mean the original was necessarily wrong.

### E4

- Location: Method, p. 4
- Quote: "In total, 100 replications were completed by 270 contributing authors."
- Key point: The 100 replications were completed by 270 contributing authors, an example of a large collaborative project in psychology.

### E5

- Location: Abstract, p. 2
- Quote: "Correlational tests suggest that replication success was better predicted by the strength of original evidence than by characteristics of the original and replication teams."
- Key point: Replication success was better predicted by the strength of the original evidence than by characteristics of the original or replication teams.

### E6

- Location: Sampling frame and study selection, p. 6
- Quote: "On average, the to-be-replicated articles contained 2.99 studies"
- Key point: The selected articles contained about 3 studies each on average; psychology papers often report several studies in one article.
