# Papoutsoglou et al. 2020: Enabling reusability of plant phenomic datasets with MIAPPE 1.1

- Source: `papoutsoglou2020enabling`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.16544)
- Version: New Phytologist 227: 260-273, 2020, CC BY open access. Text taken from the publisher PDF deposited in DiRROS, a Slovenian institutional repository. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "the reuse and meta-analyses of phenotyping data are particularly challenging due to the heterogeneity of this domain that encompasses many types of experimental sites (field, glasshouse, controlled environment), plants (crops, forest trees), collected data (images, physical measurements, chemical assays, molecular biology assays), and experimental designs (factors being tested, timing, field layouts, etc.)."
- Key point: Plant phenotyping data are hard to reuse and meta-analyze because the domain is so heterogeneous: experimental sites (field, glasshouse, controlled environments), plants (crops, trees), data types, and experimental designs (factors, timing, field layout) all vary.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Because the original experimental data followed no standardisation guidelines, the authors had to manually assemble detailed metadata during the preprocessing of the data from descriptions in unstructured text."
- Key point: An example of reusing potato field trial data: the original data followed no standard, so the reusers had to assemble metadata by hand from scattered unstructured descriptions.

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "MIAPPE (Minimum Information About a Plant Phenotyping Experiment), the first and so far only community metadata standard for the plant phenotyping domain"
- Key point: MIAPPE (Minimum Information About a Plant Phenotyping Experiment) is the first and (as of the paper) only community metadata standard for plant phenotyping.

### E4

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "to minimise the chance of a researcher missing important information in the documentation of an experiment"
- Key point: One guiding principle of MIAPPE is to minimize the chance that researchers miss important information when documenting an experiment.

### E5

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "(i) the extension of MIAPPE to accommodate a wider range of use cases (including those relevant to perennial and woody plants)"
- Key point: Version 1.1 extends MIAPPE to more use cases, including perennial and woody plants.

### E6

- Location: Results, the Event concept in MIAPPE 1.1
- Quote: "describes a discrete occurrence at a specific time that affected the whole study or one or more observation units, which can be the application of a field/glasshouse practice (e.g. planting, fungicide application) or an unpredictable happening (e.g. rainfall, pathogen attack)."
- Key point: A MIAPPE "event" records something at a specific time that affected the whole study or some observation units, either a field or glasshouse practice (planting, fungicide application) or an incident (rainfall, pathogen infection).

### E7

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Four key components are needed from research communities to meet these requirements: metadata standards which list the fields required for interpreting the data from a given experimental domain;"
- Key point: To make data findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable, research communities need four things; the first is metadata standards listing the fields required to interpret data from a given experimental domain.
