# dos Santos & Travassos 2011: Action Research Can Swing the Balance in Experimental Software Engineering

- Source: `santos2011action`, [original (arXiv 1306.2414)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2414)
- Version: A chapter in *Advances in Computers* vol. 83 (2011); the authors' version posted on arXiv (77 pages).

### E1

- Location: §2.2 Action Research Process
- Quote: "a kind of social research with experimental basis that is conceived and conducted in close association with an action or a collective problem resolution where researchers and participants are involved in a cooperative way"
- Key point: Quoting Thiollent's definition: action research is a kind of social research with an experimental basis, conceived and conducted in close association with an action or collective problem solving, in which researchers and participants are involved cooperatively.

### E2

- Location: §2.2 (the canonical action research process, based on Davison et al. 2004)
- Quote: "(1) Diagnostic: consists of exploring the research field, stakeholders and their expectations holistically." / "(2) Planning: stage where actions are defined to the faced circumstances." / "(3) Intervention: corresponds to the planned actions implementation." / "(4) Evaluation: stage where the interventions effect are analyzed considering the theoretical background used as basis to the actions definition." / "(5) Reflection: involves the dissemination of acquired knowledge among participants and other organization departments."
- Key point: The five canonical stages: diagnosis (exploring the research field, stakeholders, and their expectations holistically), planning (defining actions for the circumstances), intervention (carrying out the planned actions), evaluation (analyzing the effects of the intervention against the theory used to design the actions), and reflection (spreading the knowledge gained among participants and other parts of the organization).

### E3

- Location: §2.2
- Quote: "the research environment requires a contract/agreement to legitimize the actions and possible benefits for both parties (researchers and organization)" / "The other additional step regards the criteria to finish the study. This should occur whenever both parties involved are satisfied with the outcome."
- Key point: The research needs a contract or agreement that legitimizes the actions and states the benefits for both parties; the study ends when both parties are satisfied with the results.

### E4

- Location: §2.2
- Quote: "the researcher can return to the diagnosis stage after an initial attempt to plan the study has not been completed because of a lack of better problem description"
- Key point: You can go back between stages; for example, if planning stalls because the problem is poorly described, return to the diagnosis stage.

### E5

- Location: §2.2
- Quote: "The importance of making the criteria explicit before conducting the research is also emphasized by Avison et al. (1999) who claim" / "that without it the evaluation of the results tends to be compromised, and eventually what is being described may be action (but not research) or research (but not action)."
- Key point: Make the criteria explicit before the research starts; otherwise evaluating the results is compromised, and you may end up describing "action, not research" or "research without action."

### E6

- Location: §2.2
- Quote: "Action Research does not obtain the repeatability property present in natural sciences, but it is sufficient to clarify the processes and models that allowed the researcher to make the interpretations and derive the conclusions."
- Key point: Action research does not achieve the repeatability of the natural sciences, but it is enough to make clear the processes and models from which the researcher derived interpretations and conclusions.

### E7

- Location: §2.2.1 Other Action Research Characteristics
- Quote: "Action Research differentiates itself from routine practice because it is proactive and strategically driven by theories and research techniques."
- Key point: Action research differs from routine practice because it is proactive and strategically driven by theory and research techniques.

### E8

- Location: §5.1.1 Learning and Difficulties in Using the Action Research Methodology
- Quote: "in certain cases, it can be difficult to perceive the difference between an Action Research study and a Case Study. This difficulty is even more noticeable when there is more emphasis on observation than on action at certain moments."
- Key point: Sometimes action research is hard to tell apart from a case study, especially when some stages involve more observation than action.

### E9

- Location: §5.1.1
- Quote: "over the refactoring study meetings described in the previous section where one of the authors played both professional and researcher roles"
- Key point: In the authors' own refactoring study, one author played both roles in the project: professional and researcher.

### E10

- Location: §5.1.1
- Quote: "The previous definition of what data should be collected shields the researcher from unexpected events allowing one to focus on the monitoring of actions and the participating in interventions." / "Obviously, unplanned events will happen but the Action Research methodology can accommodate this kind of deviation. Nevertheless, it still necessary to register these events to allow the researcher to justify them."
- Key point: Deciding in advance what data to collect keeps the researcher focused on monitoring actions and taking part in interventions when unexpected things happen; unplanned events will happen, and the method can accommodate them, but record them so they can be justified.

### E11

- Location: §5.1.1
- Quote: "Automated forms (e.g., voice recording) provide the researcher with additional time to conduct the interventions."
- Key point: Automated data collection (e.g., voice recording) frees up the researcher's time to carry out interventions.

### E12

- Location: §5.1.3 Ethical Issues
- Quote: "researchers should not put the problem solution in an unfavourable position in relation to their academic interests as the initial agreement between the parties (researchers and organization) should always aim at addressing a real problem."
- Key point: Researchers should not let their academic interests push the problem solution into second place; the initial agreement between the parties should always aim at solving the real problem.

### E13

- Location: §5.1.3 Ethical Issues
- Quote: "Being a collaborative research in a real context, the ethical issues involved in an Action Research study become even more important." / "those involved feel compelled to participate as several research activities overlap their professional responsibilities." / "it should also be offered to these professionals that their individual data will not be published" / "the freedom in consenting cannot be always achieved"
- Key point: Action research is collaborative research in a real setting, so ethics matter even more. Because it takes place in participants' own organization and overlaps with their jobs, they may feel compelled to take part; promise not to publish individual data; freely given consent is not always achievable.

### E14

- Location: §5.2.1 Engineering Software and Building Theories
- Quote: "Due to the way in which theories are built in Action Research, they tend to be of level 1 (minor working relationships that are concrete and directly based on observations) or level 2 (theories of the middle range involving some abstractions but that are still closely linked to observations)."
- Key point: Theories produced by action research tend to stay at lower levels: concrete working relationships based directly on observation, or middle-range theories with some abstraction that still stay close to observation.

### E15

- Location: §5.2.2 Research Rigour and the Results Relevance
- Quote: "One is the control level applied when conducting the study, aimed at minimizing researcher bias and the influence of other variables over the outcomes." / "The second is how theoretical knowledge is kept explicit during the research actions"
- Key point: Rigour in action research has two sides: the level of control in the study, to minimize researcher bias and the influence of other variables on the outcomes; and keeping theoretical knowledge explicit during the research actions.

### E16

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "the initial findings also revealed that the rigour and control of such studies should improve in Software Engineering."
- Key point: The authors' survey of action research literature in software engineering found that the rigour and control of such studies need to improve.
