# American Philosophical Association 2022: Statement on Best Practices in Journal Publishing

- Source: `apaphil2022journal`, [original](https://www.apaonline.org/page/journalpractices)
- Version: statement of the American Philosophical Association (not to be confused with the American Psychological Association, also abbreviated APA), drafted by its task force on academic publishing and approved by the Board in February 2022; the previous version was approved in November 2011. The website blocks scripted access, so the text is taken from an Internet Archive snapshot. These are recommendations, not mandatory rules. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Guidelines for Journals / Communicating with Authors
- Quote: "It is strongly recommended that journals use triply anonymous refereeing. The author’s name and institutional affiliation should not be revealed to the editor or to the referees, and the names of the referees and their institutional affiliation should not be made known to the author."
- Key point: Journals are strongly recommended to use triply anonymous refereeing: neither the editor nor the referees know who the author is or where they are from, and the author does not know who the referees are.

### E2

- Location: Guidelines for Journals / Communicating with Authors
- Quote: "All submissions should be read anonymously before a decision to desk reject is made."
- Key point: Even before desk-rejecting a paper (without external review), the editor should have read it anonymously.

### E3

- Location: Guidelines for Authors / Manuscript Preparation
- Quote: "Prior to submitting a manuscript to a journal for review, an author should check the journal’s website."
- Key point: Before submitting, authors should check the journal's website.

### E4

- Location: Guidelines for Authors / Manuscript Preparation
- Quote: "The journal’s acceptance rate, average time for manuscript review, and average time from acceptance to publication."
- Key point: Among the information typically available on a journal's website: acceptance rate, average review time, and average time from acceptance to publication (the same list also includes the journal's areas of interest, article types and lengths, and manuscript preparation for anonymous review).

### E5

- Location: Guidelines for Authors / Manuscript Preparation
- Quote: "Authors should make use of such information to ensure that their choice of a journal is appropriate and that they have properly prepared their manuscript for anonymous review."
- Key point: Authors should use this information to choose an appropriate journal and prepare the manuscript properly for anonymous review.

### E6

- Location: Guidelines for Authors / Manuscript Submission/Resubmission
- Quote: "In submitting a manuscript for review, an author is indicating that it is not under review or forthcoming in any other publication."
- Key point: Submitting a manuscript indicates that it is not under review or forthcoming elsewhere (no simultaneous submission).

### E7

- Location: Guidelines for Authors / Requests for Information
- Quote: "[...] authors should not hesitate to confirm the status of their manuscript’s review after a period of four months, unless the journal has already specified that the review process is likely to take longer."
- Key point: After four months, authors should not hesitate to ask about the status of their review, unless the journal has said review will take longer.

### E8

- Location: Guidelines for Referees / Conflicts of Interest and Anonymity
- Quote: "Since many philosophers post their papers on the web prior to submitting them for publication, referees are often able to identify authors by a web search. Referees should not attempt to identify the authors of papers they are reviewing."
- Key point: Many philosophers post papers online before submitting, so referees can often identify authors by searching the web; referees should not try to find out who the author is.

### E9

- Location: Guidelines for Referees / Conflicts of Interest and Anonymity
- Quote: "Referees should identify any potential conflicts of interest to editors. They should, in particular, tell editors when they know the identity of the author."
- Key point: Referees should report potential conflicts of interest to editors, and in particular tell editors when they know the author's identity.

### E10

- Location: Guidelines for Referees / Professional Responsibility
- Quote: "The professional responsibility to referee falls on all members of the profession. The expectation is that refereeing is unpaid service to the profession from which all benefit."
- Key point: Refereeing is a professional responsibility of all members of the profession and is unpaid service.

### E11

- Location: Guidelines for Referees / Appropriate Response Times
- Quote: "Potential referees should respond to invitations to review within one week at most."
- Key point: Respond to invitations to review within one week at most.

### E12

- Location: Copyright and Open Access / Consideration of Previously Posted Papers
- Quote: "This practice is becoming more common among philosophers. In philosophy of science, the University of Pittsburgh provides the PhilSci Archive, an electronic archive for preprints."
- Key point: Posting papers online (for example, in preprint archives) before submission is becoming more common among philosophers; in philosophy of science, the University of Pittsburgh runs the PhilSci Archive.

### E13

- Location: Copyright and Open Access / Consideration of Previously Posted Papers
- Quote: "[...] journal editors should, in general, be willing to consider papers which have been released to pre-print archives."
- Key point: Provided copyright and other conditions are met, journal editors should in general be willing to consider papers already released in preprint archives.

### E14

- Location: Guidelines for Journals / Communicating with (Potential) Referees
- Quote: "Although the practice of having authors recommend referees may help save editors time, it should not be used. The potential for conflicts of interest outweighs the benefits."
- Key point: Authors should not be asked to recommend referees; the risk of conflicts of interest outweighs the benefits.

### E15

- Location: Guidelines for Journals / Communicating with (Potential) Referees
- Quote: "Referees should be encouraged to not give undue weight to minor stylistic flaws, as they may be due to authors having English as a second language."
- Key point: Referees should not give undue weight to minor stylistic flaws, since authors may have English as a second language.
