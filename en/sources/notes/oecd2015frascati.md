# OECD 2015: Frascati Manual 2015: Guidelines for Collecting and Reporting Data on Research and Experimental Development

- Source: `oecd2015frascati`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264239012-en) (free official OECD PDF: [link](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2015/10/frascati-manual-2015_g1g57dcb/9789264239012-en.pdf))
- Version: 2015 edition. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) manual for research and development statistics; countries use it as the standard for counting "what counts as research." It applies to all fields, including the humanities, social sciences, and the arts.

### E1

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.2, paragraph 2.5 (p. 44)
- Quote: "Research and experimental development (R&D) comprise creative and systematic work undertaken in order to increase the stock of knowledge [...] and to devise new applications of available knowledge."
- Key point: Definition of research and experimental development (R&D): creative and systematic work undertaken to increase the stock of knowledge or to devise new applications of available knowledge.

### E2

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.2, paragraphs 2.6–2.7 (p. 44)
- Quote: "R&D is always aimed at new findings, based on original concepts (and their interpretation) or hypotheses. [...] For an activity to be an R&D activity, it must satisfy five core criteria. [...] The activity must be: novel creative uncertain systematic transferable and/or reproducible."
- Key point: R&D is always aimed at new findings; to count as R&D, an activity must meet all five criteria: novel, creative, uncertain in outcome, systematic, and transferable and/or reproducible.

### E3

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.4, paragraph 2.16 (p. 46)
- Quote: "Novelty could result from a project to reproduce an existing result that finds potential discrepancies."
- Key point: A project that reproduces an existing result and finds potential discrepancies can also count as novel.

### E4

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.4, paragraph 2.18 (p. 46)
- Quote: "At the outset of an R&D project, the kind of outcome and the cost (including time allocation) cannot be precisely determined relative to the goals."
- Key point: "Uncertain": at the outset, the kind of outcome and the cost, including time, cannot be precisely determined.

### E5

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.4, paragraph 2.19 (p. 46)
- Quote: "In this context "systematic" means that the R&D is conducted in a planned way, with records kept of both the process followed and the outcome."
- Key point: "Systematic": conducted in a planned way, with records kept of both the process and the outcome.

### E6

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.4, paragraph 2.20 (p. 47)
- Quote: "An R&D project should result in the potential for the transfer of the new knowledge, ensuring its use and allowing other researchers to reproduce the results as part of their own R&D activities. This includes R&D that has negative results, in the case that an initial hypothesis fails to be confirmed or a product cannot be developed as originally intended."
- Key point: "Transferable/reproducible": the new knowledge must be transferable to others and allow them to reproduce the results; negative results (a hypothesis not confirmed) count too.

### E7

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.4, paragraph 2.20 (p. 47)
- Quote: "As the purpose of R&D is to increase of the existing stock of knowledge, the results cannot remain tacit (i.e. remain solely in the minds of the researchers), as they, and the associated knowledge, would be at risk of being lost."
- Key point: Research results cannot remain tacit, solely in the researchers' minds, or the knowledge is lost; they must be recorded and disseminated.

### E8

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.2, paragraph 2.9 (pp. 44–45)
- Quote: "Basic research is experimental or theoretical work undertaken primarily to acquire new knowledge of the underlying foundations of phenomena and observable facts, without any particular application or use in view. Applied research is original investigation undertaken in order to acquire new knowledge. It is, however, directed primarily towards a specific, practical aim or objective."
- Key point: Basic research seeks new knowledge of the underlying foundations of phenomena without any particular application in view; applied research also seeks new knowledge but is directed primarily toward a specific practical aim.

### E9

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.2, paragraph 2.9 (p. 45)
- Quote: "Experimental development is systematic work, drawing on knowledge gained from research and practical experience and producing additional knowledge, which is directed to producing new products or processes or to improving existing products or processes."
- Key point: Experimental development draws on existing knowledge and produces additional knowledge, aimed at producing new products or processes or improving existing ones.

### E10

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.2, paragraph 2.11 (p. 45)
- Quote: "The order in which the three types of R&D activity appear is not meant to suggest that basic research leads to applied research and then to experimental development."
- Key point: The three types of R&D are not a sequence or a hierarchy; knowledge flows between them in both directions.

### E11

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.6, paragraph 2.64
- Quote: "it can be useful to make a distinction between research for the arts, research on the arts and artistic expression."
- Key point: Distinguish three things: research for the arts, research on the arts, and artistic expression itself.

### E12

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.6, paragraph 2.66
- Quote: "Basic or applied research contributes to most of the studies of the arts (musicology, art history, theatre studies, media studies, literature, etc.)."
- Key point: Studies of the arts, such as musicology, art history, and theatre studies, are mostly basic or applied research.

### E13

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.6, paragraph 2.67
- Quote: "Artistic performance is normally excluded from R&D. Artistic performances fail the novelty test of R&D as they are looking for a new expression, rather than for new knowledge."
- Key point: Artistic performance is normally not R&D: it seeks a new expression rather than new knowledge, so it fails the novelty test.

### E14

- Location: Chapter 2, §2.6, paragraph 2.67
- Quote: "Higher education institutions have, nevertheless, to be evaluated on a caseby-case basis if they grant a doctoral degree to an artist as a result of artistic performances."
- Key point: However, when a higher education institution grants an artist a doctoral degree on the basis of artistic performance, it has to be evaluated case by case.

### E15

- Location: Chapter 2, paragraph 2.17
- Quote: "One area requiring care in assessment is the arts (Section 2.6): there is creativity, but the other criteria have to be confirmed for the activity to qualify as R&D."
- Key point: The arts need careful assessment: there is creativity, but the other criteria must also be met for the activity to count as R&D.

### E16

- Location: Chapter 2, introduction
- Quote: "five criteria are provided which require the activity to be novel, creative, uncertain in it outcome, systematic and transferable and/or reproducible."
- Key point: The five criteria for identifying R&D: novel, creative, uncertain in outcome, systematic, and transferable and/or reproducible.
