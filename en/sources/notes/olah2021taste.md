# Olah 2021: Research Taste Exercises

- Source: `olah2021taste`, [original](https://colah.github.io/notes/taste/)
- Version: Author's blog note, 2021-01-09, self-described as a "rough note." The end collects suggestions from others in a Twitter discussion; cite who each one comes from.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "One of the most important aspects of growing as a researcher is developing research taste -- roughly, the ability to chose good problems to work on."
- Key point: Research taste is roughly the ability to choose good problems, and developing it is one of the most important parts of growing as a researcher.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "I think the fundamental issue is that actually testing whether a research idea you come up with is good is very expensive. Often it takes months, so you only really get a few pieces of feedback on your taste every year."
- Key point: Taste is hard to train because testing whether an idea is good is very expensive, often taking months, so you get only a few pieces of feedback a year.

### E3

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "I don't recommend forcing yourself to work on something you don't find exciting, even if you intellectually think it's more important. You'll be a lot less effective."
- Key point: Don't force yourself to work on something you don't find exciting, even if you intellectually think it is more important; you will be much less effective.

### E4

- Location: Exercises › Exercise 1
- Quote: "Write down a list of research ideas. Have a mentor you respect rate each idea 1-10. Discuss ideas where you disagree with them after reflection."
- Key point: Exercise 1: write a list of research ideas, have a mentor you respect rate each 1-10, and discuss the ideas where you disagree.

### E5

- Location: Exercises › Exercise 2
- Quote: "Pay attention when other people try ideas you’ve had. How did the results compare with your expectations?"
- Key point: Exercise 2: when other people try ideas you've had, compare the results with your expectations.

### E6

- Location: Exercises › Exercise 3
- Quote: "Interview researchers around you on their taste. Why do they work on the problems they do? How do they pick problems?"
- Key point: Exercise 3: interview researchers around you about why they work on their problems and how they pick them.

### E7

- Location: Exercises › Exercise 5
- Quote: "Critically consider your research taste, and the community taste around you. Your taste is likely very influenced by your research cluster (your collaborators, advisor, etc)."
- Key point: Exercise 5: critically examine your own taste and your community's; your taste is heavily shaped by your collaborators and advisor.

### E8

- Location: Failure Modes › Failure Mode 1
- Quote: "Getting overly attached to one research direction / falling into sunk costs. [...] Set aside a week or two to step back and create a list of the other research directions you think would be most promising to work on (look at new research, talk to others, etc). Then ask what you’d want to work on if you were starting from scratch."
- Key point: Failure mode 1: getting overly attached to one direction and falling into sunk costs. Remedy: every year or two, spend a week or two stepping back, list other promising directions, and ask "what would I work on if I started from scratch?"

### E9

- Location: Failure Modes › Failure mode 2
- Quote: "Sometimes people fall into a trap of trying to pick the perfect problem or have a brilliant insight before getting their hands dirty. You can’t have research taste in a vacuum."
- Key point: Failure mode 2: trying to pick the perfect problem before getting your hands dirty. Taste cannot develop without knowledge and hands-on work.

### E10

- Location: Failure Modes › Failure mode 3
- Quote: "This generally leads to ideas optimized for compromise rather than quality."
- Key point: Failure mode 3: when your interests don't fit your environment, bending ideas into something your advisor will accept, which optimizes for compromise rather than quality.

### E11

- Location: Suggestions from other people › Rachel Prudden
- Quote: "The heuristic of imagining that another group has published the paper you have in mind. Are you excited to read it?"
- Key point: Rachel Prudden's heuristic: imagine another group has published the paper you have in mind; would you be excited to read it? Olah considers this a good test.

### E12

- Location: Suggestions from other people › Andy Matuschak
- Quote: "running to make an idea happen as soon as it seems tractable. It's exciting to have a viable approach—makes me wanna go do it! But if I sit with an idea longer I can often evolve a deeper variant and run with that instead."
- Key point: A failure mode Andy Matuschak names: rushing to execute an idea as soon as it seems tractable; sitting with an idea longer often lets it evolve into a deeper variant.

### E13

- Location: Suggestions from other people › Michael Nielsen (via Shrey Jain)
- Quote: "But a field that is a mess is really an opportunity. Chances are good that there are deep unifying and simplifying concepts still waiting to be understood and developed by someone - perhaps you."
- Key point: Olah quotes Nielsen: a field that is a mess is really an opportunity, since deep unifying and simplifying concepts may still be waiting to be found.

### E14

- Location: Suggestions from other people › Arthur Allshire
- Quote: "What are the bottlenecking problems in your field?"
- Key point: Arthur Allshire's suggestion: ask what the bottlenecking problems in your field are.

### E15

- Location: Suggestions from other people › Christian Szegedy, Sergey Gorbunov, and Olah's comments
- Quote: "In hindsight though, most of the errors are made in my research was when I listened to the criticisms of my respected mentors and most of the cool stuff I did were the ones those everybody called initially useless, uninteresting or "good luck!"..."
- Key point: Christian Szegedy recalls that most of his research mistakes came from heeding his mentors' criticism, while his coolest work was at first called useless.

### E16

- Location: Same, Olah's comment on Szegedy
- Quote: "Chris comment: I think there's a lot of truth to this, but beware survivorship bias and remember that different people may have different levels of scientific maturity / taste at different points in their careers."
- Key point: Olah cautions about survivorship bias: people reach scientific maturity at different points, and many PhD students struggle if they plan their research entirely on their own from the start.

### E17

- Location: Failure Modes › remedy for failure mode 2
- Quote: "Help someone else with their project in a space you’re interested in. You’ll learn a lot and get your hands dirty without becoming overly committed to working on something long term."
- Key point: One remedy: help someone else with a project in a space you're interested in; you learn a lot and get hands-on experience without a long-term commitment.
