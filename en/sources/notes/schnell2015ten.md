# Schnell 2015: Ten Simple Rules for a Computational Biologist's Laboratory Notebook

- Source: `schnell2015ten`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004385)
- Version: PLoS Computational Biology 11(9): e1004385, 2015, open access (CC BY), read 2026-10-06. Written for biologists doing only "dry" (computational) work, but its record-keeping principles come from standard lab notebook practice. Locations give rule numbers.

### E1

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "It is an organizational tool and memory aid, which serves as the primary record of scientific research and activity for all scientists. It also serves as a legal record of ownership of the ideas and results obtained by a scientist."
- Key point: A lab notebook is an organizational tool and memory aid, the primary record of research activity, and a legal record of ownership of ideas and results.

### E2

- Location: Rule 1
- Quote: "In the absence of an institutional policy, your research group should have a policy to explain to all team members the process for daily record-keeping and maintaining laboratory records."
- Key point: If your institution has no notebook policy, your research group should have one that tells every member how to keep daily records and maintain them.

### E3

- Location: Rule 4
- Quote: "Each lab notebook entry should be written immediately after the activity or work was performed."
- Key point: Write each entry immediately after the work is done.

### E4

- Location: Rule 5
- Quote: "If you make a mistake in your paper lab notebook, put a thin line through the mistake and write the new information next to it. Never erase or destroy an entry, because errors or mistakes are an important part of the scientific process."
- Key point: In a paper notebook, cross out a mistake with a thin line and write the correct information next to it; never erase or destroy an entry, since mistakes are part of the scientific process.

### E5

- Location: Rule 8
- Quote: "For each lab notebook entry, clearly indicate who did what work and who was present for a discussion or in silico experiment; this is particularly important for collaborative projects."
- Key point: For each entry, state clearly who did what and who was present for a discussion; this matters especially in collaborative projects.

### E6

- Location: Rule 8
- Quote: "It is important to note that lab notebooks can be subpoenaed as evidence in cases of scientific misconduct by your institution or governmental funding agencies."
- Key point: In research misconduct cases, your institution or a government funding agency can subpoena lab notebooks as evidence.

### E7

- Location: Rule 6
- Quote: "You should also store the raw data for every figure."
- Key point: Store the raw data for every figure.

### E8

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "Here, I present the best practices (summarized as ten rules) for keeping a lab notebook in computational biology, for scientists pursuing exclusively “dry” research."
- Key point: The paper's ten rules are for biologists doing exclusively "dry" (computational) research.

### E9

- Location: Rule 3
- Quote: "If you rely on your recollection to remember a good idea, a suggestion during a meeting, or an important step in your data analysis or model simulation, you can find yourself in a situation where you no longer remember that critical thought."
- Key point: If you rely on memory for a good idea, a suggestion in a meeting, or a key step in your analysis, you may well not remember it when you need it.

### E10

- Location: Rule 5
- Quote: "The most logical organization of a lab notebook is chronological. Each entry should contain the date it was made and subject of the entry."
- Key point: The most logical way to organize a notebook is chronologically, with a date and subject for every entry.

### E11

- Location: Rule 5
- Quote: "Never erase or destroy an entry, because errors or mistakes are an important part of the scientific process."
- Key point: Never erase or destroy an entry, because errors are an important part of the scientific process.
