# Lamela et al. 2019: Repeatable and Reproducible Wireless Networking Experimentation through Trace-based Simulation

- Source: `lamela2019repeatable`, [full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12033)
- Version: arXiv:1903.12033v2, March 2019. Locations give the section.

### E1

- Location: I. Introduction
- Quote: "Simulations are flexible but usually produce optimistic results, due to many simplifications required to represent complex scenarios such as those related to emerging aerial networks."
- Key point: In wireless networking research, simulations are flexible but usually produce optimistic results because of the many simplifications they require.

### E2

- Location: I. Introduction
- Quote: "Testbeds, on the other hand, are increasingly costly to maintain and are frequently unavailable; still, they provide realistic results and are an essential step to achieve proper protocol validation and fine tuning."
- Key point: Testbeds are costly to maintain and frequently unavailable, but they give realistic results and are an essential step for validating and tuning protocols.

### E3

- Location: I. Introduction
- Quote: "The problem is that real wireless testbed experiments in emerging networking scenarios are hardly repeatable. Given the same input, they can produce very different output results, since wireless communications are influenced by external random phenomena such as noise, interference, and multipath"
- Key point: Real wireless experiments are hardly repeatable: given the same input, external random factors such as noise, interference, and multipath can produce very different results.

### E4

- Location: III. Trace-based Simulation Approach
- Quote: "The TS approach focuses on capturing traces of the execution conditions of an experiment and enabling the repetition and reproduction of such conditions using the past traces in ns-3."
- Key point: The authors' compromise, trace-based simulation: capture traces of the execution conditions (e.g., channel quality, node positions) during a real experiment and replay them in a simulator so the conditions can be repeated.
