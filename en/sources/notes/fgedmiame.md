# FGED Society: MIAME (Minimum Information About a Microarray Experiment)

- Source: `fgedmiame`, [Original](https://www.fged.org/projects/miame/)
- Version: Project web page of the Functional Genomics Data Society (FGED), accessed 2026-10-08. The page states that the society has closed operations but the page is kept online. MIAME was first published in Brazma et al. 2001 (Nature Genetics). Locations are given as paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: Top of the page
- Quote: "MIAME describes the Minimum Information About a Microarray Experiment that is needed to enable the interpretation of the results of a microarray experiment unambiguously and potentially to reproduce the experiment. [ Brazma et al. (2001), Nature Genetics"
- Key point: MIAME sets out the minimum information about a microarray experiment needed to interpret its results unambiguously and potentially to reproduce the experiment; it comes from Brazma et al. (2001) in Nature Genetics.

### E2

- Location: The six critical elements
- Quote: "The six most critical elements contributing towards MIAME are:" / "The raw data for each hybridisation (e.g., CEL or GPR files)" / "The final processed (normalised) data for the set of hybridisations in the experiment (study)" / "The essential sample annotation including experimental factors and their values" / "The experimental design including sample data relationships (e.g., which raw data file relates to which sample, which hybridisations are technical, which are biological replicates)" / "Sufficient annotation of the array" / "The essential laboratory and data processing protocols (e.g., what normalisation method has been used to obtain the final processed data)"
- Key point: The six most critical elements: the raw data for each hybridization; the final processed (normalized) data; the essential sample annotation, including experimental factors and their values; the experimental design, including which data file belongs to which sample and which replicates are technical and which biological; sufficient annotation of the array; and the essential laboratory and data processing protocols (for example, the normalization method used).
