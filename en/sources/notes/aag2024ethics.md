# American Association of Geographers 2024: Statement of Professional Ethics

- Source: `aag2024ethics`, [original](https://aag.org/statement-of-professional-ethics)
- Version: web page of the American Association of Geographers (AAG) Statement of Professional Ethics, adopted in 1998 and last revised on June 12, 2024 (see E18). Accessed October 2026. Locations give section numbers and subheadings.

### E1

- Location: I. Preamble
- Quote: "Geographical scholarship spans the natural and social sciences as well as the humanities and arts and is undertaken in many different social and environmental contexts."
- Key point: Geographical scholarship spans the natural and social sciences as well as the humanities and arts, and is carried out in many different social and environmental contexts.

### E2

- Location: I. Preamble
- Quote: "Members of the AAG are required to abide by AAG’s Professional Conduct Policy and Procedures, and many geographers must also conform to ethical requirements related to research with human subjects as interpreted and enforced by institutions and funders."
- Key point: AAG members must abide by the AAG's Professional Conduct Policy; many geographers must also meet the human-subjects research ethics requirements enforced by their institutions and funders.

### E3

- Location: II. Do Good
- Quote: "The principle of respect acknowledges that all geographical knowledge is situated"
- Key point: The principle of respect acknowledges that all geographical knowledge is "situated," that is, tied to the people and settings that produce it.

### E4

- Location: II. Respect Research Participants
- Quote: "An important sign of respect and care in geographical scholarship involving human subjects is conducting research with, rather than on, participants and avoiding exploitative or extractive research."
- Key point: In geographical research involving human subjects, an important sign of respect is doing research "with" rather than "on" participants, and avoiding exploitative, extractive research.

### E5

- Location: II. Respect Research Participants
- Quote: "Geographers should also carefully reflect upon how we represent ourselves, research participants, and places in our research, teaching, and professional life."
- Key point: Geographers should carefully reflect on how they represent themselves, research participants, and places in their research.

### E6

- Location: III. Do No Harm
- Quote: "The obligation to do no harm should supersede other goals of seeking or communicating new knowledge."
- Key point: The obligation to do no harm supersedes the goal of seeking or communicating new knowledge.

### E7

- Location: III. Recognize Power Hierarchies
- Quote: "We frequently occupy powerful positions relative to our research participants, and it is all too easy for us to be unaware of, or to forget, the impact that these power imbalances can have on those affected by geographical research."
- Key point: Researchers often occupy powerful positions relative to participants, and it is easy to be unaware of, or forget, the impact this power imbalance has on those affected by the research.

### E8

- Location: IV. Collaborate with Care
- Quote: "The scope of collaboration, rights, and responsibilities of those participating, co-authorship, credit, and acknowledgment should be openly and fairly established at the outset."
- Key point: The scope of collaboration, the rights and responsibilities of participants, co-authorship, credit, and acknowledgment should be openly and fairly agreed at the outset.

### E9

- Location: VI. Obtain Informed Consent
- Quote: "The consent process should be a part of project design and continue through implementation as an ongoing dialogue and negotiation with research participants."
- Key point: Informed consent should be built into project design and continue through implementation as an ongoing dialogue and negotiation with participants.

### E10

- Location: VI. Obtain Institutional Approval
- Quote: "Geographers whose research involves humans, based in countries where there is an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or similar process, must obtain institutional approval [...] In countries where there are no IRB processes, geographers, should obtain permission from the communities they will contribute to."
- Key point: Geographers based in countries with an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or similar process must obtain institutional approval for research involving humans; in countries without such a process, they should obtain permission from the relevant communities.

### E11

- Location: VI. Take Informed Consent Seriously
- Quote: "the right to refuse research goes beyond specific individuals approached through the IRB process, and also includes the right of communities to refuse participation. [...] It is the quality of the consent, not its format, which is relevant."
- Key point: The right to refuse research belongs not only to individuals but also to communities as a whole; informed consent is about quality, not form (it need not be a written signature).

### E12

- Location: VI. Share Findings
- Quote: "Whenever appropriate, results of research should be shared with research participants, local colleagues, host agencies, and affected persons and communities in a format that is accessible to them."
- Key point: Where appropriate, research results should be shared with participants, local colleagues, host agencies, and affected people and communities in a form they can understand.

### E13

- Location: VI. Make Data Available
- Quote: "in some situations, generalization or other measures such as the use of pseudonyms will be necessary to protect privacy, confidentiality, and limit exposure to risks."
- Key point: In some situations, generalization of data or measures such as pseudonyms are needed to protect privacy and confidentiality and limit exposure to risk.

### E14

- Location: VI. Protect Privacy and Confidentiality
- Quote: "(1) automated tracking of the locations and movements of individuals or vehicles; (2) the use of images from satellites, aircraft, UAVs (drones), or ground-based sensors that are of sufficient resolution to identify individuals or vehicles; (3) the use of high resolution geographic location to link data in ways that violate personal confidentiality; and (4) any use of big data that compromises privacy, confidentiality, or violates other ethical principles in this Statement, even when such data is considered publicly available."
- Key point: The AAG lists four kinds of geospatial research that especially raise privacy and confidentiality concerns and need extra care: automated tracking of the locations and movements of individuals or vehicles; images from satellites, aircraft, drones (UAVs), or ground-based sensors detailed enough to identify individuals or vehicles; linking data through high-resolution locations in ways that reveal personal information; and any use of big data that compromises privacy, even if the data are considered public.

### E15

- Location: VII. Disclose Funding Sources
- Quote: "All funding sources, affiliations, sponsorships, and partnerships should be fully disclosed in an understandable manner at the time that informed consent is requested from research participants"
- Key point: All funding sources, affiliations, sponsorships, and partnerships should be fully disclosed, in an understandable way, when informed consent is requested.

### E16

- Location: VII. Be Transparent
- Quote: "Disclosure of funding sources is required for publication in AAG journals and for presentations at the Annual Meeting of the AAG and at the meetings of its regional divisions."
- Key point: Disclosure of funding sources is required for publication in AAG journals and for presentations at the AAG Annual Meeting and its regional division meetings.

### E17

- Location: VIII. Weigh Competing Ethical Obligations
- Quote: "These considerations may also include geographers’ own safety, especially if they are a member of a marginalized group, or in cases where research participants, funders, or sponsors are in a position of power over the researcher."
- Key point: Weighing ethical obligations can also involve the researcher's own safety, especially when the researcher belongs to a marginalized group or when participants or funders hold power over the researcher.

### E18

- Location: top of page
- Quote: "Endorsed by the Council of the American Association of Geographers: October 18, 1998; updated April 5, 2005; revised November 1, 2009; revised March 15, 2021; and revised June 12, 2024."
- Key point: The statement was endorsed by the AAG Council in 1998 and updated or revised in 2005, 2009, 2021, and 2024.
