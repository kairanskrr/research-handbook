# Thabane et al. 2010: A tutorial on pilot studies: the what, why and how

- Source: `thabane2010tutorial`, [original (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2824145/), DOI [10.1186/1471-2288-10-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-10-1)
- Version: BMC Medical Research Methodology 10:1, 2010, open access (CC BY 2.0), accessed via PubMed Central on 2026-10-07. It discusses pilot studies before Phase III clinical trials, but its reasoning that pilot studies serve feasibility, that success criteria should be written down in advance, and that effects should not be read from small samples generalizes. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "the main goal of pilot studies is to assess feasibility so as to avoid potentially disastrous consequences of embarking on a large study"
- Key point: The main purpose of a pilot study is to assess feasibility, to avoid the potentially disastrous consequences of rushing into a large study.

### E2

- Location: §3 Reasons for Conducting Pilot Studies
- Quote: "In general, the rationale for a pilot study can be grouped under several broad classifications - process, resources, management and scientific" / "Process: This assesses the feasibility of the steps that need to take place as part of the main study. Examples include determining recruitment rates, retention rates, etc." / "Resources: This deals with assessing time and budget problems that can occur during the main study."
- Key point: Reasons for doing a pilot study fall into four categories: process (whether each step of the main study works, such as recruitment and retention rates), resources (whether time and budget will be a problem), management (personnel and data management), and scientific (safety, dose, effect and its variability).

### E3

- Location: §6 How to Interpret the Results of a Pilot Study: Criteria for Success
- Quote: "It is always important to state the criteria for success of a pilot study. The criteria should be based on the primary feasibility objectives." / "In general, the outcome of a pilot study can be one of the following: (i) Stop - main study not feasible; (ii) Continue, but modify protocol - feasible with modifications; (iii) Continue without modifications, but monitor closely - feasible with close monitoring and (iv) Continue without modifications - feasible as is."
- Key point: Always state the pilot study's criteria for success, based on its primary feasibility objectives. A pilot study usually ends in one of four outcomes: stop; continue with modifications to the protocol; continue without modifications but with close monitoring; or continue with the protocol as is.

### E4

- Location: §6, the POET pilot study example
- Quote: "at least 70% of all eligible patients can be recruited" / "complete follow-up in at least 95% of all recruited subjects."
- Key point: Example: the pilot for a multicenter trial set its success criteria in advance as specific numbers, such as recruiting at least 70% of eligible patients and having at least 95% of participants complete follow-up.

### E5

- Location: §5 Challenges of and Common Misconceptions about Pilot Studies
- Quote: "it can be dangerous to use pilot studies to estimate treatment effects, as such estimates may be unrealistic/biased because of the limited sample sizes."
- Key point: Using a pilot study to estimate the effect of an intervention is dangerous: with a small sample, the estimate may be unrealistic and biased.

### E6

- Location: §5
- Quote: "Quite often the emphasis is wrongly placed on statistical significance, not on feasibility - which is the main focus of the pilot study." / "there are no clear feasibility objectives; no clear analytic plans; and certainly no clear criteria for success of feasibility."
- Key point: A common mistake is to focus on statistical significance rather than feasibility; reviewing ethics applications, the authors found that most pilot studies had no clear feasibility objectives, analysis plans, or success criteria.

### E7

- Location: §7 Frequently asked questions
- Quote: "Emphasis should not be placed on statistical significance when pilot studies are not powered to detect minimal clinically important differences." / "underpowered studies (with no statistically significant results) are inconclusive, not negative"
- Key point: When a pilot study's sample is too small to detect a meaningful difference, statistical significance should not be emphasized; a lack of significant results in an underpowered study means only "inconclusive," not "no effect."

### E8

- Location: §7 Frequently asked questions
- Quote: "a pilot study that shows the main study is not likely to be feasible is not a failed (pilot) study. In fact, it is a success - because you avoided wasting scarce resources on a study destined for failure!"
- Key point: A pilot study showing that the main study is not very feasible is not a failure of the pilot; it is a success, because it keeps you from wasting limited resources on a study doomed to fail.

### E9

- Location: §5 (sample size)
- Quote: "It is important that the sample for a pilot be representative of the target study population. It should also be based on the same inclusion/exclusion criteria as the main study. As a rule of thumb, a pilot study should be large enough to provide useful information about the aspects that are being assessed for feasibility."
- Key point: The pilot sample should be representative of the target population and use the same inclusion and exclusion criteria as the main study; as a rule of thumb, it should be large enough to provide useful information on the feasibility aspects being assessed.

### E10

- Location: §5 (sample size and combining data)
- Quote: "We recommend if any pooling of pilot and main study data is considered, this should be planned beforehand, described clearly in the protocol with clear discussion of the statistical consequences and methods."
- Key point: If you are considering combining data from the pilot and the main study, this must be planned in advance, and the statistical consequences and methods must be spelled out in the protocol.

### E11

- Location: §5
- Quote: "None of the above arguments qualifies as sound reasons for calling a study a pilot." / "if a trainee or researcher is assigned a project as a pilot it is important to discuss how the results will inform the planning of the main study."
- Key point: "It is small," "it is just a student project," and "that is all the budget allows" are not legitimate reasons to call a study a pilot study; when you are assigned a pilot study, discuss and settle how its results will feed into planning the main study.

### E12

- Location: §8 Ethical Aspects of Pilot Studies
- Quote: "the disclosure of their purpose to study participants requires special wording - that informs them of the definition of a pilot study, the feasibility objectives of the study, and also clearly defines the criteria for success of feasibility."
- Key point: Word the explanation of a pilot study's purpose to participants specifically: tell them what a pilot study is, what this pilot's feasibility objectives are, and what the success criteria for feasibility are.
