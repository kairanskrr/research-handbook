# Heckman & Moktan 2020: Publishing and Promotion in Economics: The Tyranny of the Top Five

- Source: `heckman2020tyranny`, [IZA discussion paper PDF](https://docs.iza.org/dp11868.pdf); the published version appeared in the Journal of Economic Literature 58(2)
- Version: IZA Discussion Paper No. 11868 (October 2018).

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Analyzing the job histories of tenure-track economists hired by the top 35 U.S. economics departments, we find that T5 publications have a powerful influence on tenure decisions and rates of transition to tenure."
- Key point: Analyzing the job histories of tenure-track economists at the top 35 US economics departments, the authors find that publications in the top five (T5) journals have a powerful influence on tenure decisions.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Pursuit of T5 publications has become the obsession of the next generation of economists. However, the T5 screen is far from reliable. A substantial share of influential publications appear in non-T5 outlets. Reliance on the T5 to screen talent incentivizes careerism over creativity."
- Key point: The authors argue that pursuing T5 publications has become an obsession for young economists, yet the T5 screen is far from reliable: many influential papers appear outside the T5, and relying on it to screen talent rewards careerism over creativity.

### E3

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "At the same time, the number of submissions to the T5 and the length of submitted papers have increased5 with concomitant growth in rejection rates and delays in the refereeing process."
- Key point: Submissions to the T5 journals and the length of papers have both grown, with rising rejection rates and delays in refereeing.

### E4

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Economists at highly ranked departments with established reputations are increasingly not publishing in T5 or field journals8 and more often post papers online in influential working paper series, which are highly cited, but not counted as T5s."
- Key point: Established economists at top departments increasingly skip journals and post papers in influential working paper series, which are highly cited but do not count as T5 publications.
