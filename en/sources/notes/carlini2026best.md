# Carlini 2026: How to win a best paper award (or, an opinionated take on how to do important research that matters)

- Source: `carlini2026best`, [Original](https://nicholas.carlini.com/writing/2026/how-to-win-a-best-paper-award.html)
- Version: Author's blog, 2026-03-09. The author notes in the text "for my field! yours may differ."

### E1

- Location: Coming up with your best-paper-worthy idea › Pick your ideas for impact
- Quote: "Some people go into research with the objective of writing a conference paper. Don't do that. You need to go into research with the objective of discovering something interesting, important, and new. The paper follows as a natural consequence."
- Key point: The goal of research is not to write a paper but to discover something interesting, important, and new; the paper follows naturally.

### E2

- Location: While doing the best-paper-worthy research › Put in an unreasonable amount of effort
- Quote: "Every experiment should be as precisely performed as you can manage. Run multiple trials. Control for confounders. If there's a question a skeptical reader might ask about your setup, run the experiment that answers it before they ask."
- Key point: Make every experiment as precise as you can: run multiple trials, control for confounders, and anticipate what a skeptical reader will ask and answer it with an experiment in advance.

### E3

- Location: Coming up with your best-paper-worthy idea › Have good taste for problems
- Quote: "The single most important skill to develop for high-impact research is good taste in what problems are worth solving."
- Key point: The most important skill for high-impact research is good taste in which problems are worth solving.

### E4

- Location: As above
- Quote: "They write papers that are technically correct but that no one reads, because they never asked whether the question was worth answering."
- Key point: People without taste write technically correct papers that no one reads, because they never asked whether the question was worth answering.

### E5

- Location: As above
- Quote: "But if I had to summarize it in one sentence, it would be that taste comes from practicing the skill of research, keeping your focus always on identifying what works and what doesn't."
- Key point: Taste comes from practicing research while always focusing on what works and what doesn't.

### E6

- Location: Ignore all the papers
- Quote: "Once you've read everything, the second step is to forget it all. The reason is simple: everything that's already been done has already been done."
- Key point: After reading widely, you must be able to step outside the frame of existing work, or you will not come up with anything new.

### E7

- Location: Ignore all the papers
- Quote: "These are directions where someone (usually someone famous!) published an influential paper that got something critical wrong with the approach, methodology, or evaluation... and then the rest of the field followed along the same wrong direction without thinking critically about the initial choice, because that's just how the field does things."
- Key point: Every field has "bad ideas": an influential early paper got the approach, methodology, or evaluation critically wrong, and the field followed without thinking.

### E8

- Location: Do something only you can do
- Quote: "So the magnitude of your contribution is judged, in a very real sense, by counting the months between when you publish, and when the next person would have."
- Key point: The size of your contribution is, in a real sense, the number of months by which you beat the next person who would have done it.

### E9

- Location: Do something only you can do
- Quote: "I look for areas where, when I read the work, I want to scream. Areas where you keep asking yourself "why is everyone doing this obviously wrong?""
- Key point: One signal: reading work in an area and wanting to ask "why is everyone doing this obviously wrong?"

### E10

- Location: Find your comparative advantage
- Quote: "Because this space is high-dimensional, it's highly likely you are the best in the world at some specific corner of it. Find that corner."
- Key point: The space of skill combinations is high-dimensional, so you are likely the best in the world at some specific corner of it; find that corner.

### E11

- Location: Get lucky: you're well suited to solve important problems
- Quote: "I feel pretty strongly that your overall impact is the product of your skill in some area, multiplied by how important that area is. If you're very good at a less "important" problem, you'll contribute more than working on something you're not very good at."
- Key point: Impact is roughly your skill in an area multiplied by the area's importance; being very good at a less "important" problem may contribute more than working on something you are not good at.

### E12

- Location: As above
- Quote: "But second, fortunately, if you're in an area that's not the "hot" one, you'll have a much easier time standing out."
- Key point: Outside the hottest area, it is much easier to stand out.

### E13

- Location: Pick your ideas for impact
- Quote: "At some point the marginal hour spent trying to find a good idea is less valuable than just working on ... anything."
- Key point: Past a certain point, another hour spent hunting for an idea is worth less than just starting to work on something.

### E14

- Location: While doing the best-paper-worthy research › Kill papers that are not working
- Quote: "The best way I've found to minimize wasted time is to start with the sub-problem most likely to fail."
- Key point: Start with the sub-problem most likely to fail, so you learn early whether the idea works.

### E15

- Location: While doing the best-paper-worthy research › Re-prioritize projects ruthlessly
- Quote: "new ideas always sound more exciting than ones you've been working on for a few months, so it's easy to get distracted by some shiny new problem just because it's new."
- Key point: New ideas always seem more exciting than one you have worked on for months, so it is easy to get distracted just because something is new.
