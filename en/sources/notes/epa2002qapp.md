# US Environmental Protection Agency 2002: Guidance for Quality Assurance Project Plans (EPA QA/G-5)

- Source: `epa2002qapp`, [original PDF](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-06/documents/g5-final.pdf) (the EPA site returns 403 to scripts; this project read it via an Internet Archive snapshot)
- Version: EPA/240/R-02/009, December 2002. Locations give the PDF page (counting from the cover).

### E1

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 9
- Quote: "The QA Project Plan documents the results of a project's technical planning process, providing in one place a clear, concise, and complete plan for the environmental data operation and its quality objectives and identifying key project personnel."
- Key point: A quality assurance (QA) project plan documents the results of the project's technical planning in one place: how the data work will be done, what the quality objectives are, and who the key personnel are.

### E2

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "But in all cases, the QA Project Plan should be completed and approved before the project is started."
- Key point: In all cases, the QA project plan should be completed and approved before the project starts.

### E3

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "It is EPA policy that all work funded by EPA in which environmental data will be collected, evaluated, used, or reported (including the use of existing data and modeling), or which involves the design, construction, and operation of environmental technology, have approved QA Project Plans"
- Key point: EPA policy is that all EPA-funded work in which environmental data will be collected, evaluated, used, or reported (including the use of existing data and modeling), or that involves environmental technology, must have an approved QA project plan.

### E4

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 13
- Quote: "Following the principle of the graded approach, if an element is not applicable, then indicate why it is not relevant."
- Key point: Under the graded approach, if an element of the plan is not applicable, state why it is not relevant.

### E5

- Location: Chapter 2, Element A6, PDF p. 25
- Quote: "For a modeling project, for example, indicate whether this is a model application project, i.e., evaluating existing models to determine whether they can perform the needed modeling to predict a future environmental condition in useable outputs, or whether this is a project to develop a new model because you know no applicable model exists."
- Key point: For a modeling project, state whether it evaluates or applies existing models or develops a new model because no suitable one exists.

### E6

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B3, PDF p. 36
- Quote: "A unique project numbering system helps to track each sample through the entire process to ensure samples are not switched accidentally."
- Key point: A unique sample numbering system lets you track each sample through the entire process, so samples are not switched by accident.

### E7

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B3, PDF p. 36
- Quote: "The term "chain-of-custody" is often used to designate legal custody procedures that enable tracing the possession and handling of a "sample" [...] during transfer, i.e., from sample collection through laboratory analysis, so that its physical possession is known at all points of the project."
- Key point: Chain of custody is a set of procedures for tracing who possessed and handled a sample at each transfer from collection to laboratory analysis.

### E8

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B3, PDF p. 36
- Quote: "This may include sample custody tags and written documentation to be signed by each person who handles the samples, and the use of a container sealed with custody tape. If such a procedure will be followed, describe it fully so that it can be done. Chain-of-custody procedures may be specified for projects where the data may be used in court as evidence."
- Key point: Chain of custody may involve sample custody tags, written records signed by everyone who handles the samples, and containers sealed with custody seals; projects may require it when the data may be used as evidence in court.

### E9

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B5, PDF p. 39
- Quote: "QC activities are those technical activities routinely performed, not to eliminate or minimize errors, but to measure or estimate their effect."
- Key point: Quality control (QC) activities are meant not to eliminate errors but to measure or estimate their effect.

### E10

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B5, PDF p. 39
- Quote: "Blanks are "clean" samples used to measure the sources of contamination at different collection and measurement stages."
- Key point: Blanks are "clean" samples used to measure contamination introduced at different stages of collection and measurement.

### E11

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B5, PDF p. 39
- Quote: "Bias is systematic error. A variety of QC samples can be used to determine the degree of bias, such as analysis of samples with a known concentration of the contaminant of concern. These are known as standards, matrix spike samples, and matrix-specific QC samples."
- Key point: Bias is systematic error; samples of known concentration (standards, matrix spikes, etc.) can be used to determine how large the bias is.

### E12

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B5, PDF p. 39
- Quote: "Imprecision is random error, observed as different results from repeated measurements of the same or identical samples. Replicate samples and split samples are commonly used to denote the level of precision in the measurement or collection system."
- Key point: Imprecision is random error, seen as different results from repeated measurements of the same sample; replicate and split samples are commonly used to show precision.

### E13

- Location: Chapter 2, Element B5, PDF p. 39
- Quote: "For each measurement activity, identify those QC checks that will be followed in this project, and indicate at what frequency each will occur."
- Key point: For each measurement activity, state which QC checks will be performed and how often.

### E14

- Location: Appendix B glossary, PDF p. 71
- Quote: "field blank -- a clean analyte-free sample which is carried to the sampling site and then exposed to sampling conditions, returned to the laboratory, and treated as an environmental sample."
- Key point: A field blank is a clean, analyte-free sample carried to the sampling site, exposed to sampling conditions, returned to the laboratory, and treated as an environmental sample.

### E15

- Location: Appendix B glossary, PDF p. 72
- Quote: "matrix spike sample -- a sample prepared by adding a known amount of the target analyte to a specified amount of a matrix. Spiked samples are used, for example, to determine the effect of the matrix on a method's recovery efficiency."
- Key point: A matrix spike sample is prepared by adding a known amount of the target analyte to a specified amount of matrix; it is used to determine the effect of the matrix on a method's recovery.

### E16

- Location: Appendix B glossary, PDF p. 69
- Quote: "collocated samples -- two or more portions collected at the same point in time and space so as to be considered identical. These samples are also known as field replicates and should be identified as such."
- Key point: Collocated samples are two or more portions collected at the same point in time and space so as to be considered identical; they are also called field replicates.

### E17

- Location: Appendix B glossary, PDF p. 71
- Quote: "environmental technology -- an all-inclusive term used to describe pollution control devices and systems, waste treatment processes and storage facilities, and site remediation technologies and their components that may be used to remove pollutants or contaminants from, or to prevent them from entering, the environment. Examples include wet scrubbers (air), soil washing (soil), granulated activated carbon unit (water), and filtration (air, water)."
- Key point: Environmental technology is an all-inclusive term for pollution control devices and systems, waste treatment and storage facilities, and site remediation technologies, e.g. wet scrubbers (air), soil washing (soil), granulated activated carbon units (water), and filtration (air, water).
