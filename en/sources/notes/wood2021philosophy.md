# Wood 2021: Philosophy and Christian Theology (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

- Source: `wood2021philosophy`, [original](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/christiantheology-philosophy/)
- Version: Entry in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy by William Wood (Faculty of Theology, University of Oxford), first published October 15, 2021. It covers only the relationship between Christian theology and philosophy; the entry lists several historical models of that relationship (integration, cooperation, separation, conflict, and others), of which the "Cooperation account" is one. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: Opening of §1
- Quote: "Similarly, with respect to their methods of inquiry, philosophers and theologians alike interpret authoritative texts, deploy arguments, and marshal evidence to support their conclusions."
- Key point: In their methods of inquiry, philosophers and theologians alike interpret authoritative texts, deploy arguments, and marshal evidence to support their conclusions.

### E2

- Location: Opening of §1
- Quote: "Here one might insist that Christian theological claims are grounded by appeals to “faith” or “authority”, whereas philosophical claims are grounded by appeals to “reason”. This contrast is promising when suitably developed, but it is not as sharp as one might initially suppose."
- Key point: A common view is that theology appeals to "faith" or "authority" and philosophy to "reason"; the author finds this contrast promising but less sharp than it might seem.

### E3

- Location: §1.2.1 Cooperation
- Quote: "On the Cooperation account, theology differs from philosophy chiefly because theology assumes the truth of divine revelation, whereas philosophy does not."
- Key point: On the Cooperation account, theology differs from philosophy chiefly in assuming the truth of divine revelation, which philosophy does not.

### E4

- Location: §1.2.1 Cooperation
- Quote: "Theologians can appeal to revelation—scripture and authoritative Church tradition—in order to generate new lines of inquiry, and can treat revealed truths as evidence in their investigations. For their part, philosophers must appeal only to premises and evidence that are in principle available to any rational inquirer."
- Key point: On the Cooperation account, theologians may appeal to revelation (scripture and authoritative Church tradition) and treat it as evidence; philosophers may appeal only to premises and evidence available in principle to any rational inquirer.
