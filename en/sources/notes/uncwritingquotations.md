# University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill Writing Center: Quotations

- Source: `uncwritingquotations`, [original](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/quotations/)
- Version: Free Writing Center handout (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0); accessed October 2026

### E1

- Location: When should I quote?
- Quote: "papers analyzing literature may rely heavily on direct quotations of the text"
- Key point: Papers analyzing literature may rely heavily on direct quotations of the text.

### E2

- Location: When should I quote?, Discussing specific arguments or ideas
- Quote: "If it is especially important that you formulate a counterargument to this claim, then you might wish to quote the part of the statement that you find questionable and establish a dialogue between yourself and John Doe"
- Key point: When you want to counter a scholar's claim, quote the part you question and set up a dialogue with them.

### E3

- Location: How do I set up and follow up a quotation?
- Quote: "Provide context for each quotation." / "Attribute each quotation to its source." / "Explain the significance of the quotation." / "Provide a citation for the quotation."
- Key point: Four steps for quotations: provide context, attribute the source, explain the significance, and give a citation.

### E4

- Location: How much should I quote?, Excerpt those fragments carefully!
- Quote: "Misquoting misrepresents the ideas of others."
- Key point: Misquoting (quoting out of context) misrepresents other people's ideas.

### E5

- Location: How much should I quote?, Use block quotations sparingly
- Quote: "you should use block quotations only when you fear that omitting any words will destroy the integrity of the passage."
- Key point: Use block quotations only when omitting any words would destroy the integrity of the passage.

### E6

- Location: How do I embed a quotation into a sentence?
- Quote: "In general, avoid leaving quotes as sentences unto themselves."
- Key point: Don't leave a quotation standing alone as its own sentence.
