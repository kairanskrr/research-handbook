# Bowern 2018: Reflections on linguistic fieldwork

- Source: `bowern2018fieldwork`, [Original](http://hdl.handle.net/10125/24822)
- Version: Language Documentation & Conservation Special Publication 15, pp. 202-209, open access; the author is a linguist at Yale University in the US

### E1

- Location: §2
- Quote: "elicitation and translation provides better evidence of the possible and impossible structures of languages, while natural interactions provide a valuable source of structural data"
- Key point: Elicitation and translation give better evidence of which structures are possible or impossible; natural interaction is also a valuable source of structural data.

### E2

- Location: §2
- Quote: "The kits provide visual stimuli, questionnaires, and structured, consistent tasks for use with speakers."
- Key point: The field kits of the Max Planck Institute (Nijmegen, the Netherlands) provide visual stimuli, questionnaires, and structured, consistent tasks.

### E3

- Location: §2
- Quote: "as a way of getting controlled data without the prompting of translation-based sentences or the need for speakers to be fluent in a contact language."
- Key point: Such tasks yield controlled data without translation-based prompts and without requiring speakers to be fluent in a contact language.

### E4

- Location: §2
- Quote: "extensive audiovisual recordings bring the problems that come with multiple gigabytes of data, the bottleneck of transcription, and material of incidental relevance to the project."
- Key point: Extensive audiovisual recording brings problems: gigabytes of data, a transcription bottleneck, and much material of only incidental relevance to the project.

### E5

- Location: §2
- Quote: "Perhaps most famous are the Max Planck Institute (Nijmegen) field manual kits released over the period 1992-2010."
- Key point: The best known are the field manual kits released by the Max Planck Institute in Nijmegen, the Netherlands, between 1992 and 2010.
