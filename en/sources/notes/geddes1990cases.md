# Geddes 1990: How the Cases You Choose Affect the Answers You Get

- Source: `geddes1990cases`, [Institute of Governmental Studies, University of California, Berkeley, Working Paper 90-13 (open access on eScholarship)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9r45g86c)
- Version: Working paper version (1990), a scan whose text comes from optical character recognition; the published version appeared in Political Analysis 2: 131–150 (1990). Locations are given as subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "One of our most durable conventions is the" / "selection of cases for study on the dependent variable. That is, if we want to understand something, for example, revolution, we select one or more occurrences and subject them to scrutiny"
- Key point: One of the most durable conventions in comparative politics is selecting cases on the dependent variable: to understand revolution, you pick some revolutions and study them.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "All graduate students learn in the statistics courses forced upon them that selection on the dependent variable is forbidden,"
- Key point: Every graduate student learns in statistics courses that selecting on the dependent variable is forbidden, but few remember why.

### E3

- Location: The Nature of the Problem
- Quote: "The crux of the difficulty that arises when cases are selected on the dependent variable is that if one studies only countries A and B, one can collect only half the information needed, namely what A and B have in common."
- Key point: The crux of selecting on the dependent variable: if you study only cases A and B, which share the same outcome, you get only half the information needed, namely what A and B have in common.

### E4

- Location: The Nature of the Problem
- Quote: "The only things that can actually be explained using a sample selected on the dependent variable are differences among the selected cases."
- Key point: The only things a sample selected on the dependent variable can actually explain are differences among the selected cases.

### E5

- Location: A Straightforward Case of Selection on the Dependent Variable
- Quote: "But the conclusion does not follow. Perhaps there are other countries which suppress labor to the same or greater degree and which have failed to prosper economically."
- Key point: That several fast-growing newly industrializing countries all repress labor does not show that labor repression promotes growth: other countries may repress labor as much and still fail to prosper.

### E6

- Location: A Straightforward Case of Selection on the Dependent Variable
- Quote: "When one looks at the relationship between labor repression and growth for a larger sample of cases which includes slow-growing countries as well as fast, the apparent relationship between labor repression and growth disappears."
- Key point: In a larger sample that includes slow-growing as well as fast-growing countries, the apparent relationship between labor repression and growth disappears.
