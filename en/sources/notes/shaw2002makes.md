# Shaw 2002: What Makes Good Research in Software Engineering?

- Source: `shaw2002makes`, [original](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~Compose/ftp/shaw-fin-etaps.pdf)
- Version: International Journal of Software Tools for Technology Transfer 4(1), 2002, PDF from the author's homepage. Only the parts that hold across fields are excerpted.

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Scientific and engineering research fields can be characterized by identifying what they value: [...] What kinds of questions are "interesting"? [...] What kinds of results help to answer these questions, and what research methods can produce these results? [...] What kinds of evidence can demonstrate the validity of a result, and how are good results distinguished from bad ones?"
- Key point: What a research field values can be characterized by three questions: which questions are worth asking, what results can answer them, and what evidence shows that the results hold.

### E2

- Location: §2.2 Types of Research Results
- Quote: "Research yields new knowledge. This knowledge is expressed in the form of a particular result."
- Key point: Research produces new knowledge, and that knowledge is expressed in the form of some concrete result.

### E3

- Location: §2.1 Types of Research Questions
- Quote: "Research questions may be about methods for developing software, about methods for analyzing software, about the design, evaluation, or implementation of specific systems, about generalizations over whole classes of systems, or about the sheer feasibility of a task."
- Key point: Research questions come in different types: how to do something, how to analyze or evaluate it, what a specific object is like, what generally holds for a class of objects, and whether something is possible.

### E4

- Location: §2.2 Types of Research Results
- Quote: "The result may be a specific procedure or technique for software development or for analysis. It may be more general, capturing a number of specific results in a model; such models are of many degrees of precision and formality. Sometimes, the result is the solution to a specific problem or the outcome of a specific analysis. Finally, as Brooks observed, observations and rules of thumb may be good preliminary results."
- Key point: Results also come in different types: procedures or techniques, models that generalize many results, solutions to or analyses of specific problems, and observations and rules of thumb.

### E5

- Location: §1.2.3 Broad View of Research
- Quote: "Findings: well-established scientific truths, judged by truthfulness and rigor; [...] Observations: reports on actual phenomena, judged by interestingness; [...] Rules of thumb: generalizations, signed by their author but perhaps incompletely supported by data, judged by usefulness"
- Key point: Shaw restates Brooks's three levels of results: findings (judged on truth and rigor), observations (judged on interest), and rules of thumb (judged on usefulness).

### E6

- Location: §1.2.3 Broad View of Research
- Quote: "Observations and rules of thumb provide valuable guidance for practice when findings are not available. They also help to understand and area and lay the groundwork for the research that will, in time, yield findings."
- Key point: When no findings are yet available, observations and rules of thumb still have value and lay the groundwork for findings later.

### E7

- Location: §2.3 Types of Research Validations
- Quote: "Good research requires not only a result, but also clear and convincing evidence that the result is sound. This evidence should be based on experience or systematic analysis, not simply persuasive argument or textbook examples."
- Key point: Good research needs not only results but clear and convincing evidence; the evidence should come from actual experience or systematic analysis, not persuasive argument or textbook examples.

### E8

- Location: §2.3 Types of Research Validations
- Quote: "A significant number of ICSE submissions depend only on blatant assertion to demonstrate the validity of their results, or offer no evidence at all; such submissions are only rarely accepted."
- Key point: Submissions that rely on blatant assertion, or give no evidence at all, are rarely accepted.

### E9

- Location: §4 Summary
- Quote: "The strategy of a research project should select a result, an approach to obtaining the result, and a validation strategy appropriate to the research question."
- Key point: A research strategy should make the type of result, the way it is obtained, and the way it is validated all fit the research question.

### E10

- Location: §4 Summary
- Quote: "major results that influence practice rely on accumulation of evidence from many projects. Each individual paper thus provides incremental knowledge, and collections of related research projects and reports provide both confirming and cumulative evidence."
- Key point: Major results rest on evidence accumulated across many projects; a single paper provides incremental knowledge.
