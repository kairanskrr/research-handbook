# Parikh, Batra & Lee 2020: How we write rebuttals

- Source: `parikh2020rebuttals`, [original](https://deviparikh.substack.com/p/how-we-write-rebuttals-dc84742fece1)
- Version: Blog post, 2020, based on experience with AI conferences.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "The core guiding principle is that the rebuttal should be thorough, direct, and easy for the Reviewers and Area Chair (RACs) to follow."
- Key point: The core principle: a rebuttal should be thorough, direct, and easy for reviewers and the Area Chair to follow.

### E2

- Location: Who reads the rebuttal
- Quote: "The AC, who is likely even less familiar with your work, and a good guiding principle is to assume that all they will read is the set of reviews and the rebuttal."
- Key point: A rebuttal has two audiences: the reviewers and the Area Chair (AC), who may read only the reviews and the rebuttal.

### E3

- Location: Who reads the rebuttal
- Quote: "In our experience, most new members of the research community focus on (1), but ignore (2)."
- Key point: Newcomers tend to focus on the reviewers and ignore the Area Chair, who makes the decision.

### E4

- Location: Who reads the rebuttal
- Quote: "would a neutral third-party be able to tell if the reviewer concerns were addressed purely based on your rebuttal (without reading the paper or the reviews again)?"
- Key point: The test: could a neutral third party tell, from your rebuttal alone, whether the reviewers' concerns were addressed?

### E5

- Location: Writing process
- Quote: "Itemize reviewer comments. We use a handy spreadsheet to organize individual comments/questions/concerns raised by each reviewer."
- Key point: First itemize every comment from every reviewer in a spreadsheet, to spot common concerns and avoid missing anything.

### E6

- Location: Writing process
- Quote: "Write concisely but don’t worry about space. Cover every point and trim / prioritize them later."
- Key point: Make the first draft cover every point, then trim and prioritize to fit the length limit.

### E7

- Location: Tip 2
- Quote: "Tip 2. Order matters. Start with the biggest concerns that you have good answers for and work your way to less clear-cut responses and minor points."
- Key point: Start with the biggest concerns you have good answers for, then move to less clear-cut responses and minor points.

### E8

- Location: Tip 3
- Quote: "Tip 3. Let reviewers speak for themselves, then respond directly. Quote the core of the reviewer’s question or concern concisely and completely. Then before saying anything else, respond to it directly."
- Key point: Quote the core of the reviewer's question briefly, answer it directly in the first sentence, then explain.

### E9

- Location: Tip 12
- Quote: "Tip 12. Stats speak louder than words. Rather than argue with RACs, give them data/stats to back your claim up."
- Key point: Rather than arguing, back your claim with data.

### E10

- Location: Tip 13
- Quote: "Tip 13. Don’t promise, do. Instead of saying “We will discuss Singh et al. in the paper.”, provide a discussion in the rebuttal."
- Key point: Don't just promise to add something; provide it in the rebuttal itself.

### E11

- Location: Tip 15
- Quote: "Tip 15. Be transparent."
- Key point: Be transparent about what you cannot do (e.g., the venue disallows new experiments, or you lack compute).

### E12

- Location: Tip 9
- Quote: "Tip 9. But get credit for details you already included. That said, if something a reviewer asked for was already in the paper, say so."
- Key point: If what a reviewer asks for is already in the paper, point to where and restate it in the rebuttal.

### E13

- Location: Tip 1
- Quote: "Tip 1. Start positive. Provide a summary of the reviews, highlighting positive things that reviewers said about your work."
- Key point: Open with a summary of the positive points in the reviews, so readers don't forget the paper's strengths.

### E14

- Location: Tip 18
- Quote: "So decide whether you’d like to be argumentative and risk your reviewer taking a strong stand against the paper, or if you’d like to work towards a common ground."
- Key point: A rebuttal is also a human exchange: arguing may push a reviewer to take a stronger stand against you; working toward common ground is more effective.

### E15

- Location: Tip 16
- Quote: "Tip 16. Shine a spotlight on reviewers acting in bad-faith."
- Key point: For clearly careless or unfounded reviews, point this out so others can give them less weight.

### E16

- Location: Opening (citing Bill Freeman's metaphor)
- Quote: "The reality, Bill describes, is more like a large crowded marketplace."
- Key point: Real peer review is more like a crowded marketplace: everyone has limited time and attention and incomplete information.

### E17

- Location: Writing process, Itemize reviewer comments
- Quote: "Laying everything out in the same place helps identify common concerns and keeps us from missing anything accidentally. Do this ASAP to identify any necessary new experiments (if the venue allows it) or analysis early."
- Key point: Putting all comments in one place helps spot common concerns and avoid misses; do it as soon as possible so you can identify any new experiments (if the venue allows) or analyses you need.

### E18

- Location: Tip 5
- Quote: "Tip 5. Respond to the intent of the questions. Don’t feel trapped to only discuss the quoted concern — also address the intent of the comment."
- Key point: Answer not only the literal question but the intent behind the comment, such as doubts about whether the experiments as a whole are sufficient.

### E19

- Location: Tip 10
- Quote: "Tip 10. Consolidate common concerns. Save space by responding to multiple reviewers at once if they share related concerns."
- Key point: When several reviewers raise related concerns, answer them together to save space.

### E20

- Location: Tip 14
- Quote: "Likewise, instead of arguing why a reviewer’s suggestion might not work, try it and see if it works. Maybe it will!"
- Key point: Instead of arguing why a reviewer's suggestion won't work, try it; it might.

### E21

- Location: Tip 15
- Quote: "Tip 15. Be transparent. Reviewers hinted at an additional experiment but the venue doesn’t allow it? Say so."
- Key point: Be transparent about what you cannot do, e.g., when the venue does not allow additional experiments.

### E22

- Location: Tip 17
- Quote: "Tip 17. Acknowledge reviewer efforts. On the other hand, if a reviewer goes above and beyond to be constructive, thank them for it."
- Key point: Thank reviewers who go out of their way to be constructive.
