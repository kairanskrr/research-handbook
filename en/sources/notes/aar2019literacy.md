# American Academy of Religion 2019: AAR Religious Literacy Guidelines: What U.S. College Graduates Need to Understand about Religion

- Source: `aar2019literacy`, [original](https://aarweb.org/resource/aar-religious-literacy-guidelines/)
- Version: guidelines published in 2019 by the American Academy of Religion (AAR) on the religious literacy that graduates of two-year and four-year US colleges should have. It is a teaching guideline, not a standard for research methods. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Prologue
- Quote: "The AAR is the world’s largest association of scholars who research or teach topics related to religion. There are over 8,000 members comprised largely of faculty at colleges, universities, and theological schools in North America"
- Key point: The AAR describes itself as the largest association of scholars who research or teach topics related to religion, with over 8,000 members, mostly faculty at colleges, universities, and seminaries in North America.

### E2

- Location: Approaches to Religion Found in Two- and Four-Year Colleges and Universities / Religious Studies
- Quote: "There are many different methods that can fall under the category of the academic study of religion (e.g., literary-critical, anthropological, sociological, historical, etc.). There are also sub-disciplines whose approach to the study of religion is congruent with religious studies. These include the sociology of religion, anthropology of religion, and psychology of religion, among others."
- Key point: The academic study of religion includes many methods (literary-critical, anthropological, sociological, historical, and others), and subfields such as the sociology, anthropology, and psychology of religion are consistent with the religious studies approach.

### E3

- Location: Religious Studies
- Quote: "There is distinction between the confessional or prescriptive expression of a religious belief or practice and the study of diverse religious expressions"
- Key point: One shared premise of religious studies: the confessional or prescriptive expression of a religious belief is distinct from the study of diverse religious expressions.

### E4

- Location: Religious Studies
- Quote: "Religions are internally diverse"
- Key point: Religions are internally diverse (one of the shared premises of religious studies; others are "religions evolve and change" and "religions are embedded in culture and history").

### E5

- Location: Other Approaches / Faith-based
- Quote: "Faith-based teaching about religion promotes an explicit belief and/or practice of religion."
- Key point: Faith-based teaching about religion promotes an explicit belief and/or practice of religion.

### E6

- Location: Other Approaches / Faith-based
- Quote: "The faith-based approach may or may not incorporate critical analytical tools, such as cultural and historical context, to deepen a tradition’s self-understanding by reflecting on its internal diversity."
- Key point: The faith-based approach may or may not use critical analytical tools (such as cultural and historical context) to deepen a tradition's self-understanding.

### E7

- Location: Executive Summary, list of competencies
- Quote: "Distinguish confessional or prescriptive statements made by religions from descriptive or analytical statements"
- Key point: One competency of religious literacy: distinguishing confessional or prescriptive statements made by religions from descriptive or analytical statements.

### E8

- Location: Other Approaches / Experiential
- Quote: "This approach can be compatible with a religious studies approach, especially when framed as ethnographic research."
- Key point: An experiential approach of visiting religious people and sites can be compatible with a religious studies approach, especially when framed as ethnographic research.
