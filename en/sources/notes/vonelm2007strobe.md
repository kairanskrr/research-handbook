# von Elm et al. 2007: The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) Statement

- Source: `vonelm2007strobe`, [PLoS Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0040296) (CC BY)
- Version: PLoS Med 4(10): e296, 2007.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Randomised trials cannot answer all important questions about a given intervention. For example, observational studies are more suitable to detect rare or late adverse effects of treatments, and are more likely to provide an indication of what is achieved in daily medical practice"
- Key point: Randomized trials cannot answer every question; observational studies are better suited to detecting rare or late adverse effects and are more likely to reflect effects in everyday medical practice.

### E2

- Location: Aims and Use of the STROBE Statement
- Quote: "The STROBE Statement is a checklist of items that should be addressed in articles reporting on the 3 main study designs of analytical epidemiology: cohort, case-control, and crosssectional studies."
- Key point: STROBE is a checklist for the three main designs of analytical epidemiology: cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies.

### E3

- Location: Aims and Use of the STROBE Statement
- Quote: "The intention is solely to provide guidance on how to report observational research well: these recommendations are not prescriptions for designing or conducting studies. Also, while clarity of reporting is a prerequisite to evaluation, the checklist is not an instrument to evaluate the quality of observational research."
- Key point: STROBE only guides how to report a study; it does not prescribe how to design or conduct one, and it is not a tool for rating study quality.

### E4

- Location: STROBE Components
- Quote: "The STROBE Statement is a checklist of 22 items that we consider essential for good reporting of observational studies"
- Key point: The STROBE checklist has 22 items.
