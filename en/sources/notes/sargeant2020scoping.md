# Sargeant & O'Connor 2020: Scoping Reviews, Systematic Reviews, and Meta-Analysis: Applications in Veterinary Medicine

- Source: `sargeant2020scoping`, [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6997489/), DOI [10.3389/fvets.2020.00011](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2020.00011)
- Version: Frontiers in Veterinary Science 7:11, 2020, open access (CC BY 4.0), read via the Europe PMC full-text API on 2026-10-08. The two authors are at the University of Guelph, Canada, and the College of Veterinary Medicine at Iowa State University, US. A methods review, not a society guideline or regulation. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Systematic Reviews
- Quote: "The planned approach for each of the steps is first described in a protocol, which should be completed prior to starting the actual review." / "Any deviations from the protocol should be acknowledged and justified in the final systematic review report or publication."
- Key point: Describe the planned approach for every step of a systematic review in a protocol completed before the review starts; acknowledge and justify any deviations from the protocol in the final report.

### E2

- Location: Systematic Reviews
- Quote: "Protocols may be published prior to starting the review on websites such as PROSPERO (https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/) or SYREAF (www.SYREAF.org), posted to University repositories, or submitted as supplementary materials with systematic review publications."
- Key point: The protocol can be published before the review starts on sites such as PROSPERO or SYREAF, posted to a university repository, or submitted as supplementary material with the paper.

### E3

- Location: Define the review question
- Quote: "In veterinary medicine, systematic review questions generally fall into one of four question types: descriptive questions, intervention questions, exposure or etiology questions, or diagnostic test accuracy evaluations."
- Key point: Veterinary systematic review questions generally fall into four types: descriptive questions (e.g., prevalence), intervention questions, exposure or etiology questions, and diagnostic test accuracy.

### E4

- Location: Conduct a comprehensive search for studies
- Quote: "Searching the veterinary literature can be challenging, in that search filters used in human healthcare literature searches are lacking for veterinary medicine and reporting of key features in titles and abstracts may be poor" / "It is recommended that the search include multiple databases."
- Key point: Searching the veterinary literature is hard: search filters common in human healthcare are lacking for veterinary medicine, and titles and abstracts often report key features incompletely; search several databases.

### E5

- Location: Scoping Reviews, Selecting relevant studies (the systematic review section says screening follows the same procedure)
- Quote: "To reduce the potential for selection bias in the identification of relevant literature, it is standard practice for relevance screening to be undertaken in duplicate by two reviewers working independently, with any disagreements resolved by consensus." / "screening follows the same procedures as was described previously for a scoping review"
- Key point: To reduce selection bias, standard practice is for two reviewers to screen for relevance independently, resolving disagreements by consensus; systematic reviews follow the same procedure.

### E6

- Location: Collect data from relevant studies
- Quote: "To reduce the potential for misclassification, data extraction should be conducted by two reviewers working independently with disagreements resolved by consensus"
- Key point: To reduce misclassification, two reviewers should extract data independently, resolving disagreements by consensus (the authors also list alternatives such as one person extracting and a second checking).

### E7

- Location: Assess risk of bias in relevant studies
- Quote: "At this stage of the review, it is not appropriate to remove studies from a review based on the risk of bias, unless one or more trial design features are included a priori in the eligibility criteria" / "Validated tools for assessing risk of bias are available for different study designs"
- Key point: At the risk-of-bias step, do not remove studies for high risk of bias unless certain design features were written into the eligibility criteria in advance; different study designs have their own validated risk-of-bias tools.

### E8

- Location: Synthesize the results (publication bias)
- Quote: "Publication bias is a potential concern whenever research results are considered in decision-making." / "a study of conference proceedings abstracts for swine and cattle vaccine trials found that <10% of the studies were subsequently published"
- Key point: Publication bias is a potential concern whenever research results inform decisions. Many veterinary studies are never published in peer-reviewed journals: one study found that fewer than 10% of conference abstracts on swine and cattle vaccine trials were later formally published.

### E9

- Location: Present the results
- Quote: "The methods and results of a systematic reviews should be reported in sufficient detail that the reader can evaluate the potential for bias." / "The Preferred Reporting of Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines provide recommendations for the items of information and the level of detail that should be included in a systematic review report"
- Key point: Report the methods and results of a systematic review in enough detail for readers to evaluate potential bias; the PRISMA guidelines specify what a review report should contain and in how much detail.

### E10

- Location: Interpret the results
- Quote: "One approach to evaluating the quality of evidence is by using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)" / "The overall quality of evidence is determined to be high, medium, low, or very low."
- Key point: One approach to rating the quality of evidence is GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation), which rates overall evidence quality as high, medium, low, or very low.
