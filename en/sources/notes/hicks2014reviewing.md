# Hicks 2014: Advice on reviewing papers

- Source: `hicks2014reviewing`, [original](http://www.pl-enthusiast.net/2014/08/21/advice-reviewing-papers/)
- Version: Blog post, The PL Enthusiast, 2014-08-21.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Reviews serve two purposes. They judge whether research is well done, and they give feedback to the authors to improve their research."
- Key point: Reviews serve two purposes: judging whether the research is well done, and giving the authors feedback to improve it.

### E2

- Location: Judging the work
- Quote: "There are four high-level things you are judging about the paper: Is this an interesting/useful idea or study? Is it new, or is it significantly overlapping with prior work? Are the conclusions drawn in the paper correct? Is the paper sufficiently accessible/well-written that others will understand it?"
- Key point: A review judges four main things: Is the idea or study interesting/useful? Is it new? Are the conclusions correct? Is it written so readers can understand it?

### E3

- Location: Start with a summary
- Quote: "Write a concise, judgement-free summary of the paper before the judgement and justification."
- Key point: Start the review with a concise, judgment-free summary of the paper, then give your judgment and justification.

### E4

- Location: Scores abstract text
- Quote: "You should view the text as primary, and the scores as an abstraction of the text, not a complement to it."
- Key point: The text is primary and the scores are an abstraction of it; an author reading the text should be able to guess the scores.

### E5

- Location: Put the most important parts first
- Quote: "make sure the key parts of your argument appear at the top. Supporting details should be afterward."
- Key point: Put the key parts of your argument at the top and the supporting details afterward.

### E6

- Location: Be respectful
- Quote: "Be respectful of the authors, even if the paper fails on every count. Criticize the paper, not the people. Avoid hyperbole."
- Key point: Be respectful of the authors even if the paper fails on every count; criticize the paper, not the people; avoid hyperbole.

### E7

- Location: Confidentiality
- Quote: "Submissions (double-blind or not) are made in confidence. Do not reveal that a paper has been submitted somewhere. Do not pass them on to others."
- Key point: Submissions are made in confidence: do not reveal them or pass them on to others.

### E8

- Location: Judging the work
- Quote: "On the other hand, a proposed solution may overlap with a prior solution, but still provide new ideas and useful data, and therefore enjoy some novelty."
- Key point: Overlapping with prior work does not mean a paper lacks novelty; if it offers new ideas and useful data, it still has some novelty.

### E9

- Location: Interesting or useful?
- Quote: "Making such a decision requires some sense of the values of the venue/community you are reviewing for."
- Key point: Deciding whether a paper is good enough requires a sense of the standards of the venue and the community you are reviewing for.

### E10

- Location: Interesting or useful?
- Quote: "Ultimately, the collective judgment of all reviewers will determine whether the paper is good enough; it’s more art than science."
- Key point: The final outcome is the collective judgment of all reviewers, which is more art than science.

### E11

- Location: Reading the paper
- Quote: "Therefore, a useful strategy in reviewing papers is to read the introduction and conclusions carefully, along with the related work, and then skim the middle parts."
- Key point: One reviewing strategy: read the introduction, conclusions, and related work carefully and skim the middle; if the paper fails the first two criteria, there is no need to dig into the details.

### E12

- Location: Suggestions for improvement
- Quote: "You’ve already sunk a few hours into reading the paper; why not spend another 30 minutes to an hour helping the authors make their paper even better?"
- Key point: Having already spent a few hours reading the paper, it is well worth another 30 minutes to an hour to suggest improvements.
