# Black 2022: Novelty in Science: A guide for reviewers

- Source: `black2022novelty`, [Original](https://perceiving-systems.blog/en/news/novelty-in-science)
- Version: Blog post, Perceiving Systems Blog.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "I see reviewers regularly mistake complexity, difficulty, and technicality for novelty."
- Key point: Reviewers regularly mistake complexity, difficulty, and technicality for novelty.

### E2

- Location: Novelty as complexity
- Quote: "If nobody thought to change that one term, then it is ipso facto novel."
- Key point: Changing just one thing in a way nobody thought of before is novel in itself.

### E3

- Location: Novelty as surprise
- Quote: "The novelty, however, must be evaluated before the idea existed."
- Key point: Novelty must be judged from before the idea existed; seeming obvious in hindsight does not reduce it.

### E4

- Location: Novelty as technical novelty
- Quote: "A new dataset can be novel if it does something no other dataset has done, even if all the methods used to generate the dataset are well known. A new use of an old method can be novel if nobody ever thought to use it this way."
- Key point: Novelty is not only in technical details: a new dataset, or a new use of an old method, can also be novel.

### E5

- Location: Novelty as usefulness or value
- Quote: "Lack of utility is indeed an issue but it is very hard to assess with a new idea. Reviewers should be careful here and aware that we all have limited imagination."
- Key point: The usefulness of a new idea is hard to judge in advance; reviewers should be aware that their imagination is limited.

### E6

- Location: Novelty as difficulty
- Quote: "A simple idea can be important. But it can also be trivial."
- Key point: A simple idea can be important, or it can be trivial; trivial means unimportant, not simple.

### E7

- Location: Novelty as difficulty
- Quote: "A trivial idea is an unimportant idea. If a paper has a simple idea that works better than the state of the art, then it is most likely not trivial."
- Key point: Trivial means unimportant, not simple; a simple idea that works better than the state of the art is most likely not trivial.
