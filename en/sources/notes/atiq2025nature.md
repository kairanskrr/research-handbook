# Atiq & Marmor 2025: The Nature of Law (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

- Source: `atiq2025nature`, [original](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lawphil-nature/)
- Version: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry "The Nature of Law," first published 2001, substantively revised 2025-04-29, by Emad Atiq and Andrei Marmor (both at Cornell University, US). Read 2026-10-08. Locations give entry sections. The entry mainly covers the Anglo-American analytic tradition in legal philosophy.

### E1

- Location: §2 The Methodology of Jurisprudence
- Quote: "There are five main approaches to these issues that we’ll discuss in turn." / "One view takes the subject of investigation to be a concept of law, and jurisprudence to be a form of conceptual analysis." / "A fifth view, associated principally with Dworkin’s work, takes legal theory to be a fundamentally “interpretive” enterprise that is both descriptive and evaluative."
- Key point: There are five main approaches to the method of jurisprudence; one treats jurisprudence as conceptual analysis of the concept of law, another (associated mainly with Dworkin) treats legal theory as an "interpretive" enterprise that is both descriptive and evaluative.

### E2

- Location: §2.1 Conceptual Analysis
- Quote: "Their goal is to characterize the concept in a way that is compatible with our intuitions about it"
- Key point: The goal of conceptual analysis is to characterize the concept of law in a way compatible with our intuitions about it.

### E3

- Location: §2.1 Conceptual Analysis
- Quote: "The account’s success turns on whether it yields intuitively correct results about particular cases and does so in an explanatorily satisfying way as opposed to an ad hoc manner."
- Key point: An account succeeds if it yields intuitively correct results about particular cases, and does so in an explanatorily satisfying rather than ad hoc way.

### E4

- Location: §2.1 Conceptual Analysis
- Quote: "A key question this approach raises concerns the source and reliability of the ‘raw data’ for the theory—namely, our basic conceptual intuitions."
- Key point: A key question for this approach is the source and reliability of the theory's "raw data," namely our basic conceptual intuitions.

### E5

- Location: §2.2 Metaphysical Reduction
- Quote: "One reductionist approach, prominently defended by Brian Leiter (2007), is known as “naturalized jurisprudence.”" / "But a distinguishing characteristic is the insistence on a purely empirical methodology and rejection of intuitionistic or a priori methods"
- Key point: "Naturalized jurisprudence," defended by Leiter and others, insists on a purely empirical methodology and rejects intuitionistic or a priori methods.

### E6

- Location: §2.5 Constructive Interpretation
- Quote: "Recall that legal practitioners, on Dworkin’s portrayal, interpret the social practices within their system in a way that’s guided by both considerations of “fit” and “justification”" / "However, the precise criteria for morally evaluating competing theories on the interpretivist approach remain unclear."
- Key point: On Dworkin's account, interpreting legal practice is guided by both "fit" (matching the practice) and "justification" (being morally defensible); but on this approach, the precise criteria for morally evaluating competing theories remain unclear.

### E7

- Location: end of §2.5
- Quote: "In the end, “methodological pluralism” may be the most apt characterization of the state of play in jurisprudence."
- Key point: The state of play in jurisprudence may be best described as "methodological pluralism."
