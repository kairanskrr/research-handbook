# American Philosophical Association 1996: Statement on Research

- Source: `apaphil1996research`, [original](https://www.apaonline.org/page/research)
- Version: drafted by the Committee on the Status and Future of the Profession of the American Philosophical Association and approved by its Board in 1996; originally published in Proceedings and Addresses of the American Philosophical Association 70(2): 119–121. The website blocks scripted access, so the text is taken from an Internet Archive snapshot. This is a 1996 statement describing the situation in the United States. Locations follow paragraph order.

### E1

- Location: paragraph 3
- Quote: "Research in philosophy also often takes the form of efforts to refine analyses, develop and advance or criticize interpretations, explore alternative perspectives and new ways of thinking, suggest and apply modified or novel modes of assessment, and, in general to promote new understanding."
- Key point: Philosophical research often takes the form of refining analyses, developing, advancing, or criticizing interpretations, exploring alternative perspectives and new ways of thinking, and proposing new forms of evaluation; in short, deepening understanding.

### E2

- Location: paragraph 2
- Quote: "In some disciplines the aim of research is to discover new facts, to gather new data, to put hypotheses and theories to the test by way of new experimental evidence or calculations, and, in general, to generate new knowledge. Some philosophical research is of this kind."
- Key point: In some disciplines, research aims to discover new facts, gather new data, and test hypotheses through experiments or computation; some philosophical research is also of this kind.

### E3

- Location: paragraph 4
- Quote: "Disagreement and criticism are among the hallmarks of philosophical life, and it is rare to find two philosophers working in the same area who are in complete agreement with each other."
- Key point: Disagreement and criticism are hallmarks of philosophical life; it is rare to find two philosophers in the same area in complete agreement.

### E4

- Location: paragraph 4
- Quote: "The very best research in philosophy serves more often to generate disputes and differences than to resolve them."
- Key point: The very best philosophical research more often generates disputes than resolves them.

### E5

- Location: paragraph 5
- Quote: "So, for example, inquiry in some areas of philosophy is akin to kinds of inquiry pursued in mathematics and in some of the sciences; while in others it resembles the activities of those who study languages, literatures and the arts."
- Key point: Philosophical inquiry is diverse: some areas resemble inquiry in mathematics and some sciences, others resemble the study of languages, literature, and the arts.

### E6

- Location: paragraph 7
- Quote: "Citation of contemporary literature is not practiced in a manner that yields meaningful citation statistics, however, since it is done selectively if at all (as authors may deem useful), and is as likely to be critical as favorable."
- Key point: Philosophers cite contemporary literature selectively, and as often to criticize as to endorse, so citation statistics mean little.

### E7

- Location: paragraph 8
- Quote: "Except in a few areas of philosophy with scientific or public policy connections, research grants of the sort with which those in scientific disciplines are familiar are nonexistent."
- Key point: Except in a few areas with connections to science or public policy, philosophy lacks research grants of the kind found in the sciences.

### E8

- Location: paragraph 9
- Quote: "Some philosophers write both essays ("papers" and reviews) and books, and a few write books exclusively, but many more write essays exclusively."
- Key point: Some philosophers write both essays and books, a few write only books, and many more write only essays.

### E9

- Location: paragraph 9
- Quote: "They typically involve the careful examination of difficult and complex issues, and what generally is taken to matter most in them is the quality of the reasoning set forth and the greater understanding of the matter under consideration to which it contributes."
- Key point: Philosophical essays typically examine difficult issues carefully; what matters most is the quality of the reasoning and the deeper understanding it brings.

### E10

- Location: paragraph 10
- Quote: "Some doctoral dissertations in philosophy lend themselves readily and appropriately to publication in book form, but it is neither expected nor typical that dissertations will be published as books. It is more common for portions of dissertations to appear in the form of extracted articles."
- Key point: Philosophy dissertations are neither expected nor typically published as books; it is more common to publish parts of them as essays.

### E11

- Location: paragraph 10
- Quote: "At any stage of a philosopher's career, the development of a substantial essay of sufficient quality and interest to be published in a good philosophical journal requires considerable time and effort."
- Key point: At any career stage, developing a substantial essay good enough for a good philosophy journal takes a great deal of time and effort.

### E12

- Location: paragraph 9
- Quote: "In either event, they rarely have the character of the reports on empirical studies that are common in some disciplines."
- Key point: Philosophical essays rarely resemble the reports on empirical studies common in other disciplines.
