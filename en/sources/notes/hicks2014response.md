# Hicks 2014: Advice on writing an author response

- Source: `hicks2014response`, [original](http://www.pl-enthusiast.net/2014/09/17/advice-writing-author-response/)
- Version: Blog post, The PL Enthusiast, 2014-09-17.

### E1

- Location: What is author response?
- Quote: "Author response was originally designed to redress this wrong by allowing authors to point out factual inaccuracies in reviews."
- Key point: Author response (rebuttal) was originally designed to let authors point out factual inaccuracies in reviews.

### E2

- Location: The goal of your response
- Quote: "Instead, you want to convince at least one reviewer that your paper is sufficiently above the bar that he/she will argue to accept your paper."
- Key point: The goal of a response is not to convince every reviewer but to get at least one reviewer willing to argue for accepting your paper.

### E3

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "Be polite and appreciative. Even if the reviewers didn’t like your paper as much as you think they should, they volunteered to review it and gave you feedback."
- Key point: Be polite and appreciative: the reviewers volunteered their time to give you feedback.

### E4

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "Do not address every concern. The goal of finding a champion implies you shouldn’t feel the need to address every concern."
- Key point: You do not need to address every concern; space is limited, and covering everything dilutes the main points. You can state that you have considered all comments and will address them in revision.

### E5

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "The most important concerns, shared by most of the reviewers, should be addressed right away, and decisively."
- Key point: The most important concerns, shared by most reviewers, should be addressed first and decisively.

### E6

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "Reference the key concerns in a manner close to how the reviewers phrased them, so they are easy to find."
- Key point: Point to each concern in wording close to how the reviewers phrased it, so they can find it easily.

### E7

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "Remember that a reviewer probably read your paper and wrote the review a month before reading your response to the review."
- Key point: By the time a reviewer reads your response, they may have written the review a month earlier and forgotten the details.

### E8

- Location: General themes for writing the response
- Quote: "Reference your own paper for answers to reviewer concerns."
- Key point: If the answer is already in the paper, say where in the paper.

### E9

- Location: What is author response?
- Quote: "Some forms of author response try to limit it to “factual inaccuracies.” [...] As such, author response processes I’m aware of now are more open, oftentimes having suggested rather than enforced limits, with fewer stated restrictions on what can be said."
- Key point: Some forms of author response allow only "factual inaccuracies"; the response processes the author knows of are now more open, often with suggested rather than enforced word limits and fewer restrictions.

### E10

- Location: The goal of your response
- Quote: "Indeed, if most of the reviewers are in favor of rejection, you have little hope."
- Key point: If most reviewers favor rejection, a response can do little.
