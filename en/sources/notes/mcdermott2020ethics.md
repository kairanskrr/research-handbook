# McDermott & Hatemi 2020: Ethics in field experimentation: A call to establish new standards to protect the public from unwanted manipulation and real harms

- Source: `mcdermott2020ethics`, [full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7720186/)
- Version: PNAS 117(48): 30014–30021, 2020, Perspective; free full text on PubMed Central. The authors take a clear position: it is a critique of the ethics of field experiments. Locations give the paragraph.

### E1

- Location: Introduction (paragraph 1 after the abstract)
- Quote: "a review of all articles published in the preeminent political science journals from 2013 to 2017 found that almost none of the field experiments in that period reflected compliance with the current ethical requirements that govern research with human participants"
- Key point: A review the authors cite found that almost none of the field experiments published in the leading political science journals from 2013 to 2017 showed compliance with current human subjects ethics requirements.

### E2

- Location: New Risks from Large-Scale Social Science Research: Social Pressure Manipulations
- Quote: "In seeking to identify what increases or depresses voter turnout, for example, scores of studies have undertaken large-scale interventions in real elections"
- Key point: To find out what raises or depresses voter turnout, scores of studies have run large-scale interventions in real elections.

### E3

- Location: New Risks from Large-Scale Social Science Research: Social Pressure Manipulations
- Quote: "Several studies have targeted very large minority populations in such ventures, large enough to change electoral outcomes, by sending racially charged group-conflict messages and other anxiety-inducing stimuli."
- Key point: Some studies targeted minority populations large enough to change electoral outcomes, sending them racially charged, anxiety-provoking messages.

### E4

- Location: New Requirements and a New Standard: Respect for Societies
- Quote: "This proposed fourth basic principle, respect for societies, requires addressing the potential effects manipulations can have on both local and large-scale societal outcomes."
- Key point: The authors propose a fourth basic principle, "respect for societies," which requires considering the effects of an intervention on both local and wider society.

### E5

- Location: New Requirements and a New Standard: Respect for Societies
- Quote: "we suggest that, for any field experiment on a real-life society, the relevant publics should be consented and debriefed."
- Key point: The authors argue that for any field experiment on a real-life society, the relevant publics should be asked for consent and debriefed afterward.
