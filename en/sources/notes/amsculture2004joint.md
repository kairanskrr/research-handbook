# AMS 2004: The Culture of Research and Scholarship in Mathematics: Joint Research and Its Publication

- Source: `amsculture2004joint`, [original](https://www.ams.org/profession/leaders/CultureStatement04.pdf)
- Version: 2004 statement by the Committee on the Profession of the American Mathematical Society (AMS), one-page PDF, accessed 2026-10-06.

### E1

- Location: paragraph 2
- Quote: "In most areas of mathematics, joint research is a sharing of ideas and skills that cannot be attributed to the individuals separately. The roles of researchers are seldom differentiated (in the way they are in laboratory sciences, for example)."
- Key point: In most areas of mathematics, joint research is a sharing of ideas and skills that cannot be attributed to individuals separately; researchers' roles are rarely divided as they are in the experimental sciences.

### E2

- Location: paragraph 2
- Quote: "Joint work in mathematics almost always involves a small number of researchers contributing equally to a research project."
- Key point: Joint work in mathematics almost always involves a small number of researchers contributing equally.

### E3

- Location: paragraph 3
- Quote: "For this reason, mathematicians traditionally list authors on joint papers in alphabetical order."
- Key point: For this reason, mathematicians traditionally list authors of joint papers in alphabetical order.

### E4

- Location: paragraph 3
- Quote: "An analysis of journal articles with at least one U.S. based author shows that nearly half were jointly authored. Of these, more than 75% listed the authors in alphabetical order. In pure mathematics, nearly all joint papers (over 90%) list authors alphabetically."
- Key point: An analysis of journal articles with at least one US-based author: nearly half were jointly authored; of these, more than 75% listed authors alphabetically; in pure mathematics, more than 90% of joint papers did.

### E5

- Location: paragraph 4
- Quote: "In areas of mathematics that are more closely associated to such areas, the culture and traditions may blend together."
- Key point: In areas of mathematics more closely tied to fields with large collaborations, authorship cultures may blend (and need not be alphabetical).

### E6

- Location: paragraph 5
- Quote: "While these traditions are well-known to mathematicians, they are often misunderstood by other scholars whose traditions differ. Occasionally, this works against young mathematicians--especially those with names near the end of the alphabet."
- Key point: Scholars from other fields often misunderstand this tradition, which occasionally works against young mathematicians, especially those whose surnames come late in the alphabet.

### E7

- Location: paragraph 2
- Quote: "Determining which person contributed which ideas is often meaningless because the ideas grow from complex discussions among all partners."
- Key point: Determining who contributed which idea is often meaningless, because ideas grow out of discussions among all the partners.
