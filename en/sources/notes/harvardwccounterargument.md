# Harvard College Writing Center: Counterargument

- Source: `harvardwccounterargument`, [Original](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/counterargument)
- Version: Web page in the Harvard College Writing Center's Strategies for Essay Writing series, no author named; the original URL returned 403 to command-line access, so it was read via an Internet Archive snapshot from December 2023

### E1

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "You also need to consider potential counterarguments—the arguments that your readers could reasonably raise to challenge either your thesis or any of the other claims that you make in your argument."
- Key point: Consider counterarguments: arguments your readers could reasonably raise to challenge your thesis or any of your other claims.

### E2

- Location: The list of problems a counterargument might point out
- Quote: "an alternative interpretation of the evidence"
- Key point: One kind of counterargument is an alternative interpretation of the same evidence.

### E3

- Location: Paragraph 4
- Quote: "If you come up with a counterargument that you can’t refute, then you may decide to revise your thesis or some part of your argument."
- Key point: A counterargument you can't refute may mean you need to revise your thesis or part of your argument.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph 4
- Quote: "In many cases, you will discover that a counterargument complicates your argument, but doesn’t refute it entirely."
- Key point: Often a counterargument complicates your argument without refuting it entirely.

### E5

- Location: How to address counterarguments
- Quote: "You may concede that the counterargument gets something right but then explain why it does not undermine your argument."
- Key point: You can concede that the counterargument gets something right and then explain why it does not undermine your argument.

### E6

- Location: After the Example
- Quote: "But you don’t need to address every possible counterargument."
- Key point: You don't need to address every possible counterargument.

### E7

- Location: Where to address counterarguments, Before your thesis
- Quote: "Often, your thesis will actually be a counterargument to someone else’s argument."
- Key point: Often your thesis is itself a counterargument to someone else's argument.

### E8

- Location: Where to address counterarguments, Before your conclusion
- Quote: "Don’t put a counterargument in your conclusion, however."
- Key point: Don't put a counterargument in your conclusion.
