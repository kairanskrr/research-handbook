# Vardi 2009: Conferences vs. Journals in Computing Research

- Source: `vardi2009conferences`, [original](https://cacm.acm.org/opinion/conferences-vs-journals-in-computing-research/)
- Version: Communications of the ACM 52(5), 2009, editor's letter. The original site refuses script access, so it was read via an Internet Archive snapshot from 2025-09-09.

### E1

- Location: Main text, paragraph 2
- Quote: "As far as I know, we are the only scientific community that considers conference publication as the primary means of publishing our research results."
- Key point: Computing may be the only scientific community that treats conferences as its primary publication venue.

### E2

- Location: Main text, paragraph 6
- Quote: "The reviewing process performed by program committees is done under extreme time and workload pressures, and it does not rise to the level of careful refereeing."
- Key point: Program committee reviewing is done under extreme time and workload pressure and does not rise to the level of careful refereeing.

### E3

- Location: Main text, paragraph 6
- Quote: "There is some expectation that conference papers will be followed up by journal papers, where careful refereeing will ultimately take place. In truth, only a small fraction of conference papers are followed up by journal papers."
- Key point: Conference papers are expected to be followed by journal versions with careful refereeing, but in fact only a small fraction are.

### E4

- Location: Main text, paragraph 7
- Quote: "physicists ensure fast dissemination by depositing preprints at www.arxiv.org and by having a very fast review cycle."
- Key point: Fast dissemination does not require conferences: physicists achieve it by posting preprints on arXiv and having a very fast review cycle.
