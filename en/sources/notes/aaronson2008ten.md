# Aaronson 2008: Ten Signs a Claimed Mathematical Breakthrough is Wrong

- Source: `aaronson2008ten`, [original](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=304)
- Version: post on Shtetl-Optimized, the blog of theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson, January 2008, accessed 2026-10-06. Locations give item numbers.

### E1

- Location: item 2
- Quote: "2. The authors don’t understand the question."
- Key point: One sign of a mistaken "breakthrough": the authors don't understand the question (for example, they read a statement about complexity classes as something else).

### E2

- Location: item 3
- Quote: "3. The approach seems to yield something much stronger and maybe even false (but the authors never discuss that). They’ve proved 3SAT takes exponential time; their argument would go through just as well for 2SAT."
- Key point: The approach seems to yield something much stronger, maybe even false, and the authors never discuss it; for example, an argument "proving" that 3SAT takes exponential time would apply equally to 2SAT (which has a polynomial-time algorithm).

### E3

- Location: item 4
- Quote: "4. The approach conflicts with a known impossibility result (which the authors never mention)."
- Key point: The approach conflicts with a known impossibility result that the authors never mention.

### E4

- Location: item 5
- Quote: "5. The authors themselves switch to weasel words by the end."
- Key point: The abstract says "we prove," but by the end the authors switch to weasel words such as "appears to work" or "in all the cases we tried."

### E5

- Location: item 7
- Quote: "7. The paper doesn’t build on (or in some cases even refer to) any previous work. Math is cumulative."
- Key point: The paper doesn't build on (or even cite) any previous work; math is cumulative.

### E6

- Location: conclusion
- Quote: "If a paper fails one or more tests (particularly tests 6-10), that doesn’t necessarily mean it’s wrong; conversely, if it passes all ten that still doesn’t mean it’s right."
- Key point: These are only heuristic signs: failing one doesn't necessarily mean a paper is wrong, and passing all of them doesn't mean it is right.
