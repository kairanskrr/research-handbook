# Modern Language Association 2007: Report of the MLA Task Force on Evaluating Scholarship for Tenure and Promotion

- Source: `mla2007evaluating`, [original](https://www.mla.org/content/download/3362/file/taskforcereport0608.pdf)
- Version: completed December 2006, published in Profession 2007; Modern Language Association (MLA). Words hyphenated across line breaks in the original text have been rejoined.

### E1

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "spring 2005 online survey of 1,339 departments in 734 institutions across the United States"
- Key point: Survey population: 1,339 English and foreign language departments at 734 US institutions, spring 2005.

### E2

- Location: Survey Methodology
- Quote: "While these departments and institutions do not constitute a sample representative of all four-year United States colleges and universities"
- Key point: The responding departments are not a representative sample of all four-year US colleges and universities.

### E3

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Indeed, 88.9% of departments in Carnegie Doctorategranting, 44.4% in Carnegie Master's, and 48% in Carnegie Baccalaureate institutions now rank publication of a monograph "very important" or "important" for tenure."
- Key point: 88.9% of responding departments at doctorate-granting institutions, 44.4% at master's institutions, and 48% at baccalaureate institutions rank publication of a monograph as "very important" or "important" for tenure. ("Doctorategranting" in the quote lost its hyphen when the PDF was converted to text.)

### E4

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "This expectation is even higher in doctorate-granting institutions, where 49.8% of departments now demand progress toward a second book."
- Key point: At doctorate-granting institutions, 49.8% of departments demand progress toward a second book.

### E5

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "only 1.6% of responding departments rated refereed journal articles "not important" in tenure and promotion decisions."
- Key point: Only 1.6% of responding departments rated refereed journal articles "not important."

### E6

- Location: §4
- Quote: "Scholarly journals have an advantage that books--with their longer lead time to publication--lack: they provide a forum for new ideas and research and for a timely exchange of argument."
- Key point: Journals have a shorter lead time to publication than books, so they allow a timely exchange of ideas.

### E7

- Location: §5 Evaluating Collaboration
- Quote: "is deeply embedded in the practices of humanities scholarship, including the processes of evaluation for tenure and promotion."
- Key point: Solo-authored ("one person, one work") scholarship is deeply embedded in the practices and evaluation of humanities scholarship.

### E8

- Location: §4
- Quote: "In our view, a body of essays or articles in peer-reviewed journals can demonstrate the quality of scholarly work as well as or, in some cases, better than a monograph of similar length."
- Key point: The task force holds that a body of peer-reviewed articles can demonstrate the quality of scholarly work as well as, or sometimes better than, a monograph.

### E9

- Location: §6
- Quote: "The monograph as the gold standard for tenure dossiers is a relatively recent development"
- Key point: The monograph as the "gold standard" for tenure is a relatively recent development.

### E10

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "scholarly editions by 20% of departments"
- Key point: 20% of responding departments rated scholarly editions "not important" for tenure.

### E11

- Location: §1
- Quote: "the expectation that the dissertation would be published after revision made it easier for departments to demand a monograph of tenure candidates."
- Key point: The expectation that the dissertation would be published after revision made it easier for departments to demand a monograph from tenure candidates.
