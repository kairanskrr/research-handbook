# Graziotin et al. 2021: Psychometrics in Behavioral Software Engineering: A Methodological Introduction with Guidelines

- Source: `graziotin2021psychometrics`, [arXiv:2005.09959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09959)
- Version: arXiv v4 (2021-06-08); the published version appeared in ACM TOSEM 31(1), 2021

### E1

- Location: §1.1 Misconceptions with psychological tests in software engineering research
- Quote: "when a validated test from psychology is adopted by software engineering researchers, its items are often modified, causing the destruction of its psychometric reliability and validity properties."
- Key point: When software engineering researchers adopt a validated test from psychology, they often modify its items, destroying its psychometric reliability and validity.

### E2

- Location: §1.1 Misconceptions with psychological tests in software engineering research
- Quote: "we used a single Likert item scale to represent productivity." [...] "the productivity scale itself was not validated, making the results less valuable from a psychometric perspective"
- Key point: The authors criticize their own earlier work: they measured productivity with a single Likert item, an unvalidated scale, which made the results less valuable.

### E3

- Location: §6.0.1 Item facility
- Quote: "Item facility for Likert items can be calculated with the mean value of the item."
- Key point: In psychometric item analysis, item facility for a Likert item is calculated as the item's mean.

### E4

- Location: §13 Limitations of Psychometrics
- Quote: "It might truly take years to developing and validating a measurement instrument. We highly recommend searching the literature for existing, psychometrically validated tests before developing one."
- Key point: Developing and validating a measurement instrument can take years; before building one, search the literature for existing validated tests.

### E5

- Location: §3 Pool items (end)
- Quote: "a better strategy would be to develop six to ten items per envisioned factor, or sub-construct."
- Key point: The authors recommend writing six to ten items per envisioned factor (sub-construct).

### E6

- Location: §5 Pilot test
- Quote: "All results confirm the indicated value of n = 30 as reasonable minimum recommendation for a pilot study for a psychological test."
- Key point: The authors cite Johanson and Brooks: 30 participants is a reasonable minimum for a pilot study of a psychological test.

### E7

- Location: §7 Factor analysis
- Quote: "EFA attempts to uncover patterns and clusters of items by exploring predictions, while CFA attempts to confirm hypotheses on existing factors"
- Key point: Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) looks for patterns and clusters of items; confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) tests hypotheses about a factor structure specified in advance.

### E8

- Location: §7.2 Confirmatory Factor Analysis
- Quote: "it is common to split the sample for conducting EFA on a part of it and CFA on the remaining part" / "Most authors, however, prefer conducting EFA only and rely on future independent studies towards a better psychometrics evaluation of a tool."
- Key point: With a large enough sample, it is common to split it, running EFA on one part and CFA on the other; but most authors run only EFA and leave further evaluation to future independent studies.

### E9

- Location: §7.2 Confirmatory Factor Analysis
- Quote: "the estimation of the goodness-of-fit in CFA is a long lasting debate"
- Key point: How to judge goodness of fit in CFA is a long-running debate.

### E10

- Location: §9 Reliability
- Quote: "Several factors, as defined in the SEPT [3], affect the reliability of a measurement instrument, especially adding or removing items, changing wording or intervals of items"
- Key point: According to the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing, adding or removing items and changing their wording or response intervals all affect a scale's reliability.

### E11

- Location: §10 Validity
- Quote: "The degree to which evidence and theory support the interpretation of test scores for proposed uses of tests."
- Key point: The Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing define validity as the degree to which evidence and theory support the interpretation of test scores for their proposed uses.

### E12

- Location: §11 Fairness in testing and test bias
- Quote: "A test is fair when it reflects the same constructs for all participants, and its scores have the same meaning for all individuals of the population"
- Key point: A test is fair when it reflects the same construct for all participants and its scores mean the same thing for everyone in the population.

### E13

- Location: §11.1 Item bias
- Quote: "A presence of DIF does not necessarily indicate bias but unexpected behavior on an item"
- Key point: Differential item functioning (DIF) does not necessarily mean bias; it signals unexpected behavior of an item across groups, whose cause needs investigating.
