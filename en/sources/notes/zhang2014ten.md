# Zhang 2014: Ten Simple Rules for Writing Research Papers

- Source: `zhang2014ten`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003453)
- Version: PLOS Computational Biology 10(1): e1003453, open access. Page numbers are PDF page numbers.

### E1

> Withdrawn: duplicate of [E8](#e8) (its quote is fully contained in E8).

- Location: Rule 1, p. 1
- Quote: "Never separate writing a paper from the underlying research."
- Key point: Never separate writing the paper from doing the research; bring a paper outline into the project design.

- Location: Rule 1, p. 1
- Quote: "use writing as a tool to reassess the overall project, reevaluate the logic of the experiments, and examine the validity of the results during the research."
- Key point: During the research, use writing to reassess the project, the logic of the experiments, and the validity of the results.

### E3

- Location: Rule 2, p. 1
- Quote: "By this metric, less is more"
- Key point: Measured by a paper's potential impact on the field, fewer important papers beat many unimportant ones: less is more.

### E4

- Location: Rule 8, p. 2
- Quote: "do not exaggerate or belittle the significance of the results and the elegance of the methods developed."
- Key point: When revising, be objective and honest about your own work, neither exaggerating nor belittling it.

### E5

- Location: Rule 8, p. 2
- Quote: "put yourself completely in the shoes of a referee and scrutinize all the pieces"
- Key point: When revising, put yourself completely in the referee's shoes and check significance, logic, correctness of results and conclusions, and organization and presentation. Set the draft aside for a day or two, then reread it as if someone else wrote it and look for flaws.

### E6

- Location: Rule 9, p. 2
- Quote: "It is wise to anticipate the possible questions and critiques the referees may raise and preemptively address their concerns before submission."
- Key point: Before submitting, test the paper close to home: ask colleagues and collaborators for comments and present it at group meetings or department talks, addressing likely reviewer questions in advance.

### E7

- Location: Rule 3, p. 1
- Quote: "This issue translates to choosing a target audience, as well as an appropriate journal, to cast the main messages to."
- Key point: Before writing, choose the target audience (and the matching journal); it determines how the paper is organized and how much detail it gives.

### E8

- Location: Rule 1: Make It a Driving Force
- Quote: "Never separate writing a paper from the underlying research. [...] Therefore, design a project with an ultimate paper firmly in mind. Include an outline of the paper in the initial project design documents to help form the research objectives, determine the logical flow of the experiments, and organize the materials and data to be used."
- Key point: Design the project with the final paper firmly in mind; include a paper outline in the initial project design documents and use it to set the research aims, the logical order of experiments, and the materials and data to prepare.

### E9

- Location: Rule 1: Make It a Driving Force
- Quote: "Furthermore, use writing as a tool to reassess the overall project, reevaluate the logic of the experiments, and examine the validity of the results during the research. As a result, the overall research may need to be adjusted, the project design may be revised, new methods may be devised, and new data may be collected."
- Key point: During the research, use writing to reassess the whole project; this may lead to adjusting the research, revising the project design, developing new methods, or collecting new data. The plan should change accordingly.

### E10

- Location: Rule 4: Be Logical
- Quote: "An effective tactic to help develop a sound logical flow is to imaginatively create a set of figures and tables, which will ultimately be developed from experimental results, and order them in a logical way based on the information flow through the experiments."
- Key point: First imagine the figures and tables the paper will contain and order them by the flow of information between experiments, to work out a sound logic.

### E11

- Location: Rule 4: Be Logical
- Quote: "In addition, these figures and tables, as well as the threading logical flow, may be used to direct or organize research activities, reinforcing Rule 1."
- Key point: These imagined figures and tables, and the logic running through them, can be used directly to direct and organize the research work.

### E12

- Location: Rule 5: Be Thorough and Make It Complete
- Quote: "Completeness is a cornerstone for a research paper [...] First, important and relevant aspects of a hypothesis pursued in the research should be discussed with detailed supporting data."
- Key point: Completeness is a cornerstone of a paper: every important aspect of the hypothesis studied needs detailed supporting data. This can serve as a test of whether the work is done.
