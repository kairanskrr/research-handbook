# Harvard College Writing Center: How to Do a Close Reading

- Source: `kain1998closereading`, [original](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/pages/how-do-close-reading)
- Version: By Patricia Kain, copyright 1998, a Harvard College Writing Center web page; the original URL has been redesigned and returns 403 to command-line access, so it was read from an Internet Archive snapshot of December 2021

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "When you close read, you observe facts and details about the text."
- Key point: The first step of close reading is to observe facts and details about the text.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "The second step is interpreting your observations." / "moving from the observation of particular facts and details to a conclusion, or interpretation, based on those observations."
- Key point: The second step is interpreting your observations: moving from particular facts and details to a conclusion, or interpretation, based on them.

### E3

- Location: How to Begin, 1
- Quote: "Read with a pencil in hand, and annotate the text."
- Key point: Read with a pencil in hand and annotate the text.

### E4

- Location: How to Begin, 1
- Quote: "underlining or highlighting key words and phrases—anything that strikes you as surprising or significant, or that raises questions—as well as making notes in the margins."
- Key point: Annotating means underlining or highlighting key words and phrases (anything that strikes you as surprising or significant, or that raises questions) and making notes in the margins.

### E5

- Location: How to Begin, 2
- Quote: "Look for patterns in the things you've noticed about the text—repetitions, contradictions, similarities."
- Key point: Look for patterns in what you have noticed: repetitions, contradictions, similarities.

### E6

- Location: How to Begin, 3
- Quote: "Ask questions about the patterns you've noticed—especially how and why."
- Key point: Ask questions about the patterns you notice, especially how and why.

### E7

- Location: How to Begin, 3
- Quote: "We need more evidence, so we go back to the text—the whole essay now, not just this one passage—and look for additional clues."
- Key point: An initial interpretation of one passage needs more evidence, so go back to the whole text and look for additional clues.
