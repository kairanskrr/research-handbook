# Mays & Pope 2000: Qualitative research in health care: Assessing quality in qualitative research

- Source: `mays2000assessing`, [full text on PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1117321/)
- Version: BMJ 320(7226): 50–52, 2000. Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Summary points
- Quote: "Qualitative methods are now widely used and increasingly accepted in health research, but quality in qualitative research is a mystery to many health services researchers"
- Key point: (As of 2000) qualitative methods were widely used in health research, but many health services researchers did not know how to judge the quality of qualitative research.

### E2

- Location: Two opposing views
- Quote: "There has been considerable debate over whether qualitative and quantitative methods can and should be assessed according to the same quality criteria."
- Key point: There has been considerable debate over whether qualitative and quantitative research can and should be assessed by the same quality criteria.

### E3

- Location: Separate and different: the antirealist position
- Quote: "Advocates of the antirealist position argue that qualitative research represents a distinctive paradigm and as such it cannot and should not be judged by conventional measures of validity, generalisability, and reliability."
- Key point: One camp (the antirealist position) argues that qualitative research is a distinctive paradigm and cannot and should not be judged by conventional criteria such as validity, generalizability, and reliability.

### E4

- Location: Summary points
- Quote: "Quality in qualitative research can be assessed with the same broad concepts of validity and relevance used for quantitative research, but these need to be operationalised differently to take into account the distinctive goals of qualitative research"
- Key point: The authors' position: qualitative research can be assessed with the same broad concepts of validity and relevance used for quantitative research, but how they are applied must be adapted to the goals of qualitative research.

### E5

- Location: Triangulation
- Quote: "Triangulation compares the results from either two or more different methods of data collection (for example, interviews and observation) or, more simply, two or more data sources"
- Key point: Triangulation compares the results from two or more methods of data collection (e.g., interviews and observation) or two or more data sources.

### E6

- Location: Reflexivity
- Quote: "Reflexivity means sensitivity to the ways in which the researcher and the research process have shaped the collected data, including the role of prior assumptions and experience"
- Key point: Reflexivity means being sensitive to how the researcher and the research process have shaped the collected data, including the researcher's prior assumptions and experience.

### E7

- Location: Reflexivity
- Quote: "The effects of personal characteristics such as age, sex, social class, and professional status (doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, sociologist, etc) on the data collected and on the “distance” between the researcher and those researched also needs to be discussed."
- Key point: Discuss how personal characteristics such as age, sex, social class, and professional status (doctor, nurse, physiotherapist, etc.) affect the data and the "distance" between researcher and researched.

### E8

- Location: Attention to negative cases
- Quote: "a long established tactic for improving the quality of explanations in qualitative research is to search for, and discuss, elements in the data that contradict, or seem to contradict, the emerging explanation"
- Key point: A long-established tactic for improving explanations in qualitative research is to search for and discuss data that contradict the emerging explanation.

### E9

- Location: Respondent validation
- Quote: "it is better to think of respondent validation as part of a process of error reduction which also generates further original data, which in turn requires interpretation."
- Key point: Respondent validation (asking participants to check the researcher's interpretation) is best seen as part of error reduction that also generates new data needing interpretation, not as a final test.

### E10

- Location: Assessing the validity of qualitative research
- Quote: "There are no mechanical or “easy” solutions to limit the likelihood that there will be errors in qualitative research."
- Key point: There are no mechanical or "easy" solutions to guard against errors in qualitative research.

### E11

- Location: Clear exposition of methods of data collection and analysis
- Quote: "the written account should include sufficient data to allow the reader to judge whether the interpretation proffered is adequately supported by the data."
- Key point: The written account should include enough data for readers to judge whether the interpretation is adequately supported.
