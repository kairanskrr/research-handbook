# Kool, Andersson, Giller 2020: Reproducibility and external validity of on-farm experimental research in Africa

- Source: `kool2020reproducibility`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0014479720000174)
- Version: Experimental Agriculture 56: 587–607, 2020, CC BY open access. The authors searched for papers on on-farm experiments in sub-Saharan Africa published 1986–2015 and analyzed 172 in detail. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Material and Methods
- Quote: "We selected a timespan from 1986 to 2015 [...] This resulted in 172 papers which were used in our detailed analyses"
- Key point: The authors searched the literature from 1986 to 2015 and, after screening, analyzed 172 papers on on-farm field experiments in sub-Saharan Africa in detail.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "it is often difficult or impossible to reproduce the experimental design. Nutrient management, weed management and crop information are best described, whereas land preparation, field history and management of pests and water are rarely described."
- Key point: From the information in the papers, it is often difficult or impossible to reproduce the experimental design. Nutrient management, weed management, and crop information are best described; land preparation, field history, and pest and water management are rarely described.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "on-farm experimental studies often compare treatments to a ‘farmer practice’ reference or control treatment which is assumed to be widely and uniformly practiced and known to the reader."
- Key point: On-farm experiments often use "farmer practice" as the control, assuming it is uniform everywhere and known to the reader.

### E4

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Most do not explicitly define the research population and/or environment in which (they expect) the experimental findings to work."
- Key point: Most of the analyzed studies do not explicitly say for which farmers and environments their findings are expected to hold.

### E5

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Research stations represent a highly controlled experimental environment, where single or multiple treatments and their interactions can be investigated holding other variables constant. In farmers’ fields, researchers have much less control over the experimental situation, which is embedded in a much wider and more variable bio-physical and socio-economic environment."
- Key point: Research stations are highly controlled environments where other variables can be held constant while studying treatments; in farmers' fields, researchers control much less, and experiments sit within a larger, more variable biophysical and socioeconomic environment.

### E6

- Location: Material and Methods, Analysis of the number of experimental locations
- Quote: "Although studies are often multi-seasonal (generally covering 2 to 3 seasons)"
- Key point: The analyzed on-farm experiments often span several seasons, generally 2 to 3.

### E7

- Location: Conclusion
- Quote: "we propose that experimental studies should minimally address: (1) under what conditions the treatment effect occurs and (2) for whom the experimental findings are relevant. In addition, we advocate for more transparency about the selection criteria used for research site, farmer and field selection and a reflection on the possible implications of such selection for external validity."
- Key point: The authors propose that experimental studies should at minimum address under what conditions the treatment effect occurs and for whom the findings are relevant, be transparent about how sites, farmers, and fields were selected, and consider how that selection affects generalization.

### E8

- Location: Results, reproducibility
- Quote: "A number of studies give rather unclear and vague descriptions of experimental practices."
- Key point: Many studies describe field practices vaguely (e.g., only "weeded when necessary").

### E9

- Location: Results, Crop information
- Quote: "The category ‘crop information’ reflects the yield-defining factors, with the variables ‘varieties’, ‘planting date’, ‘plant spacing’ and ‘mono-/intercrop, crop rotation’. [...] Only 10% of the studies described all four factors. Most studies provide information on the plant spacing (76%) and the variety used (69%) but fewer mention the planting date (41%)."
- Key point: Only 10% of studies described all four crop information factors (variety, planting date, plant spacing, and mono-/intercropping and rotation); 76% gave plant spacing, 69% variety, and only 41% planting date.

### E10

- Location: Results, descriptions of farmer practice
- Quote: "In none of these examples do the authors provide details on how this was done. Such uninformative descriptions provide insufficient information to make the experiments reproducible."
- Key point: Descriptions such as "managed according to farmer practice" do not say how things were done and give too little information to reproduce the experiment.

### E11

- Location: Discussion, external validity
- Quote: "Interestingly, Franke et al. (2010) found that farmers copied management methods from some of the experimental treatments, and sometimes competed with the researcher-managed plots."
- Key point: One study found that farmers copied management methods from some experimental treatments and sometimes "competed" with the researcher-managed plots.

### E12

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "we propose that experimental studies should minimally address: (1) under what conditions the treatment effect occurs and (2) for whom the experimental findings are relevant. In addition, we advocate for more transparency about the selection criteria used for research site, farmer and field selection"
- Key point: Experimental studies should at minimum address under what conditions the treatment effect occurs and for whom the findings are relevant, and be transparent about how sites, farmers, and fields were selected.

### E13

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Research stations represent a highly controlled experimental environment, where single or multiple treatments and their interactions can be investigated holding other variables constant. In farmers’ fields, researchers have much less control over the experimental situation"
- Key point: Research stations are highly controlled environments; in farmers' fields, researchers have much less control.
