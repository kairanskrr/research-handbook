# Bulley & Şahin 2021: Practice Research (Report 1: What is practice research? / Report 2: How can practice research be shared?)

- Source: `bulley2021practice`, [Original PDF](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/30222/1/2021_Bulley-Sahin_Practice-Research-reports.pdf), DOI: 10.23636/1347
- Version: April 1, 2021, published by the Practice Research Advisory Group UK (PRAG-UK), funded by Research England, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Based on interviews, questionnaires, and surveys in 2019-2021 with 62 practice researchers, theorists, research support professionals, and policymakers in England. Locations are given as paragraph numbers.

### E1

- Location: "What methods did we use?", p. 3
- Quote: "Practice research is when practice is the significant method conveyed in a research output."
- Key point: Practice research is when practice is the significant method conveyed in a research output.

### E2

- Location: Paragraph 1.1.1.3
- Quote: "Excellent practice may not necessarily translate into excellent practice research, and vice versa.11 For practice to be practice research the research has to be articulated."
- Key point: Excellent practice is not necessarily excellent practice research, and vice versa; for practice to be practice research, the research has to be articulated.

### E3

- Location: Paragraph 1.1.1.3, footnote 11 (quoting Knudsen 2018)
- Quote: "[d]oing good research is not about making successful works: it is about succeeding in generating new insights."
- Key point: Doing good research is not about making successful works but about generating new insights.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph 1.2.2.6
- Quote: "For the vast majority of practice research outputs, it is not possible for research audiences to experience the practice first hand, and as a result ‘documentation’ is employed as a proxy for practice."
- Key point: For the vast majority of practice research outputs, audiences cannot experience the practice first hand, so "documentation" serves as a proxy for it.

### E5

- Location: Paragraph 1.2.2.1
- Quote: "Our findings indicate that in most cases, an effective means to share ways of knowing in practice research is by joining a research narrative to a practice."
- Key point: Their finding: in most cases, joining a research narrative to a practice is an effective way to share ways of knowing in practice research.

### E6

- Location: Paragraph 1.2.2.10
- Quote: "Practice researchers should not feel a requirement to present their research in non-linear, cross-media and complex item types: in some instances a journal article or book chapter may be the most appropriate avenue for a research narrative."
- Key point: Practice researchers need not feel obliged to use non-linear, cross-media, complex formats; sometimes a journal article or book chapter is the best place for the research narrative.

### E7

- Location: Paragraph 1.4.1.6
- Quote: "The different components of these practice research outputs (commonly involving a research narrative alongside documentation of practice) were usually dispersed across multiple locations, including commercial video and sound hosting platforms, research repositories, blogs, and personal web pages."
- Key point: Interviewees reported that the components of practice research outputs were usually scattered across commercial video and audio platforms, institutional repositories, blogs, personal websites, and other places.

### E8

- Location: "What methods did we use?", p. 3; "Scope"
- Quote: "sixty two contemporary practice researchers, theorists, research support professionals and policymakers [...] we have limited our scope to the boundaries of England."
- Key point: The report draws on 62 practice researchers, theorists, research support professionals, and policymakers, and is limited to England.

### E9

- Location: Paragraph 1.5.4.2
- Quote: "care has to be taken to provide adequate descriptive texts for multimedia content and transcriptions should be provided where possible."
- Key point: Provide adequate descriptive text for multimedia content and transcripts where possible, for readers with disabilities.

### E10

- Location: Paragraph 1.5.4.4
- Quote: "Practice research often makes use of multimedia documentation which can have particularly complex copyright and licensing implications."
- Key point: Practice research often uses multimedia documentation, which raises particularly complex copyright and licensing issues.

### E11

- Location: Paragraph 1.5.4.7
- Quote: "it is sometimes the case that practice researchers do not consider the ethical implications of human participants in practice research projects."
- Key point: Practice researchers sometimes do not consider the ethics of involving human participants in their projects.

### E12

- Location: Paragraph 1.5.4.8
- Quote: "ensuring that where people have been involved in the production of these things, that they are cited and they are mentioned."
- Key point: Everyone involved in producing the work should be cited and mentioned.

### E13

- Location: Paragraph 1.4.1.5
- Quote: "there are substantial challenges in sharing practice publicly due to ethical concerns (for example in nursing, or in projects exploring loss and bereavement)"
- Key point: Ethical concerns make sharing some practice publicly very difficult (for example, projects in nursing or on loss and bereavement).

### E14

- Location: Paragraph 1.2.2.7, footnote 144
- Quote: "See the use of ‘exposition’ in the Research Catalogue, a repository of research attached to JAR (Journal for Artistic Research)"
- Key point: The Research Catalogue is a research repository attached to the Journal for Artistic Research (JAR) that publishes work as "expositions."

### E15

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.1.5
- Quote: "For artist researcher Carole Gray, problem formation for practitioners does not necessarily take place in a neat or predictable way, but emerges over time according to the needs of the [...] practice and the evolving sense of what a project is. That is not to say that the fundamental research questions necessarily change, but more that the final problems are not decided until the practice-led project is completed."
- Key point: For artist researcher Carole Gray, practitioners' problems do not necessarily form in a neat, predictable way but emerge over time with the needs of the practice and the evolving sense of the project; the basic research question need not change, but the final question is settled only when the project is complete.

### E16

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.3.2.3
- Quote: "One of the other challenges that commonly faces practice research PhD students, as highlighted by Redhead, is that the locus of inquiry or use of specific methods may shift as a project is underway."
- Key point: A common challenge for practice research PhD students is that the focus of inquiry or the methods used may shift while the project is underway.

### E17

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.2.9
- Quote: "As Corby observes when explaining the process of institutional assessment for practice research outputs: “We can start guessing but it’s not our job to guess. It’s the job of the researcher to articulate what is the innovation.”"
- Key point: Corby, describing institutional assessment of practice research outputs: assessors could start guessing, but guessing is not their job; it is the researcher's job to articulate where the innovation lies.

### E18

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.2.13
- Quote: "Unless relevant to describing the practice as method, there is no need for additional philosophical justification of practice as method, just as there is no need to justify the existence of qualitative or quantitative research techniques in other areas of research."
- Key point: Unless relevant to describing the method, practice as method needs no extra philosophical justification, just as other fields need not justify the existence of qualitative or quantitative methods.

### E19

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.2.13
- Quote: "with journal editors, PhD supervisors and research assessors observing the excessive length of time taken by practice researchers to get to the discussion of the research itself, prefacing their work with long philosophical and ontological explanations of how practice is the significant method within the research output."
- Key point: Journal editors, PhD supervisors, and assessors observe that practice researchers often preface their work with long philosophical and ontological explanations and take too long to reach the research itself.

### E20

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.2.14
- Quote: "“I think the simpler the language, the more powerful the proposition. What you don’t want to do is explain a little idea in big words. You want to explain a big idea in little words.”"
- Key point: Brown: the simpler the language, the more powerful the proposition; explain a big idea in little words, not a little idea in big words.

### E21

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.1.3
- Quote: "‐ (Summary / Abstract) ‐ Context / Introduction ‐ Research inquiry / Questions in practice ‐ Method statements ‐ Practice, description of practice, or documentation of practice (as a proxy) ‐ Insights / Discussion of ways of knowing ‐ Conclusion and references"
- Key point: PRAG-UK's example structure for a practice research output: (summary/abstract), context/introduction, research inquiry/questions in practice, method statements, the practice itself or a description or documentation of it, insights/discussion of ways of knowing, and conclusion and references.

### E22

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.1.2
- Quote: "it is useful to include an example of how a practice research output may be structured in cases where a research [...] narrative is not embodied in the practice."
- Key point: The example structure is meant for cases where the research narrative is not embodied in the practice itself.

### E23

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.1.5
- Quote: "does a non-linear presentation form cause accessibility issues for certain audiences? Will a non-linear presentation cause problems for long-term preservation? Does a non-linear presentation affect the ability of the research to be interoperable and discoverable?"
- Key point: Before choosing a non-linear presentation, ask: will it cause accessibility problems for some audiences? Will it hinder long-term preservation? Will it make the research harder to discover and less interoperable?

### E24

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.1
- Quote: "As theorist and researcher Michael Biggs summarises, practice “isn’t turned into research by being documented, it doesn’t have to be documented; but it’s efficient to document it, if possible.”"
- Key point: Biggs: practice is not turned into research by being documented, and it does not have to be documented, but documenting it is efficient if possible.

### E25

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.3
- Quote: "it is important to differentiate between an overall practice research output and documentation as a proxy for practice within a research output."
- Key point: Distinguish the overall practice research output from documentation that serves within it as a proxy for the practice.

### E26

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.4
- Quote: "“I tell my students to always look at when they get to a point in their PhD research where they need to capture and start owning things, particularly if they’re making things that have potential copyright.”"
- Key point: Jane Grant tells students to watch for the point in their PhD research when they need to capture things and start establishing ownership, especially if what they make may involve copyright.

### E27

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.5
- Quote: "Among the vast array of tools used by practice researchers to create documentation as a proxy for their practice, the most common we heard reference to were: film and digital cameras, notebooks, file sharing systems, sound recorders, post-production software, and code repositories."
- Key point: The documentation tools interviewees mentioned most: film and digital cameras, notebooks, file sharing systems, sound recorders, post-production software, and code repositories.

### E28

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.6
- Quote: "One general way forward with this issue is to create representative excerpts of practice (such as pages from musical scores or excerpts from films), that can then be shared under an appropriate Creative Commons license."
- Key point: When full documentation cannot be made public for copyright, licensing, or financial reasons, one option is to create representative excerpts (such as a few pages of a score or film clips) and share them under a suitable Creative Commons license.

### E29

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.7
- Quote: "One of the most common pieces of feedback we had from our interviewees regarding receiving practice research as an audience, was the need for documentation (as a proxy for practice) to be of reasonable format and length."
- Key point: Among the most common feedback from interviewees as audiences: documentation should be of reasonable format and length.

### E30

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.2.8
- Quote: "Where it is not possible for documentation to be employed as a proxy for practice (due to financial constraints, conceptual issues or licensing issues for example), a description of practice can function to elucidate the practice as part of a research narrative."
- Key point: Where documentation cannot serve as a proxy for practice (for financial, conceptual, or licensing reasons), a written description of the practice within the research narrative can do the job.

### E31

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.3.6
- Quote: "Jane Boggan, Panel Adviser for Main Panel D for REF 2014 and REF 2021, states: “Where does this piece of work fit? If you’re going to make the case that this is a new insight, unless you’ve got a sense of where this sits in the context of other work in that field, it’s very difficult to say whether it’s new or not.”"
- Key point: Boggan, panel adviser for the arts and humanities panel of the Research Excellence Framework (REF): unless you know where a piece of work sits among other work in the field, it is hard to argue that it is a new insight.

### E32

- Location: Report 2, paragraph 2.1.1.7
- Quote: "Vital resources that can be drawn upon as reference for those seeking structural guidance of this kind are the British Library’s EThOS service, Open Access Theses and Dissertations (OATD), Networked Digital Library of Theses and Dissertations, and the DART-Europe E-Theses Portal, all of which provide examples of hundreds of practice research PhDs completed over the last twenty years."
- Key point: For structural models of practice research PhD theses, consult thesis repositories such as the British Library's EThOS, OATD, NDLTD, and DART-Europe, which hold hundreds of practice research PhDs completed over the past twenty years.

### E33

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.5.4.7
- Quote: "As Chow notes, it is sometimes the case that practice researchers do not consider the ethical implications of human participants in practice research projects. Chow has found in undertaking his own practice research projects that training in methods from social sciences and qualitative research has been valuable"
- Key point: Practice researchers sometimes do not consider the ethics of human participants; Chow found training in social science and qualitative research methods very helpful.

### E34

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.5.4.10
- Quote: "Areas which merit consideration include, but are not limited to, working with vulnerable groups, working with sensitive themes such as loss and trauma, experiments involving human and non-human subjects, and physical and mental health considerations."
- Key point: Ethical areas worth considering include working with vulnerable groups, sensitive themes such as loss and trauma, experiments involving human and non-human subjects, and physical and mental health.

### E35

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.5.4.10
- Quote: "In forming the guidance and standards for ethical practice research, there is much to be learnt from the protocols already established in other research fields, particularly the social sciences"
- Key point: In developing ethical standards for practice research, there is much to learn from protocols established in other fields, especially the social sciences.

### E36

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.5.4.5
- Quote: "As professional practice is often funded outside of the institution, its dissemination within practice research outputs can cause adverse economic and intellectual property issues for practice researchers."
- Key point: Professional practice is often funded from outside the institution, so including it in public practice research outputs can cause economic and intellectual property problems for the researcher.

### E37

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.2.5
- Quote: "From our interviewees we found that dependent on the audience and the framing of the practice, practice can be practice research, or practice."
- Key point: Interviewees' view: depending on the audience and how it is framed, the same practice can be practice research or just practice.

### E38

- Location: Report 1, paragraph 1.2.1.5, under the subheading Practice-Based Research
- Quote: "Candy’s description does not entirely accord with the use of the term in other disciplines. In clinical practice, practice-based research is used commonly, especially in psychotherapy, public health and family medicine. [...] “in simple terms, refers to research conducted within practice settings.”"
- Key point: Candy's usage does not fully match other disciplines; in clinical fields (psychotherapy, public health, family medicine) "practice-based research" is common and means research carried out in practice settings.
