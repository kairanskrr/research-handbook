# Mata and Suhm 2017: Benchmarking Quantum Chemical Methods: Are We Heading in the Right Direction?

- Source: `mata2017benchmarking`, [full text](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201611308)
- Version: Angewandte Chemie International Edition 56: 11011–11018, 2017, open access under CC BY-NC. Text taken from Europe PMC (PMC5582598). This is an opinion piece (the authors call it an essay) by two researchers at the University of Göttingen, one in theoretical chemistry and one in experimental spectroscopy; the recommendations are the authors' own, not journal rules. Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Theory Benchmarking Theory
- Quote: "The quality of a model is thereby no longer measured through any relation to experiment, but purely to the similarity to another model. This practice has become so popular that many manuscripts dedicated to quantum chemistry benchmarks do not feature a single experimental result."
- Key point: Benchmarking against theoretical references measures only how similar one model is to another, not how it relates to experiment; the practice is so widespread that many quantum chemistry benchmark papers contain no experimental result at all.

### E2

- Location: Theory Benchmarking Theory
- Quote: "There are good reasons for theory‐only benchmarking."
- Key point: There are good reasons for theory-only benchmarking (e.g., some quantities are hard or impossible to measure experimentally).

### E3

- Location: Experiment Benchmarking Theory
- Quote: "A single match between theory and experiment is almost worthless, because it could always be the result of fortuitous error cancellation. A broad, disparate data set is usually needed"
- Key point: A single match between theory and experiment is almost worthless, because it could be fortuitous error cancellation; a broad, diverse data set is usually needed.

### E4

- Location: Experiment Benchmarking Theory
- Quote: "If the experimental result is known beforehand to the theoretician or the theoretical prediction known to the experimentalist, there is a non‐zero risk of mutual influence"
- Key point: If the theoretician knows the experimental result beforehand, or the experimentalist knows the prediction, there is a risk of mutual influence (which is why the authors also advocate blind tests).

### E5

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "Theoreticians should focus on measurable quantities, and/or a well‐defined connection between the computed quantity and the experimental data."
- Key point: Theoreticians should focus on measurable quantities, or on a well-defined connection between the computed quantity and the experimental data.

### E6

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "not only mention the “successful” methods, but also take the opportunity to address the approaches that fail, in particular those with wide acceptance in the community"
- Key point: Do not mention only the "successful" methods; also discuss the ones that fail, especially those with wide acceptance.

### E7

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "provide whenever possible estimates and comments on error sources and crucial approximations in the calculations carried out."
- Key point: Whenever possible, give estimates of and comments on the error sources and crucial approximations in the calculations.

### E8

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "They should provide all needed material to ensure reproducibility and make proper use of the Supporting Information (provide structures, energies)."
- Key point: Provide all material needed for reproducibility and make proper use of the Supporting Information (give structures and energies).
