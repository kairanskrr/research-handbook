# Noble 2009: A Quick Guide to Organizing Computational Biology Projects

- Source: `noble2009quick`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000424)
- Version: PLoS Computational Biology 5(7): e1000424, 2009, open access, accessed 2026-10-05. Locations give section headings.

### E1

- Location: Principles
- Quote: "The core guiding principle is simple: Someone unfamiliar with your project should be able to look at your computer files and understand in detail what you did and why. [...] Most commonly, however, that “someone” is you."
- Key point: The core principle: someone unfamiliar with your project should be able to look at your files and understand in detail what you did and why; most often that person is you, a few months later.

### E2

- Location: Principles
- Quote: "Everything you do, you will probably have to do over again."
- Key point: Everything you do, you will probably have to do again; good organization makes redoing it much easier.

### E3

- Location: File and Directory Organization
- Quote: "It is generally a good idea to store all of the files relevant to one project under a common root directory. The exception to this rule is source code or scripts that are used in multiple projects."
- Key point: Store all files for one project under a common root directory; the exception is code shared by several projects, which can be its own project.

### E4

- Location: File and Directory Organization
- Quote: "If you try to give your directories logical names, you may end up with a very long list of directories with names that, six months from now, you no longer know how to interpret."
- Key point: If you give your results directories "logical" names, six months later even you may not understand them.

### E5

- Location: File and Directory Organization
- Quote: "Instead, I have found that organizing my data and results directories chronologically makes the most sense."
- Key point: The author organizes data and results directories chronologically (e.g., dated directories like 2008-12-19).

### E6

- Location: The Lab Notebook
- Quote: "Entries in the notebook should be dated, and they should be relatively verbose, with links or embedded images or tables displaying the results of the experiments that you performed. In addition to describing precisely what you did, the notebook should record your observations, conclusions, and ideas for future work."
- Key point: Lab notebook entries should be dated and fairly verbose, with the result figures and tables included; record not only what you did but also observations, conclusions, and ideas for next steps.

### E7

- Location: The Lab Notebook
- Quote: "Before doing that, it is important to document how you know the experiment failed, since the interpretation of your results may not be obvious to someone else reading your lab notebook."
- Key point: When an experiment fails, write down how you know it failed before starting the next one.

### E8

- Location: Carrying Out a Single Experiment
- Quote: "The general principle is that you should record every operation that you perform, and make those operations as transparent and reproducible as possible."
- Key point: Record every operation you perform, and make it as transparent and reproducible as possible.

### E9

- Location: Carrying Out a Single Experiment
- Quote: "The lab notebook contains a prose description of the experiment, whereas the driver script contains all the gory details."
- Key point: The lab notebook describes the experiment in prose; the driver script holds all the details. Keep both.

### E10

- Location: Figure 1 caption
- Quote: "The README files in the data directories specify who downloaded the data files from what URL on what date."
- Key point: The README in each data directory says who downloaded the data, from which URL, on what date.

- Quote: "The core guiding principle is simple: Someone unfamiliar with your project should be able to look at your computer files and understand in detail what you did and why."
- Key point: The core principle: someone unfamiliar with your project should be able to look at your files and understand in detail what you did and why.

### E12

- Location: Conclusion
- Quote: "In addition, the need to make results accessible to and understandable by wet lab biologists may have practical implications for how a project is managed."
- Key point: Computational results need to be accessible to and understandable by wet-lab biologists, which affects how the project is managed.
