# ALLEA 2023: The European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity (revised edition)

- Source: `allea2023european`, [original](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf)
- Version: 2023 revised edition, produced by the European Federation of Academies of Sciences and Humanities (ALLEA), licensed CC BY 4.0, DOI 10.26356/ECOC.

### E1

- Location: §1 Principles
- Quote: "Reliability in ensuring the quality of research, reflected in the design, methodology, analysis, and use of resources." / "Honesty in developing, undertaking, reviewing, reporting, and communicating research in a transparent, fair, full, and unbiased way."
- Key point: Two of the four fundamental principles: reliability (ensuring research quality through design, methodology, analysis, and use of resources); and honesty (being transparent, fair, full, and unbiased from undertaking research through reporting and dissemination).

### E2

- Location: §1 Principles
- Quote: "Respect for colleagues, research participants, research subjects, society, ecosystems, cultural heritage, and the environment." / "Accountability for the research from idea to publication"
- Key point: The other two principles: respect (for colleagues, participants, research subjects, society, and the environment); and accountability (for research from idea to publication).

### E3

- Location: §3.1 Research Misconduct
- Quote: "Fabrication is making up data or results and recording them as if they were real." / "Falsification is manipulating research materials, equipment, images, or processes, or changing, omitting, or suppressing data or results without justification." / "Plagiarism is using other people’s work or ideas without giving proper credit to the original source."
- Key point: Research misconduct is traditionally defined as FFP: fabrication (making up data or results), falsification (manipulating materials, or changing, omitting, or suppressing data or results without justification), and plagiarism (using others' work or ideas without proper acknowledgment).

### E4

- Location: §3.1, other unacceptable practices
- Quote: "Misusing statistics, for example to inappropriately suggest statistical significance." / "Withholding research data or results without justification." / "Citing selectively or inaccurately."
- Key point: Unacceptable practices beyond FFP include misusing statistics to suggest significance, withholding data or results without justification, and citing selectively or inaccurately.

### E5

- Location: §3.1, other unacceptable practices
- Quote: "Chopping up research results with the specific aim of increasing the number of research publications" / "Re-publishing substantive parts of one’s own earlier publications, including translations, without duly acknowledging or citing the original"
- Key point: Chopping up results to increase the number of publications ("salami slicing"); and republishing substantive parts of your own earlier publications (including translations) without acknowledgment, i.e., "self-plagiarism."

### E6

- Location: opening of §3
- Quote: "It damages the research processes, degrades relationships among researchers, undermines trust in and the credibility of research, wastes resources, and may expose research participants and subjects, users, society, or the environment to unnecessary harm."
- Key point: Violating good research practice damages research processes and relationships among researchers, undermines the credibility of research, wastes resources, and may expose participants, society, and the environment to unnecessary harm.

### E7

- Location: §2.7 Publication, Dissemination, and Authorship
- Quote: "authorship itself is based on: (1) a significant contribution to the design of the research, relevant data collection, its analysis, and/ or interpretation; (2) drafting and/or critical reviewing the publication; (3) approval of the final publication; and (4) agreeing to be responsible for the content of the publication"
- Key point: The four conditions for authorship: a significant contribution to design, data collection, analysis, or interpretation; drafting or critically revising the paper; approving the final version; and agreeing to be accountable for its content.

### E8

- Location: §2.7
- Quote: "Authors formally agree on the sequence of authorship" / "Authors acknowledge important work and contributions of those who do not meet the criteria for authorship, including collaborators, assistants, and funders who have enabled the research."
- Key point: Authors formally agree on the order of authorship; contributors who do not meet authorship criteria (collaborators, assistants, funders) are acknowledged.

### E9

- Location: §3.1, other unacceptable practices
- Quote: "Manipulating authorship or denigrating the role of other researchers in publications."
- Key point: Manipulating authorship or denigrating the role of other researchers in publications is an unacceptable practice.

### E10

- Location: §2.7
- Quote: "Authors disclose any financial and nonfinancial conflicts of interest as well as sources of support for the research or the publication."
- Key point: Disclose all financial and nonfinancial conflicts of interest, as well as sources of support.

### E11

- Location: §2.7
- Quote: "Authors and publishers promptly issue corrections or retract publications, if necessary"
- Key point: When errors are found, promptly issue corrections or, if necessary, retract.

### E12

- Location: §2.7
- Quote: "negative results can be as relevant as positive findings for publication and dissemination."
- Key point: Negative results can be as relevant as positive findings for publication and dissemination.

### E13

- Location: §2.7
- Quote: "Authors are transparent in their communication, outreach, and public engagement about assumptions and values influencing their research as well as the robustness of the evidence, including remaining uncertainties and knowledge gaps."
- Key point: In public communication, be transparent about the assumptions behind the research, the robustness of the evidence, and the remaining uncertainties and unknowns.

### E14

- Location: §2.3 Research Procedures
- Quote: "in a way that is compatible with the accepted norms of the discipline and facilitates verification or replication, where applicable"
- Key point: Report results and methods in a way compatible with the norms of the discipline that facilitates verification or replication.

### E15

- Location: §2.5 Data Practices and Management
- Quote: "ensure appropriate stewardship, curation, and preservation of all data, metadata, protocols, code, software, and other research materials for a reasonable and clearly stated period." / "access to data is as open as possible, as closed as necessary"
- Key point: Ensure appropriate stewardship and preservation of data, code, and other research materials for a reasonable and clearly stated period; data should be "as open as possible, as closed as necessary."

### E16

- Location: §2.4 Safeguards
- Quote: "Researchers, research institutions, and organisations comply with relevant codes, guidelines, and regulations." / "Researchers recognise and weigh potential harms and risks relating to their research and its applications and mitigate possible negative impacts."
- Key point: Comply with relevant codes and regulations; recognize and weigh potential harms and risks of the research and its applications, and seek to mitigate them.

### E17

- Location: §2.5
- Quote: "Researchers inform research participants about how their data will be used, reused, accessed, stored, and deleted, in compliance with GDPR."
- Key point: Inform participants how their data will be used, reused, accessed, stored, and deleted, in compliance with the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

### E18

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "applies to research in all scientific and scholarly fields."
- Key point: The code applies to research in all scientific and scholarly fields.

### E19

- Location: §3.1, other unacceptable practices
- Quote: "Hiding the use of AI or automated tools in the creation of content or drafting of publications."
- Key point: Lists "hiding the use of AI or automated tools in the creation of content or drafting of publications" as an unacceptable practice.

### E20

- Location: §2.7 Publication, Dissemination, and Authorship
- Quote: "authors are given credit for issuing corrections post-publication"
- Key point: Authors who issue corrections after publication should be given credit.
