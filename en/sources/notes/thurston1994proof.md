# Thurston 1994: On proof and progress in mathematics

- Source: `thurston1994proof`, [original](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9404236)
- Version: Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 30(2):161-177, 1994; arXiv version math/9404236v1.

### E1

- Location: Opening of §1
- Quote: "This question brings to the fore something that is fundamental and pervasive: that what we are doing is finding ways for people to understand and think about mathematics."
- Key point: The author argues that what mathematicians do is fundamentally to find ways for people to understand and think about mathematics, not just to prove theorems.

### E2

- Location: §1, the four-color theorem example
- Quote: "Rather, it reflected a continuing desire for human understanding of a proof, in addition to knowledge that the theorem is true."
- Key point: The controversy over the computer proof of the four-color theorem reflects that people want not only to know that a theorem is true but also to understand the proof.

### E3

- Location: §3 How is mathematical understanding communicated?
- Quote: "Nonetheless, most of the audience at an average colloquium talk gets little of value from it."
- Key point: The author observes that at a typical colloquium, most of the audience gets little out of it.

### E4

- Location: §3
- Quote: "When a significant theorem is proved, it often (but not always) happens that the solution can be communicated in a matter of minutes from one person to another within the subfield."
- Key point: Within the same subfield, the idea behind the proof of an important theorem can often be explained in person in a few minutes.

### E5

- Location: §3
- Quote: "One-on-one, people use wide channels of communication that go far beyond formal mathematical language."
- Key point: In one-on-one communication, people use modes of communication that go far beyond formal mathematical language.

### E6

- Location: §4 What is a proof?
- Quote: "I didn't really understand what a "proof" was. By going to seminars, reading papers, and talking to other graduate students, I gradually began to catch on."
- Key point: As a graduate student the author did not understand what a "proof" was; he came to understand it gradually by attending seminars, reading papers, and talking with other graduate students.

### E7

- Location: §4
- Quote: "You look at other papers in the field, and you see what facts they quote without proof, and what they cite in their bibliography."
- Key point: You can learn what a field accepts as its foundations by seeing which facts and references other papers in the field cite without proof.

### E8

- Location: §4
- Quote: "There were published theorems that were generally known to be false, or where the proofs were generally known to be incomplete."
- Key point: Some published theorems are known within the community to be wrong or to have incomplete proofs.

### E9

- Location: §4
- Quote: "People are usually not very good in checking formal correctness of proofs, but they are quite good at detecting potential weaknesses or flaws in proofs."
- Key point: People are not very good at checking the formal correctness of proofs, but they are quite good at spotting potential weaknesses and gaps in proofs.

### E10

- Location: §4
- Quote: "The kind of change I would advocate is that mathematicians take more care with their proofs, making them really clear and as simple as possible so that if any weakness is present it will be easy to detect."
- Key point: The author advocates writing proofs clearly and as simply as possible, so that any weaknesses are easy to spot.

### E11

- Location: §4
- Quote: "It's just that the reliability does not primarily come from mathematicians formally checking formal arguments; it comes from mathematicians thinking carefully and critically about mathematical ideas."
- Key point: The reliability of mathematical results comes mainly not from checking formal arguments line by line, but from mathematicians thinking carefully and critically about the ideas involved.

### E12

- Location: §5 What motivates people to do mathematics?
- Quote: "That's not true: the social setting is extremely important."
- Key point: Mathematical research is not a purely individual activity; the social setting is extremely important.

### E13

- Location: §5
- Quote: "I think that our strong communal emphasis on theorem-credits has a negative effect on mathematical progress."
- Key point: The author argues that the mathematical community places too much weight on "credit for theorems," which harms mathematical progress.

### E14

- Location: §5
- Quote: "The people who see the way to proving theorems are doing it in the context of a mathematical community; they are not doing it on their own."
- Key point: People who prove theorems do so within a mathematical community, not alone.

### E15

- Location: §3
- Quote: "We mathematicians need to put far greater effort into communicating mathematical ideas. To accomplish this, we need to pay much more attention to communicating not just our definitions, theorems, and proofs, but also our ways of thinking."
- Key point: Mathematicians should work harder at communicating ideas: not only definitions, theorems, and proofs, but also ways of thinking.
