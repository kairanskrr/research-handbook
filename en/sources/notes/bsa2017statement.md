# British Sociological Association 2017: Statement of Ethical Practice

- Source: `bsa2017statement`, [Original PDF](https://www.britsoc.co.uk/media/24310/bsa_statement_of_ethical_practice.pdf)
- Version: British Sociological Association (BSA) Statement of Ethical Practice, 2017 edition (updating the version drafted in 2002 and issued in 2004), 10 pages. Locations are given as paragraph numbers.

### E1

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "The statement does not, therefore, provide a set of recipes for resolving ethical choices or dilemmas (in the context of potentially conflicting interests of the various parties involved), but recognizes that it may be necessary to make such choices, drawing on fundamental principles"
- Key point: The statement does not offer ready-made recipes for resolving ethical dilemmas; it recognizes that researchers may have to make such choices themselves, drawing on basic principles.

### E2

- Location: Paragraph 13
- Quote: "Because sociologists study the relatively powerless as well as those more powerful than themselves, research relationships are frequently characterised by disparities of power and status. Despite this, research relationships should be characterised, whenever possible, by trust and integrity."
- Key point: Sociologists study both the relatively powerless and people more powerful than themselves, so research relationships often involve disparities of power and status; even so, they should be built on trust and integrity wherever possible.

### E3

- Location: Paragraph 15 (Covert Research)
- Quote: "However, covert methods violate the principles of informed consent and may invade the privacy of those being studied." / "Participant or non-participant observation in non-public spaces or experimental manipulation of research participants without their knowledge should be resorted to only where it is impossible to use other methods to obtain essential data."
- Key point: Covert research violates the principle of informed consent and may invade privacy; observation in non-public spaces without participants' knowledge should be used only when the necessary data cannot be obtained any other way.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph 19
- Quote: "it may be necessary for the obtaining of consent to be regarded, not as a once-andfor-all prior event, but as a process, subject to renegotiation over time. In addition, particular care may need to be taken during periods of prolonged fieldwork where it is easy for research participants to forget that they are being studied."
- Key point: In field research, consent may need to be treated as a process renegotiated over time, not a once-and-for-all prior event; in prolonged fieldwork, take particular care because participants easily forget they are being studied.

### E5

- Location: Paragraph 20
- Quote: "In some situations access to a research setting is gained via a ‘gatekeeper’. In these situations members should adhere to the principle of obtaining informed consent directly from the research participants to whom access is required, while at the same time taking account of the gatekeepers’ interest."
- Key point: When access to a research setting comes through a "gatekeeper" (such as the head of an institution), still obtain informed consent directly from the participants themselves, while taking the gatekeeper's interests into account.

### E6

- Location: Paragraph 27
- Quote: "Sociologists should be careful, on the one hand, not to give unrealistic guarantees of confidentiality and, on the other, not to permit communication of research films or records to audiences other than those to which the research participants have agreed."
- Key point: Do not give unrealistic guarantees of confidentiality, and do not let research films or records reach audiences beyond those the participants agreed to.

### E7

- Location: Paragraph 31
- Quote: "Potential informants and research participants, especially those possessing a combination of attributes that make them readily identifiable, may need to be reminded that it can be difficult to disguise their identity without introducing an unacceptably large measure of distortion into the data. In some situations, presenting findings by way of hypothetical composite and/or partially fictionalised case studies can address this issue."
- Key point: Participants whose combination of attributes makes them easy to identify may need reminding that their identity is hard to disguise without distorting the data unacceptably. Sometimes findings can be presented through hypothetical composite cases or partly fictionalized cases.

### E8

- Location: Paragraph 32
- Quote: "Where there is a likelihood that data may be shared with other researchers, the potential uses to which the data might be put must be discussed with research participants and their consent obtained for the future use of the material."
- Key point: Where data may be shared with other researchers, discuss the possible uses with participants and obtain their consent for future use.

### E9

- Location: Paragraph 38
- Quote: "Research data given in confidence do not enjoy legal privilege; that is they may be liable to subpoena by a court and research participants should be informed of this."
- Key point: Research data given in confidence have no legal privilege and may be subpoenaed by a court; participants should be told this.

### E10

- Location: Paragraph 29
- Quote: "Special care should be taken where research participants are particularly vulnerable by virtue of factors such as age, disability, their physical or mental health."
- Key point: Take special care when participants are particularly vulnerable because of age, disability, or physical or mental health.

### E11

- Location: Paragraph 30
- Quote: "Research involving children requires particular care. The consent of the child should be sought in addition to that of the parent."
- Key point: Research involving children requires particular care; seek the child's own consent as well as the parent's.

### E12

- Location: Paragraph 10
- Quote: "Social researchers face a range of potential risks to their safety. Safety issues need to be considered in the design and conduct of social research projects and procedures should be adopted to reduce the risk to researchers, including, importantly, any research assistants employed."
- Key point: Social researchers face a range of safety risks; consider safety when designing and conducting research, and adopt procedures that reduce the risk to researchers, including any research assistants employed.

### E13

- Location: Paragraph 5
- Quote: "Sociologists should note that there are national laws and administrative regulations (for example Data Protection Acts, the Human Rights Act, copyright and libel laws) which may affect the conduct of their research, data distribution and storage, publication, rights of research subjects, of sponsors and employers etc."
- Key point: National laws and administrative regulations (such as data protection acts, human rights law, and copyright and libel laws) can affect how research is conducted, how data are distributed and stored, and publication.

### E14

- Location: Paragraph 59
- Quote: "Sociologists should seek to distribute their research findings/share their analyses as widely as possible and, where required, make their research data available to other researchers via appropriate archives."
- Key point: Distribute findings as widely as possible and, where required, make research data available to other researchers through appropriate archives.
