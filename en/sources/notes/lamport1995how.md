# Lamport 1995: How to Write a Proof

- Source: `lamport1995how`, [full text](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/lamport-how-to-write.pdf)
- Version: the DEC Systems Research Center report version posted on the author's website (February 1993, revised December 1993); formally published in American Mathematical Monthly 102(7), 1995. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1 Mathematical Proofs
- Quote: "Anecdotal evidence suggests that as many as a third of all papers published in mathematical journals contain mistakes--not just minor errors, but incorrect theorems and proofs."
- Key point: The author cites anecdotal evidence (not systematic statistics): as many as a third of papers in mathematical journals contain mistakes, not just minor errors but incorrect theorems and proofs.

### E2

- Location: §2.1 The High-Level Proof
- Quote: "The proof sketch serves as a "road map" to the proof, helping the reader understand intuitively why the proof works."
- Key point: The proof sketch serves as a "road map" to the proof, helping the reader understand intuitively why the proof works.

### E3

- Location: §4.1 My Experience
- Quote: "I had written five pages when I realized that Kelley's proof was wrong."
- Key point: Rewriting a half-page proof from a classic topology textbook for teaching, the author had written five pages when he realized the original proof was wrong.

### E4

- Location: §4.1 My Experience
- Quote: "In a properly written proof, where every use of an assumption or a proof step is explicit, simple text searching reveals exactly where every hypothesis is used."
- Key point: In a proof where every use of an assumption is explicit, simple text searching shows exactly where each hypothesis is used, which helps when you need to weaken a hypothesis.

### E5

- Location: §4.2 Writing Structured Proofs
- Quote: "Most errors come from not carrying out the proof to enough levels."
- Key point: Most errors come from not carrying out the proof to enough levels.

### E6

- Location: §4.2 Writing Structured Proofs
- Quote: "My own rule of thumb is to expand the proof until the lowest level statements are obvious, and then continue for one more level."
- Key point: Rule of thumb: expand the proof until the lowest-level statements are obvious, then continue for one more level.

### E7

- Location: §4.2 Writing Structured Proofs
- Quote: "The assertion "this case is similar to the previous one" is not acceptable; one is forced to find the appropriate general step that makes the proof of both cases easy."
- Key point: "This case is similar to the previous one" is not acceptable; you must find the general step that handles both cases.

### E8

- Location: §4.2 Writing Structured Proofs
- Quote: "I recommend that authors provide two versions of their proofs: a very detailed one for themselves, the referees, and interested colleagues; and a less detailed one for paper publication."
- Key point: Authors should prepare two versions of a proof: a very detailed one for themselves, the referees, and interested colleagues, and a less detailed one for publication.

### E9

- Location: §4.2 Writing Structured Proofs
- Quote: "Structured proofs are longer than conventional ones. Although the formatting is partly responsible, structured proofs are longer mainly because they include more detail."
- Key point: Structured proofs are longer mainly because they include more detail, not just because of the formatting.
