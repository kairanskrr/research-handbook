# Uhlen et al. 2016: A proposal for validation of antibodies

- Source: `uhlen2016proposal`, [original (PubMed Central author manuscript)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10335836/)
- Version: Nature Methods 13(10):823-827, 2016. Recommendations of the International Working Group for Antibody Validation (IWGAV). Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Despite their widespread use, as well as extensive and valuable discourse in the literature, a comprehensive scientific framework for antibody validation across research applications is lacking. As a result, the quality and consistency of data generated through the use of antibodies vary greatly."
- Key point: Antibodies are widely used, but a comprehensive framework for validating them across research applications is lacking, so the quality and consistency of antibody-based data vary widely.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Indeed, extensive characterization of antibody performance in western blotting may indicate nothing about the performance of the same antibody in an ELISA assay" / "Therefore, approaches for antibody validation must be carried out in an application- and context-specific manner."
- Key point: An antibody that performs well in western blotting may say nothing about how it performs in an ELISA (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay); validation must be done for the specific application and sample context.

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "We suggest five conceptual pillars for validation of antibodies: (i) genetic strategies, (ii) orthogonal strategies, (iii) independent antibody strategies, (iv) expression of tagged proteins, and (v) immunocapture followed by mass spectrometry (MS). We suggest that at least one of these pillars should be used as a minimum criterion for claiming that a particular antibody has been adequately validated for a specific application."
- Key point: Proposes five pillars of validation (genetic strategies, orthogonal strategies that do not rely on antibodies, independent antibody strategies, expression of tagged proteins, and immunocapture followed by mass spectrometry); claiming an antibody is validated for an application requires at least one of them.

### E4

- Location: Genetic strategies
- Quote: "However, genetic strategies cannot be used for some applications and types of samples; in particular, they cannot be used for human tissue samples and body fluids, such as plasma and serum."
- Key point: Genetic strategies cannot be used for human tissue samples or body fluids such as plasma and serum.

### E5

- Location: Independent antibody strategies
- Quote: "It is important to note that for the western blot validation assay, the whole gel should be shown, not only the area around the size of the target proteins."
- Key point: For western blot validation, show the whole gel, not only the area around the size of the target protein.

### E6

- Location: Suggestions for providers and users
- Quote: "We therefore recommend that users of antibodies carry out at least one of the validation strategies described here in their own particular application or sample context. We also recommend that users adhere to appropriate reporting guidelines and, at a minimum, include catalog number, lot number, and perhaps RRID to ensure that any antibodies used in their research can be unambiguously identified."
- Key point: Antibody users should carry out at least one validation strategy in their own application and sample context; papers should report at least the catalog number and lot number, ideally with a Research Resource Identifier, so others can find exactly the same antibody.

### E7

- Location: Antibody reproducibility
- Quote: "We recommend the use of Research Resource Identifiers (RRIDs), since they are free to generate, are machine readable, and may be used consistently across publishers and journals."
- Key point: Recommends Research Resource Identifiers (RRIDs): free to generate, machine readable, and usable consistently across publishers and journals.

### E8

- Location: Suggestions for providers and users
- Quote: "Producers should repeat validation experiments for each new lot to ensure that data remain relevant."
- Key point: Antibody producers should repeat validation for each new lot.

### E9

- Location: Additional methods for antibody characterization
- Quote: "Although useful to show target binding, this method will not rule out crossreactivity with proteins containing similar epitopes as the intended target."
- Key point: The absorption (preadsorption) test common in immunohistochemistry shows target binding but does not rule out cross-reactivity with proteins carrying similar epitopes.
