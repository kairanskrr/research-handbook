# ASCE Library: Data Availability Statement

- Source: `ascedataavailability`, [original](https://ascelibrary.org/page/dataavailability) (the ASCE site blocks scripted access; this excerpt was read from the Internet Archive snapshot of 2022-06-21: http://web.archive.org/web/20220621123856/https://ascelibrary.org/page/dataavailability)
- Version: Data availability statement policy page for journals of the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE), 2022 snapshot. The current version may have changed; check the target journal's page when submitting.

### E1

- Location: Data Availability Statement, para. 1
- Quote: "ASCE is introducing a new policy requiring authors to specify the availability of data, computational models, code, and other electronic materials used in work submitted for publication."
- Key point: ASCE requires authors to state whether the data, computational models, code, and other electronic materials used in the submitted work are available.

### E2

- Location: Data Availability Statement, para. 2
- Quote: "When submitting a new manuscript, authors will include a new section titled “Data Availability Statement” before the “Acknowledgments.” Within this section, authors will select one or more of the following statements verbatim"
- Key point: At submission, add a "Data Availability Statement" section before the Acknowledgments and choose one or more of the given statements verbatim.

### E3

- Location: Data Availability Statement, list of statements
- Quote: "Some or all data, models, or code that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request." / "Some or all data, models, or code generated or used during the study are proprietary or confidential in nature and may only be provided with restrictions."
- Key point: Options include "available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request" and "proprietary or confidential in nature and may only be provided with restrictions."

### E4

- Location: Data Availability Statement, after the list
- Quote: "Available data and code must be cited in the reference section. Authors may update or change their statement if information changes during the course of peer review. It may not be changed after the paper is accepted."
- Key point: Available data and code must be cited in the references; the statement may be changed during peer review but not after acceptance.

### E5

- Location: after References
- Quote: "All authors are required to make data and materials available to the editors and reviewers during the peer review process if requested."
- Key point: During peer review, authors must make data and materials available to editors and reviewers on request.

### E6

- Location: Where should my data go?
- Quote: "ASCE will publish Supplemental Materials along with a paper; however, the file types are limited and not appropriate for large data sets or code. Further, Supplemental Materials do not have their own DOI and cannot be cited on their own."
- Key point: ASCE's supplemental material file types are limited and unsuitable for large data sets or code; supplemental materials have no DOI of their own and cannot be cited separately.

### E7

- Location: What should we share?
- Quote: "Data used to generate figures or tables should be included."
- Key point: The data used to generate figures or tables should be included in what is shared.

### E8

- Location: Will my data or code be peer reviewed?
- Quote: "No. If authors make data, models, and code available, reviewers and editors may or may not review those files and provide feedback."
- Key point: ASCE does not peer-review data and code; reviewers and editors may or may not look at them.

### E9

- Location: What if my data is proprietary?
- Quote: "Authors may not claim that data is proprietary just because they do not want to share it. ASCE encourages authors who enter agreements to collect or analyze proprietary data to negotiate terms for making the data used in a paper available to readers (e.g., publish anonymously or without identifying information)."
- Key point: You may not call data proprietary just because you do not want to share it; ASCE encourages authors to negotiate, when signing data agreements, terms that let the data used in a paper be made available to readers (e.g., anonymized).

### E10

- Location: Where should my data go?
- Quote: "Ideally, the repository chosen will provide authors with a Digital Object Identifier (DOI) for the data. DOIs provide a persistent link to the data and avoids link rot."
- Key point: Ideally, choose a repository that assigns the data a DOI (Digital Object Identifier); a DOI is a persistent link and avoids link rot.

### E11

- Location: Where should my data go?
- Quote: "More general data repositories include: Dryad, Figshare, DesignSafe, Open Science Framework, Harvard Dataverse, Zenodo, HydroShare"
- Key point: General data repositories listed by ASCE include Dryad, Figshare, DesignSafe, Open Science Framework, Harvard Dataverse, Zenodo, and HydroShare.
