# Rutner & Schonfeld 2012: Supporting the Changing Research Practices of Historians

- Source: `rutner2012supporting`, [original PDF](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/supporting-the-changing-research-practices-of-historians.pdf)
- Version: Final report published December 10, 2012, by Ithaka S+R (a nonprofit research and consulting organization), based on interviews with 39 historians and history PhD students in the US. Page numbers are the report's footer page numbers. Note: the sample is not representative (see E9) and reflects conditions around 2012.

### E1

- Location: Methodology, p. 6
- Quote: "For the second phase of this study, Ithaka S+R interviewed thirty-nine practicing academic historians and graduate students about their work practices."
- Key point: The study interviewed 39 practicing academic historians and graduate students about their work practices.

### E2

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 11
- Quote: "The widespread use of digital cameras and other scanning equipment to capture source materials is perhaps the single most significant shift in research practices among historians"
- Key point: Using digital cameras and scanners to capture source materials may be the single most significant shift in historians' research practices.

### E3

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 12
- Quote: "Scholars rely on complex file structures and good memories to access their files once home from the archive."
- Key point: Back home from the archive, scholars rely on complex folder structures and good memories to find their photos.

### E4

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 12
- Quote: "One interviewee includes call slips in her photographs, which stated the name of the archive and the collection, so that she could always orient herself to the source."
- Key point: One interviewee includes the call slip, showing the archive and collection name, in her photos, so she always knows where an image came from.

### E5

- Location: Digital Research Methods, Collaboration, and Communication (GIS section), p. 31
- Quote: "This type of work can rarely be undertaken by a lone history scholar, and requires new ways of working collaboratively."
- Key point: Digital work such as geographic information systems (GIS) can rarely be done by a lone historian and requires new ways of collaborating.

### E6

- Location: Promotion and Tenure, p. 36
- Quote: "The monograph remains the centerpiece of the tenure process for these historians."
- Key point: For these historians, the monograph remains the centerpiece of tenure review.

### E7

- Location: Promotion and Tenure, p. 36
- Quote: "In most cases, digital scholarship work is seen as a part of or a supplement to the monograph."
- Key point: In most cases, digital scholarship is seen as part of, or a supplement to, the monograph.

### E8

- Location: Scholarly Communication, p. 33
- Quote: "This seems to be a significant change from the typical “lone scholar” approach to historical research."
- Key point: Some use blogs to share methodological experiments and invite peers to discuss improvements; the report sees this as a significant change from the typical "lone scholar" approach to historical research.

### E9

- Location: Methodology, p. 6
- Quote: "Still, this sample of historians is not meant to be perfectly representative of the history community."
- Key point: The report itself states that the sample is not meant to be perfectly representative of the history community.

### E10

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 12
- Quote: "Scholars were able to spend time in the reading room photographing the collections, and would often postpone viewing the images until they returned home from the trip. This was notable in that some historians reported that they no longer engage intellectually with the sources while in the archive; these trips have become more of a collection mission."
- Key point: Scholars photograph collections extensively in the reading room and often look at the images only after returning home; some say they no longer read and think about materials in the archive, and archival visits have become "collecting missions."

### E11

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 13
- Quote: "These digital photographs clearly add value to the research process, but working with them effectively and efficiently remains a struggle for most."
- Key point: Digital photos clearly help research, but most scholars have not yet found an effective, efficient way to manage and use them.

### E12

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 11
- Quote: "The archivist is also critically important for scholars who cannot travel to an archive. Interviewees reported relying on them via sometimes extensive phone and email exchanges."
- Key point: Archivists are especially important for scholars who cannot travel to an archive; interviewees report relying on them through sometimes extensive phone and email exchanges.

### E13

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 10
- Quote: "Most interviewees said they are not traveling less for research because of digitized finding aids and collections, but they have been able to travel more strategically."
- Key point: Most interviewees say digitized finding aids and collections have not made them travel less for research, but they now travel more strategically.

### E14

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 8
- Quote: "The consequence of shorter research trips is that researchers spend the majority of their time in the archives informally digitizing materials for later review and analysis."
- Key point: With shorter research trips, researchers spend most of their archive time informally photographing and scanning materials for later review and analysis.

### E15

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 13
- Quote: "However, using an unprocessed collection does require different preparation and different approaches once at the archive."
- Key point: Using an unprocessed collection requires different preparation and different approaches on site.

### E16

- Location: Discovery, p. 16
- Quote: "Some interviewees expressed concerns about limitations of keyword searching, recognizing that the corpus of materials that are available to search in are not, in fact, comprehensive."
- Key point: Some interviewees worry about the limits of keyword search: the searchable corpus is not actually comprehensive.

### E17

- Location: Discovery, p. 19
- Quote: "Historians working on international topics noted limitations of the corpus of foreign language material available on Google Books."
- Key point: Interviewees working on international topics note the limited foreign-language material on Google Books.

### E18

- Location: Discovery, p. 17
- Quote: "Another important discovery mechanism is following citation trails."
- Key point: Following citation trails is another important way of discovering sources.

### E19

- Location: Gathering and Using Primary Sources, p. 8
- Quote: "The use of primary sources remains at the heart of the historical research method."
- Key point: The report holds that using primary sources remains at the heart of the historical research method.
