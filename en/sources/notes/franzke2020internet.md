# franzke et al. 2020: Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0

- Source: `franzke2020internet`, [Original PDF](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- Version: Third edition of the internet research ethics guidelines (IRE 3.0) of the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR), unanimously adopted by the membership on October 6, 2019, released 2020, 83 pages, Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: 0. Preview
- Quote: "IRE 3.0 is written especially for researchers, students, IRB members or technical developers who face ethical concerns during their research or are generally interested in Internet Research Ethics."
- Key point: The guidelines are written for researchers, students, ethics review board (IRB) members, and technical developers who face ethical concerns in their research.

### E2

- Location: §2.3.4 Judgment Calls: Guidelines, Not Recipes
- Quote: "Taken together, all of this means that the best we can do is develop "guidelines, not recipes" (IRE 1.0, p. 3)."
- Key point: Internet research ethics can offer "guidelines, not recipes" to follow mechanically; researchers have to use their own judgment.

### E3

- Location: §2.3.5 Asking the Right Questions (looking back at IRE 2.0)
- Quote: "people were sharing more and more information online in what amounted to public or quasi-public fora (the latter protected, e.g., by passwords, registered profile requirements, etc.). But they often nonetheless expected that these exchanges were somehow private either individually private or in some form of group privacy. Even though these expectations were not warranted by the technical realities of a given forum or SNS, especially deontological ethics calls for respecting these expectations,"
- Key point: People share information in public or quasi-public online spaces yet often expect these exchanges to be private; even when they technically are not, deontological ethics calls for respecting that expectation of privacy.

### E4

- Location: §3.1.2 Informed Consent
- Quote: "Such consent is manifestly impracticable in the case of Big Data projects, however, resulting in a serious ethical dilemma."
- Key point: In Big Data projects, getting informed consent from each person is manifestly impracticable, which creates a serious ethical dilemma.

### E5

- Location: §3.1.2 Informed Consent
- Quote: "Most commonly, researchers pseudonymize their data separating keys from the actual dataset and also make sure to justify both any questions using/processing sensitive data and/or how risk in this process has been dealt with (e.g. storage, aggregation of data, publication of aggregates)."
- Key point: The most common practice is to pseudonymize the data, keep the keys separate from the dataset, and justify why sensitive data are used and how the risk is handled (storage, aggregation, publishing only aggregates, and so on).

### E6

- Location: §3.1.2 Informed Consent
- Quote: "Another way of trying to mitigate risk and resolve the dilemma is by reserving the acquisition of informed consent to the dissemination stage of a project, i.e., by asking for informed consent from specific subjects for the publication of a quote or other data that might make them and their personal information identifiable (e.g., through a string search or more sophisticated data-matching techniques)."
- Key point: Another option is to seek consent at the dissemination stage: before publishing a quote or other data that could identify someone (for example, because the quote can be searched to find the person), ask that person for consent.

### E7

- Location: §3.2.7 Assumptions, Questions, Issues, Procedures for Data Acquisition, Analysis, Storage, Dissemination
- Quote: "These questions emphasize the hard fact that the best current practices and techniques can only "de-identify" data, i.e., not perfectly anonymize data. Therefore, emphasis on data security is even more important"
- Key point: Current best practices can only "de-identify" data, not anonymize it perfectly, so data security matters even more.

### E8

- Location: §3.2.2 Legal Aspects, Terms and Conditions
- Quote: "These terms and conditions suggest that researchers relying on Twitter-based datasets must continually check if any tweets have been deleted from the platform, and then remove them for their research dataset accordingly"
- Key point: Under Twitter's terms of service at the time, researchers using Twitter data had to keep checking which tweets had been deleted from the platform and remove them from their own datasets.

### E9

- Location: §3.2.2 Legal Aspects, Terms and Conditions
- Quote: "For example, Facebook and LinkedIn prohibit the automated scraping of data from their platform, limiting researchers' ability to collect needed data through such means"
- Key point: Facebook and LinkedIn prohibit automated scraping, which limits how researchers can collect data.

### E10

- Location: Footnote 11 (§3.1.2 Informed Consent)
- Quote: "In some cases, transforming the quotations (Markham, 2012) and images (Tiidenberg, 2018; Warfield, Hoholuk, Vincent & Camargo, 2019), or creating samples of discourse that are composites from various participants may mitigate risk."
- Key point: Transforming quotations and images, or creating composite samples of discourse from several participants, can sometimes reduce risk.

### E11

- Location: §2.3.5 Asking the Right Questions (looking back at IRE 2.0)
- Quote: "informed consent may need to be sought not only from individuals, but also "from communities and online system administrators?""
- Key point: Informed consent may need to be sought not only from individuals but also from communities and online system administrators.

### E12

- Location: §3.2.7 Assumptions, Questions, Issues, Procedures for Data Acquisition, Analysis, Storage, Dissemination
- Quote: "The need to be aware of the problematic representativeness of even large datasets of social media users as compared to overall populations etc."
- Key point: Even very large datasets of social media users have representativeness problems compared with the overall population.
