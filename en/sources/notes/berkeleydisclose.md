# UC Berkeley IPIRA: When to Disclose an Invention to OTL

- Source: `berkeleydisclose`, [original](https://ipira.berkeley.edu/researchers/intellectual-property-protection/when-disclose-invention-otl)
- Version: Web page of Intellectual Property & Industry Research Alliances (IPIRA), University of California, Berkeley, read 2026-10-06. Written for US law and the university's own policies.

### E1

- Location: When to disclose
- Quote: "Before collaboration with external partners: If you are planning to collaborate with companies or other institutions, make sure to file a disclosure first to secure the university’s rights over the invention."
- Key point: Before collaborating with companies or other institutions, file an invention disclosure with the university's Office of Technology Licensing (OTL).

### E2

- Location: When to disclose
- Quote: "Ideally, submit a disclosure to OTL at least 1-2 months before a public enabling disclosure."
- Key point: Ideally, submit the invention disclosure to OTL at least 1-2 months before any public disclosure.

### E3

- Location: FAQ, Is there a grace period for filing patent applications after a public disclosure?
- Quote: "Online resources often mention a “one-year grace period” for filing U.S. patent applications. However, these resources don’t consider international patent law, first-to-file litigation risk, and other factors."
- Key point: The US "one-year grace period" often mentioned online ignores international patent law and other factors, so do not rely on it.

### E4

- Location: FAQ, What are the consequences of publishing ... before disclosing it to OTL?
- Quote: "A public enabling disclosure of an invention triggers a 1-year deadline for filing a patent application on that invention in the US and disqualifies filing a patent application on that invention in most non-US nations."
- Key point: A public enabling disclosure leaves you one year to file in the US and disqualifies filing in most other countries.
