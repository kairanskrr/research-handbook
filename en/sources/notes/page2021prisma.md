# Page et al. 2021: The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews

- Source: `page2021prisma`, [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71) (CC BY 4.0)
- Version: BMJ 2021;372:n71. Page numbers are the page order of the PDF file.

### E1

- Location: PDF p. 2
- Quote: "The PRISMA 2020 checklist includes seven sections with 27 items, some of which include sub-items"
- Key point: PRISMA 2020 is a reporting checklist: 7 sections, 27 items.

### E2

- Location: PDF p. 2
- Quote: "PRISMA 2020 is not intended to guide systematic review conduct, for which comprehensive resources are available. However, familiarity with PRISMA 2020 is useful when planning and conducting systematic reviews to ensure that all recommended information is captured."
- Key point: PRISMA guides how to report a review, not how to conduct it; but knowing it while planning and conducting the review ensures you record all the information you will need.

### E3

- Location: Table 1, item 7, PDF p. 4
- Quote: "Present the full search strategies for all databases, registers and websites, including any filters and limits used."
- Key point: Present the full search strategies for all databases, registers, and websites, including any filters and limits used.

### E4

- Location: Table 1, item 16a, PDF p. 4
- Quote: "Describe the results of the search and selection process, from the number of records identified in the search to the number of studies included in the review, ideally using a flow diagram"
- Key point: Report the number of records at each step from search to final inclusion, ideally with a flow diagram (the PRISMA flow diagram).

### E5

- Location: Main changes from the 2009 version, PDF p. 3
- Quote: "Modification of the 'Study selection' item in the Methods section to emphasise the reporting of how many reviewers screened each record and each report retrieved, whether they worked independently, and if applicable, details of automation tools used in the process"
- Key point: Report how many reviewers screened each record, whether they worked independently, and whether automation tools were used.

### E6

- Location: Table 1, item 24a
- Quote: "Provide registration information for the review, including register name and registration number, or state that the review was not registered."
- Key point: Give registration information: register name and registration number, or state that the review was not registered.

### E7

- Location: Table 1, items 24b, 24c
- Quote: "Indicate where the review protocol can be accessed, or state that a protocol was not prepared." / "Describe and explain any amendments to information provided at registration or in the protocol."
- Key point: State where the protocol can be accessed (or that none was prepared), and describe and explain any amendments to the registration or protocol.

### E8

- Location: Table 1, item 12
- Quote: "Specify for each outcome the effect measure(s) (e.g. risk ratio, mean difference) used in the synthesis or presentation of results."
- Key point: Specify the effect measure for each outcome (e.g., risk ratio, mean difference).

### E9

- Location: Table 1, item 13d
- Quote: "If meta-analysis was performed, describe the model(s), method(s) to identify the presence and extent of statistical heterogeneity, and software package(s) used."
- Key point: If you did a meta-analysis, describe the model, the methods used to identify the presence and extent of heterogeneity, and the software.

### E10

- Location: Table 1, items 13e, 13f
- Quote: "Describe any methods used to explore possible causes of heterogeneity among study results (e.g. subgroup analysis, meta-regression)." / "Describe any sensitivity analyses conducted to assess robustness of the synthesised results."
- Key point: Describe the methods used to explore causes of heterogeneity (subgroup analysis, meta-regression) and any sensitivity analyses.

### E11

- Location: Table 1, items 14, 15
- Quote: "Describe any methods used to assess risk of bias due to missing results in a synthesis (arising from reporting biases)." / "Describe any methods used to assess certainty (or confidence) in the body of evidence for an outcome."
- Key point: Describe how you assessed the risk of bias from missing results (reporting biases) and the certainty of the evidence for each outcome.

### E12

- Location: Table 1, item 18
- Quote: "Present assessments of risk of bias for each included study."
- Key point: Present risk-of-bias assessments for each included study.

### E13

- Location: Table 1, item 20b
- Quote: "If meta-analysis was done, present for each the summary estimate and its precision (e.g. confidence/credible interval) and measures of statistical heterogeneity."
- Key point: For each meta-analysis, report the summary estimate, its precision (e.g., confidence interval), and measures of heterogeneity.

### E14

- Location: Table 1, item 27
- Quote: "Report which of the following are publicly available and where they can be found: template data collection forms; data extracted from included studies; data used for all analyses; analytic code; any other materials used in the review."
- Key point: Report whether and where the data collection form templates, extracted data, analysis data, analytic code, and so on are publicly available.

### E15

- Location: Introduction, PDF p. 2
- Quote: "Terminology used to describe particular review processes has also evolved, as in the shift from assessing "quality" to assessing "certainty" in the body of evidence."
- Key point: Terminology has evolved: for the body of evidence, assessing "quality" has shifted to assessing "certainty."

### E16

- Location: PDF p. 2
- Quote: "PRISMA 2020 is not intended to guide systematic review conduct, for which comprehensive resources are available."
- Key point: PRISMA 2020 guides reporting, not the conduct of systematic reviews.

### E17

- Location: The PRISMA 2020 statement, Scope of the guideline
- Quote: "The PRISMA 2020 statement has been designed primarily for systematic reviews of studies that evaluate the effects of health interventions, irrespective of the design of the included studies." / "many items are applicable to systematic reviews with objectives other than evaluating interventions (such as evaluating aetiology, prevalence, or prognosis)"
- Key point: PRISMA 2020 is designed mainly for systematic reviews evaluating the effects of health interventions, but many items also apply to systematic reviews with other aims (e.g., evaluating etiology, prevalence, or prognosis).
