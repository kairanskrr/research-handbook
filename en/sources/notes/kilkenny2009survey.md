# Kilkenny et al. 2009: Survey of the Quality of Experimental Design, Statistical Analysis and Reporting of Research Using Animals

- Source: `kilkenny2009survey`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0007824)
- Version: PLoS ONE 4(11): e7824, 2009, open access, read 2026-10-06. A literature survey funded by the UK National Centre for the Replacement, Refinement and Reduction of Animals in Research (NC3Rs). Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Medline and EMBASE were searched for studies reporting research on live rats, mice and non-human primates carried out in UK and US publicly funded research establishments. Detailed information was collected from 271 publications"
- Key point: Scope: 271 publications reporting research on live rats, mice, and non-human primates carried out in publicly funded UK and US research establishments.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Only 59% of the studies stated the hypothesis or objective of the study and the number and characteristics of the animals used." / "Most of the papers surveyed did not use randomisation (87%) or blinding (86%), to reduce bias in animal selection and outcome assessment."
- Key point: Only 59% of studies stated the hypothesis or objective and the number and characteristics of the animals; 87% of papers did not use randomization and 86% did not use blinding.

### E3

- Location: Results, Experimental design
- Quote: "Random selection is not the same as haphazard selection; a systematic, physical approach such as tossing a coin or using a table of random numbers or a computer to pick numbers randomly, is necessary for this process."
- Key point: Random is not haphazard; use a systematic method such as tossing a coin, a table of random numbers, or a computer to pick numbers randomly.

### E4

- Location: Results, Experimental design
- Quote: "Random allocation of animals to experimental groups was reported in 12% of all 271 studies in the sample (see Table 13). Of the studies which reported using randomisation, 9% (3/33) provided details of the method used."
- Key point: 12% of the 271 studies reported random allocation; of the 33 that did, only 3 (9%) described the method.

### E5

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "It cannot be ruled out that a proportion of the studies may indeed have used blinding but did not report it."
- Key point: It cannot be ruled out that some studies used blinding but did not report it.
