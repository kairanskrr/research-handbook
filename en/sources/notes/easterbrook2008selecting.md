# Easterbrook et al. 2008: Selecting Empirical Methods for Software Engineering Research

- Source: `easterbrook2008selecting`, [original PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sme/papers/2007/SelectingEmpiricalMethods.pdf) (author version on the first author's University of Toronto homepage)
- Version: Chapter in *Guide to Advanced Empirical Software Engineering*, edited by Shull, Singer & Sjøberg (Springer, 2008), pp. 285–311. The author version has no published page numbers, so locations give the section.

### E1

- Location: §1.0 Introduction
- Quote: "These methods all have known flaws, and each can only provide limited, qualified evidence about the phenomena being studied. However, each method is flawed differently (McGrath, 1995) and viable research strategies use multiple methods, chosen in such a way that the weaknesses of each method are addressed by use of complementary methods"
- Key point: Every method has known flaws and can only provide limited evidence, but each is flawed differently; a workable research strategy combines several methods so that the weaknesses of one are offset by another.

### E2

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "Mixed method research employs data collection and analysis techniques associated with both quantitative and qualitative data. The ‘mixing’ might be within one study, by using multiple data collection techniques, or among several studies. Key decisions involve the strategy for data collection, and the sequence in which different methods are employed."
- Key point: Mixed-methods research uses data collection and analysis techniques associated with both quantitative and qualitative data; the mixing can happen within one study or across several, and the key decisions are the data collection strategy and the order of the methods.

### E3

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "While mixed method research is a powerful approach to inquiry, the researcher is challenged with the need for extensive data collection, the time-intensive nature of analyzing multiple sources of data, as well as the requirement to be familiar with both quantitative and qualitative forms of research."
- Key point: Mixed methods are powerful, but the cost is extensive data collection, time-intensive analysis of multiple data sources, and the need for the researcher to be familiar with both quantitative and qualitative research.

### E4

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "The Sequential explanatory strategy is characterized by the collection and analysis of quantitative data followed by the collection and analysis of qualitative data. The purpose of this strategy is typically to use qualitative results to assist in explaining and interpreting the findings of a quantitative study. It is particularly useful when unexpected results arise from the quantitative phase."
- Key point: Sequential explanatory strategy: collect and analyze quantitative data first, then qualitative data, using the qualitative results to explain the quantitative findings; especially useful when the quantitative phase produces unexpected results.

### E5

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "The Sequential exploratory strategy is characterized by the collection and analysis of qualitative data followed by the collection and analysis of quantitative data. Its purpose is to use quantitative data and results to assist in the interpretation of qualitative findings. This strategy is also useful for testing elements of an emerging theory resulting from a qualitative study."
- Key point: Sequential exploratory strategy: qualitative first, then quantitative, using the quantitative results to help interpret the qualitative findings; also suited to testing preliminary theories developed in qualitative work.

### E6

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "This strategy uses different methods concurrently, in an attempt to confirm, cross-validate or corroborate findings. Triangulation is motivated by the fact that often ‘what people say’ could be different than ‘what people do’, and thus collecting data from multiple sources helps improve validity."
- Key point: Concurrent triangulation strategy: use different methods at the same time to confirm, cross-validate, or corroborate findings; the motivation is that what people say often differs from what they do, and multiple data sources help increase validity.

### E7

- Location: §5.6 Mixed-Methods Approaches
- Quote: "The challenge in this approach is that it may be difficult for the researcher to compare the results of two analyses or to resolve contradictions that arise in the results. In such cases a further source of evidence, or a follow up study might be necessary."
- Key point: The challenge of the concurrent strategy is comparing the results of the two analyses and resolving contradictions between them; this may require another source of evidence or a follow-up study.

### E8

- Key point: Mixed-methods research is more often associated with a pragmatist stance, which emphasizes using whatever methods most effectively address the research question.

### E9

### E11

### E12

- Location: §5.2, p. 10
- Quote: "A precondition for conducting a case study is a clear research question concerned with how or why certain phenomena occur. - Key point: A precondition for a case study is a clear research question about how or why something happens; from it you derive study propositions, which guide which cases to select and what data to collect.

### E13

- Location: §5.2, p. 11
- Quote: "Case study research uses purposive sampling rather than random sampling. The aim is to select cases that are most relevant to the study proposition. Sometimes a single case is sufficient. This might be because it is a critical case for testing a well-formulated theory: if the theory holds for this case, it is likely to be true for many others."
- Key point: Case studies use purposive rather than random sampling; sometimes one case is enough, e.g. a critical case for testing a well-formulated theory: if the theory holds there, it likely holds in many other situations too.

### E14

- Key point: A multiple-case design usually offers greater validity; the cases are best thought of as replications rather than members of a sample.

### E15

- Location: §5.2, p. 11

- Key point: The unit of analysis can be a company, a project, a team, an individual developer, a particular episode or event, a specific work product, and so on; choosing the right unit keeps the study focused on the phenomenon you want to study.

### E16

- Location: §5.2, p. 11
- Quote: "Case study research is most appropriate for cases where the reductionism of controlled experiments is inappropriate. - Key point: Case studies are most appropriate when the reductionism of controlled experiments is inappropriate, for instance when the context itself affects the phenomenon, or when effects are wide-ranging and take a long time to appear.
