# Zeller 2012: What makes good research?

- Source: `zeller2012good`, [original](https://andreas-zeller.info/2012/10/16/what-makes-good-research.html)
- Version: The author's blog, 2012-10-16. It relays Günter Hotz's "three-axis model of good research."

### E1

- Location: Main text, Hotz model of good research
- Quote: "It is actually fairly simple. There are three criteria, which are orthogonal. [...] First, was it hard? [...] is this something which required years of training and perspiration, or is this something anyone on the street could have come up with? Second, is it elegant? Is this something which solves a specific problem, or is this something that you can apply over and over again to new problems? Third, is it useful? Is this of value only to the academic community, or is this something people outside can use to create value or make money?"
- Key point: Good research is judged by three independent (orthogonal) criteria: Was it hard (did it take years of training and perspiration)? Is it elegant (can it be applied again and again to new problems)? Is it useful (can it create value outside academia)?

### E2

- Location: Same as above
- Quote: "If you're good on one of the axes, you are doing good research. If you're good on two axes, we'll hire you."
- Key point: Being good on any one axis means you are doing good research.

### E3

- Location: Main text, the second and third lessons and the ending
- Quote: "Difficulty, elegance, usefulness – pick any two."
- Key point: All three are hard to achieve at once: usefulness often costs elegance, and elegance means simplicity.

### E4

- Location: End of main text
- Quote: "You have every right to choose the axis (or axes!) on which you plan to excel. Different people make different choices, but remember: The axes are orthogonal, and no axis dominates the others."
- Key point: You can choose the axis on which to excel; no axis dominates the others.
