# American Chemical Society 2015: Identifying and Evaluating Hazards in Research Laboratories

- Source: `acs2015identifying`, [original](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/about/governance/committees/chemicalsafety/publications/identifying-and-evaluating-hazards-in-research-laboratories.pdf)
- Version: guide written by the Hazard Identification and Evaluation Task Force of the American Chemical Society (ACS) Committee on Chemical Safety, 2015. The ACS website returned a block page to scripted access, so these excerpts come from the PDF saved by the Internet Archive on 2026-10-04.

### E1

- Location: Executive Summary, p. 9
- Quote: "it was prepared in response to a recommendation from CSB to ACS to fill a need for better guidance on hazard assessment."
- Key point: The guide was prepared in response to a recommendation from the US Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board (CSB) to ACS, to fill a need for better guidance on hazard assessment.

### E2

- Location: Executive Summary, p. 9
- Quote: "Hazard assessment is a continuous process."
- Key point: Hazard assessment is a continuous process.

### E3

- Location: Executive Summary, p. 9
- Quote: "What-if analysis: An approach that raises a series of questions to help identify things that might go wrong." / "Checklists: A method that tends to be more operational in that it helps researchers remember all of the precautions they are supposed to take."
- Key point: The five tools the guide presents include what-if analysis (raising a series of questions to identify what might go wrong) and checklists (helping researchers remember the precautions they should take).

### E4

- Location: 1.2 Application, p. 11
- Quote: "This guide was written for researchers without deference to the stage in their careers-- undergraduate students, graduate students, postdoctoral scholars, instructors, PIs, or departmental chairs"
- Key point: The guide is written for researchers at every career stage, from undergraduates and graduate students to principal investigators and department chairs.

### E5

- Location: 6. Change Control, p. 27
- Quote: "Unfortunately, the cause of many accidents and injuries can be traced back to unrecognized changes in the work scope or hazards. Fundamentally, when the work to be performed changes, that change must be evaluated against the current hazard analysis to determine if the hazard analysis continues to be sufficient."
- Key point: Many accidents can be traced back to unrecognized changes; whenever the work changes, check the existing hazard analysis to see whether it is still adequate.

### E6

- Location: 6.1 Recognizing Change, p. 27
- Quote: "Same basic synthesis, but changing the reactant to a compound with an additional functional group;" / "Scale up;" / "New person on team or losing someone with experience;"
- Key point: Examples of changes worth re-evaluating: the same synthesis with a reactant that has an additional functional group, scale-up, a new person joining the team or an experienced person leaving, and so on.

### E7

- Location: 5.3, p. 26
- Quote: "Involving multiple people in a review (students, laboratory workers with varying levels of experience, peers, and support staff) is a good defense against unconscious incompetence."
- Key point: Involving multiple people in a review (students, lab workers with varying experience, peers, and support staff) is a good defense against "unknown unknowns."

### E8

- Location: 5.3, p. 26
- Quote: "Discuss previous incidents and near misses." / "Maintain open lines of communication--talk about safety in research meetings."
- Key point: Discuss previous incidents and near misses; talk about safety in research meetings.

### E9

- Location: 4.3 Researcher and Laboratory Worker, p. 24
- Quote: "Researchers have a right and a responsibility to ask challenging and clarifying questions to ensure the scope of work and all hazards and controls are well understood before beginning an experiment or research protocol."
- Key point: Researchers have a right and a responsibility to ask challenging and clarifying questions before starting an experiment, to ensure the scope of work and all hazards and controls are well understood.

### E10

- Location: 9.4 Job Hazard Analysis Instructions and Template, JHA Development Tactics, item 2
- Quote: "Literature searches should be performed to locate related procedures and known problems with the processes or chemicals being used."
- Key point: Before a hazard analysis, search the literature for related procedures and known problems with the processes and chemicals being used.

### E11

- Location: 6.3 Strategies for Enabling Recognition of and Responding to Change, p. 28
- Quote: "Ensure the thresholds are understood and know who has the authority to authorize tasks that exceed a threshold"
- Key point: Make sure the thresholds (such as limits on scaling up energetic materials) are understood, and know who has the authority to approve work that exceeds them.
