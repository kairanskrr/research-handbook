# Modern Language Association: MLA Style Center (In-Text Citations: An Overview; Plagiarism and Academic Dishonesty; Works Cited: A Quick Guide)

- Source: `mlastylecenter`, [original](https://style.mla.org/)
- Version: free pages on the official MLA style site, based on the *MLA Handbook*, 9th edition; accessed October 2026

### E1

- Location: In-Text Citations: An Overview
- Quote: "In-text citations are brief, unobtrusive references that direct readers to the works-cited-list entries for the sources you consulted"
- Key point: In-text citations are brief references that direct readers to entries in the works-cited list at the end.

### E2

- Location: In-Text Citations: An Overview
- Quote: "if a specific part of a work is quoted or paraphrased and the work includes a page number, line number, time stamp, or other way to point readers to the place in the work where the information can be found, that location marker must be included in parentheses."
- Key point: When you quote or paraphrase a specific part of a work, give its location in parentheses: page number, line number, time stamp, or similar.

### E3

- Location: Works Cited: A Quick Guide
- Quote: "When the source being documented forms part of a larger whole, the larger whole can be thought of as a container that holds the source."
- Key point: The "container" concept: when the cited work is part of a larger whole (e.g., a short story in an anthology), that larger whole is the container.

### E4

- Location: Plagiarism and Academic Dishonesty
- Quote: "In published work, if you reuse ideas or phrases that you used in prior work and do not cite your prior work, you have plagiarized."
- Key point: In published work, reusing ideas or phrases from your own prior work without citing it is also plagiarism.

### E5

- Location: Plagiarism and Academic Dishonesty
- Quote: "Paraphrasing someone’s ideas or arguments or copying someone’s unique wording without giving proper credit is plagiarism."
- Key point: Paraphrasing someone's ideas or arguments, or copying their unique wording, without giving credit is plagiarism.
