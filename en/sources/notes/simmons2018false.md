# Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn 2018: False-Positive Citations

- Source: `simmons2018false`, [original PDF (author homepage)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/26-Simmons-Nelson-Simonsohn-2018.pdf)
- Version: Perspectives on Psychological Science 13(2):255-259. The authors look back on how their 2011 paper "False-Positive Psychology" was written and cited.

### E1

- Location: p. 256
- Quote: "we also recommended that authors be required to have at least 20 observations per cell" / "20-per-cell is a comically low threshold, insufficient to detect in a representative sample that men are heavier than women"
- Key point: The 2011 paper recommended at least 20 observations per cell. The authors later called this a comically low threshold: 20 per cell is not enough even to detect that men are heavier than women.

### E2

- Location: p. 258
- Quote: "Preregistration does not restrict the ability to conduct exploratory analyses; it merely allows the researcher and the reader to properly distinguish between analyses that were planned and exploratory." / "It is perfectly acceptable to simply pose a research question and describe exactly how you intend to answer it, and it is perfectly acceptable to publish a finding that did not conform to your original prediction."
- Key point: Preregistration does not restrict exploratory analysis; it only makes clear which analyses were planned in advance and which were exploratory. A preregistration can simply state a research question and spell out how it will be answered, and results that do not match predictions are entirely publishable.
