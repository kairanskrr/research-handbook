# Cameron & Miller 2015: A Practitioner's Guide to Cluster-Robust Inference

- Source: `cameron2015practitioner`, [PDF on the author's homepage](https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/Cameron_Miller_JHR_2015_February.pdf); final version published in Journal of Human Resources 50(2), 2015
- Version: Pre-publication final version from the author's homepage (February 2015). Locations are given as subsections.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "In such settings default standard errors can greatly overstate estimator precision. Instead, if the number of clusters is large, statistical inference after OLS should be based on cluster-robust standard errors."
- Key point: When errors are correlated within clusters, default standard errors can greatly overstate precision; with many clusters, use cluster-robust standard errors.

### E2

- Location: §I Introduction
- Quote: "It is not unusual to have applications where standard errors that control for within-cluster correlation are several times larger than default standard errors that ignore such correlation."
- Key point: It is not unusual for standard errors that account for within-cluster correlation to be several times larger than default standard errors.

### E3

- Location: §I Introduction
- Quote: "A leading example, highlighted by Moulton (1986, 1990), is when interest lies in measuring the effect of a policy variable, or other aggregated regressor, that takes the same value for all observations within a cluster."
- Key point: The leading example: estimating the effect of a policy variable, or another regressor that takes the same value for every observation in a cluster.

### E4

- Location: §I Introduction
- Quote: "There is no clear-cut definition of "few"; depending on the situation "few" may range from less than 20 to less than 50 clusters in the balanced case." / "without adjustment, test statistics based on the cluster-robust standard errors over-reject and confidence intervals are too narrow."
- Key point: There is no clear cutoff for "few" clusters; depending on the situation it ranges from fewer than 20 to fewer than 50 (with balanced clusters). With few clusters and no adjustment, tests over-reject and confidence intervals are too narrow.

### E5

- Location: §I Introduction
- Quote: "who pointed out that many differences-in-differences studies failed to control for clustered errors, and those that did often clustered at the wrong level."
- Key point: (Summarizing Bertrand, Duflo, and Mullainathan 2004.) Many difference-in-differences studies failed to account for clustered errors, and those that did often clustered at the wrong level.
