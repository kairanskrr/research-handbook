# Dredze & Wallach 2012: How to Be a Successful PhD Student (in Computer Science (in NLP/ML))

- Source: `dredze2012successful`, [Original PDF](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~mdredze/publications/HowtoBeaSuccessfulPhDStudent.1_2.pdf)
- Version: v1.2 (2022-01-25), first version 2012-03-20. Page numbers follow the PDF page order; item numbers are the original's. The PDF's font encoding is non-standard, so the original text was recovered by character mapping.

### E1

- Location: #16 Prioritize, p. 4
- Quote: "It's a good idea to do a time audit if you feel things aren't matching up."
- Key point: If your time allocation and priorities don't seem to match, do a time audit.

### E2

- Location: #18 Keep a log, p. 4
- Quote: "Keep a daily log of everything you do and everything you think. It's a good idea to make sure your log is searchable (e.g., plain text or use a note taking program)."
- Key point: Keep a daily log of everything you do and think, and make sure it is searchable.

### E3

- Location: #20 A social life, p. 4
- Quote: "Take the time to ensure you are happy and healthy, or you won't be as productive or effective in your work life."
- Key point: Take time to make sure you are happy and healthy, or your work will suffer too.

### E4

- Location: #21 It's okay to get stuck, p. 4
- Quote: "Remember that EVERYONE gets stuck/demoralized/etc."
- Key point: Everyone gets stuck and demoralized; what sets successful people apart is getting out of the slump.

### E5

- Location: #34 Learn when to quit, p. 6
- Quote: "You can learn from failure, but only if you move on. If you find yourself repeatedly lowering your goals and expectations, you aren't making progress."
- Key point: You can learn from failure, but only if you move on; if you keep lowering your goals and expectations, you are not making progress, and it may be time to let go, guided by your advisor's judgment.

### E6

- Location: You and Your Advisor, p. 2
- Quote: "Your relationship with your advisor is the single most important relationship you will have in graduate school."
- Key point: Your relationship with your advisor is the most important relationship in graduate school.

### E7

- Location: You and Your Advisor, p. 2
- Quote: "Different advisors have different advising styles. Ask them how they run their research group. Talk to their existing students to find out about their advising style and what they expect of their students."
- Key point: Advisors have different styles; ask how they run their group, and talk to their current students about their style and expectations.

### E8

- Location: #5 Listen to your advisor, p. 2
- Quote: "They won't always be right, but they have been doing research for (in some cases much) longer than you have. (And, if it wasn't clear, your advisor is your boss -- you work for them. When your advisor tells you to do something, do it.)"
- Key point: Your advisor won't always be right but has more research experience than you; and your advisor is your boss, so when they tell you to do something, do it.

### E9

- Location: #6 Ping your advisor, p. 2
- Quote: "If your adviser hasn't replied to an important email, remind them that you are waiting for their reply. (But first make sure that your email was clear."
- Key point: If your advisor hasn't replied to an important email, remind them you are waiting (but first make sure your email was clear).

### E10

- Location: #7 Feedback, p. 3
- Quote: "However, feedback is helpful and it doesn't hurt to ask for it more often, e.g., once a semester."
- Key point: Get formal feedback from your advisor at least once a year; asking more often, e.g., once a semester, doesn't hurt.

### E11

- Location: #8 Meet regularly, p. 3
- Quote: "You should meet with your advisor regularly (e.g., weekly) -- ESPECIALLY if you don't want to."
- Key point: Meet with your advisor regularly (e.g., weekly), especially when you don't want to.

### E12

- Location: #9 Make an agenda, p. 3
- Quote: "If you don't know what you want to talk about, your advisor's unlikely to know either."
- Key point: Prepare an agenda before each meeting; if you don't know what you want to discuss, your advisor won't either.

### E13

- Location: #10 Bring results, p. 3
- Quote: "Try to bring results (e.g., graphs, tables, figures) to every meeting."
- Key point: Bring results (graphs, tables) to every meeting.

### E14

- Location: #11 Start with a summary, p. 3
- Quote: "Your advisor has many research projects. Unlike you, they didn't spend the previous week working on your research project only, and will therefore need to context switch."
- Key point: Your advisor juggles many projects and has to switch context, so start each meeting by summarizing the agreed next steps from last time and what you have done since.

### E15

- Location: #14 Everyone works differently, p. 4
- Quote: "It's a good idea to discuss these preferences with your advisor so they understand you better and can work with you as effectively as possible."
- Key point: Tell your advisor about your working habits and preferences so you can work together more effectively.

### E16

- Location: #17 Make your own "next actions", p. 4
- Quote: "If you're sitting around waiting for your advisor to tell you what to do next, something is wrong. Find something to do, e.g., read papers."
- Key point: If you are sitting around waiting for your advisor to tell you what to do next, something is wrong; find your own next step.

### E17

- Location: #13 Talk to other students, p. 4
- Quote: "This is a good way to learn whether your expectations of yourself and graduate school are realistic, as well as to learn about implementation details, interesting problems and solutions, ways of thinking and problem-solving, and "tricks of the trade"."
- Key point: Talk often with students inside and outside your group; it calibrates your expectations and teaches you details, ways of thinking, and tricks.

### E18

- Location: #32 First Year, p. 6
- Quote: "As a first year, find a senior student with an interesting research project and offer to help."
- Key point: As a first-year student, find a senior student with an interesting project, offer to help, and learn from it.

### E19

- Location: #37 Ask questions, p. 7
- Quote: "Don't be afraid to ask questions and/or ask for help -- you'll learn faster and be more productive than if you try to figure absolutely everything out for yourself."
- Key point: Don't be afraid to ask questions or ask for help; you will learn faster than by figuring everything out yourself.

### E20

- Location: #45 Keep track of deadlines, p. 8
- Quote: "If you think you're not on track to meet a deadline, DO tell to your advisor -- they'd rather know sooner than later."
- Key point: If you think you will miss a deadline, tell your advisor, and the sooner the better.

### E21

- Location: #46 Finish writing early, p. 8
- Quote: "Finish your paper (at least) a week before the deadline. [...] it makes it more likely that you'll actually get any useful feedback from your advisor, who probably has several other papers to read before the deadline."
- Key point: Finish your paper at least a week before the deadline so you have time to get useful feedback from your advisor and colleagues.

### E22

- Location: #54 Ask for feedback, p. 9
- Quote: "If you give a talk (either a practice talk or a real talk ) ask your audience for feedback on clarity, style, content, presentation, etc."
- Key point: After every talk (practice talks included), ask the audience for feedback.

### E23

- Location: Progressing Through Graduate School, p. 10
- Quote: "Don't just speculate and try to compare yourself to others -- ask your advisor."
- Key point: To find out how you are doing, don't speculate or compare yourself to others; ask your advisor.
