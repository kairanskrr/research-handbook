# Cochrane 2005: Writing Tips for Ph.D. Students

- Source: `cochrane2005writing`, [Original PDF](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/phd_paper_writing.pdf)
- Version: Version of June 8, 2005, public on the author's website. Locations give section numbers and titles.

### E1

- Location: §1 Organization
- Quote: "Organize the paper in "triangular" or "newspaper" style, not in "joke" or "novel" style."
- Key point: Like a news story, a paper should put the most important result first, not save the "punch line" for the end as in a joke or a novel.

### E2

- Location: §1 Organization
- Quote: "A good paper is not a travelogue of your search process. We don't care how you came to figure out the right answer. We don't care about the hundreds of things you tried that did not work."
- Key point: A paper is not a travelogue of your search; readers don't care about the hundreds of things you tried that failed.

### E3

- Location: §2 Writing / General points
- Quote: "Document your work. A fellow graduate student must be able to sit down with your paper and all alone reproduce every number in it from instructions given in the paper, and any print or web appendices."
- Key point: Document your work: a fellow graduate student should be able to reproduce every number in the paper from the paper and its appendices alone.

### E4

- Location: §3 Tips for empirical work
- Quote: "What are the three most important things for empirical work? Identification, Identification, Identification. Describe your identification strategy clearly."
- Key point: The three most important things in empirical work are identification, identification, identification. Describe your identification strategy clearly.

### E5

- Location: §3 Tips for empirical work
- Quote: "Describe what economic mechanism caused the dispersion in your right hand variables. No, God does not hand us true natural experiments very often."
- Key point: Explain what economic mechanism caused the variation in your right-hand variables; true natural experiments are rare.

### E6

- Location: §3 Tips for empirical work
- Quote: "Consider carefully what controls should and should not be in the regression. Most papers have far too many right hand variables."
- Key point: Think carefully about which controls should and should not be in the regression; most papers have far too many right-hand variables.

### E7

- Location: §3 Tips for empirical work
- Quote: "Explain the economic significance of your results. Explain the economic magnitude of the central numbers, not just their statistical significance. Especially in large panel data sets even the tiniest of effects is "statistically significant.""
- Key point: Explain the economic significance and magnitude of your results, not just their statistical significance; in large samples even the tiniest effect is "statistically significant."

### E8

- Location: §4 Seminar presentations
- Quote: "You don't need any literature review or motivation in a seminar. Just get to the point."
- Key point: A seminar talk needs no literature review or motivation; get straight to the point.

### E9

- Location: §4 Seminar presentations
- Quote: "Listen to the questions, all the way to the end, then count to three before answering."
- Key point: Listen to the whole question, then count to three before answering.

### E10

- Location: §5 Conclusion
- Quote: "Most good economists spend at least 50% of the time they put into any project on writing."
- Key point: The author holds that good economists spend at least half the time on any project writing.

### E11

- Location: §1 Organization / Theory
- Quote: "In most papers, the "main result" is empirical. There may be some theory or a model, but if you (or the editor!) ask "does this paper expand our knowledge of economic theory?," the answer is "no." The theory is there to help understand the empirical work."
- Key point: The author holds that in most papers the main result is empirical; any theory or model is there to help readers understand the empirical results, so write only as much of it as they need for that.

### E12

- Location: §4 Seminar presentations
- Quote: "The seminar then bogs down as people start asking questions about the previewed results" / "I timed the finance workshop last winter quarter and not one paper got to the main results in under an hour!"
- Key point: In a talk, the audience interrupts with questions at any time, and a poorly organized talk gets bogged down; the author timed a quarter's finance workshop and found that not one paper reached its main results within an hour.
