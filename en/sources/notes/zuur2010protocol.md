# Zuur et al. 2010: A protocol for data exploration to avoid common statistical problems

- Source: `zuur2010protocol`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210X.2009.00001.x)
- Version: Methods in Ecology and Evolution 1(1): 3–14, 2010. Free to read on the publisher's site (Unpaywall marks it as bronze open access). The publisher's site blocks scripted access, so the text was taken from an Internet Archive full-text snapshot of 2025-02-02. The authors are statisticians who teach statistics courses to ecologists. Locations are given as section headings.

### E1

- Location: Summary, point 1
- Quote: "If a random sample of their work (including scientific papers) produced before doing these courses were selected, half would probably contain violations of the underlying assumptions of the statistical techniques employed."
- Key point: From their teaching experience, the authors estimate that if you took a random sample of the work (including papers) their course participants produced before the course, about half would violate the underlying assumptions of the statistical techniques used. This is the authors' judgment from experience, not a formal survey.

### E2

- Location: Step 8: Are observations of the response variable independent?
- Quote: "A crucial assumption of most statistical techniques is that observations are independent of one another"
- Key point: A crucial assumption of most statistical techniques is that observations are independent of one another.

### E3

- Location: Step 8
- Quote: "If birds at locations close to each other have characteristics that are more similar to each other than to birds from locations separated by larger distances, then we would violate the independence assumption."
- Key point: Example: if birds at nearby locations are more similar to each other than to birds at distant locations, the independence assumption is violated.

### E4

- Location: Step 8
- Quote: "When such dependence arises, the statistical model used to analyse the data needs to account for it."
- Key point: When observations are dependent, the statistical model must account for that dependence (for example by modeling spatial or temporal relationships, or by using a nested hierarchical structure).

### E5

- Location: Step 8
- Quote: "Quite often a residual correlation structure is caused by an important covariate that was not measured. If this is the case, it may not be possible to resolve the problem."
- Key point: Residual correlation is often caused by an important covariate that was not measured; if so, the problem may not be fixable after the fact.

### E6

- Location: Step 8
- Quote: "Hence, it is important to check whether there is dependence in the raw data before doing the analysis, and also the residuals afterwards. These checks can be made by plotting the response variable vs. time or spatial coordinates."
- Key point: Check for dependence in the raw data before the analysis and in the residuals afterward, by plotting the response variable against time or spatial coordinates.
