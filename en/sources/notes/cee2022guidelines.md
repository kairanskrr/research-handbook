# Collaboration for Environmental Evidence 2022: Guidelines and Standards for Evidence Synthesis in Environmental Management

- Source: `cee2022guidelines`, [Original](https://environmentalevidence.org/information-for-authors/)
- Version: Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (CEE), Version 5.1, 2022 (editors Pullin, Frampton, Livoreil, Petrokofsky). The official site has an access check, so the text was read from Internet Archive snapshots of May 2026: Section 1 (page note "Last updated: July 3rd 2023") and Section 9 (page note "Last updated: September 25th 2022"). Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §1.1 The CEE registration, submission and deposition process
- Quote: "Please note that CEE does not accept manuscripts of unregistered Evidence Syntheses (i.e. those without a previously registered and/or published protocol) nor does it accept retrospective protocols or registration of already completed Evidence Syntheses."
- Key point: CEE does not accept evidence syntheses without a previously registered or published protocol, nor retrospective protocols or registrations of already completed syntheses.

### E2

- Location: §9.2 Reporting conduct of evidence syntheses
- Quote: "CEE standards require a high level of reporting of the conduct of evidence syntheses so as to ensure high transparency and repeatability allowing others to test replicability of findings." / "CEE now recommends using the RepOrting standards for Systematic Evidence Syntheses (ROSES) checklist (Haddaway et al. in press) as this will be used by editors and peer reviewers when appraising reports."
- Key point: CEE requires very detailed reporting of how a synthesis was done, transparent and repeatable so others can test the findings; it recommends the ROSES reporting checklist, which editors and reviewers also use.

### E3

- Location: §9.1 The interpretation of evidence syntheses
- Quote: "Where reviews are inconclusive because there is insufficient evidence, it is important not to confuse 'no evidence of an effect' with 'evidence of no effect'."
- Key point: When a review is inconclusive for lack of evidence, do not confuse "no evidence of an effect" with "evidence of no effect."

### E4

- Location: §9.1.1 Limitations of an evidence synthesis
- Quote: "Publication bias : statistically significant results are more prone to be published than non significant ones."
- Key point: Publication bias: statistically significant results are more likely to be published than non-significant ones; such bias should be considered and reported among the review's limitations.

### E5

- Location: §9.3 Reporting findings of evidence syntheses
- Quote: "Systematic Reviews (not SMs) assess the strength of a causal inference (Hill 1971). Aspects that may be reported in the conclusion section include: 1. The quality/reliability of the included studies. 2. The relevance/external validity of the included studies. 3. The size and statistical significance of the observed effects. 4. The consistency of the effects across studies or sites"
- Key point: Systematic reviews (not systematic maps) assess the strength of a causal inference; the conclusions may report the quality and reliability of the included studies, their relevance and external validity, the size and statistical significance of the effect, whether the effect is consistent across studies or sites, and more.

### E6

- Location: §9.3.1 Implications for policy and practice
- Quote: "Authors should therefore highlight where the evidence is likely to be applicable and equally importantly where it may not be applicable with reference to variation between studies and study characteristics"
- Key point: Authors should point out where the evidence is likely to apply and, equally important, where it may not.
