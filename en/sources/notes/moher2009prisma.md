# Moher et al. 2009: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses: The PRISMA Statement

- Source: `moher2009prisma`, [PLoS Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000097) (open access)
- Version: PLoS Med 2009;6(7):e1000097. Superseded by PRISMA 2020; this handbook cites it only for its definitions of terms and its statement on protocol changes.

### E1

- Location: Terminology
- Quote: "A systematic review is a review of a clearly formulated question that uses systematic and explicit methods to identify, select, and critically appraise relevant research, and to collect and analyze data from the studies that are included in the review. Statistical methods (meta-analysis) may or may not be used to analyze and summarize the results of the included studies."
- Key point: A systematic review addresses a clearly formulated question and uses systematic, explicit methods to identify, select, and critically appraise studies and to collect and analyze data; it may or may not use statistical methods (meta-analysis).

### E2

- Location: Terminology
- Quote: "Meta-analysis refers to the use of statistical techniques in a systematic review to integrate the results of included studies."
- Key point: Meta-analysis is the use of statistical techniques in a systematic review to integrate the results of included studies.

### E3

- Location: Box 1, Completing a Systematic Review Is an Iterative Process
- Quote: "systematic reviewers may need to modify their original review protocol during its conduct. Any systematic review reporting guideline should recommend that such changes can be reported and explained without suggesting that they are inappropriate." / "Without a protocol that is publicly accessible, it is difficult to judge between appropriate and inappropriate modifications."
- Key point: Reviewers may need to modify the protocol while conducting the review; changing it is not wrong in itself, as long as the changes are reported and explained. But without a published protocol, no one can judge whether the changes were appropriate.
