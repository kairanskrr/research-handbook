# Ernst: How to write a technical paper or a research paper

- Source: `ernstwrite`, [original](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html)
- Version: Article on the author's homepage, April 2005, last updated 2024-07-01. Web page; locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: opening (introduction before the table of contents)
- Quote: "Equally importantly, writing papers and giving talks will clarify your thinking and thereby improve your research."
- Key point: Writing papers and giving talks forces you to understand your ideas more deeply and so improves the research itself.

### E2

- Location: Know your message, and stay on message
- Quote: "As a general rule, your paper needs to convince the audience of three key points. If any of these is missing or unclear, the paper will not be compelling."
- Key point: A paper must convince readers of three points: the problem is important; it is hard (obvious and existing approaches are not enough); and you have solved it. You also need to show that your contribution is new.

### E3

- Location: Know your message, and stay on message / Which details to include
- Quote: "A common mistake is to focus on what you spent the most time on."
- Key point: Do not narrate everything you tried in chronological order or allocate space by how much time you spent; the paper should tell readers the successful results and convince them the conclusions hold.

### E4

- Location: Make the organization and results clear
- Quote: "Another way of saying this is that you should give away the punchline. A technical paper is not a joke or a mystery novel."
- Key point: Give away the punchline at the start; a technical paper is not a joke or a mystery novel, and readers should meet no surprises, only deeper development of ideas already introduced.

### E5

- Location: Make the organization and results clear
- Quote: "Instead, state the point first and then support it."
- Key point: At the level of sections and paragraphs too, state the point first and then support it.

### E6

- Location: Make the organization and results clear
- Quote: "However, I prefer to write these sections early in the process (and then revise them as needed), because they frame the paper."
- Key point: Some people write the abstract and introduction last; Ernst prefers to write them early and revise as needed, because they frame the paper.

### E7

- Location: Writing style
- Quote: "Avoid puffery, self-congratulation, superlatives, and subjective or value judgments: give the objective facts and let the reader judge."
- Key point: Avoid puffery, self-congratulation, superlatives, and subjective judgments; give the objective facts and let the reader judge. A paper full of "novel" and "significant" is often covering for weak evidence.

### E8

- Location: Getting started: overcoming writer's block and procrastination
- Quote: "The key idea is to write something, and you can improve it later."
- Key point: The key to beating the blank page is to write something and improve it later; you can start by explaining it aloud, writing an outline, jotting stream-of-consciousness notes, or writing in chunks.

### E9

- Location: Know your message, and stay on message / Which details to include
- Quote: "If any part of the paper does not support your main point, then delete or change that part."
- Key point: Any part of the paper that does not support your main point, however correct, should be deleted or changed.

### E10

- Location: Brevity
- Quote: "Make every word count."
- Key point: Check every section, paragraph, sentence, and word, and delete anything that does not support the point.

### E11

- Location: Make the organization and results clear
- Quote: "Some people like to write the abstract, and often also the introduction, last. Doing so makes them easier to write, because the rest of the paper is already complete and can just be described."
- Key point: Some people write the abstract and introduction last because by then the rest of the paper is done and they just need to describe it, which makes them easier to write.
