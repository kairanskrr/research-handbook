# Bertout, Baneux, and Robertson-Plouch 2021: Recommendations for Ethical Review of Veterinary Clinical Trials

- Source: `bertout2021recommendations`, [original](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2021.715926)
- Version: Frontiers in Veterinary Science 8:715926, 2021, open access Mini Review. Discusses the US situation. The authors declare a conflict of interest (see E9). Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "While the laws and standards are clear for humans and for laboratory animals, the laws and standards for clinical research for client-owned animals are not as well-defined."
- Key point: Laws and standards are clear for human and laboratory animal research, but not for clinical research on client-owned animals.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Herein, "veterinary clinical studies" refer to clinical studies enrolling privately-owned companion animal patients (pet dogs, cats, horses, etc.,) through veterinary clinics or hospitals, similar to clinical trials with human participants. The term does not refer to studies in laboratory animals owned by a research facility."
- Key point: Here "veterinary clinical studies" means clinical studies enrolling privately owned companion animals (pet dogs, cats, horses, etc.) through veterinary clinics or hospitals, similar to human clinical trials; it excludes laboratory animals owned by research institutions.

### E3

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "because companion animal species naturally develop a number of diseases or conditions that parallel those found in humans, studies involving privately-owned pets can help fuel advances in both human and veterinary medicine."
- Key point: Companion animals naturally develop many diseases that parallel human ones, so studies with pets can advance both human and veterinary medicine.

### E4

- Location: Review, Differences in Laws and Regulations for Human and Animal Clinical Research
- Quote: "For veterinary clinical studies with client-owned animals, the laws pertaining to research are scant."
- Key point: (In the US) laws covering clinical research with client-owned animals are scant.

### E5

- Location: Review (gaps in ethical review)
- Quote: "However, the AWA laws and regulations and the IACUC review do not address some of the most critical elements of clinical research, including, as an example, the process of informed consent."
- Key point: The US Animal Welfare Act (AWA) and its regulations, and review by Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees (IACUC), do not address some of the most critical elements of clinical research, such as informed consent.

### E6

- Location: Review (gaps in ethical review)
- Quote: "There are both similarities and differences in the informed consent in human studies and in animal studies, with animal studies bearing a distinction that consent is by the owner, rather than the human participant or parent of a pediatric patient."
- Key point: In animal studies, consent is given by the owner, not by the participant or the parent of a pediatric patient; this distinguishes them from human studies.

### E7

- Location: Review (gaps in ethical review)
- Quote: "Many journals justifiably require a statement from the authors confirming that the research being submitted for publication was approved by an ethical review committee prior to study conduct. Consequently, in the absence of an ethical review, veterinary medical doctors employed in private practice may face rejection of their original research manuscripts by journals."
- Key point: Many journals require authors to state that the study was approved by an ethics committee beforehand; without ethical review, veterinarians in private practice may have original papers rejected.

### E8

- Location: Review (gaps in ethical review)
- Quote: "those doctors who are completing residencies in private practices are often applying for specialty board certification; these boards typically have a first-author publication requirement as part of the credentialing process"
- Key point: Veterinarians doing residencies in private practice often apply for specialty board certification, and these boards typically require a first-author publication.

### E9

- Location: Conflict of Interest
- Quote: "JB co-owns a veterinary clinical research organization (CASTR Alliance, Inc.) that provides clinical research services, including ethical review, for pharma- or biotech- sponsored veterinary clinical studies."
- Key point: The first author co-owns a company that provides veterinary clinical research services, including ethical review; the paper recommends expanding the role of independent ethics review boards, so readers should note this interest.

### E10

- Location: Veterinary Clinical Studies Committee
- Quote: "The AVMA policy indicates that the VCSC should ensure that informed consent is obtained from owners and that the animals enrolled in the studies are protected from conflict-of-interest issues."
- Key point: The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) policy proposes a Veterinary Clinical Study Committee (VCSC) that should ensure informed consent is obtained from owners and that enrolled animals are protected from conflicts of interest.
