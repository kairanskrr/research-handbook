# UKRIO 2023: Procedure for the Investigation of Misconduct in Research (2nd edition)

- Source: `ukrio2023procedure`, [original](https://ukrio.org/wp-content/uploads/Procedure-for-the-Investigation-of-Misconduct-in-Research_Accessible.pdf)
- Version: Published by the UK Research Integrity Office (UKRIO, a UK charity), authors James Parry and Nicola Sainsbury; version 2.0, 2023-03-10 (accessible PDF). It is a model procedure for research organizations to refer to or adopt, not law; the procedure an organization actually uses is set by its own rules.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, p. 1
- Quote: "The Procedure for the Investigation of Misconduct in Research is at the heart of, and crucial to, the aims of our charity, the UK Research Integrity Office (UKRIO)." / "The Procedure described here is designed as a model for research organisations to follow for the investigation of allegations of misconduct in research." / "organisations can use the Procedure as a benchmark when creating or revising institutional processes to investigate allegations of research misconduct or adopt it in full or in part."
- Key point: UKRIO is a charity; this procedure is a model for research organizations investigating misconduct allegations, which they can use as a reference when drafting or revising their own procedures, or adopt in whole or in part.

### E2

- Location: Purpose, Scope and Standards, paragraph 9 (p. 12)
- Quote: "A key role in the Procedure is that of the Named Person. This is the individual nominated by the Organisation (see paragraphs 236-237) to have responsibility for receiving any allegations of misconduct in research"
- Key point: A key role in the procedure is the Named Person, nominated by the organization to receive all allegations of research misconduct.

### E3

- Location: Receipt of Allegations stage, paragraph 38 (p. 23)
- Quote: "A person making an allegation or complaint will not be penalised, provided that it is done without malice and in good faith, reasonably believing it to be true."
- Key point: A person making an allegation will not be penalized, provided it is made without malice, in good faith, and in the reasonable belief that it is true.

### E4

- Location: Receipt of Allegations stage, paragraph 39 (p. 23)
- Quote: "Anyone may raise a concern relating to research misconduct; it is not limited to members of the organisation. The complainant may, in the first instance and where appropriate, attempt to address the issue with either the individual concerned or an appropriate senior colleague rather than raising a concern via this Procedure; they may also wish to seek advice from the confidential liaison point within the institution."
- Key point: Anyone may raise a concern about research misconduct, not only members of the organization; where appropriate, they may first try to address it with the person concerned or a suitable senior colleague, or first consult a confidential contact within the organization.

### E5

- Location: Receipt of Allegations stage, paragraph 40 (p. 23)
- Quote: "it is recognised that members of staff or students may fear that their own position could be jeopardised if they raise a particular concern directly." / "Depending on what is stated in an Organisation's Whistleblowing Procedure, a member of staff or a student may choose to raise a concern in the first instance with the confidential liaison point within their institution or other appropriate points referenced in the Whistleblowing Procedure and ask that person to bring the matter forward on their behalf."
- Key point: Staff or students who fear that raising a concern directly could jeopardize their position may, depending on the organization's whistleblowing policy, first raise it with a confidential contact or similar person who can pass it on for them.
