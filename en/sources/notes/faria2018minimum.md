# Faria et al. 2018: Minimum information reporting in bio–nano experimental literature

- Source: `faria2018minimum`, [original (author manuscript on PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6150419/)
- Version: Nature Nanotechnology 13(9):777–785, 2018. Read as the author accepted manuscript on PubMed Central (Europe PMC funders' manuscript, PMCID PMC6150419). The standard proposed in the paper is called MIRIBEL.

### E1

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "A significant barrier to progress in this multidisciplinary area is the variability of published literature with regards to characterisations performed and experimental details reported."
- Key point: A major barrier to progress in bio–nano research is how much published papers vary in the characterizations performed and the experimental details reported.

### E2

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "This standard consists of specific components to be reported, divided into three categories: material characterisation, biological characterisation, and details of experimental protocols."
- Key point: The MIRIBEL standard consists of specific items to report, in three categories: material characterization, biological characterization, and details of experimental protocols.

### E3

- Location: Material Characterisation — Synthesis and composition
- Quote: "one way to ensure adequate details of composition are included for a newly published nanomaterial is to provide high-quality, reproducible steps for synthesis." / "Any methods of purification used should also be specified, as the presence of precursor residues in nanoparticle mixtures may alter biological responses."
- Key point: For a newly published nanomaterial, provide high-quality, reproducible synthesis steps; also describe purification, because residual precursors may change biological responses.

### E4

- Location: Material Characterisation — Size dispersity and aggregation
- Quote: "Reporting the average size is insufficient to evaluate biological responses: consider the difference between particles with a uniform distribution in size from 10 to 990 nm versus those that range from 495 to 505 nm."
- Key point: Reporting only the average size is not enough: particles uniformly distributed from 10 to 990 nm and particles from 495 to 505 nm both average 500 nm but can behave very differently.

### E5

- Location: Material Characterisation — Size, shape, and dimensions
- Quote: "The protocol used to measure size and the “type” of size measured (e.g. geometric or hydrodynamic) should be provided"
- Key point: Provide the protocol used to measure size and the "type" of size measured (e.g. geometric or hydrodynamic).

### E6

- Location: Material Characterisation — General guidelines
- Quote: "Secondly, detailing how a parameter was assessed (i.e. what technique, equipment, and protocol were used) is essential."
- Key point: State how each parameter was assessed (what technique, equipment, and protocol were used).

### E7

- Location: Material Characterisation — General guidelines
- Quote: "Fourthly, characterising a property using two (or more) independent methods is of significantly more value"
- Key point: Characterizing a property with two or more independent methods is of significantly more value.

### E8

- Location: Material Characterisation — General guidelines
- Quote: "it is important to detail whether characterisation variance was determined through multiple independent measurements on the same batch of material or whether multiple batches were prepared."
- Key point: State whether characterization variance came from repeated measurements on the same batch of material or from preparing multiple batches.

### E9

- Location: Material Characterisation — General guidelines
- Quote: "To the degree possible, material characterisation should be performed in a medium relevant to the fluid used in the subsequent bio–nano experiment."
- Key point: As far as possible, characterize the material in a medium similar to the fluid used in the subsequent bio–nano experiment.

### E10

- Location: Material Characterisation — General guidelines
- Quote: "Thirdly, nearly all material characterisation parameters occur across a distribution, and thus including a distribution of the measured property is more informative than providing a single average value."
- Key point: Nearly all characterization parameters occur across a distribution, so reporting the distribution is more informative than reporting a single average.

### E11

- Location: Experimental Protocol Details — Details of data analysis
- Quote: "the number of independent experiments (n), and details of how uncertainty is expressed (e.g. standard deviation, standard error, or confidence intervals) should be provided."
- Key point: Report the number of independent experiments (n) and how uncertainty is expressed (standard deviation, standard error, or confidence intervals).

### E12

- Location: Experimental Protocol Details — General guidelines
- Quote: "Whenever possible, we recommend that raw data are provided for published bio–nano experimental reports, either as part of the article submission or in a trusted data repository"
- Key point: Whenever possible, provide the raw data, either with the article submission or in a trusted data repository.

### E13

- Location: Summary and Outlook
- Quote: "proposing a standard, or agreeing that standardisation is important, is insufficient to improve the field. To be useful, any standard must be widely adopted by the community"
- Key point: Proposing a standard, or agreeing that standardization matters, is not enough to improve the field; a standard is useful only if it is widely adopted.

### E14

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "This checklist is designed to be used while preparing a manuscript to ensure that each component is considered as appropriate."
- Key point: The accompanying checklist is meant to be used while preparing a manuscript, to make sure each item is considered.

### E15

- Location: Defining the Scope of a Minimum Information Standard
- Quote: "One option is to consider a material as nanoengineered when it has at least one functional dimension on the nanoscale (i.e. from 1 to 100 nm)."
- Key point: One option is to count a material as nanoengineered when it has at least one functional dimension on the nanoscale (1–100 nm).

### E16

- Location: Biological Characterisation — ARRIVE guidelines (in vivo)
- Quote: "For in vivo experiments, the Animals in Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines should be followed."
- Key point: In vivo experiments should follow the Animals in Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines.
