# Widom 2006: Tips for Writing Technical Papers

- Source: `widomtips`, [original](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html)
- Version: Stanford InfoLab talk notes, January 2006 (minor revisions 2009). Web page; locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: The Introduction
- Quote: "By the time a referee has finished the Introduction, they've probably made an initial decision about whether to accept or reject the paper"
- Key point: By the time referees finish the Introduction, they have probably made an initial decision, and they read the rest looking for evidence to support it, so the Introduction is crucial.

### E2

- Location: The Introduction
- Quote: "Unless there's a good argument against it, the Introduction should consist of five paragraphs answering the following five questions:"
- Key point: The Introduction answers five questions: What is the problem? Why is it interesting and important? Why is it hard? Why hasn't it been solved before (what is wrong with existing approaches)? What are the key components of my approach and results (including limitations)?

### E3

- Location: The Introduction
- Quote: "It should list the major contributions in bullet form, mentioning in which sections they can be found."
- Key point: The last paragraph of the Introduction lists the major contributions as bullets, with the sections where each can be found.

### E4

- Location: Related Work
- Quote: "Beginning, if it can be short yet detailed enough, or if it's critical to take a strong defensive stance about previous work right away."
- Key point: Related work can go at the beginning or the end: at the beginning if it can be short yet detailed enough, or if you must distinguish yourself from previous work right away; at the end if the comparison requires the technical content first.

### E5

- Location: The Body
- Quote: "Don't, however, fall into the common trap of telling the entire story of how you arrived at your results. Just tell the story of the results themselves."
- Key point: Don't tell the story of how you arrived at your results; tell the story of the results themselves.

### E6

- Location: The Body
- Quote: "A clear new important technical contribution should have been articulated by the time the reader finishes page 3"
- Key point: By about a quarter of the way through the paper (page 3), the reader should have seen a clear, new, important contribution.

### E7

- Location: The Abstract
- Quote: "State the problem, your approach and solution, and the main contributions of the paper."
- Key point: The abstract states the problem, your approach and solution, and the main contributions, with little or no background and motivation.

### E8

- Location: The Introduction
- Quote: "What is the problem?" / "Why is it interesting and important?" / "Why is it hard? (E.g., why do naive approaches fail?)" / "Why hasn't it been solved before? (Or, what's wrong with previous proposed solutions? How does mine differ?)" / "What are the key components of my approach and results? Also include any specific limitations."
- Key point: The Introduction's five questions: What is the problem? Why is it interesting and important? Why is it hard? Why hasn't it been solved before (what is wrong with previous solutions, and how does mine differ)? What are the key components of my approach and results, including specific limitations?

### E9

- Location: Related Work
- Quote: "End, if it can be summarized quickly early on (in the Introduction or Preliminaries), or if sufficient comparisons require the technical content of the paper."
- Key point: Put related work at the end if it can be summarized briefly early on (in the Introduction), or if a proper comparison requires the paper's technical content first.
