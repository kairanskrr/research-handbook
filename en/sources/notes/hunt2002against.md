# Hunt 2002: Against Presentism

- Source: `hunt2002against`, [original (Internet Archive snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/20220918163213/https://www.historians.org/publications-and-directories/perspectives-on-history/may-2002/against-presentism)
- Version: Lynn Hunt, then president of the American Historical Association, in her president's column in the AHA's *Perspectives on History*, May 2002. The official site returns 403 to scripted access, so this excerpt is from an Internet Archive snapshot of 2022-09-18 (old URL `historians.org/publications-and-directories/perspectives-on-history/may-2002/against-presentism`). The essay has no sections; locations describe the paragraph's topic.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Yet presentism besets us in two different ways: (1) the tendency to interpret the past in presentist terms; and (2) the shift of general historical interest toward the contemporary period and away from the more distant past."
- Key point: "Presentism" shows up in two ways: interpreting the past in present-day terms and standards, and a shift of historical interest toward the contemporary period and away from the more distant past.

### E2

- Location: Paragraph on moral superiority
- Quote: "Interpreting the past in terms of present concerns usually leads us to find ourselves morally superior"
- Key point: Interpreting the past in terms of present concerns usually leads us to feel morally superior.

### E3

- Location: Final paragraph
- Quote: "But it is possible to remind ourselves of the virtues of maintaining a fruitful tension between present concerns and respect for the past."
- Key point: There is no ready-made cure for presentism, but we can remind ourselves to maintain a fruitful tension between present concerns and respect for the past.

### E4

- Location: Final paragraph
- Quote: "The emergence of new concerns in the present invariably reveals aspects of historical experience that have been occluded or forgotten."
- Key point: New concerns in the present also reveal aspects of historical experience that were occluded or forgotten. Present-day concerns are not bad in themselves.
