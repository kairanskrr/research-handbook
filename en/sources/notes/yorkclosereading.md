# University of York Department of English and Related Literature: Close reading

- Source: `yorkclosereading`, [original](https://www.york.ac.uk/english/about/writing-at-york/writing-resources/close-reading/)
- Version: Writing resource page of the Department of English at the University of York, UK, free and public; accessed October 2026

### E1

- Location: Opening (quoting Showalter)
- Quote: "...slow reading, a deliberate attempt to detach ourselves from the magical power of story-telling and pay attention to language, imagery, allusion, intertextuality, syntax and form."
- Key point: Close reading is slow reading: detaching yourself from the pull of the story and paying attention to language, imagery, allusion, intertextuality, syntax, and form.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "It’s not reading between the lines, but reading further and further into the lines"
- Key point: Close reading is not reading between the lines but reading further and further into the lines.

### E3

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Accurate transcription of quotations is, for some, the first and last rule of close reading."
- Key point: Accurate transcription of quotations is the first rule of close reading.

### E4

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "Close reading has been criticised for being divorced from context and for pulling away from the historical and political engagements of the literary text."
- Key point: Close reading has been criticized for being divorced from context and pulling away from the text's historical and political engagements.

### E5

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "we are looking for close readings, not closed readings."
- Key point: The aim is close readings, not closed readings.

### E6

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "don’t simply list everything you have found; craft it into an argument"
- Key point: Don't list your findings; craft them into an argument.

### E7

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "Your analysis of your quotation should be twice as long as the quotation itself."
- Key point: The "rule of two": your analysis of a quotation should be twice as long as the quotation itself.

### E8

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Work from the actual text in front of you, not from a sort of mental paraphrase of what the text says."
- Key point: Work from the actual text in front of you, not from a mental paraphrase of it.

### E9

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "for essays it is often a good technique to do the close reading first and then to use very short extracts or even single words to demonstrate your insights."
- Key point: Do the close reading separately first; in the essay, use very short extracts or even single words to show your insights.

### E10

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Think of close reading as something that you do with a pencil and book in your hand. Mark up the pages; fill the margins."
- Key point: Close reading is done with a pencil in hand: mark up the pages and fill the margins.

### E11

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "If your passage isn’t transcribed meticulously, down to the last comma and (with poetry) spacing on the page, you can’t read it closely."
- Key point: Transcription must be exact down to the last comma, and for poetry down to the spacing on the page, or you cannot read closely.

### E12

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Read the passage aloud, paying close attention to the rhythms of sentences."
- Key point: Read the passage aloud, paying attention to the rhythms of the sentences.

### E13

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "it’s important not only to detect certain features but also to consider their effects."
- Key point: Don't just detect features; consider their effects.

### E14

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "don’t simply list everything you have found; craft it into an argument, and be prepared to downplay or leave out some of the elements you have spotted if they don’t relate to the larger picture."
- Key point: Don't list your findings; craft them into an argument, and be prepared to downplay or drop findings that don't relate to the larger picture.

### E15

- Location: Close, not closed readings
- Quote: "vary the lengths of quoted textual evidence so that an essay doesn't end up with only very brief quotations or long block quotations"
- Key point: Vary the length of quotations, so the essay doesn't end up with only very brief quotations or only long block quotations.

### E16

- Location: Some questions you may like to ask
- Quote: "the writer can assume that their reader knows the text and doesn't need extensive plot summary in the introduction or start of the essay."
- Key point: You can assume the reader knows the text; no lengthy plot summary is needed at the start.

### E17

- Location: Some questions you may like to ask
- Quote: "Are there words or phrases which are ambiguous (could mean more than one thing)? If so, are we directed to privilege one reading over the other or do we keep both in play?"
- Key point: Watch for ambiguous words and phrases: are we directed to privilege one reading, or kept with both in play?

### E18

- Location: Some questions you may like to ask
- Quote: "Have you been to the dictionary (remember the full Oxford English Dictionary is available online through the library)?"
- Key point: Consult the dictionary (the handout notes that the full Oxford English Dictionary is available online through the library).

### E19

- Location: Extra Reading
- Quote: "remember you can close read secondary as well as primary texts"
- Key point: Secondary texts (criticism) can be close read too.
