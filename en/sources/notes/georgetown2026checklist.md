# Georgetown Law Writing Center 2026: Checklist for Scholarly Writing

- Source: `georgetown2026checklist`, [Original PDF (Writing Center, Georgetown University Law Center, US; free)](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/academics/wp-content/uploads/sites/58/2026/10/Checklist-for-Scholarly-Writing_fcd.pdf)
- Version: Handout from the Writing Center at Georgetown University Law Center, revised by Alexandra Kotter in 2026, citing Volokh's Academic Legal Writing in many places. Written for **US law students**. Locations are given as checklist part numbers.

### E1

- Location: I. Pre-Writing and Planning
- Quote: "Tip: If you can't state your thesis in one to two sentences with reasons to support your main claim, your paper isn't ready to draft."
- Key point: If you can't state your thesis and its main supporting reasons in one or two sentences, your paper isn't ready to draft.

### E2

- Location: II. Structure and Organization
- Quote: "Is there a comprehensive roadmap of the entire paper?"
- Key point: The introduction should include a roadmap of the entire paper.

### E3

- Location: II. Structure and Organization
- Quote: "Are all points of view accurately presented and responded to?" / "Have you directly addressed counterarguments?"
- Key point: Present all points of view accurately and respond to them; address counterarguments directly.

### E4

- Location: III. Analysis
- Quote: "Is the writing analytical and creative, offering new insights, rather than providing a description?" / "If you can't answer, you're likely summarizing, not analyzing."
- Key point: Writing should be analytical and offer new insights, not description; the handout suggests asking "so what?" after each paragraph, and if you can't answer, you are probably summarizing rather than analyzing.
