The publisher marks the article as free to read (contentStatus "Free"). Most authors are from Ottawa, Canada. Locations give the section name.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Although systematic reviews of diagnostic test accuracy studies share elements with those of intervention studies, there are important differences. Study design and measures of effect differ from those of randomized clinical trials. Accuracy can differ between studies due to differences in patients, setting, prior testing, and use of different reference standards. Consequently, the methods for evaluating risk of bias, summarizing results, and exploring variability for diagnostic test accuracy studies differ from those used for intervention studies."
- Key point: Systematic reviews of diagnostic test accuracy differ in important ways from reviews of interventions: study designs and effect measures differ from those of randomized controlled trials; accuracy varies between studies with patients, settings, prior tests, and reference standards; so the methods for assessing risk of bias, pooling results, and exploring variation differ too.

### E2

- Location: Results (explanation of new item D2)
- Quote: "Meta-analyses of diagnostic test accuracy studies typically require multivariate models (eg, bivariate and hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic), which allow for the tradeoff between sensitivity and specificity due to the positivity threshold, for potential correlation between estimates of sensitivity and specificity across studies, and for variability through the inclusion of random effects." / "Traditional univariate methods ignore this correlation and can give misleading results."
- Key point: Meta-analyses of diagnostic test accuracy typically require multivariate models (e.g., bivariate models), which handle the trade-off between sensitivity and specificity across positivity thresholds, their possible correlation across studies, and between-study variation; traditional univariate methods ignore this correlation and can give misleading results.
