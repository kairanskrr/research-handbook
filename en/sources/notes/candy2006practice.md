# Candy 2006: Practice Based Research: A Guide

- Source: `candy2006practice`, [Original PDF](https://www.creativityandcognition.com/resources/PBR%20Guide-1.1-2006.pdf) (the direct link on the official site did not respond to scripts; the text was taken from the same PDF in an Internet Archive snapshot under `web.archive.org/web/2020id_/`)
- Version: CCS Report 2006-V1.0 of the Creativity & Cognition Studios at the University of Technology, Sydney, Australia, November 2006, by Linda Candy. Written for graduate students on practice-based PhD theses. Locations are given as subheadings and page numbers.

### E1

- Location: Summary, p. 1
- Quote: "If a creative artefact is the basis of the contribution to knowledge, the research is practice-based. 2. If the research leads primarily to new understandings about practice, it is practice-led."
- Key point: Practice-related research comes in two kinds: if a creative artefact is the basis of the contribution to knowledge, the research is practice-based; if it leads primarily to new understandings about practice, it is practice-led.

### E2

- Location: Summary, p. 1
- Quote: "Whilst the significance and context of the claims are described in words, a full understanding can only be obtained with direct reference to the outcomes."
- Key point: In practice-based research, the significance and context of the claims are described in words, but full understanding requires direct reference to the outcomes themselves.

### E3

- Location: Practice Led Research, p. 3
- Quote: "In a doctoral thesis, the results of practice-led research may be fully described in text form without the inclusion of a creative outcome. [...] Such research includes practice as an integral part of its method and often falls within the general area of action research."
- Key point: A practice-led doctoral thesis may be written entirely in text without a creative outcome; such research often falls within the broad area of action research.

### E4

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2
- Quote: "However, this kind of research is, for the most part, directed towards the individual’s particular goals of the time rather than seeking to add to our shared store of knowledge in a more general sense."
- Key point: Practitioners' everyday "research" mostly serves their own goals of the moment rather than adding to our shared store of knowledge; that is what sets it apart from research.

### E5

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2
- Quote: "However, the outcomes of practice must be accompanied by documentation of the research process, as well as some form of textual analysis or explanation to support its position and to demonstrate critical reflection."
- Key point: The outcomes of practice must be accompanied by documentation of the research process and some form of textual analysis or explanation, to support its position and demonstrate critical reflection.

### E6

- Location: Practice-Based Research, p. 3
- Quote: "Practice-based doctoral submissions must include a substantial contextualisation of the creative work."
- Key point: Practice-based doctoral submissions must include a substantial contextualization of the creative work.

### E7

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2
- Quote: "It must define a series of research questions or problems that will be addressed in the course of the research. [...] It must specify a research context for the questions or problems to be addressed. [...] It must specify the research methods for addressing and answering the research questions or problems."
- Key point: The 2000 definition of the UK Arts and Humanities Research Board (AHRB, now the Arts and Humanities Research Council): research must have three elements, research questions, a research context, and research methods (as summarized by Candy).

### E8

- Location: The PhD and Knowledge, p. 5
- Quote: "That new knowledge is expected to have two characteristics: it is shared and it can be verified or challenged."
- Key point: New knowledge at doctoral level is expected to have two characteristics: it is shared, and it can be verified or challenged.

### E9

- Location: The PhD and Knowledge, Preliminaries, p. 5
- Quote: "That we have achieved new implicit or tacit knowledge is clearly not relevant because, by definition, it is not shared."
- Key point: Tacit knowledge that exists only in one person and is not shared does not count as knowledge in the sense of doctoral research.

### E10

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q4, p. 8
- Quote: "Keep a written record either in the form of an online diary or blog, or a hand written notebook. [...] Do not be tempted to only review and reflect on an ad hoc basis. Build it in to your timetable."
- Key point: Record your reflection continuously in an online diary, a blog, or a handwritten notebook; schedule reflection rather than doing it only ad hoc.

### E11

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q5, p. 9
- Quote: "The artefact is not an explanation in itself: - it requires linguistic description that relates the development and nature of the artefact to understandings about creative process"
- Key point: The artefact is not an explanation in itself; it needs a written description relating its development and nature to an understanding of the creative process.

### E12

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q1, p. 8
- Quote: "Do not quote someone’s reference to someone else’s work: always go to the original text."
- Key point: In a literature review, do not quote someone's reference to someone else's work; always go to the original text.

### E13

- Location: Ethical Considerations, p. 9
- Quote: "Researchers must obtain the informed consent of persons participating in research before the research begins."
- Key point: When research involves people, obtain participants' informed consent before the research begins.

### E14

- Location: Domain Differences in Practice-based and Practice-led Research, p. 3
- Quote: "In design research, for example, where the nature of practice is a major research topic and is often conducted by research specialists rather than design practitioners, the emphasis is on achieving new knowledge about the nature of practice and how to improve it, rather than creating and reflecting on new artefacts. [...] By contrast, in the visual arts, the emphasis is on creative process and the works that are generated from that process: the artefact plays a vital part in the new understandings about practice that arise."
- Key point: Fields differ in emphasis: design research mostly studies how practice works and how to improve it, while visual arts research focuses more on the creative process and the works.

### E15

- Location: Historical Background, p. 4
- Quote: "It would be required that the thesis, as lodged in the Library, would include a permanent record of any artefacts submitted towards the examination."
- Key point: The thesis deposited in the library must include a permanent record of all artefacts submitted for examination.

### E16

- Location: Practice-Based and Practice-Led Research, p. 3
- Quote: "are often used interchangeably."
- Key point: "Practice-based" and "practice-led" are often used interchangeably.

### E17

- Location: Thesis Outline, 5. New Studies, p. 7
- Quote: "In a practice based PhD an artwork, for example, might be presented for examination. If so, this chapter will illuminate it by explaining, at the very least, what is important and novel"
- Key point: If a work is submitted for examination, a core chapter of the thesis must explain what is important and novel about it.

### E18

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2 (summarizing the 2000 research definition of the UK Arts and Humanities Research Board, AHRB)
- Quote: "It must define a series of research questions or problems that will be addressed in the course of the research. It must also define its objectives in terms of seeking to enhance knowledge and understanding relating to the questions or problems to be addressed."
- Key point: The first element of a research plan: define the research questions to be addressed, and frame the objectives as enhancing knowledge and understanding of those questions.

### E19

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2 (as above)
- Quote: "It must specify a research context for the questions or problems to be addressed. It must specify why it is important that these particular questions or problems should be addressed, what other research is being or has been conducted in this area and what particular contribution this project will make"
- Key point: The second element: specify the research context, that is, why these questions matter, what other research has been done in the area, and what particular contribution the project will make.

### E20

- Location: Practice and Research, p. 2 (as above)
- Quote: "It must specify the research methods for addressing and answering the research questions or problems. [...] It should also explain the rationale for the chosen research methods and why they provide the most appropriate means by which to answer the research questions."
- Key point: The third element: specify the methods for answering the questions and explain why they are the most appropriate.

### E21

- Location: Practice-Based Research, p. 3
- Quote: "Practice-based doctoral submissions must include a substantial contextualisation of the creative work. This critical appraisal or analysis not only clarifies the basis of the claim for the originality and location of the original work, it also provides the basis for a judgement as to whether general scholarly requirements are met."
- Key point: Practice-based doctoral submissions must include a substantial contextualization of the creative work; this critical appraisal both clarifies the basis of the originality claim and the work's place, and provides the basis for judging whether general academic requirements are met.

### E22

- Location: Thesis Outline, 1. Introduction, p. 7
- Quote: "i.The Problem This is a concise statement of the research question or issue that the thesis addresses. ii.The Context What is the main work that has been done that gives rise to the question and what is its significance? iii.The Method"
- Key point: In Candy's suggested thesis structure, the introduction briefly states the problem, the context, the method (and the outcomes).

### E23

- Location: Thesis Outline, 1. Introduction, p. 7
- Quote: "The value of these outcomes will be to one or more community (computer scientists, artists, theoreticians etc) and it is important to be clear who they are."
- Key point: Be clear which community or communities the outcomes are valuable to.

### E24

- Location: Thesis Outline, 2. State of the Art Review, p. 7
- Quote: "It should be a critical review in the context of the stated research question and related issues. This chapter answers questions such as: Who is doing what? Who has done what? Who first did it or published it? The survey is taken from published papers, research monographs, catalogues etc."
- Key point: The state-of-the-art review should be a critical review framed by the research question, answering "who is doing what, who has done what, who did or published it first"; sources include papers, monographs, catalogues, and the like.

### E25

- Location: Thesis Outline, 2. State of the Art Review, p. 7
- Quote: "It must be based on and refer to primary sources, not textbooks or other such reports on the work of others. It is to be expected that this chapter provides a new structured view of the field of study."
- Key point: The review must be based on primary sources, not textbooks or others' reports, and this chapter should offer a new structured view of the field.

### E26

- Location: Thesis Outline, 3. Methodology, p. 7
- Quote: "This is a key chapter that provides a description and justification of the research methods used. Normally, the methods will be selected from known and proven examples."
- Key point: The methods chapter describes and justifies the methods used, normally selected from known and proven ones.

### E27

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q3, p. 8
- Quote: "1. start with the actual things you have done/will do (as if instructing an assistant) 2. then add a brief description of the origins of the method(s) - sources from which they have been selected 3. if any aspect is not yet justified develop the argument to justify it."
- Key point: Writing the methods: first state what you actually did or will do (as if instructing an assistant), then briefly describe where the methods come from, and finally argue only for the parts not yet justified.

### E28

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q3, p. 8
- Quote: "If the argument includes negatives (\"statistics is no good for this\" or \"semiotics does not work in this case\") be sure that you are knowledgeable about what you are rejecting."
- Key point: If your argument rejects a method (for example, "statistics is no good for this"), make sure you understand what you are rejecting.

### E29

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q4, p. 8
- Quote: "Keep a written record either in the form of an online diary or blog, or a hand written notebook. • Having a notebook with you at all times to record any observations or events during the process of making a work is advisable."
- Key point: Keep a record in an online diary, a blog, or a handwritten notebook; carrying a notebook at all times to note observations and events while making a work is advisable.

### E30

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q4, p. 8
- Quote: "If you are collecting audio records, you will probably need to transcribe them at some point and may need to factor that in to your time and effort schedule. • Keep an overview chart to be able to see at a glance what has been recorded and what you plan to record."
- Key point: Audio recordings will probably need transcribing, so build that into your schedule; keep an overview chart showing at a glance what has been recorded and what you plan to record.

### E31

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q4, p. 8
- Quote: "It is advisable to designate time for reflection after the events and to record your reactions and emerging thoughts at specified Review points. Do not be tempted to only review and reflect on an ad hoc basis. Build it in to your timetable."
- Key point: Set aside time for reflection after events and record your reactions and emerging thoughts at specified review points; schedule reflection rather than doing it only ad hoc.

### E32

- Location: Questions and Answers, Q4, p. 8
- Quote: "Good questions to ask yourself are: - what was proposed, discussed, decided and carried through, - what stumbling blocks arose and how they were addressed…., - whether the ideas were workable, interesting, challenging…. - whether the collaboration worked well or not - reasons for success or otherwise - did the solutions work well, if not why not? - whether there were different viewpoints between you and your collaborating parties - whether lessons were learnt from failures."
- Key point: Questions to ask yourself when reflecting: what was proposed, discussed, decided, and carried through; what stumbling blocks arose and how they were handled; whether the ideas were workable and interesting; whether the collaboration worked well; why things succeeded or failed; whether solutions were effective; whether there were disagreements with collaborators; and whether lessons were learned from failures.

### E33

- Location: Ethical Considerations, p. 8
- Quote: "The first stage in designing a research project is to prepare a research proposal which outlines the proposed methodology and any ethical issues. Researchers must obtain the informed consent of persons participating in research before the research begins"
- Key point: The first stage of designing a project is a research proposal outlining the methodology and any ethical issues; obtain participants' informed consent before the research begins.

### E34

- Location: Domain Differences in Practice-based and Practice-led Research, p. 3
- Quote: "In design research, for example, where the nature of practice is a major research topic and is often conducted by research specialists rather than design practitioners, the emphasis is on achieving new knowledge about the nature of practice and how to improve it, rather than creating and reflecting on new artefacts."
- Key point: Design research mostly studies how design practice works and how to improve it, often carried out by research specialists, rather than creating and reflecting on new works.
