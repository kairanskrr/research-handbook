# Bertrand, Duflo & Mullainathan 2004: How Much Should We Trust Differences-in-Differences Estimates?

- Source: `bertrand2004trust`, [NBER working paper PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8841/w8841.pdf); published in Quarterly Journal of Economics 119(1), 2004
- Version: NBER Working Paper 8841 (March 2002). Excerpts follow the working paper; numbers in the published version may differ slightly.

### E1

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "To illustrate the severity of this issue, we randomly generate placebo laws in state-level data on female wages from the Current Population Survey. For each law, we use OLS to compute the DD estimate of its "effect" as well as the standard error for this estimate. The standard errors are severely biased: with about 20 years of data, DD estimation finds an "effect" significant at the 5% level of up to 45% of the placebo laws."
- Key point: The authors randomly generate nonexistent "placebo laws" in state-level data on women's wages from the US Current Population Survey and estimate their "effects" with standard difference-in-differences. With about 20 years of data, up to 45% of the placebo laws come out "significant" at the 5% level, showing that the standard errors are severely understated.

### E2

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "We also suggest a third technique, based on randomization inference testing methods, which works well irrespective of sample size. This technique uses the empirical distribution of estimated effects for placebo laws to form the test distribution."
- Key point: The authors' third suggested remedy: use the estimated effects of many placebo laws as the test distribution (randomization inference), which works well at any sample size.
