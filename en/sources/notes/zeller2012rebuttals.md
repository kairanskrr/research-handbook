# Zeller 2012: Patterns for writing good rebuttals

- Source: `zeller2012rebuttals`, [original](https://andreas-zeller.info/2012/10/01/patterns-for-writing-good-rebuttals.html)
- Version: Blog post, 2012, based on the author's experience as a program committee chair of software engineering conferences.

### E1

- Location: Identify the champion.
- Quote: "If there’s no potential champion, chances of getting your paper accepted are slim."
- Key point: Without a potential champion, the chances of your paper being accepted are slim.

### E2

- Location: Arm the champion.
- Quote: "Refute every single issue raised by the detractors to give your champion extra arguments for acceptance."
- Key point: Refute every issue raised by the detractors, giving your champion arguments for the discussion.

### E3

- Location: Write for the committee.
- Quote: "If there’s one thing you want the committee to know, say so in the very beginning (because that’s all most reviewers will read)."
- Key point: Put the one thing you most want the committee to know at the very beginning, because that is all most reviewers will read.

### E4

- Location: Convince.
- Quote: "You don’t need to provide the full change; just sketch the change you will make."
- Key point: You need not provide the full change, but sketch concretely how you will change the paper, rather than just saying "thanks, we will add that."

### E5

- Location: Choose comments wisely.
- Quote: "You do not need to convince the reviewer that you’re able to fix typos, straight-forward presentation issues, language issues, or anything else that can be fixed by simple proofreading."
- Key point: Don't argue in the rebuttal about typos or simple presentation and language issues; focus on major concerns.

### E6

- Location: Write for the PC chair.
- Quote: "If all reviewers misunderstood your paper, the problem is not the reviewers."
- Key point: If all reviewers misunderstood your paper, the problem is not the reviewers.

### E7

- Location: Thank the reviewers.
- Quote: "Reviewing is hard work and even the least significant review contains grains of truth which will be helpful for your future work."
- Key point: Reviewing is hard work, and even the weakest review contains something useful to you. The rebuttal is not the place to vent.

### E8

- Location: Don’t expect too much.
- Quote: "Your rebuttal will not save your paper. Your rebuttal will play a role only if your paper is on the edge, or if reviewers made very obvious mistakes."
- Key point: A rebuttal will not save your paper; it matters only when the paper is on the edge or the reviewers made obvious mistakes.

### E9

- Location: Don’t expect too much.
- Quote: "But regardless of the outcome, your rebuttal will help to improve your paper and eventually get it accepted - if not now, there’s always another deadline on the horizon."
- Key point: Whatever the outcome, writing the rebuttal helps you improve the paper; if not this time, there is always another deadline.

### E10

- Location: Organize your rebuttal.
- Quote: "Make it easy for reviewers to find their questions (and your arguments). Refer to reviewers by R1, R2, …; questions by R1Q1, R1Q2, …"
- Key point: Number reviewers and questions (R1, R1Q1, ...) so each reviewer can find their questions and your answers.

### E11

- Location: Identify the undecided.
- Quote: "These are reviewers who may be swayed by your arguments. [...] Focus your arguments on these reviewers."
- Key point: Undecided reviewers who could be swayed are the ones a rebuttal should focus on.

### E12

- Location: Identify the detractors.
- Quote: "A strong detractor can only be countered by a strong champion. Rather than trying to dissuade a strong detractor, your aim should be on arming the champion"
- Key point: A strong detractor can only be countered by a strong champion; rather than trying to change the detractor's mind, arm the champion with arguments.

### E13

- Location: Write for the PC chair.
- Quote: "If your reviewers have done a bad job, say so in the beginning, and the PC chair may assign you another reviewer. Stick to facts [...] that the PC chair can check in less than a minute."
- Key point: If a reviewer clearly did a bad job, you may say so at the beginning, but stick to facts the program committee chair can check in under a minute.

### E14

- Location: Choose comments wisely.
- Quote: "Focus on major concerns - the ones where the reviewer assumes you may not be able to improve the paper."
- Key point: Focus on the major concerns, the ones where the reviewer assumes you may not be able to fix the paper.
