# Mahoney 2005: Clarifying Comparative-Historical Methodology

- Source: `mahoney2005clarifying`, [full text](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.998194)
- Version: Qualitative Methods (newsletter of the American Political Science Association's Qualitative Methods section, later renamed Qualitative & Multi-Method Research) 3(1), pp. 19–22, Spring 2005; open archive on Zenodo (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). The author was then at Brown University (US). This is a response to an article by Kittel and represents the author's own views. Locations give the newsletter page number.

### E1

- Location: p. 20
- Quote: "Comparative-historical analysis stands between these two alternatives; it is a mode of research in which scholars compare multiple cases in the effort to formulate causal generalizations about those cases."
- Key point: Comparative-historical analysis stands between single-case studies and large-N studies: scholars compare multiple cases in an effort to generalize causal conclusions about those cases.

### E2

- Location: p. 20
- Quote: "Rather, the generalizations are bounded by particular scope conditions that define comparable cases."
- Key point: These causal generalizations are not universal laws; they are bounded by scope conditions that define which cases are comparable.

### E3

- Location: p. 21
- Quote: "Still, if one is working with a very small N (e.g., N = 2 or 3), then one would have to draw probabilistic inferences by relying primarily on within-case analysis rather than cross-case comparison."
- Key point: With very few cases (e.g., two or three), inference relies mainly on within-case analysis rather than on comparison across cases.

### E4

- Location: p. 20
- Quote: "In the field of comparative historical analysis, substantive knowledge accumulation has occurred across diverse areas, including studies of revolutions, welfare states, and democracy and authoritarianism"
- Key point: Comparative-historical analysis has accumulated substantive knowledge on diverse topics, including revolutions, welfare states, and democracy and authoritarianism.
