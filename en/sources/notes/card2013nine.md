# Card & DellaVigna 2013: Nine Facts about Top Journals in Economics

- Source: `card2013nine`, [NBER working paper PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18665/w18665.pdf); final version published in Journal of Economic Literature 51(1)
- Version: NBER Working Paper 18665 (January 2013). Data through 2012.

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction, paragraph 1
- Quote: "Publications in the top journals have a powerful influence on the direction of research in economics, on the career paths of young researchers, and on the pay of academic economists."
- Key point: Publication in the top journals strongly shapes the direction of research in economics, the career paths of young researchers, and the pay of academic economists.

### E2

- Location: §1 Introduction, paragraph 1
- Quote: "In this paper we present a descriptive overview of trends among the papers published in the "top-5" economics journals: the American Economic Review (AER), Econometrica (EMA), the Journal of Political Economy (JPE), the Quarterly Journal of Economics (QJE), and the Review of Economic Studies (RES)."
- Key point: The "top five" are the American Economic Review (AER), Econometrica, the Journal of Political Economy (JPE), the Quarterly Journal of Economics (QJE), and the Review of Economic Studies.

### E3

- Location: §1 Introduction, paragraph 2
- Quote: "The combination of rising submissions and falling publications led to a sharp fall in the aggregate acceptance rate, from around 15% in 1980 to 6% today."
- Key point: With rising submissions and falling publications, the aggregate acceptance rate of the top five fell from about 15% in 1980 to about 6% around 2012.

### E4

- Location: §1 Introduction, paragraph 2
- Quote: "Fifth, the number of authors per paper has increased monotonically from 1.3 in 1970 to 2.3 in 2012, partly offsetting the decrease in the number of articles published per year."
- Key point: The average number of authors per paper rose from 1.3 in 1970 to 2.3 in 2012.
