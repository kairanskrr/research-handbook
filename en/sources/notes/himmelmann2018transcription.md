# Himmelmann 2018: Meeting the transcription challenge

- Source: `himmelmann2018transcription`, [original](http://hdl.handle.net/10125/24806)
- Version: Language Documentation & Conservation Special Publication 15 (Reflections on Language Documentation 20 Years after Himmelmann 1998), pp. 33-40, open access; the author is a linguist at the University of Cologne, Germany

### E1

- Location: §1
- Quote: "Factors smaller than 10 (i.e. ten minutes are necessary to transcribe and translate one minute of recording) are rarely mentioned, and factors as high as 150 and higher are not unrealistic in the case of complex multiparty conversations."
- Key point: Few people report a factor below ten minutes to transcribe and translate one minute of recording; for complex multiparty conversation, factors of 150 or higher are not unrealistic.

### E2

- Location: §1
- Quote: "More often than not, segmentation units above the word (i.e. prosodic units and/or syntactic phrases) are not explicitly discussed or justified, and are thus difficult to reconstruct and evaluate for users of a documentation."
- Key point: Segmentation units above the word (prosodic units, syntactic phrases) are often not explained, which makes them hard for users to reconstruct and evaluate.

### E3

- Location: §2
- Quote: "Transcription involves interpretation and hence considerable enrichment of the acoustic signal."
- Key point: Transcription involves interpretation and adds a great deal of information to the acoustic signal.

### E4

- Location: §2
- Quote: "the tendency for the native speaker to paraphrase what is said rather than repeating it more or less directly. There is also the tendency to edit out elements typical of spoken language such as particles, hesitations and the like."
- Key point: Native speakers helping with transcription tend to paraphrase rather than repeat what was said, and to edit out features of spoken language such as particles and hesitations.

### E5

- Location: §2
- Quote: "transcriptions of the same recording produced by different transcribers/transcription teams will differ in some details."
- Key point: Different transcribers transcribing the same recording will differ in some details.

### E6

- Location: §4, footnote 6
- Quote: "current practice is often wanting even on the most basic level of including the information of who was involved in the transcription"
- Key point: Current practice often fails to record even the most basic information, such as who was involved in the transcription.

### E7

- Location: §4
- Quote: "Usually no attempt is made to include a larger group of community members in these decisions at the transcription stage."
- Key point: Decisions at the transcription stage, such as orthography, usually do not involve a wider group of community members.

### E8

- Location: §4
- Quote: "The production of such materials involves standardization on many levels: determining base forms for lexical entries, orthographic conventions (e.g., how clitics are written), and so on. Many of these decisions are also part of the transcription process."
- Key point: Producing dictionaries and literacy primers involves many standardization decisions, such as base forms for lexical entries and spelling conventions; many of these decisions are already made during transcription.
