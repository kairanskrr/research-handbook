# Georgetown Law Writing Center 2026: You Have Your Scholarly Paper Topic, Now . . . Get the Research Done

- Source: `georgetown2026research`, [Original PDF (Writing Center, Georgetown University Law Center, US; free)](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/academics/wp-content/uploads/sites/58/2026/10/You-Have-Your-Scholarly-Paper-Topic-Now-Get-the-Research-Done_Kelsey-Handschuh-2026-Revisions_FINAL-1.pdf)
- Version: Handout from the Writing Center at Georgetown University Law Center, first drafted by Laura Klein (with law librarian Sara Sampson) and revised by Kelsey Handschuh in 2026 (with librarian Jill Smith). Written for **US law students** writing scholarly papers; the databases it mentions (Westlaw, Lexis, HeinOnline, etc.) and the legislative history sources are all American. Locations are given as handout section numbers.

### E1

- Location: I.A.1 Formulate a Research Strategy
- Quote: "Your research strategy should incorporate two considerations: (1) the sources you want to consult and the order in which you expect to consult them, and (2) your deadlines for research and writing--both self-imposed and externally imposed."
- Key point: A research strategy covers two things: which sources to consult and in what order, and your research and writing deadlines (both self-imposed and external).

### E2

- Location: I.A.1
- Quote: "Commit your research strategy to paper, write it down, and consult it often as you proceed. That way, diverging from the plan requires a conscious decision on your part."
- Key point: Write your research strategy down and consult it often as you go; that way, departing from the plan has to be a conscious decision.

### E3

- Location: I.A.1
- Quote: "One way to keep your research organized is to create a research log. You should design this research log in a way that makes sense to you, but helpful things to incorporate include where you found the source, how you found the source," / "what you found, and why you are examining the source."
- Key point: Keep a research log recording where you found each source, how you found it, what you found, and why you are examining it.

### E4

- Location: I.A.2 Conduct a Preemption Check
- Quote: "It's important to conduct the preemption check at this early stage--after you've selected a topic but before you've developed your thesis--so that you can focus all your subsequent research on developing a unique thesis."
- Key point: Run the preemption check (whether someone has already published the same thesis) after choosing a topic but before developing your thesis, so all later research can focus on a unique thesis.

### E5

- Location: I.A.2
- Quote: "Remember that you are almost guaranteed to find articles on your topic--that does not mean you have been preempted."
- Key point: You are almost guaranteed to find articles on your topic; that does not mean you have been preempted.

### E6

- Location: I.B Consulting Secondary Sources
- Quote: "After you have solidified your research plan, consider beginning with secondary sources to conduct background research for your paper."
- Key point: Once your research plan is set, consider starting with secondary sources for background research.

### E7

- Location: I.B.1–2
- Quote: "These sources are descriptive, meaning they simply explain the state of the law and do not analyze or critique legal theories." / "analytical sources, such as a law review article, evaluate the state of the law and may make suggestions for what the law should be."
- Key point: Legal encyclopedias, treatises, Restatements, and similar works are descriptive secondary sources that simply explain the state of the law; analytical sources such as law review articles evaluate it and may suggest what the law should be.

### E8

- Location: I.C.1 Use Headnotes and Citing References/Sources
- Quote: "Headnotes are editor-written descriptions of the major issues of law in the case."
- Key point: Headnotes are editor-written descriptions of the major legal issues in a case, not part of the decision itself.

### E9

- Location: I.D What to Do When You're Stuck
- Quote: "Research is recursive, so retracing your steps once you've learned more is all part of the process."
- Key point: Research is recursive; retracing your steps once you have learned more is part of the process.

### E10

- Location: I.E.3 Consider Contrary Sources
- Quote: "Be sure, however, to consider carefully the countervailing arguments that have been made, and address those in your paper."
- Key point: Consider carefully the countervailing arguments that have been made, and address them in your paper.

### E11

- Location: II. Knowing When to Stop--or at Least Pause
- Quote: "First, you must ask yourself whether you have researched the most-cited cases and articles on your topic. Next you must be certain that you have checked recent publications. Last, but certainly not least, you must be certain that you can support the essential components of your thesis."
- Key point: Three tests for whether you can pause your research: you have covered the most-cited cases and articles on the topic, you have checked recent publications, and you can support the essential components of your thesis.
