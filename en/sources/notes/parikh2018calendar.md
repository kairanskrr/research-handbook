# Parikh 2018: Calendar. Not to-do lists.

- Source: `parikh2018calendar`, [original](https://deviparikh.substack.com/p/calendar-in-stead-of-to-do-lists-9ada86a512dd)
- Version: Author's Substack blog, 2018-04-25, read 2026-10-05. Locations give page subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Opening, Philosophy
- Quote: "Calendars convert time to space. They make the finiteness of time apparent."
- Key point: A calendar converts time into space and makes the finiteness of time visible, which a to-do list cannot.

### E2

- Location: Principle 2
- Quote: "Measuring the multiplier is much easier. Measure it. How long did you think something would take? How long did it actually take?"
- Key point: Most people underestimate how long things take. Rather than trying to estimate better, measure your own "multiplier" (actual time / estimated time) and apply it when scheduling.

### E3

- Location: Principle 4
- Quote: "Re-planning is not failure. Re-planning is part of the plan."
- Key point: When your plan is disrupted, re-plan; re-planning is part of the plan.

### E4

- Location: Principle 5
- Quote: "Planning for a large project — breaking it up into calendar-sized tasks, estimating how long each task will take, incorporating the multiplier, and marking them on your calendar in available slots — is a task."
- Key point: Break a large project into calendar-sized tasks; doing that planning is itself a task to put on the calendar.

### E5

- Location: Principle 6
- Quote: "A conference deadline is in 3 months. That means you need to start writing the paper at least a month before the deadline."
- Key point: Work backward from the deadline: when you must start writing, and when you must have the main results.

### E6

- Location: Principle 6
- Quote: "Backtracking tells you when is the latest you need to get something done. Don’t mistake it to be the time when you should do something. That gives you no buffer."
- Key point: Working backward gives you the latest possible time, not the time to start; treating it as the start leaves no buffer.
