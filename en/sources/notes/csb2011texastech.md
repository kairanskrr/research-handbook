# US Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board 2011: Texas Tech University Laboratory Explosion (case study)

- Source: `csb2011texastech`, [Original](https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/20/CSB_Study_TTU_.pdf) (incident page: https://www.csb.gov/texas-tech-university-chemistry-lab-explosion/ )
- Version: Case study no. 2010-05-I-TX of the US Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board (CSB), published 2011. A US federal government publication, free on the official website. PDF downloaded with curl on 2026-10-06 and extracted with pdftotext. The incident happened in the Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry at Texas Tech University (not the chemical engineering department).

### E1

- Location: §1.0 Introduction, p. 1
- Quote: "On January 7, 2010, a graduate student within the Chemistry and Biochemistry Department at Texas Tech University (Texas Tech) lost three fingers, his hands and face were burned, and one of his eyes was injured after the chemical he was working with detonated."
- Key point: On January 7, 2010, a compound being handled by a graduate student in the Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry at Texas Tech University exploded; he lost three fingers, his hands and face were burned, and one eye was injured.

### E2

- Location: §4.0 Incident Description, p. 3
- Quote: "they decided to scale-up the synthesis of NHP to make approximately 10 grams. [...] The PIs of the research were not consulted on the decision to scale up."
- Key point: The students decided to scale up the synthesis of this energetic compound to about 10 grams; the two principal investigators were not consulted on the decision to scale up.

### E3

- Location: §4.0 Incident Description, p. 4
- Quote: "Based on experience, the two students had discovered that smaller amounts of the compound would not ignite or explode on impact when wet with water or hexane, and they assumed the hazards of larger quantities of NHP would be controlled in a similar manner."
- Key point: From experience, the students had found that small amounts of the compound did not ignite or explode on impact when wet with water or hexane, and they assumed the hazards of larger quantities could be controlled the same way.

### E4

- Location: §3.0 Pre-incident Events, p. 3
- Quote: "Safety restrictions, such as a 100 milligram limit on the amount of compound permitted to be synthesized, were verbally communicated"
- Key point: Safety restrictions such as a 100-milligram limit on the amount synthesized were communicated only verbally.

### E5

- Location: §3.0 Pre-incident Events, p. 3
- Quote: "none of the students the CSB interviewed as part of its investigation, including those directly involved in the incident, stated they were aware of a strict 100 milligram limit."
- Key point: None of the students the CSB interviewed (including those directly involved) said they were aware of a strict 100-milligram limit.

### E6

- Location: §4.0 Incident Description, p. 4
- Quote: "No formal hazard evaluation13 was conducted to analyze the effectiveness of using either water or hexane to mitigate the potential explosive hazards associated with the quantity of NHP synthesized the day of the incident."
- Key point: No formal hazard evaluation was done to analyze whether water or hexane could mitigate the explosion hazard for the quantity synthesized that day. (The "13" after "evaluation" in the original is a footnote number.)

### E7

- Location: §4.0 footnote 13, p. 4
- Quote: "A hazard evaluation is an organized effort to identify and analyze the significance of hazardous situations associated with a process or activity (CCPS, 2008, p. 15)"
- Key point: A hazard evaluation is an organized effort to identify and analyze the significance of hazardous situations in a process or activity.

### E8

- Location: §6.2 Hazard Determination, p. 8
- Quote: "While not all standards and practices used in the chemical process industry can be directly applied to an academic laboratory environment, drawing applicable parallels to harness the field's experience and knowledge of hazardous activities involving chemicals can be useful."
- Key point: Not all standards and practices of the chemical process industry can be applied directly to academic laboratories, but drawing applicable parallels from that experience is useful.

### E9

- Location: §9.0 Key Lessons, item 3, p. 17
- Quote: "Current standards on hazard evaluations, risk assessments, and hazard mitigation are geared toward industrial settings and are not fully transferable to the academic research laboratory environment."
- Key point: Current standards for hazard evaluation, risk assessment, and hazard mitigation are geared toward industrial settings and are not fully transferable to academic research laboratories.

### E10

- Location: §9.0 Key Lessons, item 4, p. 17
- Quote: "Research-specific written protocols and training are necessary to manage laboratory research risk."
- Key point: Managing laboratory research risk requires research-specific written protocols and training.

### E11

- Location: §9.0 Key Lessons, item 6, p. 17
- Quote: "Near-misses and previous incidents provide opportunities for education and improvement only if they are documented, tracked, and communicated to drive safety change."
- Key point: Near-misses and previous incidents become opportunities for learning and improvement only if they are documented, tracked, and communicated.

### E12

- Location: §7.0 Lessons Not Learned From Previous Incidents and Near-Misses, p. 13
- Quote: "The second previous incident involved a scale-up situation within the same research groups; a student unintentionally used the wrong units of measure and created an excess of a known energetic material."
- Key point: About three years earlier, the same research groups had a scale-up near-miss: a student used the wrong units of measure and made an excess of a known energetic material.

### E13

- Location: §7.0, p. 13
- Quote: "the near-miss was not reported to anyone outside of the research groups."
- Key point: That near-miss was not reported to anyone outside the research groups.

### E14

- Location: §6.2.3 Lack of Formal Documentation and Communication, p. 12
- Quote: "Prior to the incident, weekly group meetings between students and PIs were held, but the focus was primarily on experimental results, not actual research activities and the safety implications of the work."
- Key point: Before the incident there were weekly group meetings, but they focused mainly on experimental results, not on what was actually being done and its safety implications.

### E15

- Location: §4.0 footnote 7, p. 3
- Quote: "Laboratory notebooks were not dated and did not always indicate amounts of reactants used during synthesis."
- Key point: Lab notebooks were not dated and did not always record the amounts of reactants used in synthesis.

### E16

- Location: §4.0 Incident Description, p. 4
- Quote: "Several individuals from the lab indicated that the decision to wear goggles was a personal choice which they based on how dangerous an activity was perceived to be."
- Key point: Several people in the lab said wearing goggles was a personal choice, based on how dangerous they perceived an activity to be.

### E17

- Location: 3.0 Pre-incident Events, p. 3
- Quote: "Safety restrictions, such as a 100 milligram limit on the amount of compound permitted to be synthesized, were verbally communicated by the two Principal Investigators (PIs) of the research to some students."
- Key point: Safety restrictions such as a maximum of 100 milligrams per synthesis were communicated only verbally, by the two principal investigators, to some students.

### E18

- Location: p. 3, footnote 7
- Quote: "The events leading up to the January 7 incident were documented mainly with interviews conducted by the CSB because little written documentation was available." / "Laboratory notebooks were not dated and did not always indicate amounts of reactants used during synthesis."
- Key point: The events leading to the incident were reconstructed mainly from CSB interviews because little written documentation existed; lab notebooks were not dated and did not always record how much reactant was used.

### E19

- Location: 6.2.3 Lack of Formal Documentation and Communication, p. 13
- Quote: "Lab notebooks, often seen as a primary means of documenting research plans and activities, were not consistently reviewed by PIs and varied greatly among students"
- Key point: Notebooks were supposed to be the main record of research plans and activities, but the principal investigators did not review them consistently and they varied greatly between students.

### E20

- Location: 6.2.3, p. 13
- Quote: "weekly group meetings between students and PIs were held, but the focus was primarily on experimental results, not actual research activities and the safety implications of the work."
- Key point: Weekly group meetings focused mainly on experimental results, not on the actual work and its safety implications.

### E21

- Location: 7.0 Lessons Not Learned From Previous Incidents and Near-Misses, pp. 13–14
- Quote: "a student unintentionally used the wrong units of measure and created an excess of a known energetic material."
- Key point: Earlier, a student in the same research groups had used the wrong units and made an excess of a known energetic material (a near-miss).

### E22

- Location: 6.2.2 No Training for Research-Specific Safety Risks, p. 12
- Quote: "the safety training needs of undergraduates, who often perform "cookbook" laboratory work with well-understood and defined risks, differ greatly from those of students performing research"
- Key point: Undergraduates mostly do "cookbook" lab work with well-understood risks; students doing research have very different safety training needs.

### E23

- Location: 6.2.3, p. 13
- Quote: "Students in the energetic labs were not required to obtain permission or seek approval from their PIs prior to changing research experiment variables, including subjectively determined "small" increases of material synthesized and temperature variations."
- Key point: Students did not need their principal investigators' approval before changing experimental variables, including subjectively "small" increases in the amount synthesized and changes in temperature.

### E24

- Location: p. 3, footnote 6
- Quote: "At a university, the PI is typically a professor in charge of the research, students, postdoctoral researchers, and technicians working in the laboratory."
- Key point: At a university, the principal investigator (PI) is typically the professor in charge of the research and of the students, postdocs, and technicians in the lab.

### E25

- Location: 7.0, p. 14
- Quote: "Entities looking to improve safety should encourage the reporting of such incidents, even when injuries or damage do not result"
- Key point: Organizations that want to improve safety should encourage reporting of such incidents even when no injury or damage results.
