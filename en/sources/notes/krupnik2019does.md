# Krupnik et al. 2019: Does Size Matter? A Critical Review of Meta-analysis in Agronomy

- Source: `krupnik2019does`, [full text (Cambridge University Press, open access)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0014479719000012), DOI [10.1017/S0014479719000012](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0014479719000012)
- Version: Experimental Agriculture 55(2):200–229, 2019, open access (CC BY 4.0); PDF downloaded from the publisher on 2026-10-08. The authors are from the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), the French Agricultural Research Centre for International Development (CIRAD), and the University of California, Santa Cruz. This is a critical review of meta-analyses on organic agriculture and conservation agriculture, not a reporting guideline. Locations give the section.

### E1

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "This paper consequently offers three suggestions for how scientists and readers of scientific literature can more carefully evaluate meta-analyses. First, the ways in which papers and data are collected should be critically assessed. Second, the justification of research questions, framing of farming systems, and the scales at which research results are extrapolated and discussed should be carefully evaluated."
- Key point: The authors offer three suggestions for evaluating meta-analyses more carefully. First, scrutinize how the papers and data were collected; second, scrutinize the rationale for the research question, how the farming systems are defined, and the scale at which results are extrapolated and discussed.

### E2

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "Third, when applied to strongly politicized topics situated in an arena of scientific debate, as is the case with OA and CA, more" [...] "conservative interpretations of meta-analytical results that recognize the socially and politically embedded nature of agricultural research is are needed."
- Key point: Third, when meta-analysis is applied to strongly politicized, scientifically debated topics such as organic agriculture and conservation agriculture, interpret the results more conservatively and acknowledge that agricultural research is embedded in social and political contexts.

### E3

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "Small-plot and research station-based experiments may not be representative of whole field or whole farm functioning"
- Key point: Small-plot and research-station experiments may not be representative of how a whole field or whole farm functions.

### E4

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "The presentation of `global' average results that are decoupled from the context-specific and diverse qualities of farming systems is unlikely to meaningfully inform policy and investment decisions, nor inform ways to improve farmer practice."
- Key point: "Global" average results decoupled from the context-specific, diverse qualities of farming systems are unlikely to meaningfully guide policy, investment decisions, or farmers' efforts to improve their practices.

### E5

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "Our case studies indicate that scientists designing meta-analyses in agronomy grapple with challenges related to the simplification of farmers' complex and variable crop management practices into categories that can be analysed statistically."
- Key point: People designing meta-analyses in agronomy grapple with simplifying farmers' complex and variable crop management practices into categories that can be analyzed statistically.

### E6

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "This paper describes the ways in which meta-analysis has been applied to support claims and counter-claims regarding two topics widely debated in agricultural research, namely organic agriculture (OA) and conservation agriculture (CA)."
- Key point: The paper examines how meta-analysis has been used to support claims and counter-claims on two widely debated topics in agricultural research (organic agriculture and conservation agriculture).
