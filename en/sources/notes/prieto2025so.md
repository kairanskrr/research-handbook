# Prieto 2025: A PhD So Good It Can't Be Ignored: Passion, Practice, and Control

- Source: `prieto2025so`, [original](https://ahappyphd.org/posts/phd-so-good-1/)
- Version: A Happy PhD blog, 2025 (excerpt from a book manuscript). The post applies ideas from Cal Newport's So Good They Can't Ignore You to doing a PhD.

### E1

- Location: "passion trap" section
- Quote: "It’s good to have preferences and tastes, but be flexible in the face of feedback from knowledgeable people (and research literature!) that tell you some other topic is more relevant and feasible for you to investigate."
- Key point: Having preferences is good, but stay flexible when knowledgeable people (or the literature) tell you another topic is more relevant and feasible.

### E2

- Location: Section on "missions"
- Quote: "cool missions often require many years of trial and error to be found." / "the PhD is more about “getting the driver’s license” than making the “best lap of your life”"
- Key point: A good long-term mission often takes years of trial and error to find; a PhD is more about "getting the driver's license" than making "the best lap of your life."

### E3

- Location: "rare and valuable skills" section
- Quote: "If rare and valuable skills (not pre-existing passion) are the key ingredient for developing a satisfying career"
- Key point: Newport's core idea: the key to a satisfying career is rare and valuable skills, not pre-existing passion.

### E4

- Location: Deliberate practice section
- Quote: "Experts don’t become experts just by innate talent or spending years on the job" / "practicing at the edge of our abilities"
- Key point: Experts become experts not through innate talent or years on the job but through deliberate practice at the edge of their abilities.

### E5

- Location: Deliberate practice section
- Quote: "we can ask explicitly about our weaker research skills and advice for improving them."
- Key point: You can ask your advisor and colleagues directly about your weaker research skills and how to improve them.

### E6

- Location: "control traps" section
- Quote: "prioritize positions (and supervisors) where we will learn the most"
- Key point: Early on, prioritize positions and supervisors where you will learn the most.
