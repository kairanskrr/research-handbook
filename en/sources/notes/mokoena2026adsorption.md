# Mokoena et al. 2026: What is the adsorption efficiency and capacity of biodegradable polymer blend foams filled with carbonaceous materials in the removal of heavy metals from water? A systematic review protocol

- Source: `mokoena2026adsorption`, [original (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13151128), DOI [10.1186/s13750-026-00384-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13750-026-00384-x)
- Version: Environmental Evidence 15:4, 2026, open access (CC BY 4.0); authors based in South Africa (University of the Free State). Full text read via Europe PMC on 2026-10-08. This is a systematic review protocol and represents one team's practice only. Locations give section headings.

### E1

- Location: Methods
- Quote: "The systematic review and the present protocol will be in alignment with the guidelines by the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (CEE) [...] and the ROSES checklist"
- Key point: The systematic review and its protocol follow the guidelines of the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence (CEE) and the RepOrting standards for Systematic Evidence Syntheses (ROSES) checklist.

### E2

- Location: Methods, Searching for articles
- Quote: "The review will search through and retrieve information from both grey and peer-reviewed sources, like journal articles, books, book chapters, conference papers and reports, that include laboratory and pilot scale investigations."
- Key point: The search covers both grey and peer-reviewed literature, including laboratory-scale and pilot-scale studies.

### E3

- Location: Study validity assessment
- Quote: "This checklist will assess internal validity (e.g., study design and control of confounding factors), external validity (e.g., relevance and generalizability to other contexts), and reporting transparency (e.g., completeness and clarity of methods and results)."
- Key point: A checklist adapted from the CEE guidelines assesses each included study's internal validity (design, control of confounding factors), external validity (generalizability to other settings), and reporting transparency.

### E4

- Location: Study validity assessment, Risk of bias
- Quote: "Studies should clearly account for potential confounding parameters, like the presence of competing ions and the complexity of water matrices."
- Key point: Included studies should account for potential confounding parameters, such as competing ions in the water and complex water matrices.
