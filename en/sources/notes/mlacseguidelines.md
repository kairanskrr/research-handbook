# MLA Committee on Scholarly Editions: Guidelines for Editors of Scholarly Editions

- Source: `mlacseguidelines`, [original](https://www.mla.org/Resources/Guidelines-and-Data/Reports-and-Professional-Guidelines/Guidelines-for-Editors-of-Scholarly-Editions)
- Version: web page of the MLA Committee on Scholarly Editions, no revision date given; accessed October 2026

### E1

- Location: 1.1 Principles
- Quote: "The scholarly edition's basic task is to present a reliable text: scholarly editions make clear what they promise and keep their promises."
- Key point: The basic task of a scholarly edition is to present a reliable text: make clear what it promises, and keep those promises.

### E2

- Location: 1.1 Principles
- Quote: "A scholarly edition commonly includes appropriate textual apparatus or notes documenting alterations and variant readings of the text, including alterations by the author, intervening editors, or the editor of this edition."
- Key point: A scholarly edition commonly includes a textual apparatus or notes documenting alterations and variant readings of the text.

### E3

- Location: 1.1 Principles
- Quote: "editors of scholarly editions establish and follow a proofreading plan that serves to ensure the accuracy of the materials presented."
- Key point: Editors should establish and follow a proofreading plan.

### E4

- Location: 1.1 Principles
- Quote: "A scholarly edition commonly includes appropriate textual apparatus or notes documenting alterations and variant readings of the text"
- Key point: A scholarly edition commonly includes a textual apparatus or notes documenting alterations and variant readings of the text.

### E5

- Location: 1.1 Principles
- Quote: "Scholarly editions generally include a statement, or series of statements, setting forth the history of the text and its physical forms, explaining how the edition has been constructed or represented"
- Key point: A scholarly edition generally includes a statement setting out the history of the text and its physical forms and explaining how the edition was prepared.

### E6

- Location: 1.2.1 Considerations with Respect to Source Material
- Quote: "Max Perkins might be considered the coauthor of the novelists he edited as an employee of Scribner's—most notably Thomas Wolfe, whose published novels bear little resemblance to the manuscripts that Wolfe turned over to Perkins."
- Key point: The guidelines' example: Thomas Wolfe's novels as edited by Max Perkins bear little resemblance to the manuscripts Wolfe submitted.
