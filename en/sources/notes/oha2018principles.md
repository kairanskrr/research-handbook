# Oral History Association 2018: OHA Principles and Best Practices

- Source: `oha2018principles`, [Statement on Ethics](https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/), [Best Practices](https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/)
- Version: a set of documents adopted in October 2018 by the US Oral History Association (OHA), including core principles, a statement on ethics, best practices, and information for narrators. This excerpt draws on two web pages, the OHA Statement on Ethics and Oral History Best Practices, accessed 2026-10-06. Locations give the page name and section. Footnote numbers in quotes are omitted with `[...]`.

### E1

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Preparation and Communication
- Quote: "Prior to beginning the interview, the interviewer obtains the narrator’s informed consent, which means, most generally, documenting the knowing agreement of the narrator to participate in the process and overall project"
- Key point: Before the interview begins, obtain the narrator's informed consent, i.e., document the narrator's knowing agreement to participate.

### E2

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Preparation and Communication
- Quote: "While oral historians strive to protect the narrator, they are careful, at every point in the process, not to make promises that they cannot keep."
- Key point: Oral historians strive to protect the narrator but are careful, at every point, not to make promises they cannot keep.

### E3

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Preparation and Communication
- Quote: "The interviewer clearly communicates the goals of the project, the potential risks of participating in it, and the proviso that, once accessible[...], the oral history can be used[...] in any number of ways, by any number of potential users."
- Key point: Clearly explain the project's goals, the potential risks of participating, and that once the interview is accessible, anyone may use it in any way.

### E4

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "Options for narrator review include the right to delete, restrict, and/or redact portions of an interview; the ability to add clarifications and correct mistakes; and the choice to keep the interview closed to the public until a set date or to decline to release it to the public in the first place."
- Key point: When reviewing the interview, the narrator may delete, restrict, or redact portions, add clarifications and corrections, or choose to keep it closed until a certain date or not release it at all.

### E5

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "During the course of the interview itself, oral historians attempt to minimize potential harm to the narrator, communicate the narrator’s right to refuse to answer questions, and honestly describe their institutional, professional, political, and other affiliations, as well as obligations and demands."
- Key point: During the interview, minimize potential harm to the narrator, tell them they have the right to refuse to answer, and disclose your institutional, professional, political, and other affiliations and obligations.

### E6

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Using Oral Sources
- Quote: "This means users of oral sources should provide analyses, including when edited or excerpted, that remain true to the words and meanings offered by the narrator and take care to not quote words out of context or otherwise contort the original meaning."
- Key point: When you use oral sources, even edited or excerpted, stay true to the narrator's words and meanings; do not quote out of context.

### E7

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "During initial preparation, oral historians should locate an appropriate repository[...] to house the project’s finished oral histories and other documentation."
- Key point: At the start of a project, locate an appropriate repository (archive, library, etc.) to house the finished oral histories and related documentation.

### E8

- Location: Best Practices, Access and Use
- Quote: "recognizing the subjectivity of the interview, including, when possible, verification of information presented as factual"
- Key point: Users should recognize the subjectivity of the interview and, where possible, verify information presented as factual.

### E9

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "First-time interviewers and others involved in oral history projects should seek training"
- Key point: First-time interviewers should seek training first.

### E10

- Location: Best Practices, Access and Use
- Quote: "take all steps practicable to abide by any restrictions set forth by the narrator, while also making clear that certain legal challenges—such as subpoenas or open-record requests—may make some restrictions unenforceable"
- Key point: Repositories should do all they practicably can to honor the narrator's restrictions, but make clear that legal challenges such as subpoenas or open-record requests may make some restrictions unenforceable.

### E11

- Location: Best Practices, Preservation
- Quote: "transfer the original recording from whatever device was used, make an appropriate number of redundant digital copies,[...] and store those in different physical locations, as soon as possible after any interview is completed"
- Key point: As soon as possible after each interview, transfer the original recording off the device, make redundant copies, and store them in different physical locations.

### E12

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "Power operates in every human engagement, and no less so in oral history interviewing."
- Key point: Power operates in every human engagement, and oral history interviewing is no exception.

### E13

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "This step sets oral history apart[...] from other[...] methodologies in that it ensures the narrator’s account enters the public record"
- Key point: Letting the narrator review and approve public release is what sets oral history apart from other interview methods: the narrator's account enters the public record.

### E14

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing
- Quote: "interviewers must respect the rights of interviewees to refuse to discuss certain subjects, to restrict access to the interview, or, under certain circumstances, to choose a pseudonym."
- Key point: Narrators have the right to refuse to discuss certain subjects, to restrict access to the interview, and in some circumstances to choose a pseudonym.

### E15

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "Oral historians should choose potential narrators based on the relevance of their experiences to the subject at hand, while striving to identify and incorporate as many diverse voices as possible."
- Key point: Choose narrators for the relevance of their experiences to the subject, while trying to include as many diverse voices as possible.

### E16

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "Oral historians should consider the goals of the research project and seek narrators who are able, collectively, to present a variety of points of view."
- Key point: Taken together, the narrators should present a variety of points of view.

### E17

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "In preparing to ask informed questions, interviewers should become familiar with the person, topic, and historical context by doing research in primary and secondary sources"
- Key point: To ask informed questions, research the person, topic, and historical context in primary and secondary sources before the interview.

### E18

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "Interviewers should prepare an open-ended guide or outline of the themes to be covered and general questions to be asked before conducting the interview."
- Key point: Before the interview, prepare an open-ended guide: the themes to cover and general questions to ask.

### E19

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "Interviewers should create, when possible, a high-quality recording of the interview(audio or video format) to capture the narrator’s interview accurately with consideration of future audiences and long-term preservation."
- Key point: Make a high-quality audio or video recording where possible, with future audiences and long-term preservation in mind.

### E20

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing
- Quote: "The interview should be conducted, whenever possible, in a quiet location with minimal background noises and possible distractions"
- Key point: Interview in a quiet place with minimal background noise and distractions whenever possible.

### E21

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing
- Quote: "The interviewer should record a lead-in at the beginning of each session." / "location of the interview (being mindful to not list personal residence address, but rather generic “narrator’s home”)"
- Key point: Record a lead-in at the start of each session. For the location, do not give a home address; say "narrator's home."

### E22

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing
- Quote: "Along with asking open-ended questions and actively listening to the answers, interviewers should ask follow-up questions, seeking additional clarification, elaboration, and reflection."
- Key point: Ask open-ended questions, listen actively, and ask follow-up questions seeking clarification, elaboration, and reflection.

### E23

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing (b)
- Quote: "Interviewers should provide challenging and perceptive inquiry, fully and respectfully exploring appropriate subjects, and not being satisfied with superficial responses. At the same time, they should encourage narrators to respond to questions in their own style and language and to address issues that reflect their concerns."
- Key point: Provide challenging and perceptive inquiry and do not settle for superficial answers, while encouraging narrators to answer in their own way and words and to talk about what matters to them.

### E24

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing (c)
- Quote: "Interviewers should be prepared to extend the inquiry beyond the specific focus of the project to allow the narrator to freely define what is most relevant."
- Key point: Be prepared to go beyond the project's focus and let the narrator define what is most relevant.

### E25

- Location: Best Practices, Interviewing
- Quote: "The interviewer should secure a signed legal release form, ideally when the interview is completed."
- Key point: Secure a signed legal release form, ideally when the interview is completed.

### E26

- Location: Best Practices, footnote 6
- Quote: "Although many oral historians prefer to request signatures for any legal release forms assigning rights to the interview after it is completed in order to better address any sensitive issues that may have come up during the course of the interview."
- Key point: Many oral historians prefer to request signatures on release forms assigning rights after the interview, so they can better address any sensitive issues that came up.

### E27

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation (d)
- Quote: "When an understanding on how to proceed is reached, a formal record of that agreement should be completed prior to the beginning of recording."
- Key point: Once both sides agree on how to proceed, make a formal record of that agreement before recording begins.

### E28

- Location: Best Practices, footnote 8
- Quote: "in some cases, such as interviews with vulnerable communities, particularly those with surveillance concerns, there will be a need to gather only the very basic contextual information."
- Key point: When interviewing vulnerable communities, especially those with surveillance concerns, you may gather only the most basic contextual information.

### E29

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Stewardship: Preservation and Access
- Quote: "Oral historians and their archivist partners clearly document relevant metadata so that future users will know easily who was interviewed, when and where the interview was conducted, and other key pieces of data about the interview."
- Key point: Work with archivists to document metadata clearly, so future users can easily tell who was interviewed, when and where, and other key information.

### E30

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Stewardship: Preservation and Access
- Quote: "oral historians should educate themselves about legal concerns such as libel, invasion of privacy, and other issues that might endanger the narrator."
- Key point: Learn about legal concerns such as libel and invasion of privacy that might endanger the narrator.

### E31

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Using Oral Sources
- Quote: "Users may arrive at conclusions that diverge from those offered by a narrator, but conclusions should be derived from evidence properly cited."
- Key point: Users may reach conclusions that differ from the narrator's, but conclusions must be derived from properly cited evidence.

### E32

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "After the interview, oral history ethics strongly recommends that the narrator be given the opportunity to review the interview (recording and/or transcript) and approve what was said for public release or other use."
- Key point: After the interview, oral history ethics strongly recommends letting the narrator review the recording or transcript and approve it for public release or other uses.

### E33

- Location: Best Practices, Preservation (b)
- Quote: "whenever possible, all efforts should be made to preserve electronic files in formats that are cross platform and nonproprietary"
- Key point: Preserve electronic files in cross-platform, nonproprietary formats whenever possible.

### E34

- Location: Best Practices, Preparation
- Quote: "Oral historians should take care to select a repository that aligns with the project’s goals, has the capacity to preserve the oral histories, can enforce any signed agreements, and will make them accessible to the public."
- Key point: Choose a repository on four criteria: it aligns with the project's goals, can preserve the oral histories, can enforce signed agreements, and will make them accessible to the public.

### E35

- Location: Best Practices, Access & Use
- Quote: "an archive should provide, when possible, written documentation such as transcripts, indexes with time tags linking to the recording, detailed descriptions of interview content, or other guides to the contents."
- Key point: Archives should, where possible, provide written guides such as transcripts, time-tagged indexes, and descriptions of interview content.

### E36

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Preparation & Communication
- Quote: "oral historians should conduct preliminary research about the topics they intend to study and be comfortable with the recording technology they intend to utilize."
- Key point: Do preliminary research on your topics and get comfortable with the recording technology you will use.

### E37

- Location: Statement on Ethics, Collaboration: The Oral History Interview
- Quote: "The interview should not be made public until the narrator, as the original recording’s copyright holder, has provided formal authorization to do so."
- Key point: The narrator holds the copyright in the original recording; the interview should not be made public until they formally authorize it.

### E38

- Location: Best Practices, Access & Use (a)
- Quote: "avoiding stereotypes, misrepresentations, and manipulations of the narrator’s words"
- Key point: When using interviews, avoid stereotypes, misrepresentations, and manipulation of the narrator's words.
