# Weissgerber et al. 2015: Beyond Bar and Line Graphs: Time for a New Data Presentation Paradigm

- Source: `weissgerber2015beyond`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002128)
- Version: PLOS Biology 13(4):e1002128, 2015, open access. Text extracted from the journal PDF with pdftotext, 2026-10-06. Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Bar and line graphs of continuous data are ‘visual tables’ that typically show the mean and standard error (SE) or standard deviation (SD). This is problematic for three reasons. First, many different data distributions can lead to the same bar or line graph"
- Key point: Bar and line graphs of continuous data show only the mean and the standard error or standard deviation; one problem is that many different data distributions produce exactly the same bar graph.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Bar graphs of paired data erroneously suggest that the groups being compared are independent and provide no information about whether changes are consistent across individuals"
- Key point: Bar graphs of paired data wrongly suggest that the groups are independent and do not show whether changes are consistent across individuals.

### E3

- Location: Recommendations for a New Data Presentation Paradigm
- Quote: "The best option for small datasets is to show the full data, as summary statistics are only meaningful if there are enough data to summarize."
- Key point: For small datasets, the best option is to plot every data point, because summary statistics are only meaningful when there are enough data to summarize.

### E4

- Location: Introduction; Are Your Figures Worth a Thousand Words?
- Quote: "We systematically reviewed all full-length, original research articles published in the top 25% of physiology journals between January 1 and March 31, 2014 (n = 703)" / "85.6% of papers included at least one bar graph." / "13.4% of articles included at least one univariate scatterplot"
- Key point: The authors systematically reviewed 703 original research articles published January-March 2014 in the top 25% of physiology journals: 85.6% had at least one bar graph, and only 13.4% had at least one univariate scatterplot.

### E5

- Location: Are Your Figures Worth a Thousand Words?
- Quote: "Showing the SE rather than the SD magnifies the apparent visual differences between groups."
- Key point: Showing the standard error instead of the standard deviation visually magnifies differences between groups.
