# Molléri, Petersen & Mendes 2020: An Empirically Evaluated Checklist for Surveys in Software Engineering

- Source: `molleri2020empirically`, [arXiv:1901.09850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09850)
- Version: arXiv v1 (2019-01-28, preprint before acceptance); final version published in Information and Software Technology 119 (2020) 106240

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "To construct the checklist, we systematically aggregated knowledge from 14 methodological papers supporting survey-based research in software engineering."
- Key point: This checklist for survey research was built by systematically aggregating 14 methodological papers on survey research in software engineering.

### E2

- Location: §2, Instrument validation
- Quote: "After design, the ability of the instrument to measure what is intended should be assessed. The most frequently cited approaches for the assessment are P66) piloting, P65) retest, P62) focus groups, and P63) expert or P58) non-expert reviews."
- Key point: After design, assess whether the instrument measures what it is intended to measure; the most often cited approaches are pilot surveys, test-retest, focus groups, and expert or non-expert reviews.

### E3

- Location: §2, Response management
- Quote: "After distribution of the instrument to the selected participants, it is important to observe the response rate (P64) in order to identify the reasons for non-responses (P25)."
- Key point: After distributing the questionnaire, observe the response rate and identify the reasons for non-response.

### E4

- Location: §2, Data analysis
- Quote: "An additional suggestion to ensure their reliability is P79) to have more than one item measuring the same variable."
- Key point: For reliability, measure the same variable with more than one item.

### E5

- Location: §3.3.1, EQ1 Completeness
- Quote: "Among the checklist groups that are more scarcely addressed are: 2) study plan; 3) identify the population; 6) instrument validation; and 8) response management."
- Key point: The authors applied the checklist to 24 survey papers; the least addressed groups were study plan, identifying the population, instrument validation, and response management.

### E6

- Location: §4.2.1 Research Practice
- Quote: "Very often the demographics of the participants are described, but scarce information is provided regarding the target population."
- Key point: Papers often describe the participants' demographics but say little about the target population.

### E7

- Location: §4.2.1 Research Practice
- Quote: "When designing the data collection instrument, one should carefully consider the order (P34) and amount (P36) of questions. Additional sources of data (P69), such as work repositories can provide means to cross-check the results."
- Key point: When designing the instrument, consider carefully the order and number of questions; additional data sources such as work repositories can be used to cross-check survey results.

### E8

- Location: §4.2.1 Research Practice
- Quote: "Furthermore, it is also important to report the improvements made as resulting of the instrument validation (P61)."
- Key point: Report the improvements made as a result of instrument validation.

### E9

- Location: §4.2.1 Research Practice
- Quote: "Besides the response rates, it is valuable to report any strategy used to improve responses, such as reminders (P70) or searching for additional databases (P16)."
- Key point: Besides the response rate, report any strategies used to improve responses, such as reminders or searching additional databases for participants.

### E10

- Location: §3.3.2 Evaluation by the corresponding authors
- Quote: "As a recommended practice, researchers are encouraged to make additional information (e.g., research diary, questionnaire instrument, ancillary documents) accessible to the target audience."
- Key point: Researchers are encouraged to make supplementary material available (research diary, questionnaire, invitation letters and other ancillary documents) to work around page limits.
