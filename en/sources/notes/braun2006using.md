# Braun & Clarke 2006: Using thematic analysis in psychology

- Source: `braun2006using`, [Author's archived version (UWE institutional repository)](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/1043060)
- Version: Qualitative Research in Psychology 3(2), 77-101; the version read is the author's revised manuscript in the UWE institutional repository (via the Internet Archive copy of `eprints.uwe.ac.uk/11735/2/thematic_analysis_revised_-_final.pdf`). Page numbers refer to that manuscript.

### E1

- Location: What is thematic analysis?
- Quote: "Thematic analysis is a method for identifying, analysing, and reporting patterns (themes) within data."
- Key point: Thematic analysis is a method for identifying, analysing, and reporting patterns (themes) in data.

### E2

- Location: Inductive vs theoretical thematic analysis
- Quote: "An inductive approach means the themes identified are strongly linked to the data themselves" / "researchers cannot free themselves of their theoretical and epistemological commitments, and data are not coded in an epistemological vacuum."
- Key point: In inductive analysis, themes are strongly linked to the data themselves; but researchers cannot free themselves from their theoretical and epistemological commitments, and coding does not happen in a vacuum.

### E3

- Location: Inductive vs theoretical thematic analysis
- Quote: "This form of thematic analysis tends to provide less a rich description of the data overall, and more a detailed analysis of some aspect of the data."
- Key point: Theory-driven (deductive) thematic analysis gives a less rich description of the data overall and a more detailed analysis of some aspect of the data.

### E4

- Location: What counts as a theme?
- Quote: "Ideally there will be a number of instances of the theme across the data set, but more instances do not necessarily mean the theme itself is more crucial." / "So researcher judgement is necessary to determine what a theme is."
- Key point: Ideally a theme recurs across the data set, but more instances do not make a theme more important; deciding what counts as a theme takes researcher judgment, not a fixed proportion.

### E5

- Location: Phase 1, Transcription of verbal data
- Quote: "recognised as an interpretative act, where meanings are created, rather than simply a mechanical one of putting spoken sounds on paper"
- Key point: Transcription is an interpretative act that creates meaning, not just a mechanical act of putting spoken sounds on paper.

### E6

- Location: Phase 1, Transcription of verbal data
- Quote: "If your data have already been, or will be, transcribed for you, it is important that you spend more time familiarising yourself with the data, and also check the transcripts back against the original audio recordings for" [...] "(as should always be done)."
- Key point: If someone else transcribes for you, spend more time familiarizing yourself with the data and check the transcripts against the original recordings; this check should always be done.

### E7

- Location: Phase 1: familiarising yourself with your data
- Quote: "During this phase, it is a good idea to start taking notes or marking ideas for coding that you will then go back to in subsequent phases."
- Key point: While familiarizing yourself with the data, start taking notes and marking coding ideas to come back to in later phases.

### E8

- Location: Table 1: Phases of Thematic Analysis
- Quote: "Familiarising yourself with your data: 2. Generating initial codes:" / "3. Searching for themes: 4. Reviewing themes:" / "5. Defining and naming themes:" / "6. Producing the report:"
- Key point: Thematic analysis has six phases: familiarizing yourself with the data, generating initial codes, searching for themes, reviewing themes, defining and naming themes, and producing the report.

### E9

- Location: Phase 2: generating initial codes
- Quote: "However, your coded data differs from the units of analysis (your themes) which are (often) broader."
- Key point: Codes and themes differ: themes are usually broader and are the units of analysis.

### E10

- Location: Phase 2: generating initial codes
- Quote: "It is important to retain accounts which depart from the dominant story in the analysis, so do not ignore these in your coding."
- Key point: Retain accounts that depart from the dominant story; do not ignore them when coding.

### E11

- Location: Phase 4: reviewing themes
- Quote: "Data within themes should cohere together meaningfully, while there should be clear and identifiable distinctions between themes."
- Key point: Data within a theme should cohere meaningfully, and themes should be clearly distinguishable from each other.

### E12

- Location: Phase 5: defining and naming themes
- Quote: "One test for this is to see whether you can describe the scope and content of each theme in a couple of sentences."
- Key point: A test of whether a theme is well defined: can you describe its scope and content in a couple of sentences?

### E13

- Location: Phase 6: producing the report
- Quote: "Choose particularly vivid examples, or extracts which capture the essence of the point you are demonstrating, without unnecessary complexity." / "Extracts need to be embedded within an analytic narrative that compelling illustrates the story that you are telling about your data"
- Key point: Choose vivid extracts that capture the essence of the point, and embed them in an analytic narrative.

### E14

- Location: Potential pitfalls
- Quote: "Thematic analysis is not just a collection of extracts strung together with little or no analytic narrative."
- Key point: Thematic analysis is not a string of extracts with little or no analysis.

### E15

- Location: Potential pitfalls
- Quote: "A second, associated, pitfall is the using of the data collection questions (such as from an interview schedule) as the" [...] "In such a case, no analytic work has been done to identify themes across the entire data set, or make sense of the patterning of responses."
- Key point: A common pitfall is reporting the interview schedule questions as themes, which means no analysis has been done.

### E16

- Location: Potential pitfalls
- Quote: "A weak or unconvincing analysis can also stem from a failure to provide adequate examples from the data" / "it is important not to misrepresent them as an overarching theme."
- Key point: One cause of unconvincing analysis is too few data examples; a few individual cases can be interesting, but do not present them as an overarching theme.

### E17

- Location: Potential pitfalls
- Quote: "A weak analysis does not appear to consider other obvious alternative readings of the data, or fails to consider variation (and even contradiction) in the account that is produced."
- Key point: A weak analysis ignores other obvious readings of the data and ignores variation and contradiction in the accounts.

### E18

- Location: What is thematic analysis?
- Quote: "it denies the active role the researcher always plays in identifying patterns/themes, selecting which are of interest, and reporting them to the readers"
- Key point: Saying themes "emerged" from the data denies the active role the researcher always plays in identifying, selecting, and reporting them.

### E19

- Location: What makes good thematic analysis?
- Quote: "what you say you are doing needs to match up with what you actually do."
- Key point: What you say you are doing must match what you actually do.
