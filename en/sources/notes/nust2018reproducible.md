# Nüst et al. 2018: Reproducible research and GIScience: an evaluation using AGILE conference papers

- Source: `nust2018reproducible`, [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6047504/), doi:10.7717/peerj.5072
- Version: PeerJ 6:e5072 (2018), open access (CC BY 4.0). The authors are geographic information scientists in Germany, Spain, Austria, the Netherlands, and elsewhere. The study examined 32 papers nominated for best paper at the annual conference of the Association of Geographic Information Laboratories in Europe (AGILE) (2010 and 2012–2017) and surveyed their authors. Full text read via Europe PMC. Locations give sections.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "the Association of Geographic Information Laboratories in Europe (AGILE), which has organised annual conferences on GIScience topics since 1998"
- Key point: AGILE has held annual conferences on geographic information science (GIScience) since 1998.

### E2

- Location: Materials & Methods, The paper corpus
- Quote: "Since 2007, the AGILE conference has had a full paper track (cf. Pundt & Toppen, 2017) and a short paper track with blind peer review. The latter is published for free on the AGILE website."
- Key point: Since 2007, the AGILE conference has had a full paper track and a short paper track (the original says "a short paper track with blind peer review"); short papers are published free on the AGILE website.

### E3

- Location: Materials & Methods, The paper corpus
- Quote: "The corpus contains 32 documents: 20 full papers (7.9% of 253 full papers since 2007) and 12 short papers"
- Key point: The evaluated corpus has 32 papers: 20 full papers (7.9% of the 253 full papers since 2007) and 12 short papers.

### E4

- Location: Results, Assessment of reproducibility
- Quote: "None of the papers reach the highest level of reproducibility in any category. [...] Especially problematic is the high number of papers (19) with level 0 for data, meaning that the specific data is not only unavailable but it is not re-createable from the information in the paper."
- Key point: No paper reached the highest level of reproducibility in any category; 19 were at level 0 for data, meaning the data were neither available nor reproducible from the information in the paper.

### E5

- Location: Materials & Methods, Definition and criteria
- Quote: "The level 0 unavailable means that there is insufficient documentation in the paper, or the information is only available upon request"
- Key point: In the evaluation, "available only upon request" also counted as level 0 (unavailable).

### E6

- Location: Results, Author feedback (survey)
- Quote: "Out of a total of 82 authors, 22 filled in the survey"
- Key point: 22 of the 82 authors completed the survey.

### E7

- Location: Results, Author feedback (survey)
- Quote: "Table 6 shows that the most frequently mentioned reasons were legal restrictions and lack of time"
- Key point: The barriers authors mentioned most often were legal restrictions and lack of time.

### E8

- Location: Discussion, Improving day-to-day research in GIScience
- Quote: "It seems that current practises in scientific publications lack full access to data and code. Instead, only methods and results are documented in writing."
- Key point: Current papers generally do not give full access to data and code; they only describe methods and results in writing.

### E9

- Location: Discussion, Suggestions to authors
- Quote: "Ultimately, proprietary software is a deal-breaker for reproducibility"
- Key point: The authors argue that proprietary (commercial, closed-source) software is ultimately a deal-breaker for reproducibility.
