# EGAP Methods Guide: 10 Conversations that Implementers and Evaluators Need to Have

- Source: `egapevaluationconversations`, [original web page](https://methods.egap.org/guides/implementation/evaluation-conversations_en.html)
- Version: Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) methods guide by Jake Bowers and Rebecca Wolfe; accessed October 2026. Written mainly for implementers, also useful to evaluators. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1 What is the goal of the evaluation?
- Quote: "Without being clear about what you and your organization need to learn, the evaluator may not design the most appropriate evaluation method."
- Key point: If implementers are not clear about what they need to learn, the evaluator may not design the most appropriate evaluation method.

### E2

- Location: §4 How can our evaluation enable learning within our organization?
- Quote: "Even when the purpose is to learn, not grade, some within an organization may still be fearful of the results, as they believe their careers are tied to achieving strong results."
- Key point: Even when an evaluation is meant for learning rather than grading, some people in the organization may fear that the results will affect their careers and may not cooperate.

### E3

- Location: §8 How and where shall we make the results of this study public?
- Quote: "The timelines that implementers and evaluators, especially ones who come from academia, have for publishing results are likely different."
- Key point: Implementers and academic evaluators likely have different timelines for publishing results.

### E4

- Location: §9 What if things do not go as expected or hoped?
- Quote: "One approach arising from project management in business involves the creation of pre-mortems in which the implementer and evaluator begin by imagining that the evaluation has returned a null or negative result."
- Key point: One approach is a "pre-mortem": both sides first imagine that the evaluation finds a null or negative result, list the possible reasons, and prepare in advance.
