# Easterday, Rees Lewis & Gerber 2014: Design-Based Research Process: Problems, Phases, and Applications

- Source: `easterday2014design`, [original PDF (International Society of the Learning Sciences repository)](https://repository.isls.org/bitstream/1/1130/1/317-324.pdf), DOI [10.22318/icls2014.317](https://doi.org/10.22318/icls2014.317)
- Version: Proceedings of the 11th International Conference of the Learning Sciences (ICLS 2014), pp. 317–324, freely available from the repository of the International Society of the Learning Sciences (ISLS); read via an Internet Archive snapshot of 2025-08-05. Locations give the subheading and proceedings page.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, p. 317
- Quote: "There is general agreement that DBR should generate effective educational interventions and useful theory"
- Key point: There is general agreement that design-based research should produce both effective educational interventions and useful theory.

### E2

- Location: Introduction, p. 317
- Quote: "The classical model of research and development, that is, basic research leading to applied research, leading to development, leading to products, does not work well (Stokes 1997). Alternatively, design, unguided by theory, is likely to be incremental and haphazard."
- Key point: The authors argue that the linear model of basic research leading to applied research, development, and products does not work well; yet design without theoretical guidance tends to be piecemeal and hit-or-miss.

### E3

- Location: Problem 1, p. 317
- Quote: "The first problem is the uncertainty about the phases of DBR--the process typically looks different depending on who conducts it. There seems to be no accepted precisely described DBR process at the level of specificity dedicated to other methodologies such as experiments or grounded theory."
- Key point: Design-based research has no accepted, concrete process comparable to those of experiments or grounded theory; it looks different depending on who conducts it.

### E4

- Location: Design Process, p. 319
- Quote: "The design and DBR processes consist of 6 iterative phases in which designers: focus the problem, understand the problem, define goals, conceive the outline of a solution, build the solution, and test the solution (Figure 1)."
- Key point: The authors' process has six iterative phases: focus the problem, understand the problem, define goals, conceive the outline of a solution, build the solution, and test the solution.

### E5

- Location: Focus, p. 319
- Quote: "In the focus phase, designers bound the audience, topic, and scope of the project. The audience specifies whom the product serves, including learners and the other stakeholders affected, such as parents or the community."
- Key point: In the focus phase, designers bound the audience (learners and others affected, such as parents and communities), the topic, and the scope.

### E6

- Location: Understand, p. 319
- Quote: "In the understand phase, designers study learners, domains, contexts and existing solutions."
- Key point: In the understand phase, designers study learners, domains, contexts, and existing solutions.

### E7

- Location: Define, p. 320
- Quote: "In the define phase, designers set goals and assessments."
- Key point: In the define phase, designers set goals and assessments.

### E8

- Location: Define, p. 320
- Quote: "Only after the goal has been defined can a design be said to succeed or fail."
- Key point: Only after the goal has been defined can a design be said to succeed or fail.

### E9

- Location: Conceive, p. 320
- Quote: "In this phase, the designer has not committed to implementing the design in a given medium, but rather creates a non-functional, symbolic or graphical representation that allows the designer to conceptually analyze the solution by determining the components of the design and how they might work together."
- Key point: In the conceive phase, the designer does not yet commit to building the product but creates a non-functional, symbolic or graphical representation of the design, analyzing its components and how they work together.

### E10

- Location: Build, p. 320
- Quote: "This implementation can be of lower or higher fidelity depending on the stage of the project and the question that the designer wants to test"
- Key point: The implemented prototype can be of lower or higher fidelity, depending on the stage of the project and the question being tested.

### E11

- Location: Test, pp. 320–321
- Quote: "Testing often uses formative evaluation, which may not establish causality to the extent possible in controlled, randomized experiments, but which can quickly reject bad designs."
- Key point: Testing often uses formative evaluation, which cannot establish causality the way randomized controlled experiments can, but can quickly weed out poor designs.

### E12

- Location: Iteration, p. 321
- Quote: "Rather than design an entire intervention and discover only at the end that it does not work, iterative design argues for quickly building low fidelity prototypes, testing them, and re-designing--gradually evolving the intervention over time."
- Key point: Rather than build an entire intervention and discover only at the end that it does not work, quickly build low-fidelity prototypes, test, and redesign, so that the intervention evolves step by step.

### E13

- Location: The DBR Process Includes Recursively Nested Research Processes, p. 322
- Quote: "For example, a DBR study of a journalism curriculum might conduct a qualitative study about learners' media practices in the understand phase, or a controlled randomized test of the curriculum in the test phase."
- Key point: Other studies can be nested within design-based research, e.g. a qualitative study in the understand phase or a randomized controlled test in the test phase.

### E14

- Location: Stage Dependent Search, p. 322
- Quote: "So in the early stages of a project, researchers should focus on low-fidelity prototyping and collecting the minimal amount of data needed to quickly reject failure and identify potential successes."
- Key point: Early in a project, focus on low-fidelity prototyping and collect the minimal data needed to quickly reject failed designs and identify promising ones.

### E15

- Location: Stage Dependent Search, p. 322
- Quote: "Once researchers have a plausible, well-grounded theory and an implementation with some evidence of success, they can conduct randomized controlled experiments to verify the efficacy of the theory and intervention. If researchers use randomized, controlled, experiments at the beginning stages of a complex design problem, they are likely to waste resources verifying a bad design."
- Key point: Once you have a plausible, well-grounded theory and an implementation with some evidence of success, verify the effect with randomized controlled experiments; running them from the start likely wastes resources on validating a poor design.

### E16

- Location: Stage Dependent Search, p. 322
- Quote: "Likewise, if researchers never advance beyond theory building and radically novel designs, they are unlikely to provide strong evidence for the efficacy of an intervention or principle."
- Key point: Conversely, researchers who never move beyond theory building and novel designs are unlikely to provide strong evidence for the efficacy of an intervention or principle.

### E17

- Location: Problem 3 resolution, p. 322
- Quote: "By incorporating scientific processes, DBR produces theories connected to the literature and more rigorously tests interventions."
- Key point: What sets design-based research apart from ordinary design work: it incorporates scientific processes, produces theories connected to the literature, and tests interventions more rigorously.

### E18

- Location: Better Design, p. 322
- Quote: "For example, when planning DBR projects, thinking about the test phase has prevented us from jumping to formal evaluation too early or dwelling in theory building too long."
- Key point: Thinking about the test phase during planning kept the authors from jumping to formal evaluation too early and from dwelling too long on theory building.

### E19

- Location: Communicating Research Process, p. 323
- Quote: "In publishing research and grant applications, the phases more concisely communicate the past history or future plans of a DBR project."
- Key point: In papers and grant applications, organizing by phases communicates the past history and future plans of a project more concisely.

### E20

- Location: Stage Dependent Search, p. 322
- Quote: "As researchers identify promising prototypes they can focus on theory building with qualitative methods to better understand the issues a design might address and the mechanism through which it affects learning."
- Key point: Once promising prototypes are found, researchers can focus on theory building with qualitative methods, to understand the problem a design addresses and the mechanisms by which it affects learning.
