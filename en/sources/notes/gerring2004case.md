# Gerring 2004: What Is a Case Study and What Is It Good For?

- Source: `gerring2004case`, [PDF posted on the author's Boston University website (Internet Archive snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://blogs.bu.edu/jgerring/files/2013/06/CasestudyAPSR.pdf)
- Version: Publisher's typeset PDF of American Political Science Review 98(2): 341–354, posted by the author on his website; locations are given as journal page numbers.

### E1

- Location: Abstract, p. 341
- Quote: "A "case study," I argue, is best defined as an intensive study of a single unit with an aim to generalize across a larger set of units."
- Key point: A case study is best defined as an intensive study of a single unit with the aim of generalizing across a larger set of units.

### E2

- Location: Scope of Proposition, p. 348
- Quote: "Research designs invariably face a choice between knowing more about less and knowing less about more."
- Key point: Research designs invariably face a choice between knowing more about less and knowing less about more.

### E3

- Location: Unit Homogeneity, p. 348
- Quote: "Single-unit research designs often fall short in their representativeness—the degree to which causal relationships evidenced by that single unit may be assumed to be true for a larger set of (unstudied) units."
- Key point: Single-unit designs often fall short on representativeness: whether the causal relationships seen in that one unit can be assumed to hold for a larger set of unstudied units.

### E4

- Location: Causal Insight, p. 349
- Quote: "Ceteris paribus, case studies are more likely to shed light on causal mechanisms and less likely to shed light on true causal effects."
- Key point: Other things being equal, case studies are better at shedding light on causal mechanisms than on true causal effects.

### E5

- Location: Strategy of Research, p. 350
- Quote: "The point is, theory confirmation/disconfirmation is not the case study's strong suit."
- Key point: Confirming or disconfirming theory is not the case study's strong suit.

### E6

- Location: Strategy of Research, p. 350
- Quote: "I do not mean to suggest that case studies never serve a confirmatory role. As discussed, evidence drawn from a single unit may disconfirm a necessary or sufficient hypothesis."
- Key point: Case studies can still play a confirmatory role: evidence from a single unit can disconfirm a hypothesis about a necessary or sufficient condition.

### E7

- Location: Conclusion, p. 352
- Quote: "case studies are generally more useful (1) when inferences are descriptive rather than causal, (2) when propositional depth is prized over breadth and boundedness, (3) when (internal) case comparability is given precedence over (external) case representativeness, (4) when insight into causal mechanisms is more important than insight into causal effects, (5) when the causal proposition at issue is invariant rather than probabilistic, (6) when the strategy of research is exploratory, rather than confirmatory, and (7) when useful variance is available for only a single unit or a small number of units."
- Key point: Case studies are generally more useful in seven situations: when inferences are descriptive, when depth is prized, when case comparability takes precedence over representativeness, when insight into causal mechanisms is the goal, when the proposition is deterministic, when research is exploratory, and when useful variation exists only in a few units.

### E8

- Location: Conclusion, p. 353
- Quote: "A field dominated by case studies may have little need for another case study. A field where cross-unit studies are hegemonic may be desperately in need of in-depth studies focused on single units."
- Key point: A field already full of case studies may have little need for another; a field dominated by cross-unit studies may badly need in-depth single-unit studies.
