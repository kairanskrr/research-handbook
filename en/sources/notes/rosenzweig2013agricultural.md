# Rosenzweig et al. 2013: The Agricultural Model Intercomparison and Improvement Project (AgMIP): Protocols and pilot studies

- Source: `rosenzweig2013agricultural`, [original PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150002678/downloads/20150002678.pdf) (DOI 10.1016/j.agrformet.2012.09.011)
- Version: Agricultural and Forest Meteorology 170:166-182, 2013. The typeset journal version made public on the NASA Technical Reports Server; the server returns 403 to scripts, so it was downloaded via an Internet Archive snapshot from December 2024. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §3.2 Crop Modeling Team Protocols, objective 1
- Quote: "Evaluate models for a range of crops and regions by comparing outputs with observed growth and yield data, including responses to [CO2], temperature, water shortage, water excess, and interactions with management factors."
- Key point: The crop modeling team's first objective: evaluate models for a range of crops and regions by comparing outputs with observed growth and yield data, including responses to CO2 concentration, temperature, water shortage, water excess, and management.

### E2

- Location: §3.2 Crop Modeling Team Protocols
- Quote: "multiple models for the same crop are initially calibrated using Sentinel Site-specific datasets" / "The primary goal of this activity is to obtain an estimate of crop model uncertainty calculated from the responses of an ensemble of crop models for a given crop and region."
- Key point: Multiple models for the same crop are first calibrated with Sentinel Site data; the main goal is to estimate crop model uncertainty from the spread of responses across the model ensemble.

- Location: §3.2 Crop Modeling Team Protocols

- Key point: The crop modelers' calibration of model parameters and code improvements are documented.

### E4

- Location: §5.1 AgMIP protocol examples, wheat pilot (Obregón, Mexico)
- Quote: "Most of the variability in absolute yield response to a given emission scenario was caused by the five crop models rather than by differences in the five GCMs. For example, 88% of the simulated variance in A2 yields was a first-order effect due to variability between crop models (which may be influenced by biases in the baseline calibration), 10% was the first-order effect due to variability between GCMs"
- Key point: In the wheat pilot near Obregón, Mexico, most of the variability in yield response came from the five crop models, not the five global climate models (GCMs); under the A2 scenario, 88% of the simulated yield variance came from differences among crop models (possibly affected by calibration bias in the baseline period) and 10% from differences among climate models.
