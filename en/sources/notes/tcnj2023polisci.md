# The College of New Jersey Department of Political Science: Disciplinary Standards for Reappointment, Tenure, and Promotion in Political Science

- Source: `tcnj2023polisci`, [original](https://academicaffairs.tcnj.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/171/2023/05/Political-Science-Disciplinary-Standards.pdf)
- Version: Disciplinary standards for reappointment, tenure, and promotion of the Department of Political Science at The College of New Jersey (US); the text was last revised April 5, 2023, and signed by the department chair in June 2022, the dean in September 2022, and the provost in May 2023. Read from the Internet Archive copy of the PDF on the college website (https://web.archive.org/web/20250325153514/https://academicaffairs.tcnj.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/171/2023/05/Political-Science-Disciplinary-Standards.pdf).

### E1

- Location: Scholarship section
- Quote: "In evaluating an applicant's scholarship, there is no precise formula to weigh a book vs. articles, but generally a book would be considered the equivalent of two to four articles."
- Key point: When the department evaluates scholarship, there is no precise conversion formula between books and articles; a book generally counts as two to four articles.

### E2

- Location: Scholarship section
- Quote: "Conference papers, which typically lack peer review, are an important type of scholarly activity, but are regarded primarily as evidence of ongoing scholarly activity and a precursor to journal submission and acceptance."
- Key point: Conference papers are usually not peer-reviewed and are mainly seen as evidence of ongoing research being prepared for journal submission.

### E3

- Location: Scholarship section
- Quote: "Conference papers published as part of a conference Proceedings (rare for our discipline) should be similarly explained and the extent of peer review must be clarified."
- Key point: Formal publication in conference proceedings is rare in political science; where it occurs, the candidate should explain how much peer review was involved.
