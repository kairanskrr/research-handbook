# National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health 2009: Approaches to Safe Nanotechnology

- Source: `niosh2009approaches`, [original (Internet Archive snapshot)](http://web.archive.org/web/20260726080036/https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2009-125/pdfs/2009-125.pdf)
- Version: publication of the US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH), DHHS (NIOSH) Publication No. 2009-125, 2009. cdc.gov returned 403 to scripts; we read the PDF in a 2026-07-26 Internet Archive snapshot.

### E1

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Engineered nanoparticles are materials purposefully produced with at least one dimension between 1 and 100 nanometers."
- Key point: Engineered nanoparticles are materials purposefully produced with at least one dimension between 1 and 100 nanometers.

### E2

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Until the results from research studies can fully elucidate the characteristics of nanoparticles that may pose a health risk, precautionary measures are warranted."
- Key point: Until research can fully elucidate which nanoparticle characteristics may pose a health risk, precautionary measures are warranted.

### E3

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Nanomaterials have the greatest potential to enter the body through the respiratory system if they are airborne and in the form of respirable-sized particles (nanoparticles)."
- Key point: Nanomaterials are most likely to enter the body through the respiratory system when they are airborne as respirable-sized particles.

### E4

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Current knowledge indicates that a well-designed exhaust ventilation system with a high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filter should effectively remove nanomaterials."
- Key point: A well-designed exhaust ventilation system with a high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filter should effectively remove nanomaterials.

### E5

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "Examples of good practices include cleaning of work areas using HEPA vacuum pickup and wet wiping methods, preventing the consumption of food or beverages in workplaces where nanomaterials are handled, providing hand-washing facilities"
- Key point: Good practices include cleaning work areas with HEPA vacuums and wet wiping, banning food and drink where nanomaterials are handled, and providing handwashing facilities.

### E6

- Location: Executive Summary
- Quote: "It should be recognized that exposure limits recommended for nonnanoscale particles may not be health protective for nanoparticle exposures"
- Key point: Exposure limits recommended for non-nanoscale particles may not protect health against nanoparticle exposure.

### E7

- Location: 8.3.3 Work practices
- Quote: "Storing dispersible nanomaterials, whether suspended in liquids or in a dry particle form in closed (tightly sealed) containers whenever possible."
- Key point: Store dispersible nanomaterials, whether suspended in liquid or as dry powder, in tightly sealed containers whenever possible.

### E8

- Location: 8.3.3 Work practices
- Quote: "Dry sweeping or air hoses should not be used to clean work areas."
- Key point: Do not clean work areas by dry sweeping or with air hoses.

### E9

- Location: 6.1 Fire and Explosion Risk
- Quote: "Decreasing the particle size of combustible materials can increase combustion potential and combustion rate, leading to the possibility of relatively inert materials becoming highly reactive in the nanometer size range."
- Key point: Smaller particle size can increase the combustion potential and combustion rate of combustible materials; relatively inert materials can become highly reactive at the nanoscale.

### E10

- Location: 8.3.3 Work practices
- Quote: "Avoiding handling nanomaterials in the open air in a 'free particle' state."
- Key point: Avoid handling nanomaterials in the open air in a "free particle" state.
