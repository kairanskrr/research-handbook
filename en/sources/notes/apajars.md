# APA Style: Journal Article Reporting Standards (JARS)

- Source: `apajars`, [original](https://apastyle.apa.org/jars)
- Version: web page; the site blocks scripted access, so a 2025 Internet Archive snapshot was used.

### E1

- Location: What are APA Style JARS?
- Quote: "APA Style Journal Article Reporting Standards (APA Style jars) are a set of standards designed for journal authors, reviewers, and editors to enhance scientific rigor in peer-reviewed journal articles."
- Key point: APA Style Journal Article Reporting Standards (JARS) are standards for journal authors, reviewers, and editors, designed to enhance the scientific rigor of journal articles.

### E2

- Location: Guidelines
- Quote: "Use JARS–Quant when you collect your study data in numerical form or report them through statistical analyses." / "Use JARS–Qual when you collect your study data in the form of natural language and expression." / "Use JARS–Mixed when your study combines both quantitative and qualitative methods."
- Key point: The three standards divide the work: JARS–Quant for numerical data and statistical analyses; JARS–Qual for data in textual or linguistic form; JARS–Mixed for combinations of the two.

### E3

- Location: What are APA Style JARS?
- Quote: "jars–rec should be applied to all research, whether it is quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods."
- Key point: A separate standard on discussing race, ethnicity, and culture (JARS–REC) applies to all research.
