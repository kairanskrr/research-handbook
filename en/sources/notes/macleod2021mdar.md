# Macleod et al. 2021: The MDAR (Materials Design Analysis Reporting) Framework for transparent reporting in the life sciences

- Source: `macleod2021mdar`, [full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8092464/), DOI [10.1073/pnas.2103238118](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2103238118); the accompanying [MDAR Framework](https://osf.io/xfpn4/), [Checklist](https://osf.io/bj3mu/), and [Elaboration document](https://osf.io/xzy4s/) are hosted on the Open Science Framework (OSF)
- Version: PNAS 118(17):e2103238118, 2021, open access (CC BY 4.0), read 2026-10-08. The authors are editors from several publishers (Nature Portfolio, Cell Press, Science, PLOS, eLife, Wiley) and staff of the Center for Open Science (US). The Elaboration document is version V4 from 2020. Locations give the subheading or the Elaboration section number.

### E1

- Location: main text, Devising a Harmonized Reporting Framework
- Quote: "The scope of the MDAR Framework was the life sciences, and within this our project had a strong focus on information that is relevant for exploratory laboratory research" / "The MDAR Framework is not comprehensive and should not supersede any existing reporting standards."
- Key point: MDAR's scope is the life sciences, with a strong focus on the information exploratory laboratory research needs to report; it does not aim to be comprehensive or to replace existing specialized reporting standards.

### E2

- Location: main text, opening and Competing interest statement
- Quote: "Our MDAR working group came together in late 2017 with the intention to develop a policy framework to promote transparent reporting" / "Several members of the working group are employed by publishing organizations and they acknowledge that their participation does not commit their organization to endorse the MDAR Framework."
- Key point: The MDAR working group formed in late 2017 to develop a journal policy framework promoting transparent reporting; most members work for publishers, but participation does not mean their organizations will adopt MDAR.

### E3

- Location: Elaboration §2.4 Sample definition and replicates
- Quote: "Number of times the experiment was replicated in the laboratory: when describing the results of an experiment, state how many times the experiment was independently performed in the laboratory."
- Key point: When reporting the results of an experiment, state how many times it was independently replicated in the laboratory.

### E4

- Location: Elaboration §2.4
- Quote: "define how many replicates were performed and whether they are biological (derived from different experimental units or subjects) or technical (multiple contemporary measurements from the same experimental unit or subject) replicates."
- Key point: State how many replicates were performed and whether they are biological (from different experimental units) or technical (multiple measurements of the same unit at once).

### E5

- Location: Elaboration §2.4, Best practice
- Quote: "Samples must be unambiguously described including a clear definition of the unit of study. In studies using model organisms, cell lines, primary cell cultures, plants or microbes, the unit of study is the smallest object that could be randomly and independently assigned to an intervention."
- Key point: Define the unit of study unambiguously. In studies with model organisms, cell lines, primary cells, plants, or microbes, it is the smallest entity that can be randomly and independently assigned to a treatment.

### E6

- Location: Elaboration §2.4, Best practice
- Quote: "The groups being compared, including control groups should be clearly defined. If no control group has been used, the rationale for this should be stated."
- Key point: Define the groups being compared clearly, including control groups; if there is no control group, state the rationale.

### E7

- Location: Elaboration §3.2 Statistics, Small sample sizes
- Quote: "When technical replicates are reported, error and significance measures reflect the experimental variability, not the variability of the biological process; it is misleading not to state this clearly."
- Key point: When technical replicates are reported, error and significance measures reflect experimental variability, not the variability of the biological process itself; failing to make this clear misleads readers.

### E8

- Location: Elaboration §2.3 (listed under the requirements for in vivo animal studies), Randomization
- Quote: "Whether samples are randomly assigned to experimental groups, to processing order, or to positions in a multiwell device may influence experimental outcome. Ideally, data should be collected randomly or the samples appropriately blocked."
- Key point: Whether samples are randomly assigned to groups, to processing order, or to positions in a multiwell plate can all affect results; ideally collect data in random order, or block samples appropriately.

### E9

- Location: Elaboration §2.3, Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Quote: "Investigators should define the criteria for identifying and dealing with outliers before running the experiments to prevent confirmatory bias"
- Key point: Define the criteria for identifying and handling outliers before running the experiments, to prevent confirmatory bias (keeping only the data that support what you expected).

### E10

- Location: Elaboration §1.4 Cell materials (Cell lines), Best practice
- Quote: "researchers should check their cell lines stocks against the list of commonly misidentified cell lines maintained by the International Cell Line Authentication Committee" / "Best practice requires that cell lines from the ICLAC Register of Misidentified Cell Lines are not used and that all other cell lines are routinely authenticated."
- Key point: Researchers should check their cell lines against the list of commonly misidentified cell lines maintained by the International Cell Line Authentication Committee (ICLAC); best practice is to avoid lines on that list and authenticate other lines regularly.
