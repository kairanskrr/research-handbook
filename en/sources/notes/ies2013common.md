# US Institute of Education Sciences and National Science Foundation 2013: Common Guidelines for Education Research and Development

- Source: `ies2013common`, [original PDF](https://ies.ed.gov/pdf/commonguidelines.pdf)
- Version: August 2013, published jointly by the Institute of Education Sciences (IES) of the US Department of Education and the US National Science Foundation (NSF). Locations give section headings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Most simply, the six types of research described in this document form a "pipeline" of evidence that begins with basic and exploratory research, moves to design and development of interventions or strategies, and, for interventions or strategies with initial promise, results in examination of the effectiveness for improving learning or another related education outcome."
- Key point: Most simply, the six types of research described form a "pipeline" of evidence: from basic and exploratory research, to design and development of interventions, to testing the effects of promising interventions.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "However, as we describe later in this document, the reality of scientific investigation is more complicated, less orderly, and less linear than such a "pipeline" suggests."
- Key point: But real scientific investigation is more complicated, less orderly, and less linear than a "pipeline."

### E3

- Location: Types of Research
- Quote: "Research Type #2: Early-Stage or Exploratory Research examines relationships among important constructs in education and learning to establish logical connections that may form the basis for future interventions or strategies to improve education outcomes. These connections are usually correlational rather than causal."
- Key point: Early-stage or exploratory research examines relationships among important constructs in education to lay the groundwork for future interventions; these relationships are usually correlational, not causal.

### E4

- Location: Types of Research
- Quote: "Research projects of this type draw on existing theory and evidence to design and iteratively develop interventions or strategies, including testing individual components to provide feedback in the development process."
- Key point: Design and development research draws on existing theory and evidence to design and iteratively develop interventions, including testing individual components to feed back into development.

### E5

- Location: Types of Research
- Quote: "Research Type #4: Efficacy Research allows for testing of a strategy or intervention under "ideal" circumstances, including with a higher level of support or developer involvement than would be the case under normal circumstances."
- Key point: Efficacy research tests an intervention under "ideal" conditions, for example with more support from the developer than usual.

### E6

- Location: Types of Research
- Quote: "Research Type #5: Effectiveness Research examines effectiveness of a strategy or intervention under circumstances that would typically prevail in the target context."
- Key point: Effectiveness research tests an intervention under the conditions that typically prevail in the target context.

### E7

- Location: Types of Research
- Quote: "Research Type #6: Scale-up Research examines effectiveness in a wide range of populations, contexts, and circumstances, without substantial developer involvement in implementation or evaluation."
- Key point: Scale-up research tests effectiveness across a wide range of populations, contexts, and circumstances, without substantial developer involvement in implementation or evaluation.

### E8

- Location: Purpose of Design and Development Research (Table 1)
- Quote: "(3) Collection of data on the feasibility of implementing the solution(s) in typical delivery settings by intended users; and (4) Conducting a pilot study to examine the promise of generating the intended outcomes."
- Key point: Design and development research typically includes collecting data on the feasibility of implementation by intended users in typical settings, and a pilot study to see whether the intervention shows promise of producing the intended outcomes.

### E9

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "In contrast, ED concentrates its investments on developing and testing the effectiveness of well-defined curricula, programs, and practices that could be implemented by schools."
- Key point: The US Department of Education (ED) concentrates its investment on developing and testing the effectiveness of curricula, programs, and practices that schools can implement.

### E10

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "For example, a project that involves design and development of an intervention or strategy should, at its conclusion, have generated a theory of action, a set of intervention components, and preliminary evidence regarding promise for improving education outcomes."
- Key point: A project that designs and develops an intervention should end with a theory of action, a set of intervention components, and preliminary evidence that the intervention may improve education outcomes.

### E11

- Location: Table 4: Guidelines for Evidence to Be Produced by Studies — Design and Development Research, Project Outcomes
- Quote: "The project's outcomes should include (1) a fully developed version of the proposed design-research (including all materials necessary for its implementation ); (2) a well-specified theory of action, including evidence supporting or refuting key assumptions of the intervention's original theoretical basis ; (3) descriptions of the major design iterations and the resulting evidence to support or question key assumptions about the theory of action; (4) description and empirical evidence of the adjustments to the theory of action and intervention design that resulted from design testing;"
- Key point: Design and development research should produce: (1) a fully developed intervention with all materials needed to implement it; (2) a well-specified theory of action, with evidence supporting or refuting its key assumptions; (3) descriptions of and evidence from the major design iterations; (4) descriptions of and evidence for adjustments to the theory of action and design after testing.

### E12

- Location: Table 4 — Design and Development Research, Project Outcomes
- Quote: "(5) measures with evidence of technical quality for assessing the implementation of the intervention in an authentic education delivery setting and data demonstrating the project's success in such implementation; and (6) pilot data on the intervention's promise for generating the intended beneficial learner outcomes."
- Key point: It should also produce: (5) measures, with evidence of their quality, for assessing implementation in an authentic education setting, plus data showing successful implementation; and (6) pilot data showing the intervention's promise for the intended learning outcomes.

### E13

- Location: Table 4 — Design and Development Research, Research Plan
- Quote: "The research plan should describe the (1) method for developing the intervention to the point where it can be used by the intended end-users (iterative development process); (2) method for collecting evidence on the feasibility that end users can implement the intervention in an authentic education or learning setting (evidence of feasibility of implementation); and (3) method for obtaining pilot data on the promise of the intervention for achieving the expected outcomes (pilot study)."
- Key point: The research plan should describe three things: how the intervention will be iteratively developed until intended users can use it; how evidence will be collected on whether users can implement it in real settings; and how a pilot study will gather data on its promise for achieving the intended outcomes.

### E14

- Location: Appendix, Evidence to be Produced by Design and Development Research — External Feedback Plan
- Quote: "The project should be subject to a series of external, critical reviews of its design and activities (including theoretical framework, data collection, analyses, and reporting)."
- Key point: The project's design and activities (theoretical framework, data collection, analysis, reporting) should undergo a series of external critical reviews.
