# Mastrandrea et al. 2010: Guidance Note for Lead Authors of the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report on Consistent Treatment of Uncertainties

- Source: `mastrandrea2010ipcc`, [full text](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)
- Version: a 4-page guidance note issued in November 2010 by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) for the Lead Authors of the Fifth Assessment Report (AR5). Locations give the paragraph number.

### E1

- Location: p. 1, Introduction
- Quote: "These guidance notes are intended to assist Lead Authors of the Fifth Assessment Report (AR5) in the consistent treatment of uncertainties across all three Working Groups."
- Key point: The note is for the Lead Authors of the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report, to make the treatment of uncertainty consistent across the three Working Groups.

### E2

- Location: p. 1, the two metrics
- Quote: "Confidence in the validity of a finding, based on the type, amount, quality, and consistency of evidence (e.g., mechanistic understanding, theory, data, models, expert judgment) and the degree of agreement. Confidence is expressed qualitatively."
- Key point: The first metric is confidence: a qualitative judgment, expressed in words, based on the type, amount, quality, and consistency of evidence and the degree of agreement.

### E3

- Location: p. 1, the two metrics
- Quote: "Quantified measures of uncertainty in a finding expressed probabilistically (based on statistical analysis of observations or model results, or expert judgment)."
- Key point: The second metric is quantified uncertainty expressed probabilistically, based on statistical analysis of observations or model results, or on expert judgment.

### E4

- Location: paragraph 9
- Quote: "A level of confidence is expressed using five qualifiers: "very low," "low," "medium," "high," and "very high.""
- Key point: Confidence has five levels: very low, low, medium, high, and very high.

### E5

- Location: paragraph 9
- Quote: "Confidence should not be interpreted probabilistically, and it is distinct from "statistical confidence.""
- Key point: Confidence is not a probability, and it is distinct from "statistical confidence."

### E6

- Location: paragraph 10 and Table 1
- Quote: "A statement that an outcome is "likely" means that the probability of this outcome can range from 66% (fuzzy boundaries implied) to 100% probability." / "Virtually certain 99-100% probability Very likely 90-100% probability Likely 66-100% probability About as likely as not 33 to 66% probability Unlikely 0-33% probability Very unlikely 0-10% probability Exceptionally unlikely 0-1% probability"
- Key point: Likelihood is expressed with fixed terms, each tied to a probability range; e.g., "likely" means 66–100% and "very unlikely" means 0–10%.

### E7

- Location: paragraph 10
- Quote: ""About as likely as not" should not be used to express a lack of knowledge"
- Key point: "About as likely as not" should not be used to express a lack of knowledge.

### E8

- Location: paragraph 10
- Quote: "When there is sufficient information, it is preferable to specify the full probability distribution or a probability range"
- Key point: When there is sufficient information, it is preferable to give the full probability distribution or a probability range rather than words alone.

### E9

- Location: paragraph 2
- Quote: "Be prepared to make expert judgments in developing key findings, and to explain those judgments by providing a traceable account"
- Key point: Authors must make expert judgments and explain them with a traceable account of what they are based on.

### E10

- Location: paragraph 3
- Quote: "Be aware of a tendency for a group to converge on an expressed view and become overconfident in it."
- Key point: Be aware of a tendency for a group to converge on an expressed view and become overconfident in it.

### E11

- Location: paragraph 6
- Quote: "Experts tend to underestimate structural uncertainty arising from incomplete understanding of or competing conceptual frameworks for relevant systems and processes."
- Key point: Experts tend to underestimate structural uncertainty, which comes from incomplete understanding of the system or competing conceptual frameworks.

### E12

- Location: paragraph 4
- Quote: "Be aware that the way in which a statement is framed will have an effect on how it is interpreted (e.g., a 10% chance of dying is interpreted more negatively than a 90% chance of surviving)."
- Key point: How a statement is framed affects how readers interpret it.

### E13

- Location: p. 1, Introduction
- Quote: "Author teams are therefore encouraged to provide information on the tails of distributions of key variables"
- Key point: Author teams are encouraged to give information on the tails of distributions of key variables (low-probability, high-consequence outcomes).

### E14

- Location: paragraph 10
- Quote: "there is evidence that readers may adjust their interpretation of this likelihood language according to the magnitude of perceived potential consequences."
- Key point: Readers may adjust their interpretation of likelihood terms according to how severe the potential consequences seem.
