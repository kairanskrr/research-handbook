# College Art Association 2014: Standards for the Practice of Art History

- Source: `caa2014standards`, [Original](https://www.collegeart.org/standards-and-guidelines/guidelines/art-history-ethics)
- Version: Adopted by the College Art Association (CAA) Board of Directors on October 26, 2014, replacing the Code of Ethics for Art Historians in place since 1973. Web page. Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: II. Research and Publishing
- Quote: "Art historians are responsible for carefully documenting their findings and then making available to others their sources, evidence, and data. They must guard against misrepresenting evidence and against plagiarism."
- Key point: Art historians must document their findings carefully, make their sources, evidence, and data available to others, and guard against misrepresenting evidence and plagiarism.

### E2

- Location: II. Research and Publishing
- Quote: "Scholarly integrity demands an awareness of personal and cultural bias and openness to issues of difference as these may inflect methodology and analysis."
- Key point: Scholarly integrity demands awareness of personal and cultural bias.

### E3

- Location: II.B.1
- Quote: "It is a maxim of scholarship that authors should be scrupulous in crediting sources, not only of ideas and textual material but also of photographs,"
- Key point: Credit sources not only for ideas and text but also for photographs.

### E4

- Location: II.B.1
- Quote: "Comments made by other art historians on the mounts of drawings, on the backs of photographs, recorded in museum dossiers, or reported orally should be cited when relevant to one"
- Key point: Other scholars' opinions written on drawing mounts, on the backs of photographs, or in museum dossiers, or reported orally, should be cited when relevant.

### E5

- Location: II.B.2
- Quote: "It is, however, the responsibility of art historians working in the living art traditions to deposit original or copies of all field data related to said publications [...] The art historian is responsible for depositing some form of any published material within the community."
- Key point: Art historians working on living art traditions should deposit originals or copies of field data related to their publications with institutions in the local community, and send their published work back to the community.

### E6

- Location: II.B.2
- Quote: "All too often, art historians fail to consider the host community, provide the published results of their research or archive field data with the host community or elsewhere."
- Key point: Art historians often fail to consider host communities and do not leave their findings or field data with them.

### E7

- Location: II.A.1
- Quote: "An art historian has the moral obligation to share the discovery of primary source material with his or her colleagues and serious students. [...] The finder should seek to publish research within three years,"
- Key point: Finding primary source material creates a moral obligation to share it with colleagues; the finder should aim to publish within three years.

### E8

- Location: II.A.4
- Quote: "An art historian may also influence archival custodians to reserve material, even though it may be ten or more years before a book can be published. These practices are improper professional conduct."
- Key point: Influencing archival custodians to reserve material for yourself and keep others from using it is improper professional conduct.

### E9

- Location: IV.C.2.c
- Quote: "The tactful but outright refusal of gifts from artists may be frustrating, but such practice insures integrity of the scholarly process."
- Key point: Declining gifts from artists protects the integrity of the scholarly process (publication may raise the value of the work).

### E10

- Location: II.E
- Quote: "Especially in digital media, the understanding of fair use and intellectual property is rapidly evolving, so it is particularly important to seek the most current resources possible."
- Key point: Understanding of fair use and intellectual property is changing fast, so seek the most current resources.

### E11

- Location: III.B.1
- Quote: "providing that the student is given full credit for what he or she has done."
- Key point: When a teacher and student publish together, the student must get full credit for their contribution.

### E12

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "These Standards do not at the present time include provisions for their enforcement."
- Key point: These standards currently include no provisions for enforcement.

### E13

- Location: II.F.1
- Quote: "There is nothing professionally unethical about an author submitting a manuscript to more than one publisher simultaneously as long as the author notifies the publishers that he or she has made multiple submissions."
- Key point: Submitting a book manuscript to several publishers at once is not unethical, as long as each publisher is told.

### E14

- Location: I.C.1
- Quote: "guidelines for evaluating academic publications in a world in which art-history monograph publishing opportunities are diminished"
- Key point: CAA's tenure guidelines address how to evaluate scholarship in a world where opportunities to publish art-history monographs are shrinking.
