# Anderson & Shattuck 2012: Design-Based Research: A Decade of Progress in Education Research?

- Source: `anderson2012design`, [author manuscript (Athabasca University institutional repository)](https://auspace.athabascau.ca/handle/2149/3184)
- Version: author's accepted manuscript (Word file) in the institutional repository of Athabasca University, the first author's institution; the published version appeared in Educational Researcher 41(1): 16–25, 2012. Locations give subheadings of the author manuscript.

### E1

- Location: introduction
- Quote: "DBR is a methodology designed by and for educators that seeks to increase the impact, transfer, and translation of education research into improved practice. In addition, it stresses the need for theory building and development of design principles that guide, inform, and improve both practice and research in educational contexts."
- Key point: Design-based research (DBR) is a methodology designed by and for educators that seeks to increase the translation of research into improved practice, while emphasizing the development of theory and design principles.

### E2

- Location: What is DBR?
- Quote: "Being situated in a real educational context."
- Key point: One characteristic: it is situated in a real educational context.

### E3

- Location: What is DBR? — Involving multiple iterations
- Quote: "This evolution, through multiple iterations is but one of the challenges of the methodology in that it is difficult to know when (or if ever) the research program is completed."
- Key point: It requires multiple iterations, which creates a challenge: it is hard to know when (or whether) the research is finished.

### E4

- Location: What is DBR? — Involving a collaborative partnership between research(s) and practitioner(s)
- Quote: "In action research the educator is both researcher and teacher (Kuhn & Quigley, 1997). The partnership in a design-based study recognizes that teachers are usually too busy and often ill trained to conduct rigorous research."
- Key point: In action research, the educator is both researcher and teacher; design-based research is a partnership between researchers and teachers, because teachers are usually too busy and often lack training to conduct rigorous research.

### E5

- Location: Comparison to Action Research
- Quote: "Finally, we repeat that action research is normally carried on by the teacher(s) alone, thus not benefitting from the expertise and energy of a research and design team that characterizes DBR."
- Key point: Action research is normally carried out by teachers alone, without the research and design team that design-based research has.

### E6

- Location: Problems with DBR
- Quote: "They argued that "if a researcher is intimately involved in the conceptualization, design, development, implementation, and re-searching of a pedagogical approach, then ensuring that researchers can make credible and trustworthy assertions is a challenge" (p. 10)."
- Key point: The authors cite Barab and Squire's critique: when a researcher is closely involved in conceptualizing, designing, implementing, and researching a pedagogical approach, ensuring credible claims is a challenge.

### E7

- Location: Problems with DBR
- Quote: "Herrington, McKenney, Reeves, and Oliver (2007) addressed this concern head-on in an article demonstrating ways in which DBR can be used as a basis for a doctoral dissertation over a four year period."
- Key point: On the problem of many iterations and an open-ended timeline, the authors point to an article on how design-based research can be done as a doctoral dissertation within four years.

### E8

- Location: Results
- Quote: "There was no meta-analysis which is not surprising given that the studies generally lacked control group comparisons, in line with DBR philosophy."
- Key point: The 47 highly cited articles the authors analyzed included no meta-analysis; the studies generally lacked control groups, in line with the methodology's philosophy.

### E9

- Location: Results
- Quote: "Unlike quantitative studies most DBR studies do not produce measureable effect sizes that demonstrate "what works.""
- Key point: Unlike quantitative studies, most design-based research studies do not produce effect sizes that demonstrate "what works."

### E10

- Location: What is DBR? — Use of mixed methods
- Quote: "DBR is largely agnostic when it comes to epistemological challenges to choice of methodologies used and typically involves mixed methods using a variety of research tools and techniques."
- Key point: Design-based research is largely agnostic about method choice and typically uses mixed methods with a variety of research tools.

### E11

- Location: What is DBR? — Focussing on the design and testing of a significant intervention
- Quote: "The creation begins with an accurate assessment of the local context; is informed by relevant literature, theory and practice from other contexts; and is designed specifically to overcome some problem or create an improvement in local practice."
- Key point: The design of an intervention begins with an accurate assessment of the local context, draws on relevant literature, theory, and practice from other contexts, and is designed to solve a specific problem or improve local practice.

### E12

- Location: What is DBR? — Focussing on the design and testing of a significant intervention
- Quote: "The intervention may be a learning activity, a type of assessment, the introduction of an administrative activity (such as a change in holidays), or be a technological intervention- to mention just a few of the common types of interventions."
- Key point: The intervention may be a learning activity, a type of assessment, an administrative change (such as a change in holidays), or a technology tool, among others.

### E13

- Location: What is DBR? — Focussing on the design and testing of a significant intervention
- Quote: "The researcher is careful to document the time, commitment, and contingencies that are involved in the creation and implementation of the intervention. These are documented so that readers of the research can judge for themselves the possibility of achieving similar –or even better results of the use of this intervention in their own context."
- Key point: The researcher carefully documents the time, commitment, and contingencies involved in designing and implementing the intervention, so readers can judge whether similar results are likely in their own context.

### E14

- Location: What is DBR? — Involving a collaborative partnership between research(s) and practitioner(s)
- Quote: "The partnership in a design-based study recognizes that teachers are usually too busy and often ill trained to conduct rigorous research. Likewise, the researcher is often not knowledgeable of the complexities of the culture, technology, objectives, and politics of an operating educational system to effectively create and measure the impact of an intervention."
- Key point: The reasons for partnership: teachers are usually too busy and often ill trained to conduct rigorous research; researchers often do not understand the culture, technology, goals, and personnel politics of real educational systems.

### E15

- Location: What is DBR? — Involving a collaborative partnership between research(s) and practitioner(s)
- Quote: "Thus, a partnership is developed that negotiates the study from initial problem identification, through literature review, to intervention design and construction, implementation, assessment, and the creation and publication of theoretical and design principles."
- Key point: The partnership runs throughout: from problem identification, through literature review, designing and building the intervention, implementation, and evaluation, to developing and publishing theory and design principles.

### E16

- Location: What is DBR? — Evolution of design-principles
- Quote: "These principles are not designed to create de-contextualized principles or grand theories that function with equal effect in all contexts. Rather, design principles reflect the conditions in which they operate."
- Key point: Design principles are not de-contextualized grand theories that work equally well everywhere; they reflect the conditions under which they operate.

### E17

- Location: Problems with DBR
- Quote: "Good research demands "skepticism, commitment and detachment" (Norris, 1997) but DBR also requires comradeship, enthusiasm, and a willingness to actively support the intervention. Thus, a certain wisdom is needed to walk this narrow line between objectivity and bias."
- Key point: Good research demands skepticism and detachment, but design-based research also requires comradeship, enthusiasm, and active support for the intervention; you have to walk a narrow path between objectivity and bias.

### E18

- Location: Problems with DBR
- Quote: "We also note a partial solution is for established educational researchers to develop multi-year DBR research agendas that have legitimate space and roles for graduate students to undertake and ‘own’ significant pieces of this larger agenda."
- Key point: A partial solution: established researchers set up multi-year research agendas in which graduate students take on, and "own," a significant piece.
