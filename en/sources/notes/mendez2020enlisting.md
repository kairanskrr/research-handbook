# Mendez 2020: Enlisting the Gatekeeper: Chain-Referral and Elite Access in Foreign Policy Analysis

- Source: `mendez2020enlisting`, [full text](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/103025/4/Mendez_2020_Foreign_Policy_Analysis_Case_Study.pdf)
- Version: SAGE Research Methods Cases, 2020, DOI 10.4135/9781529711318; the published version deposited by the author in the institutional repository of the London School of Economics (LSE). The author, Alvaro Mendez, describes his experience interviewing US and Colombian policymakers while researching Plan Colombia; it is one person's case experience, not a systematic study.

### E1

- Location: Abstract; Project Overview and Context
- Quote: "The author found that non-random "snowball sampling" or chain-referral can begin with the gatekeepers, who themselves give access to elite interviewees. In the context of a process-tracing study, this is a potent methodology. In short, the key to getting "snowball access" rolling is the interpersonal relationships that the researcher should establish with the first few persons who grant an interview." / "A single method solved both problems, and aided the author to interview more than half of the decision makers known to have had a hand in the making of Plan Colombia"
- Key point: The author's experience: non-random "snowball sampling" (chain referral) can start with gatekeepers who give access to elite interviewees; it works well in process-tracing research, and the key is building relationships with the first few interviewees. This way he interviewed more than half of the policymakers known to have been involved in making Plan Colombia.

### E2

- Location: Research Design--The Case for Snowballing into Elite Interviews
- Quote: "random sampling is hit-or-miss if "the causal processes of interest are very specific episodes of decisionmaking at the elite level, where a limited set of actors are involved in deliberations, decisions, and actions regarding a particular political outcome" (Tansey, 2007, p. 769). Thus, it is that more and more methodology theorists are acknowledging the validity of non-probability sampling of prospective elite interviewees."
- Key point: Mendez cites Tansey 2007, p. 769: when the causal processes of interest are very specific episodes of elite decision-making involving few people, random sampling is hit-or-miss, and more and more methodologists accept non-probability sampling of elite interviewees as valid.

### E3

- Location: Practical Lessons Learned, items 6 and 7
- Quote: "A gentler way of signaling your status would be to use your institution's email address or letterhead in all correspondence." / "Mention your best accomplishments in casual conversation or in a covering letter."
- Key point: When contacting interviewees, use your institution's email address or letterhead in all correspondence, and mention your best achievement in the cover letter or in conversation to show you deserve to be taken seriously.
