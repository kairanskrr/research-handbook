# Brodeur, Lé, Sangnier & Zylberberg 2016: Star Wars: The Empirics Strike Back

- Source: `brodeur2016star`, [IZA discussion paper PDF](https://docs.iza.org/dp7268.pdf); final version published in American Economic Journal: Applied Economics 8(1)
- Version: IZA Discussion Paper No. 7268 (March 2013). The journal version's full text was not read; the excerpts follow the discussion paper, and exact figures in the journal version may differ slightly.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Journals favor rejection of the null hypothesis. This selection upon tests may distort the behavior of researchers."
- Key point: Journals favor papers that reject the null hypothesis (significant results), and this selection may distort researchers' behavior.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Using 50,000 tests published between 2005 and 2011 in the AER, JPE, and QJE, we identify a residual in the distribution of tests that cannot be explained by selection. The distribution of p-values exhibits a camel shape with abundant p-values above 0.25, a valley between 0.25 and 0.10 and a bump slightly below 0.05."
- Key point: The authors collected about 50,000 tests published in three top journals from 2005 to 2011 and found a camel-shaped distribution of p-values: too few between 0.25 and 0.10 and too many just below 0.05, which selection by journals alone cannot explain.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Our interpretation is that researchers might be tempted to inflate the value of those almost-rejected tests by choosing a "significant" specification."
- Key point: The authors' interpretation: when a result is almost significant, researchers may be tempted to pick a "significant" specification.

### E4

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Inflation is a publication-oriented choice of the displayed specification among the set of acceptable specifications."
- Key point: The authors call this "inflation": choosing, with publication in mind, which of several acceptable specifications to display.

### E5

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Inflation is also smaller in articles with theoretical models, or in articles using data from randomized control trials or laboratory experiments."
- Key point: Inflation is smaller in papers with theoretical models and in papers using data from randomized controlled trials or laboratory experiments.

### E6

- Location: Note on the inside cover of the IZA discussion paper
- Quote: "IZA Discussion Papers often represent preliminary work and are circulated to encourage discussion. Citation of such a paper should account for its provisional character."
- Key point: IZA discussion papers are often preliminary work circulated for discussion; citations should take their provisional character into account.
