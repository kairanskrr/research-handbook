# Candy & Edmonds 2018: Practice-Based Research in the Creative Arts: Foundations and Futures from the Front Line

- Source: `candy2018practice`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/LEON_a_01471); full-text PDF from OPUS, the University of Technology Sydney institutional repository ([record page](http://hdl.handle.net/10453/134306), marked open access)
- Version: Linda Candy and Ernest Edmonds, *Leonardo* vol. 51, no. 1, pp. 63-69, 2018, MIT Press. The authors draw on more than 35 years of supervising interdisciplinary PhD research in art, design, and digital media. Locations are given as subheadings and journal page numbers.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, p. 63
- Quote: "We draw on examples spanning over 35 years of experience in supervising interdisciplinary PhD research programs in the arts, design and digital media."
- Key point: The article draws on examples from the authors' more than 35 years of supervising interdisciplinary PhD research in art, design, and digital media.

### E2

- Location: Introduction, p. 63
- Quote: "A basic principle of practice-based research is that not only is practice embedded in the research process but research questions arise from the process of practice, the answers to which are directed toward enlightening and enhancing practice."
- Key point: A basic principle of practice-based research: practice is embedded in the research process, the research questions arise from the process of practice, and the answers aim to enlighten and enhance practice.

### E3

- Location: Introduction, p. 63
- Quote: "This form of research is usually set in a [...] specific context, yet it must also reach beyond the particular cases if it is to be perceived as contributing to knowledge in any way."
- Key point: Such research is usually set in a specific context, but to count as a contribution to knowledge it must reach beyond the particular cases.

### E4

- Location: Practice-Based Research Defined and Differentiated, pp. 63–64
- Quote: "Moreover, research and practice are different, and it is ill advised to use the terms in ways that suggest they are interchangeable"
- Key point: Research and practice are different; it is ill advised to use the terms as if they were interchangeable.

### E5

- Location: Different Approaches to Practice-Related Research, p. 65
- Quote: "For practicebased researchers, making an artifact is pivotal, and the insights from making, reflecting and evaluating may be fed back directly into the artifact itself."
- Key point: For practice-based researchers, making the artifact is pivotal, and insights from making, reflecting, and evaluating can feed straight back into the artifact.

### E6

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "If, on the other hand, the research is in the art domain and the way that we perceive the artifact is of central concern, then there may be no alternative to providing that video. The role of artifact as art object is not illustrating anything but rather is a subject of interest in itself."
- Key point: A rotating video in a geometry paper is only illustration; but if the research is about how people perceive the work, there may be no alternative to providing the video, because the artifact is then not an illustration but the object of study.

### E7

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "To be able to achieve a full appreciation of the creative works themselves, PhD examiners need to have access to a form that conveys as near as possible a genuine sense of the experience of the works."
- Key point: PhD examiners need access to the works in a form as close as possible to the genuine experience of them.

### E8

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "typically, this is achieved by viewing exhibitions of works and live performances and, where that is not possible, recordings of music, films, photographs of paintings, etc."
- Key point: Typically this means visiting exhibitions and live performances; where that is not possible, recordings of music, films, photographs of paintings, and the like.

### E9

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "In fact, it may not be possible to lodge the artifact itself in the university library as is normally required and in these cases, the submission of sufficiently good documentation for the complete work to be fully understood is necessary to meet the PhD requirements."
- Key point: The artifact itself may not be lodgeable in the university library; in that case, documentation good enough for the complete work to be fully understood must be submitted.

### E10

- Quote: "That said, the creative works cannot be expected to speak for themselves in the context of a PhD submission. - Key point: In a PhD submission, the creative works cannot be expected to speak for themselves; they must be accompanied by some form of textual analysis or explanation.

### E11

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "As such it is a vital part of the research outcomes and cannot be viewed simply as an optional extra. The role of the written thesis is to share the understandings achieved through the research and to affirm that it is important to have a clear structure that sets out the aims, background, methods and outcomes of the research."
- Key point: The written thesis is a vital part of the research outcomes, not an optional extra; it needs a clear structure stating the aims, context, methods, and outcomes.

### E12

- Location: Practice-based Research and the PhD, p. 65
- Quote: "This is where the candidate shows how the work relates to the state of the art in the field and that the work is in some way \"new.\" They also have to facilitate an understanding of just what the knowledge is, for example by explaining how to approach or view the new creative work."
- Key point: The thesis must show how the work relates to the state of the art in the field and what is new about it, and help readers understand what the knowledge is, for example by explaining how to approach the new work.

### E19

- Location: Example Contributions, the Tebby example, p. 67
- Quote: "Later, Susan Tebby [17] carried out research into the use of various formal procedures and patterns in making art. [...] Without realizing the artworks, it would not have been possible to grasp these implications. In this case, the role of practice was both central and unavoidable."
- Key point: Tebby's PhD research explored the use of various formal procedures and patterns in making art; without realizing the artworks, these implications could not have been grasped, so the role of practice was both central and indispensable.

### E20

- Location: Example Contributions, the Tebby example, p. 67
- Quote: "The submission of the PhD consisted of a thesis together with an exhibition of the artworks generated during and through the research. The examiners spent a significant amount of time in the exhibition, which illuminated the understandings that they had obtained from reading the text. The exhibition was fully documented and a full set of 35mm slides (the medium of the day), lodged in the library with the text."
- Key point: The submission was a thesis plus an exhibition; the examiners spent a significant amount of time in the exhibition, which was fully documented, with the slides lodged in the library alongside the thesis.

### E21

- Location: Example Contributions, the Bell example, p. 67
- Quote: "consisted of an iterative process of making (writing computer code), looking (evaluating) and revising. The writing of code was, of course, quite an objective process, in which algorithms postulated to be interesting were implemented. The evaluation, on the other hand, was entirely subjective. Although others were consulted, the central figure in the evaluation process was the artist himself"
- Key point: Bell's research process was an iterative cycle of making (writing code), looking (evaluating), and revising; the evaluation was entirely subjective, and although others were consulted, the artist was at its center.

### E22

- Location: Example Contributions, the Johnston example, p. 68
- Quote: "Indeed, the research goals and opportunities were only discovered out of practice. The evaluation element came about in trying to see what the implications were for the performing musicians; that was done by observing and discussing their practice in the context of these new forms."
- Key point: Johnston's research goals and opportunities were only discovered through practice; the evaluation was done by observing and discussing the performing musicians' practice.

### E23

- Location: Example Contributions, the Seevinck example, p. 68
- Quote: "The central results were embodied in artworks that facilitated and encouraged emergent thinking in members of the participating audience. It was necessary to demonstrate that this was, in fact, the case; so, as well as delivering the results of the research in both text and documentedartifact forms, the results of evaluations of participant responses were reported."
- Key point: Seevinck's central results were embodied in artworks that encouraged emergent thinking in participating audiences; this had to be demonstrated, so besides the text and documentation of the works, an evaluation of participants' responses was reported.

### E24

- Location: Conclusions, p. 68
- Quote: "This means that reporting the research in a PhD submission requires a written thesis that might well include a description of how a submitted artifact should be apprehended, as well as other evidence that demonstrates that the results are new, not just to the practitioner researcher, but to the wider world."
- Key point: A PhD submission needs a written thesis, which may explain how the submitted artifact should be apprehended and provide evidence that the results are new not just to the researcher but to the wider world.
