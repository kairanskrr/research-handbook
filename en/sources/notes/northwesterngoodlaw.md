# Northwestern University law library: Determining Whether Cases Are Still Good Law

- Source: `northwesterngoodlaw`, [original web page](https://library.law.northwestern.edu/cases/updating)
- Version: research guide page of the legal research center of Northwestern University Pritzker School of Law, accessed October 6, 2026. It covers updating and checking **US** case law. Locations give paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: main text, paragraph 1
- Quote: "Legal citators are valuable tools that tell you what has happened to a law after it’s been promulgated and what primary and secondary sources have cited to the law."
- Key point: A legal citator tells you what has happened to a law or case since it was issued and which primary and secondary sources have cited it.

### E2

- Location: main text, paragraph 2
- Quote: "This is always the last step in the legal research process, and it is imperative for ensuring that your cases and statutes are current prior to entering the courtroom and representing a case for your client."
- Key point: Checking whether cases and statutes are still good law is the last step of legal research, and it is imperative.

### E3

- Location: the "Don't rely on the signal alone!" paragraph
- Quote: "A red signal doesn’t necessarily mean it’s bad law."
- Key point: A red flag from a citator does not necessarily mean the case is bad law; perhaps only one of its legal issues was overturned.

### E4

- Location: the "Don't rely on the signal alone!" paragraph
- Quote: "It is critical that you spend the time reading the context of your subsequent citing decision."
- Key point: Always take the time to read the later citing decision in context.
