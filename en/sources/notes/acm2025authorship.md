# ACM 2025: ACM Policy on Authorship

- Source: `acm2025authorship`, [original](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship) (the site returned 403 to scripted access, so we used the Internet Archive snapshot from 2025-12-15: https://web.archive.org/web/20251215000740/https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship)
- Version: web page, marked "Updated on September 16, 2025".

### E1

- Location: Scope of Policy
- Quote: "This policy applies to all submitted, accepted, and published articles and abstracts in all ACM Publication venues, including ACM journals, ACM conferences, ICPS conferences, ACM magazines, and ACM books."
- Key point: This authorship policy applies to all ACM journals, conferences, magazines, and books.

### E2

- Location: Criteria for Authorship
- Quote: "Anyone listed as author on an ACM submission must meet all the following criteria:" / "They are an identifiable human being." / "They have made substantial intellectual contributions to some components of the original Work described in the manuscript, such as contributing to the conception, design, and analysis of the study reported on in the Work and participating in the drafting and/or revision of the manuscript." / "They take full responsibility for all content in the published Works."
- Key point: Every author on an ACM submission must meet all of these criteria: be an identifiable human being; have made substantial intellectual contributions to some part of the work (such as conception, design, or analysis) and taken part in drafting or revising it; and take full responsibility for the published content.

### E3

- Location: Criteria for Authorship, Note
- Quote: "All individuals who meet the above criteria should be listed as authors on the Work. The practices of gift authorship, guest authorship, ghost authorship" [...] "and purchased authorship are clear violations of ACM Publications Policy"
- Key point: Everyone who meets the criteria should be listed as an author; gift authorship, guest authorship, ghost authorship, and buying or selling authorship are explicit violations of ACM publication policy.

### E4

- Location: Criteria for Authorship
- Quote: "Those who contributed to a Work, but whose contribution does not rise to the level of authorship, may be acknowledged at the end of the Work, before the Bibliography, with explicitly described roles, preferably using the roles found in the CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy)."
- Key point: Contributors whose contribution does not rise to the level of authorship may be acknowledged at the end of the work, before the bibliography, with their roles stated, preferably using role names from CRediT (the Contributor Roles Taxonomy).

### E5

- Location: Criteria for Authorship
- Quote: "Generative AI tools and technologies, such as ChatGPT, may not be listed as authors of an ACM published Work. The use of generative AI tools and technologies to create content is permitted but must be fully disclosed in the Work."
- Key point: Generative AI tools may not be listed as authors; using them to generate content is allowed but must be fully disclosed in the work.
