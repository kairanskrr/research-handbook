# Karpathy 2016: A Survival Guide to a PhD

- Source: `karpathy2016survival`, [original](http://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/)
- Version: Author's blog, 2016-09-07, read 2026-10-05. Locations give section names and the bold lead-ins at the start of paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: Adviser / Student adviser relationship
- Quote: "It’s important to understand the nature of the relationship: the adviser-student relationship is a symbiosis; you have your own goals and want something out of your PhD, but they also have their own goals, constraints and they’re building their own career."
- Key point: The advisor-student relationship is a symbiosis: you have your own goals, and your advisor has their own goals, constraints, and career.

### E2

- Location: Adviser / Student adviser relationship
- Quote: "Therefore, it is very helpful to understand your adviser’s incentive structures"
- Key point: Understanding your advisor's incentive structure (how they are evaluated, how they get funding, how they fund you) helps avoid or ease most advisor-student friction.

### E3

- Location: Adviser / Axes of variation
- Quote: "Some will try to exercise a lot of influence on the details of your work and some are much more hands off."
- Key point: Advisors vary widely: some try to influence the details of your work, others are very hands-off; they also differ in how often they meet and how fast they answer email.

### E4

- Location: Adviser / Impressing an adviser
- Quote: "The ideal student from their perspective is someone with interest and passion, someone who doesn’t need too much hand-holding, and someone who takes initiative - who shows up a week later having done not just what the adviser suggested, but who went beyond it; improved on it in unexpected ways."
- Key point: From the advisor's point of view, the ideal student takes initiative: someone who comes back a week later having done not just what was suggested, but gone beyond it.

### E5

- Location: Adviser / Consider the entire lab
- Quote: "In most cases you will also end up collaborating with some of the senior PhD students or postdocs and they will play a role very similar to that of your adviser."
- Key point: You will usually also collaborate with senior PhD students or postdocs, who play a role very similar to your advisor's.

### E6

- Location: Research topics / Resistance
- Quote: "If you feel this way during your phd you should have the courage to sometimes ignore your adviser."
- Key point: Advisors can misjudge too; if you are convinced your advisor is wrong, you sometimes need the courage to ignore them.

### E7

- Location: Research topics / Resistance
- Quote: "I ended up working on a problem he wasn’t very excited about and, surprise, he turned out to be very right and I wasted a few months."
- Key point: The author once ignored his advisor's advice, the advisor turned out to be right, and he wasted a few months. Going your own way is a bet you can win or lose.

### E8

- Location: Section "Writing papers," paragraph "An internal deadlines 2 weeks prior"
- Quote: "Not many labs do this, but luckily Fei-Fei is quite adamant about an internal deadline 2 weeks before the due date in which you must submit at least a 5-page draft with all the final experiments (even if not with final numbers) that goes through an internal review process identical to the external one (with the same review forms filled out, etc)."
- Key point: The author's advisor insisted on an internal deadline two weeks before the due date: a draft of at least 5 pages with all final experiments (numbers need not be final), reviewed internally in the same way as the external review.

### E9

- Location: Same as above
- Quote: "I found this practice to be extremely useful because forcing yourself to lay out the full paper almost always reveals some number of critical experiments you must run for the paper to flow and for its argument flow to be coherent, consistent and convincing."
- Key point: Forcing yourself to lay out the full paper early almost always reveals some critical experiments you still need to run.

### E10

- Location: Section "Writing code," paragraph "Think of the future you"
- Quote: "I guarantee you that you will come back to your code base a few months later (e.g. to do a few more experiments for the camera ready version of the paper), and you will feel completely lost in it. I got into the habit of creating very thorough readme.txt files in all my repos (for my personal use) as notes to future self on how the code works, how to run it, etc."
- Key point: You will come back to your code a few months later (e.g., to run more experiments for the camera-ready version) and feel completely lost; the author got into the habit of writing a thorough readme in every repository, as notes for his future self on how the code works and how to run it.

### E11

- Location: Closing thoughts
- Quote: "anything shady you try to do to pad your academic resume (e.g. self-citing a lot, publishing the same idea multiple times with small remixes, resubmitting the same rejected paper over and over again with no changes, conveniently trying to leave out some baselines etc.) will eventually catch up with you"
- Key point: Anything shady done to pad your resume, including resubmitting the same rejected paper again and again without changes, will eventually come back to hurt you.
