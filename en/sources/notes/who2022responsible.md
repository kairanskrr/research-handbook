# World Health Organization 2022: Global guidance framework for the responsible use of the life sciences: mitigating biorisks and governing dual-use research

- Source: `who2022responsible`, [original PDF](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/362313/9789240056107-eng.pdf)
- Version: 2022, ISBN 978-92-4-005610-7. License: CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. The WHO repository returns only the page frame to scripts, so the same PDF was downloaded via an Internet Archive snapshot (`web.archive.org/web/2023id_/...`). Locations give the chapter or the glossary.

### E1

- Location: Glossary, Dual-use research of concern
- Quote: "Dual-use research of concern (DURC) describes research that is conducted for peaceful and beneficial purposes, but could easily be misapplied to do harm with no, or only minor, modification."
- Key point: Dual-use research of concern (DURC) is research conducted for peaceful and beneficial purposes that could easily be misapplied to do harm with no, or only minor, modification.

### E2

- Location: Glossary, Dual-use research
- Quote: "Research conducted for peaceful and beneficial purposes that has the potential to produce knowledge, information, methods, products or technologies that could also be intentionally misused to endanger the health of humans, nonhuman animals, plants and agriculture, and the environment."
- Key point: Dual-use research is research conducted for peaceful and beneficial purposes whose knowledge, information, methods, products, or technologies could also be intentionally misused to endanger humans, animals, plants, agriculture, and the environment.

### E3

- Location: Glossary, Biosafety
- Quote: "Containment principles, technologies, measures and practices that are implemented to prevent unintentional exposure to biological agents or their inadvertent release."
- Key point: Biosafety is the containment principles, technologies, and practices that prevent unintentional exposure to biological agents or their inadvertent release.

### E4

- Location: Glossary, Biosecurity
- Quote: "Biosecurity aims to prevent their unauthorized access, loss, theft, misuse, diversion or release."
- Key point: Biosecurity aims to prevent unauthorized access to, loss, theft, misuse, diversion, or release of biological agents, data, equipment, and the like.

### E5

- Location: Executive summary
- Quote: "Robust biorisk management relies on three core pillars: biosafety, laboratory biosecurity and the oversight of dual-use research."
- Key point: Biorisk management rests on three pillars: biosafety, laboratory biosecurity, and oversight of dual-use research.

### E6

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 1
- Quote: "Outputs: Potential risks of work are identified and assessed before beginning the work."
- Key point: First step on the checklist for researchers: identify and assess potential risks before beginning the work.

### E7

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 1
- Quote: "Could the information, data and research methods generated by the proposed work be misused to cause harm? What mitigation strategies have been put into place to reduce this risk?"
- Key point: Ask yourself: could the information, data, and methods produced by this work be misused to cause harm? What mitigation strategies are in place to reduce this risk?

### E8

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 1
- Quote: "Can a different methodology, experimental design or different organism be used to make the experiment safer or less of a biosecurity risk?"
- Key point: Could a different methodology, experimental design, or organism make the experiment safer or less of a biosecurity risk?

### E9

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 1
- Quote: "Is the proposed work falling under the scope of export controls?"
- Key point: Does the work fall under the scope of export controls?

### E10

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 1
- Quote: "Does the proposed work follow institutional guidelines, or national or regional legislation, regulations or guidelines for safe, secure and responsible research?"
- Key point: Does the work follow institutional guidelines and national or regional laws, regulations, and guidelines?

### E11

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, STEP 4
- Quote: "Risk mitigation strategies cannot reduce risks to zero unless the work or research is not undertaken."
- Key point: Risk mitigation cannot reduce risk to zero unless the research is not done at all.

### E12

- Location: Section 5.3, Box 6 Checklist for scientists, Note
- Quote: "Although the checklists identify examples of considerations targeted at different stakeholders, biorisk management is a shared responsibility between different stakeholders."
- Key point: Biorisk management is a responsibility shared among all stakeholders.
