# Jia-Bin Huang 2023: How to do experiments?

- Source: `huang2023experiments`, [original tweet](https://twitter.com/jbhuang0604/status/1647098218172252160), read via the [Thread Reader mirror](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1647098218172252160.html)
- Version: Tweet thread, 2023-04-15, 11 tweets. Locations follow the subheadings in the thread.

### E1

- Location: The *Why?* tweet
- Quote: "Your goal of experiments should be to validate/test your research questions."
- Key point: The goal of experiments is to test your research questions (hypotheses), not to push up the numbers.

### E2

- Location: The *One thing at a time* tweet
- Quote: "If you change multiple things at once, then you are trying to validate a complicated hypothesis with multiple factors."
- Key point: Changing several things at once means testing a complicated hypothesis with multiple factors, which makes the result of little value.

### E3

- Location: The *Anticipate the results* tweet
- Quote: "Learn to predict the outcome *before* you do the experiments."
- Key point: Predict the outcome before you run the experiment.

### E4

- Location: The *Anticipate the results* tweet
- Quote: "This helps you interpret the outcomes and identify errors."
- Key point: Predicting in advance helps you interpret the outcomes and spot errors.

### E5

- Location: The *Start with a baseline* tweet
- Quote: "You will be in trouble later if you don't have a solid baseline first."
- Key point: Experiments usually look at a change, so without a solid baseline first you will be in trouble later.

### E6

- Location: The *Document everything* tweet
- Quote: "Use your brain to think, reason, and not store tedious information."
- Key point: Write down experiment details instead of relying on memory.

### E7

- Location: The *Two valid design choices? Do both!* tweet
- Quote: "If both A and B are sensible choices, do BOTH and let the results tell the story."
- Key point: When two design choices are both sensible, do both and let the results tell the story.
