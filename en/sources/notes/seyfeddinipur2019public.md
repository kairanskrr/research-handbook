# Seyfeddinipur et al. 2019: Public access to research data in language documentation: Challenges and possible strategies

- Source: `seyfeddinipur2019public`, [original](http://hdl.handle.net/10125/24901)
- Version: Language Documentation & Conservation 13: 545-563 (open access, CC BY-NC 4.0); 30 authors from universities, language archives, and international organizations in several countries

### E1

- Location: §2.1 Challenges
- Quote: "Determining who has the cultural and legal authority to provide consent may be complex where individuals, families, or communities hold rights to specific stories, songs, dances, or other cultural expressions."
- Key point: Stories, songs, and the like may belong to individuals, families, or communities, so working out who has authority to consent can be complex.

### E2

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Ownership of material and questions around access options need to be discussed early, both with individuals and the wider community, with discussion continuing on a regular basis"
- Key point: Discuss who owns the material and who can access it early, with individuals and the wider community, and keep discussing it regularly.

### E3

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Who can listen to, view, or read particular materials, and what does it mean if anyone in the world could do this?"
- Key point: When discussing access levels, ask: who can listen to, view, or read these materials, and what would it mean if anyone in the world could?

### E4

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Workshops can be held to discuss these topics."
- Key point: Workshops can be held to discuss these questions.

### E5

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Where written consent is not suitable, speakers' agreement can be recorded orally, as can relevant discussions with the wider community."
- Key point: Where written consent is unsuitable, speakers' consent can be recorded orally, as can relevant discussions with the community.

### E6

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Individual and community views of consent can change over time"
- Key point: Individual and community views on consent can change over time.

### E7

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "informed consent should include discussion of the level of access (open, or restricted in some way), and this discussion should be included as part of the collection's metadata."
- Key point: Informed consent should cover the level of access (open or restricted in some way), and the outcome should go into the collection's metadata.

### E8

- Location: §2.2 Strategies
- Quote: "It may be appropriate to err on the side of caution and restrict access, at least in the early stages of research."
- Key point: At least in the early stages of research, err on the side of caution and restrict access.

### E9

- Location: §3.1 Challenges
- Quote: "In some jurisdictions research permits are required in order to conduct a language documentation project, and the permits may place explicit restrictions on access to research data."
- Key point: Some jurisdictions require research permits for language documentation, and the permits may explicitly restrict access to the data.

### E10

- Location: §3.2 Strategies
- Quote: "Intellectual Property law typically vests copyright in language documentation materials with the individuals who made the recordings"
- Key point: Under intellectual property law, copyright in recordings usually belongs to the person who made them (the researcher), not to the speaker.

### E11

- Location: §5.2.2 Possible strategies
- Quote: "From the outset of a project, researchers should work with archives to address issues of licensing and access, to develop a succession plan stating who will be responsible for materials in the future, and to make plans for future treatment of restricted materials."
- Key point: From the start of a project, work with archives on licensing and access, make a succession plan (who will be responsible for the materials in the future), and plan what will happen to restricted materials later.

### E12

- Location: §5.2.2 Possible strategies
- Quote: "it is too easy for researchers or archives to use `community sensitivity' as an excuse for not making their records available"
- Key point: It is too easy for researchers or archives to use "community sensitivity" as an excuse for not making records available.

### E13

- Location: §6.2.1
- Quote: "in small societies it may be impossible to anonymize speakers."
- Key point: In small societies it may be impossible to anonymize speakers.

### E14

- Location: §6.2.1
- Quote: "we recommend using restricted access only with a clearly specified embargo period, after which the restrictions can be lifted."
- Key point: Where it does not go against the community's wishes, restricted access should come with a clearly specified embargo period, after which restrictions can be lifted.
