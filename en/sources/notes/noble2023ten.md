# Noble 2023: Ten simple rules for defining a computational biology project

- Source: `noble2023ten`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010786)
- Version: PLoS Computational Biology 19(1): e1010786, 2023, open access, accessed 2026-10-05. Locations give rule numbers.

### E1

- Location: Rule 1: Write it down
- Quote: "The act of writing your idea down will inevitably clarify it for you. I typically think of this writeup as a first draft of the introduction of the paper you eventually aim to write."
- Key point: Writing your idea down will inevitably clarify it; you can treat this write-up as a first draft of the introduction of the final paper.

### E2

- Location: Rule 1: Write it down
- Quote: "Unfortunately, if you get excited by an idea and immediately dive into implementing and validating it, then you can end up pursuing a half-baked idea that, on second thought, really doesn’t make sense."
- Key point: If you dive into implementing an idea the moment you have it, you can end up pursuing a half-baked idea that doesn't hold up.

### E3

- Location: Rule 2: Define the problem you want to solve
- Quote: "It is important up front to clearly delineate the scope and precise nature of the problem your work will address."
- Key point: Delineate the scope and precise nature of the problem clearly at the start.

### E4

- Location: Rule 3: Define the potential impact
- Quote: "You should think carefully about who would benefit from a solution to this problem and what those benefits will be."
- Key point: Think carefully about who would benefit from a solution to the problem, and what those benefits would be.

### E5

- Location: Rule 4: Summarize the related work
- Quote: "A terrible trap to fall into is carrying out an entire research project only to find that the same work was already published before you began. Avoid this trap by doing your literature review up front."
- Key point: The worst trap is finishing a whole project only to find the same work was already published; do the literature search first.

### E6

- Location: Rule 5: State your central hypothesis
- Quote: "Boiling down your idea to a single sentence can be a powerful way to achieve focus and ensure that your project does not wander."
- Key point: Boiling your idea down to a single sentence keeps the project focused and stops it from wandering.

### E7

- Location: Rule 5: State your central hypothesis
- Quote: "Thus, it will be worthwhile to periodically revisit your stated hypothesis and ask yourself whether it is still the driving idea behind your research."
- Key point: Your hypothesis may change during the project; revisit it periodically to check whether it is still the idea driving your research.

### E8

- Location: Rule 7: Identify available data
- Quote: "In particular, it’s important to be sure that you not only have the input data, but also the expected outputs; i.e., you need to have some way to demonstrate that your method works as expected."
- Key point: Make sure in advance that you have a way to demonstrate that the method works, i.e., decide beforehand what will count as evidence.

### E9

- Location: Rule 8: Choose appropriate validation measures
- Quote: "Ask yourself what the end user cares about and what will make the work most impactful"
- Key point: When choosing validation metrics, first ask what the end user cares about and what will make the work most impactful.

### E10

- Location: Rule 10: Share your idea with colleagues
- Quote: "Once you have written out your idea, share it with trusted colleagues. The goal here is to elicit feedback about whether the idea makes sense and addresses an important, unsolved problem."
- Key point: Once it is written up, share it with trusted colleagues to get feedback on whether the idea makes sense and addresses an important, unsolved problem.

### E11

- Location: closing remarks after Rule 10
- Quote: "It is important to keep in mind that the order in which these rules are applied is not set in stone."
- Key point: The order of these steps is not set in stone.

### E12

- Location: Rule 9: Set up version control
- Quote: "Use a modern version control system, such as git, and use it right away. [...] You do not want to get several weeks into your project and then realize that you have no record of the initial versions of your code."
- Key point: Use version control from the very start of the project; don't get several weeks in and then realize you have no record of earlier versions.
