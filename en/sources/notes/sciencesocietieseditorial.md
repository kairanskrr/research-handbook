# American Society of Agronomy, Crop Science Society of America, Soil Science Society of America: Editorial Policies

- Source: `sciencesocietieseditorial`, [original](https://www.sciencesocieties.org/publications/journals/author-resources/editorial-policies)
- Version: Web page, marked "Last updated June 30, 2026," accessed 2026-10-06. Editorial policies for the journals of the three societies (ASA, CSSA, SSSA), including the data policy, preprint policy, and author ethics statement. Locations give page subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Data Policy
- Quote: "The Data Policy of ASA, CSSA, and SSSA expects the storage and availability of data necessary to understand and evaluate phenomena reported in our publications. Data should be stored or archived in domain repositories that are recognized widely and available to the community."
- Key point: The three societies' journals expect the data needed to understand and evaluate reported results to be stored and available, in recognized domain repositories accessible to the community.

### E2

- Location: Data Policy
- Quote: "Data not stored in such a repository should be available upon request to the authors."
- Key point: Data not stored in a repository should be available from the authors on request.

### E3

- Location: Prior Publication
- Quote: "Posting of preprints to a not-for-profit preprint server is considered acceptable but requires citing of the preprint."
- Key point: Posting a preprint to a not-for-profit preprint server is acceptable, but the submission must cite the preprint.

### E4

- Location: Ethics, Statement of Ethics for Authors
- Quote: "Honor the rights and accomplishments of others and properly credit the work and ideas of others."
- Key point: Authors should honor the rights and accomplishments of others and properly credit others' work and ideas.

### E5

- Location: Ethics, Statement of Ethics for Authors
- Quote: "Demonstrate social responsibility in scientific and professional practice, by considering whom their scientific and professional activities benefit, and whom they neglect."
- Key point: Authors should show social responsibility by considering whom their research benefits and whom it neglects.
