# Krueger 2002: Designing and Conducting Focus Group Interviews

- Source: `krueger2002designing`, [PDF (public copy on the Eastern Illinois University website)](https://www.eiu.edu/ihec/Krueger-FocusGroupInterviews.pdf); the SHADAC center at the University of Minnesota also hosts the same handout: [FocGrp_Krueger_Oct02.pdf](https://www.shadac.org/sites/default/files/publications/FocGrp_Krueger_Oct02.pdf)
- Version: an October 2002 handout by Richard A. Krueger (professor and evaluation leader at the University of Minnesota), 17 pages, cited by footer page number. From program evaluation and applied research. Read on 2026-10-17 from the Eastern Illinois University copy; the SHADAC link showed an access check that day and could not be downloaded to compare.

### E1

- Location: p. 1, Characteristics of Focus Group Interviews
- Quote: "Carefully recruited" / "5 to 10 people per group, 6-8 preferred" / "Similar types of people" / "Repeated groups"
- Key point: Participants are carefully recruited, 5 to 10 per group (6 to 8 preferred), similar types of people within a group, with several groups run.

### E2

- Location: p. 1, Characteristics of Focus Group Interviews
- Quote: "Moderator" / "Skillful in group discussions" / "Uses pre-determined questions" / "Establishes permissive environment"
- Key point: The moderator is skilled at group discussion, uses pre-determined questions, and creates a permissive environment where people feel free to speak.

### E3

- Location: p. 2, Moderator Skills
- Quote: "Use an assistant moderator" / "Takes careful notes" / "Monitors recording equipment"
- Key point: Use an assistant moderator, who takes careful notes and monitors the recording equipment.

### E4

- Location: p. 2, Moderator Skills
- Quote: "Use pauses and probes" / "5 second pause" / "Probes:" / "\"Would you explain further?\"" / "\"Would you give an example?\""
- Key point: The moderator uses pauses and probes: a five-second pause, and probes such as "Would you explain further?" and "Would you give an example?"

### E5

- Location: p. 2, Moderator Skills
- Quote: "Control reactions to participants" / "Head nodding" / "Short verbal responses" / "(avoid \"that's good\", \"excellent\")" / "Use subtle group control" / "Experts" / "Dominant talkers" / "Shy participants" / "Ramblers"
- Key point: The moderator controls their reactions to what participants say, avoiding "that's good" or "excellent," and uses subtle control to manage experts and dominant talkers and to handle shy participants and ramblers.

### E6

- Location: p. 3, Bulleted Outline (opening outline)
- Quote: "No right or wrong answers, only differing points of view" / "You don't need to agree with others, but you must listen respectfully as others share their views" / "Talk to each other"
- Key point: The opening sets the ground rules: no right or wrong answers, only different points of view; you need not agree but must listen respectfully; talk to each other.

### E7

- Location: p. 4, Beginning the Focus Group Discussion
- Quote: "You may be assured of complete confidentiality."
- Key point: Krueger's sample opening tells participants they "may be assured of complete confidentiality."

### E8

- Location: p. 6, Asking Questions that Yield Powerful Information
- Quote: "Use open-ended questions" / "Avoid dichotomous questions" / "Use \"think back\" questions." / "Take people back to an experience and not forward to the future"
- Key point: Ask open-ended questions and avoid yes/no questions; use "think back" questions that take people back to an experience rather than forward to the future.

### E9

- Location: p. 6, Asking Questions that Yield Powerful Information
- Quote: "Five Types of Questions" / "1. Opening Question (round robin)" / "2. Introductory Question" / "3. Transition Questions" / "4. Key Questions" / "5. Ending Questions" / "Sequence that goes from general to specific"
- Key point: There are five types of questions, opening (round robin), introductory, transition, key, and ending, sequenced from general to specific.

### E10

- Location: p. 7, Ending Questions
- Quote: "After the brief oral summary the question asked is:" / "\"Is this an adequate summary?\"" / "The moderator reviews the purpose of the study and then asks the participants:" / "\"Have we missed anything?\""
- Key point: At the end the moderator gives a brief oral summary and asks "Is this an adequate summary?", then restates the study's purpose and asks "Have we missed anything?"

### E11

- Location: p. 9, Note Taking
- Quote: "Note taking is a primary responsibility of the assistant moderator" / "The moderator should not be expected to take written notes during the discussion."
- Key point: Note taking is mainly the assistant moderator's job; the moderator should not be expected to take notes during the discussion.

### E12

- Location: p. 10, Systematic Analysis Process
- Quote: "Conduct moderator and assistant moderator debriefing" / "Note themes, hunches, interpretations, and ideas" / "Compare and contrast this focus group to other groups"
- Key point: After each group the moderator and assistant debrief, noting themes, hunches, and ideas, and compare the group with the others.

### E13

- Location: p. 11, Focus Group Analysis Tips, Context
- Quote: "Participant responses were triggered by a stimulus--a question asked by the moderator or a comment from another participant. Examine the context by finding the triggering stimulus and then interpret the comment in light of that environment."
- Key point: Every comment is triggered by a stimulus, either the moderator's question or another participant's remark; find that trigger and interpret the comment in its context.

### E14

- Location: p. 11, Focus Group Analysis Tips, Internal consistency
- Quote: "Participants in focus groups change and sometimes even reverse their positions after interaction with others. When there is a shift in opinion, the researcher typically traces the flow of the conversation to determine clues that might explain the change."
- Key point: Participants change and sometimes reverse their positions after talking with others; when that happens, trace the flow of the conversation for clues that explain the shift.

### E15

- Location: p. 11, Focus Group Analysis Tips, Specificity
- Quote: "Responses that are specific and based on experiences should be given more weight than responses that are vague and impersonal."
- Key point: Specific responses based on personal experience deserve more weight than vague, impersonal ones.

### E16

- Location: p. 15, Transcribing Focus Group Interviews
- Quote: "Use bold print for the moderator's statements and questions. If possible, type the name of each speaker followed by his or her comment." / "Typically it takes about eight hours to type one hour of tape."
- Key point: Transcripts should mark the moderator's statements and, where possible, name each speaker; one hour of tape takes about eight hours to transcribe.

### E17

- Location: p. 15, Transcribing Focus Group Interviews
- Quote: "For example, if there is laughter, loud voices, shouting, etc. be sure that these are noted in the transcript in parenthesis. Make note if someone was interrupted."
- Key point: Transcripts should note laughter, raised voices, and similar sounds in parentheses, and record when someone was interrupted.
