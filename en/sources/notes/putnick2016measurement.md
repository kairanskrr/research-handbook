# Putnick & Bornstein 2016: Measurement Invariance Conventions and Reporting: The State of the Art and Future Directions for Psychological Research

- Source: `putnick2016measurement`, [original (PubMed Central author manuscript)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5145197/)
- Version: Developmental Review 41:71-90 (2016); author manuscript publicly available in PubMed Central. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Measurement noninvariance suggests that a construct has a different structure or meaning to different groups or on different measurement occasions in the same group, and so the construct cannot be meaningfully tested or construed across groups or across time."
- Key point: Measurement noninvariance means a construct has a different structure or meaning across groups or measurement occasions, so it cannot be meaningfully compared across them.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Hence, prior to testing mean differences across groups or measurement occasions (e.g., boys and girls, pretest and posttest), or differential relations of the construct across groups, it is essential to assess the invariance of the construct."
- Key point: Before comparing means across groups (e.g., boys and girls) or measurement occasions (pretest and posttest), you must test measurement invariance.

### E3

- Location: Introduction (depression example)
- Quote: "In this example, men may score lower than women on the depression scale because they cry less and gain less weight. However, crying and weight gain are not associated with depression in men in the first place."
- Key point: If crying and weight gain reflect depression only in women, men may score lower on the scale simply because they cry less and gain less weight, not because they are less depressed.

### E4

- Location: Testing Measurement Invariance
- Quote: "(1) configural, equivalence of model form; (2) metric (weak factorial), equivalence of factor loadings; (3) scalar (strong factorial), equivalence of item intercepts or thresholds; and (4) residual (strict or invariant uniqueness), equivalence of items' residuals or unique variances."
- Key point: The four steps of measurement invariance: configural (same model form), metric (same factor loadings), scalar (same item intercepts or thresholds), and residual (same residual variances).

### E5

- Location: Testing Measurement Invariance (comparing group means)
- Quote: "Once the configural, metric, and scalar invariance steps have been passed, the researcher is free to compare group means on the latent factors"
- Key point: Only after passing the configural, metric, and scalar steps can you compare latent means across groups.

### E6

- Location: Configural invariance
- Quote: "Redefining the construct in any step of invariance testing (e.g., by omitting items or making other post-hoc alterations to the model)" / "exploratory in nature, and results should be interpreted with this concern in mind."
- Key point: Dropping items or altering the model post hoc during invariance testing is data-driven exploration, and results should be interpreted with that in mind.

### E7

- Location: Introduction (intervention study example)
- Quote: "the intervention, protocol, or trial could change the way participants interpret the constructs under study."
- Key point: An intervention may itself change how participants interpret a construct, so invariance also needs testing between pretest and posttest.
