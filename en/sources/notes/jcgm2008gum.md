# JCGM 100:2008: Evaluation of measurement data — Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement (GUM)

- Source: `jcgm2008gum`, [original](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)
- Version: First edition 2008 from the Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology (JCGM), i.e., GUM 1995 with minor corrections, freely available on the website of the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM). Locations give clause numbers.

### E1

- Location: 0.1
- Quote: "When reporting the result of a measurement of a physical quantity, it is obligatory that some quantitative indication of the quality of the result be given so that those who use it can assess its reliability. Without such an indication, measurement results cannot be compared, either among themselves or with reference values given in a specification or standard."
- Key point: When you report a measurement result for a physical quantity, you must give a quantitative indication of its quality so users can assess its reliability; without one, results cannot be compared with each other or with reference values in a specification or standard.

### E2

- Location: 3.3.2
- Quote: "In practice, there are many possible sources of uncertainty in a measurement, including: [...] inadequate knowledge of the effects of environmental conditions on the measurement or imperfect measurement of environmental conditions; [...] finite instrument resolution or discrimination threshold; [...] approximations and assumptions incorporated in the measurement method and procedure; j) variations in repeated observations of the measurand under apparently identical conditions."
- Key point: Uncertainty has many sources, for example inadequate knowledge of environmental effects or imperfect measurement of environmental conditions, finite instrument resolution, approximations and assumptions in the measurement method, and variation in repeated observations under apparently identical conditions.

### E3

- Location: 2.3.2, 2.3.3
- Quote: "Type A evaluation (of uncertainty) method of evaluation of uncertainty by the statistical analysis of series of observations [...] Type B evaluation (of uncertainty) method of evaluation of uncertainty by means other than the statistical analysis of series of observations"
- Key point: Type A evaluation assesses uncertainty by statistical analysis of a series of observations; Type B evaluation assesses it by other means.

### E4

- Location: 3.3.3
- Quote: "These categories apply to uncertainty and are not substitutes for the words “random” and “systematic”."
- Key point: Type A and Type B classify methods of evaluation; they are not substitutes for the words "random" and "systematic."

### E5

- Location: 3.4.7
- Quote: "Blunders in recording or analysing data can introduce a significant unknown error in the result of a measurement. [...] Measures of uncertainty are not intended to account for such mistakes."
- Key point: Blunders in recording or analyzing data can introduce a large unknown error; measures of uncertainty are not meant to account for such mistakes.

### E6

- Location: 3.4.8
- Quote: "Although this Guide provides a framework for assessing uncertainty, it cannot substitute for critical thinking, intellectual honesty and professional skill. The evaluation of uncertainty is neither a routine task nor a purely mathematical one; it depends on detailed knowledge of the nature of the measurand and of the measurement."
- Key point: The Guide provides a framework but cannot substitute for critical thinking, intellectual honesty, and professional skill; evaluating uncertainty is neither routine nor purely mathematical and depends on detailed knowledge of the measurand and the measurement.

### E7

- Location: 7.1.4
- Quote: "when reporting the result of a measurement and its uncertainty, it is preferable to err on the side of providing too much information rather than too little."
- Key point: When reporting a measurement result and its uncertainty, err on the side of too much information rather than too little.

### E8

- Location: 7.1.4
- Quote: "list all uncertainty components and document fully how they were evaluated;"
- Key point: List all uncertainty components and document fully how each was evaluated.

### E9

- Location: 7.2.3
- Quote: "give the value of k used to obtain U [or, for the convenience of the user of the result, give both k and uc( y)];"
- Key point: When reporting an expanded uncertainty U, give the coverage factor k used to obtain it (or give both k and the combined standard uncertainty).

### E10

- Location: 3.2.4
- Quote: "It is assumed that the result of a measurement has been corrected for all recognized significant systematic effects and that every effort has been made to identify such effects."
- Key point: The GUM assumes the result has been corrected for all recognized significant systematic effects and that every effort has been made to identify such effects.

### E11

- Location: 3.1.1
- Quote: "A measurement therefore begins with an appropriate specification of the measurand, the method of measurement (B.2.7), and the measurement procedure (B.2.8)."
- Key point: A measurement begins by specifying three things: the measurand, the method of measurement, and the measurement procedure.

### E12

- Location: 3.1.3 (with example)
- Quote: "The measurand should be defined with sufficient completeness with respect to the required accuracy so that for all practical purposes associated with the measurement its value is unique." / "If the length of a nominally one-metre long steel bar is to be determined to micrometre accuracy, its specification should include the temperature and pressure at which the length is defined."
- Key point: The measurand must be defined completely enough, for the required accuracy, that its value is unique for all practical purposes. Example: to measure a nominally one-meter steel bar to the micrometer, the definition must state the temperature and pressure at which the length is defined.

### E13

- Location: D.1.1
- Quote: "incomplete definition of the measurand introduces into the uncertainty of the result of a measurement a component of uncertainty"
- Key point: An incomplete definition of the measurand itself adds a component of uncertainty to the result.

### E14

- Location: 3.4.2
- Quote: "all relevant quantities should be varied to the fullest practicable extent so that the evaluation of uncertainty can be based as much as possible on observed data."
- Key point: All relevant quantities should be varied as far as practicable, so the uncertainty evaluation rests as much as possible on observed data.

### E15

- Location: 3.4.2
- Quote: "A well-designed experiment can greatly facilitate reliable evaluations of uncertainty and is an important part of the art of measurement."
- Key point: A well-designed experiment greatly helps reliable uncertainty evaluation and is an important part of the art of measurement.

### E16

- Location: 3.4.2
- Quote: "The mathematical model should always be revised when the observed data, including the result of independent determinations of the same measurand, demonstrate that the model is incomplete."
- Key point: Revise the model whenever observed data (including independent determinations of the same measurand) show it is incomplete.

### E17

- Location: 3.4.3
- Quote: "the experimentally observed variability of its output values, as measured by their observed standard deviation, is often compared with the predicted standard deviation obtained by combining the various uncertainty components"
- Key point: To check that a measurement system is working properly, its observed standard deviation is often compared with the standard deviation predicted by combining the uncertainty components.

### E18

- Location: 3.2.3
- Quote: "Systematic error, like random error, cannot be eliminated but it too can often be reduced." / "a correction (B.2.23) or correction factor (B.2.24) can be applied to compensate for the effect."
- Key point: Systematic error, like random error, cannot be eliminated but can often be reduced; a correction or correction factor can be applied to compensate for a recognized systematic effect.

### E19

- Location: 3.2.4, Note 1
- Quote: "Often, measuring instruments and systems are adjusted or calibrated using measurement standards and reference materials to eliminate systematic effects; however, the uncertainties associated with these standards and materials must still be taken into account."
- Key point: Instruments are often adjusted or calibrated against measurement standards and reference materials to eliminate systematic effects, but the uncertainties of those standards and materials must still be included.

### E20

- Location: 3.2.3, Note
- Quote: "The terms “error” and “uncertainty” should be used properly and care taken to distinguish between them."
- Key point: Use the terms "error" and "uncertainty" properly and keep them distinct.

### E21

- Location: 4.2.7
- Quote: "If the random variations in the observations of an input quantity are correlated, for example, in time, the mean and experimental standard deviation of the mean as given in 4.2.1 and 4.2.3 may be inappropriate estimators"
- Key point: If repeated observations are correlated (e.g., in time, as with drift), the ordinary mean and experimental standard deviation of the mean may be inappropriate estimators.

### E22

- Location: 4.3.7
- Quote: "one can only assume that it is equally probable for Xi to lie anywhere within it (a uniform or rectangular distribution of possible values" / "If the difference between the bounds, a+ − a−, is denoted by 2a, then Equation (6) becomes u2(xi) = a2/3"
- Key point: If all you know is that a quantity lies within an interval, you can only assume it is equally likely to be anywhere in it: a uniform (rectangular) distribution. For a half-width a, the variance is u² = a²/3, so the standard uncertainty is a/√3.

### E23

- Location: 5.1.2
- Quote: "Each u(xi) is a standard uncertainty evaluated as described in 4.2 (Type A evaluation) or as in 4.3 (Type B evaluation)."
- Key point: When combining, each input's standard uncertainty may come from a Type A or a Type B evaluation; both go into the same propagation formula.

### E24

- Location: 5.2.1
- Quote: "If some of the Xi are significantly correlated, the correlations must be taken into account."
- Key point: If inputs are significantly correlated, the correlations must be taken into account when combining uncertainties.

### E25

- Location: 3.3.7
- Quote: "an expanded uncertainty U is obtained by multiplying the combined standard uncertainty uc by a coverage factor k."
- Key point: The expanded uncertainty U is the combined standard uncertainty multiplied by a coverage factor k.

### E26

- Location: 7.2.6
- Quote: "It usually suffices to quote uc(y) and U [as well as the standard uncertainties u(xi) of the input estimates xi] to at most two significant digits"
- Key point: The combined standard uncertainty and expanded uncertainty (and the inputs' standard uncertainties) usually need at most two significant digits.

### E27

- Location: 3.4.8
- Quote: "Although this Guide provides a framework for assessing uncertainty, it cannot substitute for critical thinking, intellectual honesty and professional skill."
- Key point: The Guide provides a framework but cannot substitute for critical thinking, intellectual honesty, and professional skill.

### E28

- Location: 0.1
- Quote: "Without such an indication, measurement results cannot be compared, either among themselves or with reference values given in a specification or standard."
- Key point: Without a quantitative indication of quality, measurement results cannot be compared with each other or with reference values in a standard.
