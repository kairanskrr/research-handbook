# US Environmental Protection Agency 2006: Life Cycle Assessment: Principles and Practice

- Source: `epa2006lca`, [original PDF](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPDF.cgi?Dockey=P1000L86.PDF)
- Version: EPA/600/R-06/060, May 2006. Prepared by Scientific Applications International Corporation (SAIC) for EPA's National Risk Management Research Laboratory. Locations give the PDF page.

### E1

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 9
- Quote: "Life cycle assessment is a "cradle-to-grave" approach for assessing industrial systems. "Cradle-to-grave" begins with the gathering of raw materials from the earth to create the product and ends at the point when all materials are returned to the earth."
- Key point: Life cycle assessment (LCA) is a "cradle-to-grave" approach: it starts with the gathering of raw materials from the earth and ends when all materials are returned to the earth.

### E2

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "The LCA process is a systematic, phased approach and consists of four components: goal definition and scoping, inventory analysis, impact assessment, and interpretation"
- Key point: LCA has four components: goal definition and scoping, inventory analysis, impact assessment, and interpretation.

### E3

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 11
- Quote: "An LCA allows a decision maker to study an entire product system hence avoiding the suboptimization that could result if only a single process were the focus of the study."
- Key point: LCA looks at the entire product system, avoiding the suboptimization that results from focusing on a single process.

### E4

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 11
- Quote: "For example, when selecting between two rival products, it may appear that Option 1 is better for the environment because it generates less solid waste than Option 2. However, after performing an LCA it might be determined that the first option actually creates larger cradle-to-grave environmental impacts when measured across all three media (air, water, land)"
- Key point: Example: Option 1 produces less solid waste and seems better for the environment, but an LCA may show that its total impact across air, water, and land is actually larger.

### E5

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 13
- Quote: "Performing an LCA can be resource and time intensive. Depending upon how thorough an LCA the user wishes to conduct, gathering the data can be problematic, and the availability of data can greatly impact the accuracy of the final results."
- Key point: LCA can be resource- and time-intensive, data can be hard to gather, and the data available strongly affect the accuracy of the results.

### E6

- Location: Chapter 1, PDF p. 13
- Quote: "LCA will not determine which product or process is the most cost effective or works the best."
- Key point: LCA will not tell you which product or process is the most cost-effective or works best.

### E7

- Location: Chapter 2, PDF p. 19
- Quote: "Careful selection of the functional unit to measure and display the LCA results will improve the accuracy of the study and the usefulness of the results."
- Key point: Careful selection of the functional unit (the reference for measuring and presenting the results) improves the accuracy of the study and the usefulness of the results.

### E8

- Location: Chapter 2, PDF p. 19, example of choosing a functional unit
- Quote: "An LCA study comparing two types of wall insulation to determine environmental preferability must be evaluated on the same function, the ability to decrease heat flow."
- Key point: An LCA comparing two types of wall insulation must compare them on the same function (the ability to reduce heat flow), not on equal area and thickness.

### E9

- Location: Chapter 2, PDF p. 26
- Quote: "If assumptions are omitted, the final results may be taken out of context or easily misinterpreted."
- Key point: If assumptions are omitted, the final results may be taken out of context or misinterpreted.

### E10

- Location: Chapter 4, PDF p. 60
- Quote: "According to ISO 14042, any judgment of preferability is a subjective judgment regarding the relative importance of one impact category over another."
- Key point: According to ISO 14042, any judgment of which option is preferable is a subjective judgment about the relative importance of impact categories.
