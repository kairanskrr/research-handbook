# Tscharntke et al. 2007: Author Sequence and Credit for Contributions in Multiauthored Publications

- Source: `tscharntke2007author`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0050018)
- Version: PLoS Biology 5(1): e18, 2007, open access, accessed 2026-10-06. The authors are from ecology and environmental science. The text has no subheadings, so locations give paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: Paragraph 1
- Quote: "Traditionally, the first author contributes most and also receives most of the credit, whereas the position of subsequent authors is usually decided by contribution, alphabetical order, or reverse seniority."
- Key point: Traditionally, the first author contributes most and receives the most credit; the order of the following authors reflects their contribution, alphabetical order, or reverse seniority.

### E2

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "Following informal practices in the biomedical sciences, the last author often gets as much credit as the first author, because he or she is assumed to be the driving force, both intellectually and financially, behind the research."
- Key point: By informal convention in the biomedical fields, the last author often receives as much credit as the first, because he or she is seen as the driving force behind the research, both intellectually and financially.

### E3

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "Evaluation committees and funding bodies often take last authorship as a sign of successful group leadership and make this a criterion in hiring, granting, and promotion. This practice is unofficial, and hence not always followed"
- Key point: Evaluation committees and funding agencies often treat last authorship as a sign of successfully leading a research group and use it in hiring, funding, and promotion; but this is an informal convention and is not always followed.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph 3
- Quote: "authors often adopt different methods of crediting contributions for the following authors, because of very different traditions across countries and research fields"
- Key point: Traditions differ greatly across countries and fields, and practices vary in how the order of authors after the first reflects their contributions.

### E5

- Location: Paragraph 3
- Quote: "We suggest that the approach taken should be stated in the acknowledgements section"
- Key point: The authors suggest stating in the acknowledgments which author-ordering approach the paper uses.
