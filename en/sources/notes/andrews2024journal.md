# Andrews 2024: Insights from the American Philosophical Association's Journal Surveys Project

- Source: `andrews2024journal`, [original](https://blog.apaonline.org/2024/06/07/insights-from-the-american-philosophical-associations-journal-surveys-project/)
- Version: post on the Blog of the APA (American Philosophical Association), June 7, 2024, by Sam Andrews. The data come from the APA's Journal Surveys project, in which authors voluntarily report their submission experiences; the reports are not verified by the journals. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Authors can submit information about how long a journal took to review their manuscript, how good their experience was with the editors, if and what quality of comments they received, and if the manuscript was accepted for publication. Since its creation by Andy Cullison in 2009 over 9,400 surveys have been completed as of April 2024."
- Key point: Authors can report how long a journal took to review their manuscript, their experience with the editors, whether they got comments and of what quality, and whether the paper was accepted; the project was created in 2009 and had received over 9,400 surveys by April 2024.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "There are drawbacks to using author-reported surveys to glean insights into journal metrics as we do not know how accurate the surveys are and most journals do not provide the stats that would allow us to verify the survey results. For example, the acceptance rate for most journals is widely believed to be overstated in the surveys"
- Key point: Author-reported data have drawbacks: we do not know how accurate they are, and most journals do not publish statistics to check them against; for example, the surveys are generally thought to overestimate acceptance rates for most journals.

### E3

- Location: Setup
- Quote: "However, many of these surveys are from some time ago and only 1,568 surveys (18.42%) were submitted in the last 5 years."
- Key point: Of the 8,512 surveys left after cleaning, only 1,568 were submitted in the last five years; the author analyzes only these five years (2019–2023).

### E4

- Location: Setup
- Quote: "Survey responses are spread out among 97 journals, for an average of 16.2 per journal."
- Key point: The surveys from these five years cover 97 journals.

### E5

- Location: Results of the Journal Surveys: Response Length
- Quote: "Across all surveys, the average time for journals to return the initial verdict was 3.22 months. 866 surveys (55.3%) reported a response time of less than 3 months, 468 (29.8%) reported between 3-6 months and 234 (14.9%) reported longer than 6 months."
- Key point: Across these surveys, journals took an average of 3.22 months to return an initial decision; 55.3% reported less than 3 months, 29.8% reported 3–6 months, and 14.9% reported more than 6 months.

### E6

- Location: Results of the Journal Surveys: Comment Chances
- Quote: "Across all surveys, 67% of surveys reported receiving 1 or more sets of reviewer comments."
- Key point: 67% of surveys reported receiving at least one set of reviewer comments (the rest got none, for example because of desk rejection).
