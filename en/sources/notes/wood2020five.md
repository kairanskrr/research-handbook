# Wood 2020: Five tips for starting (and continuing) a PhD

- Source: `wood2020five`, [original](https://blogs.ed.ac.uk/ellie-wood/2020/09/04/five-tips-for-starting-and-continuing-a-phd/)
- Version: University of Edinburgh blog, 2020-09-04

### E1

- Location: Tip 1 (reading notes)
- Quote: "It’s useful to keep track of what you’ve been reading because you won’t remember all of it, but you will want to come back to a lot of it."
- Key point: Keep track of what you read, because you won't remember all of it, but you will want to come back to much of it.

### E2

- Location: Tip 4 (using the flexibility of a PhD)
- Quote: "What things do you want to do outside of your PhD (sports, reading non-PhD-related books, joining local clubs and groups, always protecting weekends off) to actively maintain a healthy work-life balance (which is better for both your wellbeing, and the state of your thesis)?"
- Key point: Actively plan a life outside research (sports, non-PhD reading, local clubs, protected weekends) to maintain a healthy work-life balance, which is better for both your well-being and your thesis.

### E3

- Location: Tip 4
- Quote: "Are there times when you’re going to be working extra hard (like fieldwork)? How do you want to balance that with rest and recuperation afterwards"
- Key point: Anticipate periods of extra-hard work (such as fieldwork) and plan in advance how you will rest and recover afterward.

### E4

- Location: Tip 5 (talking with other PhD students)
- Quote: "help you feel connected to and supported by your colleagues and peers."
- Key point: Talking with other PhD students helps you feel connected to and supported by your peers.
