# Low & Meghir 2017: The Use of Structural Models in Econometrics

- Source: `low2017use`, [full text](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.31.2.33)
- Version: Journal of Economic Perspectives 31(2): 33–58, 2017. E1 and E2 are from the abstract on the American Economic Association's article page; from E3 on we read the full text, the journal's typeset PDF held in UCL Discovery, the institutional repository of University College London (https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10089195/1/jep.31.2.33.pdf). The article presents the role of structural models in empirical analysis and represents the authors' views.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The central payoff of a structural econometric model is that it allows an empirical researcher to go beyond the conclusions of a more conventional empirical study that provides reduced-form causal relationships."
- Key point: The central payoff of a structural econometric model is that it lets an empirical researcher go beyond the conclusions of conventional empirical work that gives only "reduced-form" causal relationships.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Structural models identify mechanisms that determine outcomes and are designed to analyze counterfactual policies, quantifying impacts on specific outcomes as well as effects in the short and longer run."
- Key point: Structural models identify the mechanisms that determine outcomes and are designed to analyze counterfactual policies (ones never implemented), quantifying their short- and long-run impacts on specific outcomes.

### E3

- Location: Introduction, p. 33
- Quote: "The set of assumptions under which the model inferences are valid should be clear: indeed, the clarity of the assumptions is what gives value to structural models."
- Key point: The assumptions under which the model's inferences are valid must be clear; the authors argue that this clarity is precisely what gives structural models their value.

### E4

- Location: Standard Errors and Post-Estimation Checks, p. 53
- Quote: "First, calculate parameter standard errors." / "Second, use the finite difference approach in the first check to show how moments change with estimated structural parameters. This information helps make estimation more transparent, by showing which parameters are pinned down by which moments."
- Key point: After estimating the parameters, run checks: first compute parameter standard errors; then show which feature of the data (moment) pins down which parameter, making the estimation more transparent.

### E5

- Location: Standard Errors and Post-Estimation Checks, p. 53
- Quote: "A further test compares simulated predictions with additional moments or reduced form evidence, preferably not targeted in estimation."
- Key point: A further test compares the model's simulated predictions with additional moments or reduced-form evidence, preferably not targeted in estimation.
