# Berez-Kroeker et al. 2018: Reproducible research in linguistics: A position statement on data citation and attribution in our field

- Source: `berezkroeker2018reproducible`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1515/ling-2017-0032)
- Version: Linguistics 56(1), open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0); text taken from the US National Science Foundation public access repository par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10055458

### E1

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "represents the collective views of some 41 colleagues"
- Key point: The position statement represents the collective views of some 41 colleagues.

### E2

- Location: §2
- Quote: "The difference between reproducible research and replicable research is that the latter produces new data, which can then ostensibly be analyzed for either confirmation or disconfirmation of previous results; the former provides access to the original data for independent analysis."
- Key point: Replicable research collects new data; reproducible research gives others access to the original data for independent analysis.

### E3

- Location: §2
- Quote: "Clearly, linguists cannot expect their colleagues to replicate data collection conditions"
- Key point: Linguists cannot expect colleagues to recreate the original data collection conditions.

### E4

- Location: §3
- Quote: "In a survey of one hundred descriptive grammars from a ten-year span between 2003 and 2012, Gawne and colleagues (2017) found that even with the benefit of years of pervasive discussion of data management methods in language documentation, very few authors in this genre make their methods or data sources explicit in their writing."
- Key point: A survey of 100 descriptive grammars from 2003-2012 found that very few authors state their methods or data sources.

### E5

- Location: §3
- Quote: "In short, we are in danger of being a social science asking its audience to take our word for it."
- Key point: Linguistics is in danger of being a social science that asks its audience to take its word for it.

### E6

- Location: §2
- Quote: "Transparency about methods of data collection and analysis may include: a description of the conditions under which data were collected"
- Key point: Methodological transparency includes describing the conditions under which data were collected.

### E7

- Location: §2
- Quote: "transparency about how to locate relevant subparts of a data set (page numbers, corpus line numbers, time-codes of starting and ending points in an audio or video recording, etc.)"
- Key point: Source transparency includes letting readers locate specific parts of the data: page numbers, corpus line numbers, start and end time codes in audio or video recordings.

### E8

- Location: §2, footnote 6
- Quote: "we also acknowledge that this must be done with sensitivity toward the privacy concerns of the people whose language is being recorded"
- Key point: Fine-grained citation must respect the privacy of the people whose language is recorded.

### E9

- Location: §4.2
- Quote: "authors should include in their publications an explicit statement that data come from introspection, either their own or that of others."
- Key point: When data come from introspection (your own or others' judgments), say so explicitly in the publication.

### E10

- Location: §4.2
- Quote: "In order for data to be citable, it should be stored in an accessible location, preferably a data archive or some other repository with a demonstrated commitment to both preserving the data and making them accessible for many years to come."
- Key point: To be citable, data should be stored in an accessible place, preferably a data archive committed to long-term preservation and access.

### E11

- Location: §4.2
- Quote: "though restricted data is citable (and should be cited by those who are permitted to access it), only unrestricted data can be re-used and cited"
- Key point: Restricted data can be cited, but only unrestricted data can be reused.

### E12

- Location: §4.3
- Quote: "Data work is intellectually valuable, and linguists who do data work need to be recognized for their work during professional evaluation."
- Key point: Data work is intellectually valuable and should be recognized in professional evaluation.

### E13

- Location: §5
- Quote: "We recommend that individual linguists proactively seek to develop a relationship with a data archive."
- Key point: Every linguist should proactively build a relationship with a data archive.

### E14

- Location: §3
- Quote: "precious endangered language data can disappear, and expensive experiments may be recreated out of ignorance."
- Key point: Precious endangered language data can disappear, and expensive experiments may be redone out of ignorance.

### E15

- Location: §4.2
- Quote: "Data should be available in formats that do not require proprietary software and that will be usable or portable as technology changes."
- Key point: Data should be in formats that do not require proprietary software and will remain usable as technology changes.
