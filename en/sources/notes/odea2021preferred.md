# O'Dea et al. 2021: Preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses in ecology and evolutionary biology: a PRISMA extension

- Source: `odea2021preferred`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12721)
- Version: Biological Reviews 96(5): 1695–1722, 2021, open access under CC BY-NC 4.0. Text taken from Europe PMC (PMC8518748). This is PRISMA-EcoEvo 1.0, the extension of the PRISMA reporting guideline for ecology and evolutionary biology. Locations give section headings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Because of these risks, meta‐analyses are increasingly conducted as part of systematic reviews, which use structured, transparent, and reproducible methods to collate and summarise evidence."
- Key point: Because of these risks, meta-analyses are increasingly conducted as part of systematic reviews, which use structured, transparent, and reproducible methods to collect and synthesize evidence.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "For readers to determine whether the conclusions from a systematic review or meta‐analysis should be trusted – and to be able to build upon the review – authors need to report what they did, why they did it, and what they found."
- Key point: For readers to judge whether the conclusions of a systematic review or meta-analysis can be trusted and built upon, authors must report what they did, why, and what they found.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "we surveyed 208 researchers with relevant experience (as authors, reviewers, or editors), and conducted detailed evaluations of 102 systematic review and meta‐analysis papers published between 2010 and 2019. Reporting quality was far below optimal and approximately normally distributed."
- Key point: The authors surveyed 208 researchers with relevant experience and evaluated in detail 102 systematic reviews and meta-analyses published 2010–2019, finding reporting quality far from ideal.

### E4

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The minority of assessed papers that referenced a guideline (~16%) showed substantially higher reporting quality than average"
- Key point: Among the assessed papers, the minority that referenced a reporting guideline (about 16%) showed substantially higher reporting quality than average.

### E5

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "PRISMA‐EcoEvo is a checklist of 27 main items that, when applicable, should be reported in systematic review and meta‐analysis publications summarising primary research in ecology and evolutionary biology."
- Key point: PRISMA-EcoEvo is a checklist of 27 main items that, when applicable, should be reported in systematic reviews and meta-analyses summarizing primary research in ecology and evolutionary biology.

### E6

- Location: Table 1, Review registration, 3.1
- Quote: "Register review aims, hypotheses (if applicable), and methods in a time‐stamped and publicly accessible archive and provide a link to the registration in the methods section of the manuscript. Ideally registration occurs before the search, but it can be done at any stage before data analysis"
- Key point: Register the review's aims, hypotheses (if any), and methods in a time-stamped, publicly accessible archive and link to the registration in the methods; ideally register before searching, and at the latest before data analysis.
