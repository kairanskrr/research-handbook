# PLDI 2026: PACMPL Issue PLDI 2026 Call for Papers

- Source: `pldi2026cfp`, [original](https://pldi26.sigplan.org/track/pldi-2026-papers)
- Version: Call for research papers, with the attached double-blind review FAQ, for the ACM SIGPLAN Conference on Programming Language Design and Implementation (PLDI) 2026, from the conference website, read 2026-10-08. Web page; locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Start of Call for Papers; Scope
- Quote: "PACMPL Issue PLDI 2026 seeks contributions on all aspects of programming languages research, broadly construed, including design, implementation, theory, applications, and performance." / "Novel system designs, thorough empirical work, well-motivated theoretical results, and new application areas are all in scope for PLDI."
- Key point: PLDI 2026 seeks contributions on all aspects of programming languages research: design, implementation, theory, applications, and performance; novel system designs, thorough empirical work, well-motivated theoretical results, and new application areas are all in scope.

### E2

- Location: Start of Call for Papers
- Quote: "Authors of papers published in PACMPL Issue PLDI 2026 will be invited – but not required – to present their work in the PLDI conference in June 2026"
- Key point: PLDI 2026 papers are published in the PLDI issue of the journal PACMPL (Proceedings of the ACM on Programming Languages); authors are invited, but not required, to present at the conference in June 2026.

### E3

- Location: Evaluation Criteria and Process
- Quote: "Reviewers will evaluate submissions for accuracy, significance, originality, and clarity." / "Authors of empirical papers are encouraged to consider the seven categories of the SIGPLAN Empirical Evaluation Guidelines when preparing submissions."
- Key point: Reviewers evaluate accuracy, significance, originality, and clarity; authors of empirical papers are encouraged to consider the seven categories of the SIGPLAN Empirical Evaluation Guidelines.

### E4

- Location: Evaluation Criteria and Process
- Quote: "The selection of papers will be made in two rounds of reviewing." / "At the end of the first round, the Review Committee will conditionally accept a subset of the submissions and all other submissions will be rejected." / "The same reviewers as in the first round will then assess how the revision requests have been acted upon by the authors." / "Revisions that fail to adequately address the reviewers’ original concerns will result in rejection."
- Key point: Reviewing has two rounds: at the end of the first, some papers are conditionally accepted and the rest rejected; in the second, the original reviewers check the revisions, and papers that do not resolve the original concerns are still rejected.

### E5

- Location: Evaluation Criteria and Process
- Quote: "Authors will be given several days to compose a written response to the reviews received in the first round – e.g., to correct errors and clarify technical concerns."
- Key point: After the first round, authors have several days to write a response, e.g., to correct errors and clarify technical concerns.

### E6

- Location: Double-Blind Reviewing
- Quote: "Author names and affiliations must be omitted from submissions." / "Any supplementary material must also be anonymized." / "The Review Committee will only learn the identities of authors of accepted papers following the second round of reviewing."
- Key point: Remove author names and affiliations from submissions and anonymize supplementary material too; the review committee learns the authors of accepted papers only after the second round.

### E7

- Location: FAQ on Double-Blind Reviewing, What exactly do I have to do to anonymize my paper?
- Quote: "For example, if your paper is the first to describe a system with a well-known name or codename, or you use a personally-identifiable naming convention for your work, then use a different name for your submission (which you may indicate has been changed for the purposes of double-blind reviewing)."
- Key point: If your paper is the first to describe a system with a well-known name, or your naming identifies you, use a different name in the submission; you may note that it was changed for double-blind review.

### E8

- Location: FAQ on Double-Blind Reviewing, Am I allowed to post my (non-blinded) paper ...?
- Quote: "Submit work previously discussed at an informal workshop, previously posted on arXiv or a similar site" / "Publicize your work on major mailing lists used by the community (because potential reviewers likely read these lists)."
- Key point: You may submit work discussed at an informal workshop or posted on arXiv, but do not publicize it on the community's major mailing lists, since reviewers are likely to see it.

### E9

- Location: Declaring Conflicts
- Quote: "Listing spurious conflicts can be grounds for rejection."
- Key point: Listing spurious conflicts of interest (to exclude reviewers) can be grounds for rejection.

### E10

- Location: Formatting Requirements
- Quote: "Each paper should have no more than 20 pages of text, excluding bibliography, using the ACM Proceedings format."
- Key point: The main text is limited to 20 pages, excluding references.

### E11

- Location: Supplementary Material
- Quote: "Reviewers are not required to examine the supplementary material but may refer to it if they would like to find further evidence supporting the claims in the paper." / "All appendices must be submitted as supplemental material, not as part of the main submission PDF."
- Key point: Reviewers need not examine supplementary material and consult it only for further evidence; all appendices must be submitted as supplementary material, not in the main PDF.

### E12

- Location: Artifact Evaluation and Artifact Intent
- Quote: "Artifact Evaluation is run by a separate committee whose task is to assess how well the artifacts support the work described in the papers." / "Artifact submission is voluntary but encouraged. The results of the artifact evaluation process will not influence the final decision regarding the papers."
- Key point: Artifact evaluation is run by a separate committee that assesses how well the artifacts support the paper; submitting an artifact is voluntary, and the result does not affect acceptance.

### E13

- Location: Artifact Evaluation and Artifact Intent
- Quote: "At paper submission time, authors will be asked whether they intend to submit an artifact for evaluation, and to provide an explanation if they do not intend to do so."
- Key point: New in PLDI 2026: at submission, authors must say whether they intend to submit an artifact, and explain if they do not.
