# J-PAL research resource: Implementation monitoring

- Source: `jpal2021implementation`, [original web page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/implementation-monitoring)
- Version: J-PAL research resource page by Michael Gibson, updated February 2021; accessed October 2026. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "If aspects of the program are not implemented correctly, researchers may falsely conclude that the program had no effect, when in reality, the program wasn’t actually implemented as designed."
- Key point: If the program is not implemented as designed, researchers may wrongly conclude that it had no effect.

### E2

- Location: Selecting indicators
- Quote: "Research teams should consider a few key indicators that focus on things the implementing partner can control."
- Key point: For monitoring, pick a few key indicators focused on things the implementing partner can control.

### E3

- Location: Indicators to monitor
- Quote: "Compliance can be monitored by comparing a list of respondents who were treated to a list of respondents who were randomly assigned to the treatment group."
- Key point: You can monitor compliance by comparing the list of people actually treated with the list of people randomly assigned to the treatment group.

### E4

- Location: Indicators to monitor
- Quote: "Evaluation-driven effects occur when respondents change their behavior in response to the evaluation instead of the intervention."
- Key point: Evaluation-driven effects occur when respondents change their behavior in response to the evaluation rather than the intervention.

### E5

- Location: Indicators to monitor
- Quote: "To inform ex-post analysis and reporting, thoroughly document any threats to internal validity that were found during the course of the intervention."
- Key point: Thoroughly document any threats to internal validity found during the intervention, to inform later analysis and reporting.

### E6

- Location: Indicators to monitor
- Quote: "To inform ex-post generalizability and scaling-up collect data on the costs of the intervention and contextual details."
- Key point: To judge generalizability and scale-up later, collect data on the intervention's costs and contextual details.

### E7

- Location: Monitoring methods
- Quote: "they are most useful when research staff show up unannounced—and can induce Hawthorne effects (Evans, 2014)."
- Key point: Field visits are most useful when unannounced, but they can induce Hawthorne effects (people changing behavior because they are observed).
