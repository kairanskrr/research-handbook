# Education Endowment Foundation 2022: Guidance on Recruitment and Retention

- Source: `eef2022recruitment`, [original PDF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/public/files/Recruitment_and_retention_guidance_FINAL..pdf)
- Version: Updated October 2022. Guidance on recruitment and retention from the Education Endowment Foundation (EEF, a charity that funds randomized controlled trials in English schools) for the delivery and evaluation teams of its funded projects. It applies only to EEF-funded trials, and the laws it refers to (GDPR etc.) are those of the UK. Locations give the section title.

### E1

- Location: 2. Creating a recruitment strategy
- Quote: "Recruiting to trials is time intensive and is often one of the biggest risks for a trial. If insufficient schools are recruited onto the study, a low sample size would reduce its sensitivity to detect an effect, and the trial may not be able to go ahead."
- Key point: Recruiting schools to a trial is time-intensive and often one of the biggest risks; if too few schools are recruited, the sample is too small to detect an effect and the trial may fail.

### E2

- Location: What are the timelines for recruitment?
- Quote: "Delivery teams have told us that it takes longer to recruit schools to an evaluation than to their normal programme."
- Key point: Delivery teams report that recruiting schools to an evaluation takes longer than recruiting them to their normal program.

### E3

- Location: What are the timelines for recruitment?
- Quote: "Be aware of busy times of years for schools, for example it is particularly difficult to recruit schools in the weeks preceding and including the SATs and GCSEs. Also be mindful of when schools are able to make decisions, for example for a project that requires a lot of staff time or big changes to standard practice, schools may be less able to make such a commitment late in the academic year"
- Key point: Mind schools' busy times of year: recruitment is especially hard in the weeks before and during national exams (SATs, GCSEs), and at the end of the school year schools find it harder to commit to programs that need a lot of teacher time or big changes to normal practice.

### E4

- Location: What are the timelines for recruitment?
- Quote: "For most projects that start delivery in schools in September, EEF would usually expect recruitment to begin in the prior January."
- Key point: For most projects that start delivery in schools in September, EEF usually expects recruitment to begin the previous January.

### E5

- Location: How many schools will be targeted?
- Quote: "It is not unusual for projects to need to contact dozens of schools for every school they eventually recruit."
- Key point: It is not unusual to contact dozens of schools for every school eventually recruited.

### E6

- Location: Why is this offer worthwhile?
- Quote: "It is imperative schools understand they are signing up to the evaluation and may be allocated to the intervention or control group, and are committing to take part in the evaluation regardless."
- Key point: Schools must understand that they are signing up to the evaluation, may be allocated to the intervention or the control group, and commit to taking part in the evaluation whichever group they are in.

### E7

- Location: 3. Ethical approval
- Quote: "The ethics review process usually takes 3-6 weeks but varies between organisations."
- Key point: Ethics review usually takes 3–6 weeks, varying between organizations.

### E8

- Location: c) Parent information sheet and withdrawal form
- Quote: "Opt-out consent is not a valid legal basis under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)"
- Key point: Under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), opt-out consent is not a valid legal basis for processing personal data.

### E9

- Location: c) Parent information sheet and withdrawal form
- Quote: "Thus, parents or legal guardians – and/ or, where appropriate, pupils – should always be given the opportunity to withdraw their child's (or, if applicable, their own) data from processing. Typically, schools send these out to parents at the start of the autumn term or at the end of the summer term, depending on the timeline of the project."
- Key point: Parents (and, where appropriate, pupils) should always be given the opportunity to withdraw their data; schools usually send the information to parents at the start of the autumn term or the end of the summer term.

### E10

- Location: c) Parent information sheet and withdrawal form
- Quote: "For participants who are 13 years or older, they themselves, rather than their parents, should be given the right to object to data processing."
- Key point: Participants aged 13 or older should themselves, rather than their parents, be given the right to object to data processing.

### E11

- Location: Pre-testing
- Quote: "Pre-testing should be completed before randomisation takes place. If schools are randomised, allocated to the control group, and then asked to complete testing, they might refuse to take part, or the data may be biased once schools/pupils are aware of their allocation."
- Key point: Pre-testing should be completed before randomization; otherwise schools allocated to the control group may refuse testing, and data collected after allocation is known may be biased.

### E12

- Location: 5. Retention
- Quote: "If schools or pupils decline to take part in data collection or withdraw from outcome data collection during the study (known as "outcome attrition"), this will reduce the sample size and can introduce biases."
- Key point: Schools or pupils dropping out of outcome data collection (outcome attrition) reduce the sample size and may introduce bias.

### E13

- Location: 5. Retention
- Quote: "It can be more difficult to retain control schools in the post-test, as they have had little contact with the delivery team and will not have engaged very much with the project since recruitment."
- Key point: Control schools have had little contact with the delivery team, so they are harder to retain for the post-test.

### E14

- Location: Pre-intervention survey to establish usual practice
- Quote: "Especially for targeted interventions, pupils in control schools are often given additional support or training of a different kind."
- Key point: Especially for targeted interventions, pupils in control schools often receive other kinds of additional support or training, so find out in advance what "business as usual" looks like in the control group.

### E15

- Location: Keeping a recruitment log
- Quote: "This log will be used to create a participant flow diagram which will become part of the final report. Please keep a record of all schools contacted and reasons for non-participation."
- Key point: The recruitment log is used to create the participant flow diagram in the final report; keep a record of all schools contacted and their reasons for not taking part.

### E16

- Location: 5. Retention
- Quote: "The validity of the findings from a trial is illustrated by the padlock rating5 which is heavily influenced by outcome attrition (i.e., did the school provide post-test data or not)."
- Key point: EEF uses a "padlock rating" to show how secure a trial's findings are, and the rating is heavily influenced by outcome attrition (whether schools provided post-test data).
