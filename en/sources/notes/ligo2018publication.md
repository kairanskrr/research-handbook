# LIGO Scientific Collaboration 2018: Publication and Presentation Policy

- Source: `ligo2018publication`, [full text](https://dcc.ligo.org/public/0026/T010168/010/T010168-10.pdf)
- Version: LIGO-T010168-10, February 2018. The public publication and presentation policy of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration (LSC), the gravitational-wave detection collaboration.

### E1

- Location: §2.A The LSC Author List, item 1
- Quote: "The author list of the scientific publications of LSC observations will include all members of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration who have earned that status. The author list will be alphabetical"
- Key point: The author list of LSC observational papers includes all members who have earned author status, in alphabetical order.

### E2

- Location: §2.A, item 1
- Quote: "The nominal goal of the specific implementation described in 2 is that LSC members earn author status by devoting at least 50% of their research effort to the LSC for a period of approximately one year."
- Key point: Members earn author status by devoting at least 50% of their research effort to the LSC for about a year.

### E3

- Location: §2.A, item 6
- Quote: "Individual LSC members eligible for authorship may request their names not to be included in specific papers."
- Key point: Members eligible for authorship may ask not to be listed on a specific paper.

### E4

- Location: §2.B Archival Journals, item 5
- Quote: "Technical papers originating from individual or collaborating LSC institutions shall assign authorship in accordance with generally accepted principles."
- Key point: Technical papers from a single LSC institution or a few collaborating institutions assign authorship according to generally accepted principles, not the full-collaboration author list.

### E5

- Location: §2.B, item 3
- Quote: "The primary authors can petition to the P&P committee for a limited author list of those individuals whose work substantially contributed to the paper."
- Key point: The primary authors of a methods paper can petition the publication committee for a limited author list of those who contributed substantially.

### E6

- Location: §3.A Triggers for an LSC review, item 1
- Quote: "This determination must be made prior to submission for publication or posting on any public archive (e.g., LANL gr-qc, arXiv)."
- Key point: Whether an internal collaboration review is needed must be decided before submission or posting on any public archive such as arXiv.

### E7

- Location: §3.B Procedures for Review, item 2
- Quote: "The reviewer(s) confirms the most important results presented in the manuscript and provides comments on the content and recommendations on the authorship."
- Key point: The internal reviewers confirm the paper's most important results and comment on its content and authorship.

### E8

- Location: §4 Presentations, 4.1
- Quote: "Only public observational results or results that have been approved by the Collaboration"
- Key point: Only public observational results or results approved by the Collaboration may be shown in invited talks and conference proceedings.

### E9

- Location: §5 Theses, item 1
- Quote: "An analysis claiming detection cannot be published in a thesis until an official announcement has been made by the LSC and the LIGO Directorate."
- Key point: An analysis claiming a detection cannot be published in a thesis until the Collaboration has made an official announcement.

### E10

- Location: §5 Theses, item 3
- Quote: "If the thesis does not undergo a review or does not meet the above criteria, a disclaimer shall be added to the introduction, stating that the work does not reflect the scientific opinion of the LSC and it was not reviewed by the collaboration."
- Key point: A thesis not reviewed by the Collaboration must state in its introduction that it does not represent the Collaboration's views and was not reviewed by it.

### E11

- Location: §1 Introduction, policy goals
- Quote: "encourage younger scientists and engineers to participate in the presentation and publication of results."
- Key point: One goal of the policy is to encourage younger scientists and engineers to take part in presenting and publishing results.
