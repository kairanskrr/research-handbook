# Ioannidis 2005: Why Most Published Research Findings Are False

- Source: `ioannidis2005why`, [PLoS Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124) (CC BY)
- Version: PLoS Med 2(8): e124, 2005. An essay whose conclusions come from a probability model, not from measuring published studies.

### E1

- Location: Corollary 4
- Quote: "Corollary 4: The greater the flexibility in designs, definitions, outcomes, and analytical modes in a scientific field, the less likely the research findings are to be true. Flexibility increases the potential for transforming what would be “negative” results into “positive” results"
- Key point: The greater the flexibility in designs, definitions, outcomes, and analyses, the less likely research findings are to be true; flexibility makes it easier to turn "negative" results into "positive" ones.

### E2

- Location: Corollary 5
- Quote: "Corollary 5: The greater the financial and other interests and prejudices in a scientific field, the less likely the research findings are to be true. Conflicts of interest and prejudice may increase bias"
- Key point: The greater the financial and other interests and prejudices, the less likely research findings are to be true; conflicts of interest and prejudice may increase bias.

### E3

- Location: How Can We Improve the Situation?
- Quote: "In some research designs, efforts may also be more successful with upfront registration of studies, e.g., randomized trials"
- Key point: For designs such as randomized trials, upfront registration of studies may help.
