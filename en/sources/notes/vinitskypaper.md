# Vinitsky: Ways to Engage Deeply with a Paper

- Source: `vinitskypaper`, [original](https://www.eugenevinitsky.com/posts/paper-games)
- Version: Personal blog, year to be confirmed

### E1

- Location: A template
- Quote: "The goal of this template is to help you avoid the situation where you've been reading a paper for an hour or two but come away realizing you've in fact learned nothing."
- Key point: The point of the paper-reading template is to avoid spending an hour or two on a paper and coming away having learned nothing.

### E2

- Location: Why am I reading this paper?
- Quote: "Fill in this section to convince yourself that it's worthwhile to invest the time into reading this paper. If you can't fill it in, maybe you shouldn't actually read the paper?"
- Key point: Before reading, write down why you are reading this paper; if you can't, maybe you shouldn't read it.

### E3

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "1 sentence summary [...] Paragraph summary [...] Complete summary (I do this for vanishingly few papers)"
- Key point: Summaries come in three levels: one sentence, one paragraph, and a complete summary; the complete summary is done for very few papers.

### E4

- Location: Unresolved question
- Quote: "What bit of the paper didn't you understand? You can link this to a main sheet so that maybe you can come back to it later and see that you get it now."
- Key point: Note what you didn't understand and collect it in one place so you can come back to it later.

### E5

- Location: Unresolved question
- Quote: "Once you've completed this section, you might consider stopping if the "Why am I reading this paper" section was not super compelling. Everything below requires a lot more work and should not be done for most papers."
- Key point: After the summaries and questions, you can stop if your reason for reading the paper was not compelling; the more demanding parts that follow are not needed for most papers.

### E6

- Location: Paper games
- Quote: "Imagine there is a panel grilling you on the paper, what question might they ask you that you wouldn't be able to answer?"
- Key point: Imagine a panel grilling you on the paper: what question could you not answer?

### E7

- Location: Paper games
- Quote: "What experiment would I have run to make this paper more convincing?"
- Key point: Ask yourself: what experiment would I have run to make this paper more convincing?

### E8

- Location: Paper games
- Quote: "If I were to do a new project based on what's in this paper, what would it be?"
- Key point: Ask yourself: if I did a new project based on this paper, what would it be?
