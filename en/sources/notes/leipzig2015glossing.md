# Comrie, Haspelmath & Bickel 2015: The Leipzig Glossing Rules

- Source: `leipzig2015glossing`, [full text](https://www.eva.mpg.de/lingua/pdf/Glossing-Rules.pdf)
- Version: rules text as revised in February 2008, file last modified May 31, 2015; developed jointly by the Department of Linguistics of the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology (Germany) and the Department of Linguistics of the University of Leipzig; free PDF

### E1

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "Interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glosses give information about the meanings and grammatical properties of individual words and parts of words."
- Key point: Interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glosses give the meanings and grammatical properties of individual words and parts of words.

### E2

- Location: Rule 1: Word-by-word alignment
- Quote: "Interlinear glosses are left-aligned vertically, word by word, with the example."
- Key point: Interlinear glosses are left-aligned vertically, word by word, with the example.

### E3

- Location: Rule 2: Morpheme-by-morpheme correspondence
- Quote: "Segmentable morphemes are separated by hyphens, both in the example and in the gloss. There must be exactly the same number of hyphens in the example and in the gloss."
- Key point: Segmentable morphemes are separated by hyphens in both the example and the gloss, and the two lines must have exactly the same number of hyphens.

### E4

- Location: Rule 2
- Quote: "Clitic boundaries are marked by an equals sign, both in the object language and in the gloss."
- Key point: Clitic boundaries (elements that attach to another word) are marked with an equals sign.

### E5

- Location: Rule 3: Grammatical category labels
- Quote: "Grammatical morphemes are generally rendered by abbreviated grammatical category labels, printed in upper case letters (usually small capitals)."
- Key point: Grammatical morphemes are rendered by abbreviated grammatical category labels in upper case (usually small capitals).

### E6

- Location: Rule 4: One-to-many correspondences
- Quote: "When a single object-language element is rendered by several metalanguage elements (words or abbreviations), these are separated by periods." / "Latin insul-arum island-GEN.PL 'of the islands'"
- Key point: When one element is rendered by several words or abbreviations in the gloss, they are separated by periods.

### E7

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "Glosses are part of the analysis, not part of the data."
- Key point: Glosses are part of the analysis, not part of the data.

### E8

- Location: About the rules
- Quote: "most authors will feel the need to add (or modify) certain conventions (especially category labels)."
- Key point: Most authors will need to add or modify certain conventions, especially category labels.

### E9

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "The main purpose that is assumed here is the presentation of an example in a research paper or book. When an entire corpus is tagged, somewhat different" [...] "considerations may apply"
- Key point: The rules are mainly for presenting examples in research papers and books; tagging an entire corpus involves somewhat different considerations.

### E10

- Location: About the rules
- Quote: "The Leipzig Glossing Rules have been developed jointly by the Department of Linguistics of the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology (Bernard Comrie, Martin Haspelmath) and by the Department of Linguistics of the University of Leipzig (Balthasar Bickel)."
- Key point: The rules were developed jointly by the Department of Linguistics of the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology and the Department of Linguistics of the University of Leipzig.

### E11

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "the main purpose of this document is to make the most widely used conventions explicit."
- Key point: The main purpose of the document is to make the most widely used glossing conventions explicit.

### E12

- Location: About the rules
- Quote: "an appendix with a proposed" [...] "of abbreviated category labels."
- Key point: The rules come with an appendix proposing a list of abbreviated grammatical category labels.
