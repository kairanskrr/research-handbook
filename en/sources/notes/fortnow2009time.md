# Fortnow 2009: Time for Computer Science to Grow Up

- Source: `fortnow2009time`, [Original](https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2009/8/34492-viewpoint-time-for-computer-science-to-grow-up/fulltext)
- Version: Communications of the ACM 52(8): 33–35, 2009, Viewpoint article. The original site refused script access, so it was read via the Internet Archive snapshot of 2022-08-15.

### E1

- Location: Abstract paragraph
- Quote: "the conference model has fractured the discipline and skewered it toward short-term, deadline-driven research."
- Key point: The conference model has fractured the discipline and skewed it toward short-term, deadline-driven research.

### E2

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "We end up living in a deadline-driven world, submitting a paper when we reach an appropriate conference deadline instead of when the research has been properly fleshed out. Many also just publish "least-publishable units," doing just enough to get accepted into a conference."
- Key point: People submit when a conference deadline arrives rather than when the research is properly fleshed out; many publish "least-publishable units," doing just enough to get accepted.

### E3

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "The most difficult decisions are made by consensus. This leads to an emphasis on safe papers (incremental and technical) versus those that explore new models and research directions outside the established core areas of the conference."
- Key point: Program committees decide by consensus, which favors safe, incremental papers over those that explore new models and research directions.

### E4

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "(author information removed from papers, author responses to initial reviews, multilevel program committees, separate tracks for areas and quality, higher/lower acceptance ratios)"
- Key point: Attempts conferences have made to improve fairness: removing author information (double-blind review), author responses to initial reviews (rebuttal), multilevel program committees, separate tracks, and adjusted acceptance ratios.

### E5

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "The great papers almost always are accepted and the worst papers mostly get rejected. The problem occurs for the vast majority of solid papers landing in the middle."
- Key point: Great papers are almost always accepted and the worst mostly rejected; the problem lies with the large mass of solid papers in the middle.

### E6

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "When we judge candidates for an academic position, we first check the quality and quantity of the conferences where their work has appeared."
- Key point: When hiring, people first check the quality and quantity of the conferences where a candidate has published.

### E7

- Location: Abstract paragraph
- Quote: "We should use archive sites as the main method of quick paper dissemination and the journal system as the vehicle for advancing researchers' reputations."
- Key point: The author argues for using archive (preprint) sites for quick dissemination and journals for building researchers' reputations.

### E8

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "nearly half of the Gödel Prize winners (given to the best CS theory papers after they've appeared in journals) were initially rejected or didn't appear at all in the top theoretical computer science conferences."
- Key point: Nearly half of the Gödel Prize winners (the prize for the best theoretical computer science papers) were initially rejected by, or never appeared in, the top theory conferences.
