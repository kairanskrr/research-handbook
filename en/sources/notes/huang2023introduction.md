# Huang 2023: How to write the Introduction?

- Source: `huang2023introduction`, [original tweet](https://twitter.com/jbhuang0604/status/1638029709073166336), [Thread Reader mirror](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1638029709073166336.html)
- Version: Tweet thread, 2023-03-21, 20 tweets, read via the Thread Reader App mirror. The examples come from computer vision; this handbook uses only the structure of the introduction.

### E1

- Location: Start of the thread
- Quote: "As a junior student, writing the introduction of a research paper is arguably the most daunting part of paper writing."
- Key point: For junior students, the introduction is often the hardest part of a paper to write. The author offers a template: three figures (what, why, how) plus five questions.

### E2

- Location: Q1–Q3
- Quote: "Explain why all these existing solutions are NOT satisfactory (in some aspects)."
- Key point: The introduction answers, in order: what the problem is (and why readers should care); what others have done; and where the gap is, that is, why existing solutions are not satisfactory.

### E3

- Location: Q4
- Quote: "It helps provide a clean separation of the past/current work."
- Key point: When describing what you did, start with "In this paper, ..." to cleanly separate past work from this work; cover the task, the high-level idea, and how you evaluate it.

### E4

- Location: Q5
- Quote: "Not everything you do is "novel" e.g., a large part of your work may build upon some existing methods. Thus, it is a good practice to explicitly state your contributions."
- Key point: Much of your work builds on existing methods, so state explicitly which parts are your contributions.

### E5

- Location: The WHY figure section
- Quote: "The best WHY figure illustrates the existing work's core issue/problem with a concrete example."
- Key point: Motivate the research by illustrating the core problem of existing work with a concrete example.
