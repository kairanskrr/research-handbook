# NeurIPS: Paper Checklist Guidelines

- Source: `neurips2025checklist`, [original](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- Version: NeurIPS Paper Checklist guidelines page (accompanying the NeurIPS 2025 call for papers, including item 16, "Declaration of LLM usage"), accessed 2026-10-08. Locations give the checklist item number or paragraph. Used only as an example from one machine learning conference; other conferences and later years may differ.

### E1

- Location: opening paragraph
- Quote: "Do not remove the checklist: The papers not including the checklist will be desk rejected"
- Key point: Submissions without the checklist are desk rejected (not sent for review).

### E2

- Location: opening paragraph (instructions for answering)
- Quote: "Reviewers will be asked to use the checklist as one of the factors in their evaluation." / "it is perfectly acceptable to answer "no" provided a proper justification is given"
- Key point: Reviewers use the checklist as one factor in their evaluation; answering "no" is perfectly acceptable as long as you give a proper justification.

### E3

- Location: opening paragraph (instructions for answering)
- Quote: "In general, answering "no" or "n/a" is not grounds for rejection."
- Key point: In general, answering "no" or "n/a" is not in itself grounds for rejection.

### E4

- Location: 2. Limitations
- Quote: "Reviewers will be specifically instructed to not penalize honesty concerning limitations."
- Key point: Reviewers are specifically instructed not to penalize authors for being honest about limitations.

### E5

- Location: 7. Experiment Statistical Significance
- Quote: "The factors of variability that the error bars are capturing should be clearly stated (for example, train/test split, initialization, random drawing of some parameter, or overall run with given experimental conditions)."
- Key point: State which source of variability the error bars capture, e.g., train/test split, initialization, or random drawing of some parameter.

### E6

- Location: 7. Experiment Statistical Significance
- Quote: "It should be clear whether the error bar is the standard deviation or the standard error of the mean."
- Key point: State whether the error bars are standard deviations or standard errors of the mean.

### E7

- Location: 8. Experiments Compute Resource
- Quote: "The paper should disclose whether the full research project required more compute than the experiments reported in the paper (e.g., preliminary or failed experiments that didn’t make it into the paper)"
- Key point: Disclose whether the full research project used more compute than the experiments reported in the paper (e.g., preliminary or failed experiments that didn't make it into the paper).

### E8

- Location: 4. Experimental Result Reproducibility
- Quote: "While NeurIPS does not require releasing code, we do require all submissions to provide some reasonable avenue for reproducibility"
- Key point: NeurIPS does not require releasing code, but it does require every submission to provide some reasonable avenue for reproducibility.

### E9

- Location: 5. Open Access to Data and Code
- Quote: "Papers cannot be rejected simply for not including code, unless this is central to the contribution (e.g., for a new open-source benchmark)."
- Key point: Papers cannot be rejected simply for not including code, unless code is central to the contribution (e.g., a new open-source benchmark).

### E10

- Location: 14. Crowdsourcing and Research with Human Subjects
- Quote: "According to the NeurIPS Code of Ethics, you must pay workers involved in data collection, curation, or other labor at least the minimum wage in your country."
- Key point: Under the NeurIPS Code of Ethics, workers involved in data collection, curation, or other labor must be paid at least the minimum wage in their country.

### E11

- Location: 10. Broader Impacts
- Quote: "However, if you see a direct path to any negative applications, you should point it out."
- Key point: Foundational research need not list every possible misuse, but if you see a direct path to harmful applications, point it out.

### E12

- Location: 1. Claims
- Quote: "Claims in the paper should match theoretical and experimental results in terms of how much the results can be expected to generalize."
- Key point: Claims in the paper should match how far the theoretical and experimental results can be expected to generalize.

### E13

- Location: 12. Licenses
- Quote: "Remember to state which version of the asset you're using." / "State the name of the license (e.g., CC-BY 4.0) for each asset."
- Key point: If you use others' code, data, or models, state the version and the name of the license.

### E14

- Location: opening paragraph (instructions for answering)
- Quote: "You will be asked to also include it (after eventual revisions) with the final version of your paper, and its final version will be published with the paper."
- Key point: The checklist is submitted with the final version and published with the paper.

### E15

- Location: 6. Experimental Setting/ Details
- Quote: "information about how hyperparameters were selected should appear either in the paper or supplementary materials"
- Key point: Explain how hyperparameters were selected, either in the paper or in the supplementary material.

### E16

- Location: 3. Theory, Assumptions and Proofs
- Quote: "If you are including theoretical results, did you state the full set of assumptions of all theoretical results, and did you include complete proofs of all theoretical results?"
- Key point: For theoretical results, state the full set of assumptions and give complete proofs (these can go in the supplementary material, with a proof sketch in the main text).

### E17

- Location: 2. Limitations
- Quote: "It is worth keeping in mind that a worse outcome might be if reviewers discover limitations that aren't acknowledged in the paper."
- Key point: A worse outcome may be reviewers discovering limitations the paper does not acknowledge.

### E18

- Location: 10. Broader Impacts
- Quote: "We expect many papers to be foundational research and not tied to particular applications, let alone deployments."
- Key point: Many papers are foundational research, not tied to particular applications, let alone deployments.
