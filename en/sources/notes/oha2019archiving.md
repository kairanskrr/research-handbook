# Oral History Association 2019: Archives Principles and Best Practices

- Source: `oha2019archiving`, [web version](https://oralhistory.org/archives-principles-and-best-practices/), [full PDF](https://oralhistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/OHA-Archives-Principles-and-Best-Practices-Complete-Manual.pdf)
- Version: Adopted by the Oral History Association (OHA, United States) in October 2019 as an appendix to its 2018 Principles and Best Practices; written by the OHA Archives Best Practices Task Force. Web version read 2026-10-06. Locations give section names.

### E1

- Location: Introduction, and footnote 1
- Quote: "Some archiving plans may eschew traditional repositories, particularly when working with vulnerable communities, or when inclusion in a repository carries the potential for legal or personal danger." / "Examples include interviews conducted with undocumented immigrants who would be at risk of deportation or survivors of domestic violence or stalking who risk being located and harmed by former abusers."
- Key point: When working with vulnerable communities, or when placing material in a repository could bring legal or personal danger, an archiving plan may bypass traditional repositories. Examples: undocumented immigrants (risk of deportation) and survivors of domestic violence or stalking (risk of being found by the abuser).

### E2

- Location: Metadata and Description, Overview
- Quote: "It is important to collect the following types of information or metadata categories: administrative, descriptive, technical, preservation, and rights and access."
- Key point: Collect five categories of metadata: administrative, descriptive, technical, preservation, and rights and access.

### E3

- Location: Metadata and Description, What to Collect
- Quote: "Descriptive metadata is information that is necessary for the discovery of oral history interviews and that documents the content and the context of the resource (e.g., the name of the interviewer and narrator, the date and place of the interview, and content information in the form of keywords, summary, and controlled vocabulary)."
- Key point: Descriptive metadata lets people discover interviews and documents their content and context, e.g., names of interviewer and narrator, date and place of the interview, keywords, abstract, and controlled vocabularies.

### E4

- Location: Preservation, How to Preserve
- Quote: "Maintaining a backup is not the same as having a fully vested archival program."
- Key point: Having a backup is not the same as having a full archival program.

### E5

- Location: Preservation, What to Preserve
- Quote: "A legal release signed by the narrator, interviewer, and any others present during the interview."
- Key point: Documents kept with the recording should include, at minimum, a legal release signed by the narrator, the interviewer, and anyone else present.

### E6

- Location: Access, Permissions and Ethical Use
- Quote: "Oral histories are co-created by the interviewer and the narrator and copyright is most often considered as belonging to both parties unless or until they sign an agreement to transfer that right."
- Key point: Oral histories are co-created by interviewer and narrator; copyright is usually considered to belong to both until they sign an agreement transferring it.

### E7

- Location: Access, Permissions and Ethical Use
- Quote: "If a potential donor does not have either recorded verbal or legal release for an interview, it is not recommended that the archive accept the donation"
- Key point: If a donor has neither a recorded verbal release nor a legal release, the archive is advised not to accept the donation.

### E8

- Location: Access, Transcription
- Quote: "Because human transcribers and automated transcription services are both fallible, it is best practice to review first-draft transcripts for accuracy." / "All transcripts should be reviewed before publishing, carefully checking to ensure that proper names are spelled correctly and that dates reflect what is said on the recording."
- Key point: Both human and automated transcription make errors, so best practice is to review first-draft transcripts; review all transcripts before publication, checking especially the spelling of proper names and dates against the recording.

### E9

- Location: Access, Transcription
- Quote: "In addition to, or in lieu of (if transcription isn’t feasible), a transcript, the interview could be indexed to make it more searchable."
- Key point: When transcription is not feasible, an index can stand in for a transcript to make the interview searchable.

### E10

- Location: Ownership and Rights Management, Definitions of Ownership
- Quote: "These physical carriers can be owned by the narrator, repository, or other third parties, regardless of the owner of the intellectual property."
- Key point: Ownership of the physical recording carriers and of the intellectual property (copyright) can belong to different parties.

### E11

- Location: Ownership and Rights Management, Definitions of Ownership
- Quote: "Legal realities of ownership of physical property or intellectual property should not be used to alienate narrators from their stories."
- Key point: Legal ownership should not be used to alienate narrators from their own stories.

### E12

- Location: Ownership and Rights Management, Narrator Rights in an Archival Relationship
- Quote: "In some cases, a narrator’s position on the public’s access and use of their oral history may evolve, resulting in requests for the repository to increase or limit access or to remove the oral history from the collection altogether."
- Key point: A narrator's position on public access may change; they may ask to widen or limit access, or even to remove the whole interview.

### E13

- Location: Appraisal & Accessioning, Accessioning
- Quote: "Establish a numbering or naming system to uniquely identify interviews and their components to ensure that materials are unambiguously connected to any information about them."
- Key point: Set up a numbering or naming system so every interview and its components have a unique identifier and are unambiguously linked to the information about them.

### E14

- Location: Ownership and Rights Management, Documenting Provenance and Context of Interview
- Quote: "Language in contracts and agreements—including informed consent agreements, legal release forms, and deeds of gift—should clearly articulate the type of rights that have been discussed and transferred, and they should also include and reference examples of known and potential uses of oral history interviews."
- Key point: Informed consent agreements, release forms, and deeds of gift should state clearly which rights were discussed and transferred, and list known and potential uses.
