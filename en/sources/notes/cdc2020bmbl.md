# US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and National Institutes of Health 2020: Biosafety in Microbiological and Biomedical Laboratories, 6th Edition

- Source: `cdc2020bmbl`, [Original PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/labs/pdf/SF__19_308133-A_BMBL6_00-BOOK-WEB-final-3.pdf)
- Version: 6th edition, revised June 2020. A US government publication. The CDC website returned 403 to scripts, so the same PDF was downloaded through an Internet Archive snapshot (`web.archive.org/web/2024id_/...`). Locations are given as section names.

### E1

- Location: Foreword
- Quote: "We wish to emphasize that the sixth edition of BMBL remains an advisory document recommending best practices for the safe conduct of work in biomedical and clinical laboratories from a biosafety perspective. The BMBL is not intended to be a regulatory document although we recognize that some may use it in that way."
- Key point: The sixth edition of BMBL is an advisory document recommending best practices for safe work in biomedical and clinical laboratories; it is not intended as a regulatory document, although some use it that way.

### E2

- Location: Foreword
- Quote: "The core principle of this document is protocol-driven risk assessment; it is not possible for a single document to identify all of the possible combinations of risks and mitigations feasible in biomedical and clinical laboratories."
- Key point: The core principle of the book is protocol-driven risk assessment; no single document can list every combination of risks and mitigations.

### E3

- Location: Section II, Biological Risk Assessment
- Quote: "The biological risk assessment process is used to identify the hazardous characteristics of an infectious or potentially infectious agent or material, if known; the activities that can result in a person’s exposure to an agent; the likelihood that such exposure will cause an LAI; and the probable consequences of such an infection."
- Key point: Biological risk assessment identifies four things: the hazardous characteristics of the agent or material; the activities that can expose people to it; the likelihood that exposure causes a laboratory-acquired infection; and the possible consequences of infection.

### E4

- Location: Section II, Biological Risk Assessment
- Quote: "Risk assessment requires careful judgment and is an important responsibility for directors and principal investigators (PI) of microbiological and biomedical laboratories. Institutional leadership and oversight resources, such as Institutional Biosafety Committees (IBCs) or equivalent resources, animal care and use committees, biological safety professionals, occupational health staff, and laboratory animal veterinarians also share in this responsibility."
- Key point: Risk assessment requires careful judgment and is an important responsibility of laboratory directors and principal investigators; institutional biosafety committees, animal care committees, biosafety professionals, and others share this responsibility.

### E5

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Levels
- Quote: "The four primary Biosafety Levels (BSLs) for laboratories described in Section IV of BMBL consist of combinations of facility design features and safety equipment (primary and secondary barriers), facility practices and procedures, and personal protective equipment."
- Key point: Each of the four Biosafety Levels combines facility design and safety equipment (primary and secondary barriers), practices and procedures, and personal protective equipment.

### E6

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Level 1
- Quote: "Biosafety Level 1 (BSL-1) standard practices, safety equipment, and facility specifications are generally appropriate for undergraduate and secondary educational training and teaching laboratories and for other laboratories that work with defined and characterized strains of viable biological agents not known to consistently cause disease in healthy adult humans."
- Key point: BSL-1 suits teaching laboratories and laboratories working with well-characterized live strains not known to consistently cause disease in healthy adults.

### E7

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Level 2
- Quote: "Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2) standard practices, safety equipment, and facility specifications are applicable to laboratories in which work is performed using a broad-spectrum of biological agents and toxins that are associated with causing disease in humans of varying severity."
- Key point: BSL-2 suits laboratories working with a broad spectrum of agents and toxins that cause human disease of varying severity.

### E8

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Level 2
- Quote: "Work done with any human, animal, or plant-derived specimens (e.g., blood, body fluids, tissues, or primary cell lines), where the presence of a biological agent or toxin may be unknown, can often be safely conducted under conditions typically associated with BSL-2."
- Key point: Work with human, animal, or plant specimens (blood, body fluids, tissues, primary cell lines) whose agent or toxin content is unknown can often be done safely under BSL-2 conditions.

### E9

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Level 3
- Quote: "Biosafety Level 3 (BSL-3) standard practices, safety equipment, and facility specifications are applicable to laboratories in which work is performed using indigenous or exotic biological agents with a potential for respiratory transmission and those that may cause serious and potentially lethal infection."
- Key point: BSL-3 suits agents with potential for respiratory transmission that may cause serious or lethal infection.

### E10

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Level 4
- Quote: "Biosafety Level 4 (BSL-4) standard practices, safety equipment, and facility specifications are applicable primarily for laboratories working with dangerous and exotic biological agents that pose a high individual risk of life-threatening disease that may be transmitted via the aerosol route and for which there is no available vaccine or therapy."
- Key point: BSL-4 is mainly for dangerous exotic agents with a high individual risk of life-threatening disease, possible aerosol transmission, and no vaccine or treatment.

### E11

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Levels
- Quote: "It is important to note that the four Biosafety Levels described below are not to be confused and equated with Agent Risk Groups as described in the National Institutes of Health Guidelines for Research Involving Recombinant or Synthetic Nucleic Acid Molecules (NIH Guidelines). [...] However, just because a biological agent is listed as a Risk Group 3 agent, it does not mean the activities conducted with that biological agent must occur in a BSL-3 laboratory."
- Key point: Biosafety Levels must not be equated with the agent Risk Groups in the NIH Guidelines; an agent in Risk Group 3 does not automatically require work in a BSL-3 laboratory.

### E12

- Location: Section III, Biosafety Levels
- Quote: "For example, handling large volumes or high concentrations of a biological agent or toxin may require additional practices outlined in Sections IV and V of BMBL."
- Key point: Handling large volumes or high concentrations of an agent or toxin may require additional practices.

- Key point: Reading only selected sections will not prepare even an experienced laboratory worker to handle potentially infectious agents safely.
