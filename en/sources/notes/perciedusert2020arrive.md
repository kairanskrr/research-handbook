# Percie du Sert et al. 2020: The ARRIVE guidelines 2.0: Updated guidelines for reporting animal research

- Source: `perciedusert2020arrive`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000410)
- Version: PLoS Biology 18(7): e3000410, 2020, open access (CC0), read 2026-10-06. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The ARRIVE guidelines (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) were originally developed in 2010 to improve the reporting of animal research. They consist of a checklist of information to include in publications describing in vivo experiments to enable others to scrutinise the work adequately, evaluate its methodological rigour, and reproduce the methods and results."
- Key point: ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments), first published in 2010, is a checklist of information that papers describing in vivo animal experiments should include, so others can scrutinize them, assess methodological rigor, and reproduce the methods and results.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "the “ARRIVE Essential 10,” which constitutes the minimum requirement, and the “Recommended Set,” which describes the research context."
- Key point: Version 2.0 splits the items into two groups: the "ARRIVE Essential 10," the minimum requirement, and the "Recommended Set," which describes the research context.

### E3

- Location: Introducing ARRIVE 2.0
- Quote: "It includes details on the study design, the sample size, measures to reduce subjective bias, outcome measures, statistical methods, the animals, experimental procedures, and results."
- Key point: The Essential 10 cover study design, sample size, measures to reduce subjective bias, outcome measures, statistical methods, the animals, experimental procedures, and results.

### E4

- Location: Why good reporting is important
- Quote: "Factors that contribute to poor reproducibility include flawed study design and analysis, variability and inadequate validation of reagents and other biological materials, insufficient reporting of methodology and results, and barriers to accessing data."
- Key point: Factors behind poor reproducibility include flawed study design and analysis, variable and inadequately validated reagents and other biological materials, insufficient reporting of methods and results, and poor access to data.

### E5

- Location: Why good reporting is important
- Quote: "This includes details on randomisation (reported in only 30%–40% of publications), blinding (reported in only approximately 20% of publications), sample size justification (reported in less than 10% of publications)"
- Key point: Studies the authors cite show that among sampled animal research papers, only 30%-40% reported randomization, about 20% reported blinding, and fewer than 10% justified the sample size.

### E6

- Location: Why good reporting is important
- Quote: "Approval for experiments involving animals is generally based on a harm–benefit analysis, weighing the harms to the animals involved against the benefits of the research to society. If the research is not reported in enough detail, even when conducted rigorously, the benefits may not be realised, and the harm–benefit analysis and public trust in the research are undermined."
- Key point: Approval of animal experiments is generally based on a harm-benefit analysis; even rigorous research whose reporting lacks detail may fail to deliver its benefits, undermining that analysis and public trust.

### E7

- Location: Introducing ARRIVE 2.0 (second half)
- Quote: "While the primary focus of the guidelines has been on the reporting of animal studies, ARRIVE also has other applications earlier in the research process, including in the planning and design of in vivo experiments."
- Key point: ARRIVE is mainly for reporting, but it can also be used earlier, to plan and design in vivo experiments.

### E8

- Location: Why good reporting is important
- Quote: "They apply to any area of research using live animal species and are especially pertinent to describe comparative research in the laboratory or other formal test setting."
- Key point: ARRIVE applies to any research field using live animals and is especially suited to comparative research in the laboratory.

### E9

- Location: Introducing ARRIVE 2.0
- Quote: "This includes the ethical statement, declaration of interest, protocol registration, and data access, as well as more detailed information on the methodology such as animal housing, husbandry, care, and monitoring."
- Key point: The Recommended Set covers the ethical statement, declaration of interests, protocol registration, data access, and more detailed methods such as housing, husbandry, care, and monitoring.

### E10

- Location: Introducing ARRIVE 2.0
- Quote: "While reporting animal research in adherence to all 21 items of ARRIVE 2.0 represents best practice, the classification of the items into 2 groups is intended to facilitate the improved reporting of animal research by allowing an initial focus on the most critical issues." / "The first step should be to ensure compliance with the ARRIVE Essential 10 as a minimum requirement."
- Key point: Reporting all 21 items is best practice; the two groups exist to prioritize the most critical items, and the first step is at least the Essential 10.

### E11

- Location: Why good reporting is important
- Quote: "This is illustrated by a randomised controlled trial at PLOS ONE, designed to test the effect of requesting a completed ARRIVE checklist in the manuscript submission process. This single editorial intervention, which did not include further verification from journal staff, failed to improve the disclosure of information in published papers"
- Key point: A randomized controlled trial at PLOS ONE found that merely requiring a completed ARRIVE checklist at submission, without further editorial checks, did not make published papers more completely reported.
