# JAR: Peer Reviewing and Artistic Research (web page) and Peer Review Form 2023

- Source: `jar2023peerreview`, [peer review page](https://www.jar-online.net/en/peer-reviewing-and-artistic-research), [Peer Review Form 2023 (docx)](https://jar-online.net/sites/default/files/2023-03/JAR_Peer-Review_Form_2023.docx)
- Version: The Journal for Artistic Research (JAR) web page on peer review (read 2026-10-06) and the 2023 version of its form for reviewers. It represents this one journal's practice only. Locations give the web page subheading or the form's section number.

### E1

- Location: Web page, JAR's Peer Review process
- Quote: "JAR employs a 'single blind' review process. The reviewers are anonymous while the names of the authors are known. In artistic research a 'double blind' review process is not workable since artwork often carries the ‘signature’ of those who created it."
- Key point: JAR uses single-blind review: reviewers are anonymous, authors' names are known. Double-blind review is not workable in artistic research because artwork often carries the "signature" of its creators.

### E2

- Location: Web page, JAR's Peer Review process
- Quote: "We recommend that prospective authors also consult the form in order to understand how their submission is reviewed."
- Key point: JAR recommends that prospective authors also read the review form to understand how their submission will be reviewed.

### E3

- Location: Review form, Appendix A, Section 3
- Quote: "Whether or not the submission contains a description of the question, issue or problem that is explored, and if not, if such an omission matters; Whether or not the submission shows evidence of innovation in content, form or technique in relation to a genre of practice, and if not, if such an omission matters;"
- Key point: Reviewers check whether the submission describes the question, issue, or problem it explores; whether it shows innovation in content, form, or technique relative to a genre of practice; and, if something is missing, whether the omission matters.

### E4

- Location: Review form, Appendix A, Section 3
- Quote: "Whether or not the submission is contextualized and the context is referenced, which may include social, artistic and/or theoretical issues, and if not, if such an omission matters; Whether or not the submission provides new (kinds of) knowledge, interpretation, insights or experiences, and if not, if such an omission matters; Whether or not the submission’s methodology is adequate and thorough, and if not, if such an omission matters."
- Key point: They also check whether the submission is contextualized and its context referenced (social, artistic, and/or theoretical); whether it provides new knowledge, interpretations, insights, or experiences; whether its methods are appropriate and rigorous; and, if something is missing, whether that matters.

### E5

- Location: Review form, Appendix A, Section 3
- Quote: "Ultimately, a submission may successfully expose practice as research despite not adhering to conventional academic criteria for the assessment of research. If applicable, please state where the breaching of such criteria is detrimental to the submission."
- Key point: A submission may successfully expose practice as research without meeting conventional academic criteria; reviewers should state where breaching such criteria actually harms the submission.

### E6

- Location: Review form, Section 1
- Quote: "Ethical or legal concerns may e.g. arise from the practice with human or other animal subjects, the use of intellectual property or copyright, incidences of plagiarism or any other issue that you may find relevant."
- Key point: Ethical or legal concerns may arise from practice involving human or other animal subjects, the use of intellectual property or copyrighted material, plagiarism, and similar issues.

### E7

- Location: Review form, Appendix A, Section 2
- Quote: "JAR considers the form of a submission to be as important as its content."
- Key point: JAR considers the form of a submission to be as important as its content.

### E8

- Location: Review form, Appendix B
- Quote: "Pitfall: Seek to find all flaws in the submission, in part to show your expertise as a reviewer. Recommendation: Look for reasons to support a submission. Despite its flaws, does it point in new directions or expose promising insights?"
- Key point: One reviewing pitfall is hunting for every flaw to show your expertise; the recommendation is to look for reasons to support the submission: despite its flaws, does it point in new directions or expose promising insights?

### E9

- Location: Review form, Appendix B
- Quote: "Pitfall: Be less critical because you support the art that is presented.Recommendation: Even work you like can benefit from a critical reading."
- Key point: Another pitfall is being less critical because you like the work; the recommendation is that even work you like benefits from a critical reading.
