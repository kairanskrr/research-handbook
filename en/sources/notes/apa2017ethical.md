# American Psychological Association 2017: Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct

- Source: `apa2017ethical`, [original PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- Version: adopted in 2002, with amendments in 2010 and 2016, effective January 1, 2017. Locations give standard numbers.

### E1

- Location: Introduction and Applicability
- Quote: "Membership in the APA commits members and student affiliates to comply with the standards of the APA Ethics Code and to the rules and procedures used to enforce them."
- Key point: Members and student affiliates of the American Psychological Association (APA) must comply with the code.

### E2

- Location: Standard 8.01 Institutional Approval
- Quote: "When institutional approval is required, psychologists provide accurate information about their research proposals and obtain approval prior to conducting the research. They conduct the research in accordance with the approved research protocol."
- Key point: When institutional approval is required, submit accurate information about the research proposal and obtain approval before conducting the research, then follow the approved protocol.

### E3

- Location: Standard 8.02(a) Informed Consent to Research
- Quote: "(2) their right to decline to participate and to withdraw from the research once participation has begun"
- Key point: Informed consent must tell participants, among other things, that they may decline to participate and withdraw once participation has begun. The standard lists eight items in total (purpose, duration, risks, limits of confidentiality, incentives, contacts, and so on).

### E4

- Location: Standard 8.04(b)
- Quote: "When research participation is a course requirement or an opportunity for extra credit, the prospective participant is given the choice of equitable alternative activities."
- Key point: When research participation is a course requirement or a way to earn extra credit, students must be offered equitable alternative activities.

### E5

- Location: Standard 8.07(a) Deception in Research
- Quote: "Psychologists do not conduct a study involving deception unless they have determined that the use of deceptive techniques is justified by the study's significant prospective scientific, educational, or applied value and that effective nondeceptive alternative procedures are not feasible."
- Key point: Deception may be used in research only when it is justified by the study's significant value and no effective non-deceptive alternative is feasible.

### E6

- Location: Standard 8.07(c)
- Quote: "Psychologists explain any deception that is an integral feature of the design and conduct of an experiment to participants as early as is feasible, preferably at the conclusion of their participation, but no later than at the conclusion of the data collection, and permit participants to withdraw their data."
- Key point: If deception is used, explain it to participants as early as feasible (preferably at the end of their participation, and no later than the end of data collection), and allow them to withdraw their data.

### E7

- Location: Standard 8.08(a) Debriefing
- Quote: "Psychologists provide a prompt opportunity for participants to obtain appropriate information about the nature, results, and conclusions of the research, and they take reasonable steps to correct any misconceptions that participants may have"
- Key point: After the study, promptly give participants appropriate information about its nature, results, and conclusions, and correct any misconceptions they may have (debriefing).

### E8

- Location: Standard 8.12(b) Publication Credit
- Quote: "Principal authorship and other publication credits accurately reflect the relative scientific or professional contributions of the individuals involved, regardless of their relative status. Mere possession of an institutional position, such as department chair, does not justify authorship credit."
- Key point: Authorship order must reflect relative contributions, regardless of status. Merely holding a position such as department chair does not justify authorship.

### E9

- Location: Standard 8.12(c)
- Quote: "Except under exceptional circumstances, a student is listed as principal author on any multiple-authored article that is substantially based on the student's doctoral dissertation. Faculty advisors discuss publication credit with students as early as feasible"
- Key point: Except in exceptional circumstances, a student is the first author of any multi-author article substantially based on the student's dissertation; advisors should discuss authorship with students as early as possible.

### E10

- Location: Standard 8.14(a) Sharing Research Data for Verification
- Quote: "After research results are published, psychologists do not withhold the data on which their conclusions are based from other competent professionals who seek to verify the substantive claims through reanalysis"
- Key point: After publication, do not withhold the data behind your conclusions from competent professionals who seek to reanalyze them to verify the claims (provided participants' confidentiality can be protected).

### E11

- Location: Standard 9.02(b) Use of Assessments
- Quote: "Psychologists use assessment instruments whose validity and reliability have been established for use with members of the population tested."
- Key point: Use assessment instruments whose validity and reliability have been established for the population tested.

### E12

- Location: Standard 8.10(b) Reporting Research Results
- Quote: "If psychologists discover significant errors in their published data, they take reasonable steps to correct such errors in a correction, retraction, erratum, or other appropriate publication means."
- Key point: If you discover significant errors in your published data, correct them through a correction, retraction, or similar means.
