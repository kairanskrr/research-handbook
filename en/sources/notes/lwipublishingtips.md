# Legal Writing Institute: Publishing Tips

- Source: `lwipublishingtips`, [PDF](https://www.lwionline.org/sites/default/files/Publishing-Tips.pdf)
- Version: submission guide on the website of the Legal Writing Institute (a professional organization of US legal writing teachers), 8 pages, undated, describing itself as a "work in progress." It is about submitting to **US law reviews**. Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Deciding Which Journals to Submit to
- Quote: "There are hundreds of law reviews. Most law reviews are student run."
- Key point: There are hundreds of law reviews in the US, and most are student run.

### E2

- Location: Deciding Which Journals to Submit to
- Quote: "You can submit your article to multiple law reviews at the same time."
- Key point: You can submit an article to multiple law reviews at the same time.

### E3

- Location: Deciding Which Journals to Submit to
- Quote: "A few journals, e.g., the Journal of Legal Education, require exclusive submissions"
- Key point: A few journals (e.g., the Journal of Legal Education) require exclusive submissions.

### E4

- Location: Deciding Which Journals to Submit to
- Quote: "The vast majority of journals, however, do not require or indicate a preference for exclusive submissions."
- Key point: The vast majority of journals neither require nor indicate a preference for exclusive submissions.

### E5

- Location: Expediting Your Submission
- Quote: "In practice, many top journals may not read your manuscript until they get an "expedited review" request."
- Key point: In practice, many top journals may not read your manuscript until they get an "expedited review" request.

### E6

- Location: Expediting Your Submission
- Quote: "If you receive multiple publication offers, you should accept one offer and reject your other offer(s)."
- Key point: If you receive multiple publication offers, accept one and reject the others.

### E7

- Location: What to Include in a Cover Letter
- Quote: "SSRN downloads (if a lot), inclusion on SSRN Top Ten Downloads lists"
- Key point: Your cover letter can mention attention the article has received, such as SSRN downloads (if many) or inclusion on SSRN top-download lists.

### E8

- Location: The Publication Process
- Quote: "The time from when you accept a publication offer to when the article is published can range from a few months to a couple of years (in extreme cases)."
- Key point: The time from accepting an offer to publication ranges from a few months to, in extreme cases, a couple of years.

### E9

- Location: Expediting Your Submission
- Quote: "You should do your best to keep track of the law reviews that have rejected your article, so as to avoid expediting your article with a law review that has already rejected your article."
- Key point: Keep track of which law reviews have rejected your article so you do not send them expedite requests.

### E10

- Location: Using ExpressO and/or Scholastica
- Quote: "Expresso and Scholastica are the two most used online submission systems by which legal scholars can submit articles to law reviews"
- Key point: ExpressO and Scholastica are the two most used online systems for submitting to US law reviews.
