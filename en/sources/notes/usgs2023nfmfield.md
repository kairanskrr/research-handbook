# USGS 2023: Guidelines for Field-Measured Water-Quality Properties (National Field Manual for the Collection of Water-Quality Data, chapter A6.0)

- Source: `usgs2023nfmfield`, [original PDF](https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/09/a6.0/tm9a6.0.pdf), https://doi.org/10.3133/tm9A6.0
- Version: USGS Techniques and Methods, book 9, chapter A6.0, version 1.1 (July 17, 2023). Covers how to measure and record field properties such as water temperature, dissolved oxygen, specific conductance, and pH. A US government publication, free to read. Locations give the PDF page number (counted from the cover).

### E1

- Location: §1.0 Introduction, PDF p. 9
- Quote: "Determinations of physical or chemical properties that must be measured on site, as close as possible in time and space to the sampling of the medium."
- Key point: Field measurements are physical or chemical properties that must be determined on site, as close as possible in time and space to the sampling.

### E2

- Location: §2.1 Records, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "Electronic and (or) paper records must be established and maintained for each uniquely identified sampling location to" / "permanently document field activities, measurement readings, instrument calibration, and any other information needed to meet programmatic or regulatory requirements"
- Key point: Every uniquely identified sampling location must have electronic or paper records that permanently document field activities, readings, instrument calibration, and other information.

### E3

- Location: §2.1 Records, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "Much of the documentation process should be completed at the field site. Records should be checked independently by a field partner and (or) data administrator."
- Key point: Most documentation should be completed at the field site, and records should be checked independently by a field partner or data administrator.

### E4

- Location: §2.1 Records, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "A separate paper or electronic log book must be maintained for each field instrument or replaceable sensor where instrument repair, maintenance, and calibration history information is recorded. The log book travels with the instrument."
- Key point: Each field instrument or replaceable sensor must have its own log book recording repair, maintenance, and calibration history; the log book travels with the instrument.

### E5

- Location: §2.1 Records, PDF p. 10
- Quote: "Use of a pencil is not acceptable" / "Erroneous or mistaken entries should be crossed out with one line and initialed."
- Key point: Paper records must not be written in pencil; cross out mistaken entries with a single line and initial them instead of obliterating them.

### E6

- Location: Instrument notes after §2.1, PDF p. 12
- Quote: "Make field measurements only with properly calibrated instruments. Instruments are required to be calibrated before each field trip and checks are required at field sites for most instruments."
- Key point: Make measurements only with properly calibrated instruments; calibrate before every field trip, and check most instruments at the field site.

### E7

- Location: Instrument notes after §2.1, PDF p. 12
- Quote: "If the instrument or measurement is new to you, practice your measurement technique with a mentor who has current experience and is up to date with USGS field-method protocol and procedures."
- Key point: The first time you use an instrument or make a measurement, practice with an experienced mentor.

### E8

- Location: Performance, PDF p. 12
- Quote: "Check instrument precision and accuracy (variability and bias) periodically while at a field site"
- Key point: At the field site, periodically check instrument precision and accuracy (variability and bias).

### E9

- Location: Performance, PDF p. 12
- Quote: "or apply knowledge of the site and professional judgment to select the most representative of the final readings and document in field notes how the decision was made."
- Key point: When readings are unstable and you use your knowledge of the site to pick the most representative of the final readings, document in the field notes how the decision was made.
