# Linguistic Society of America 2019: LSA Ethics Statement

- Source: `lsa2019ethics`, [full text](https://www.lsadc.org/lsa_ethics_statement)
- Version: adopted July 2019, updated November 2023 together with the Code of Conduct for Events; Linguistic Society of America

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "This statement is not meant to replace formal research ethics oversight, nor is it meant to provide an exhaustive code of conduct."
- Key point: The statement does not replace formal research ethics oversight, nor is it an exhaustive code of conduct.

### E2

- Location: §3 Participants
- Quote: "Even if an institutional ethics committee deems that a research project is exempt from obtaining the committee’s approval, linguists should do everything in their power and in their capacities to ensure that their research follows the core principles of respect for persons"
- Key point: Even if an institutional ethics committee deems a project exempt from review, linguists should do everything they can to follow core principles such as respect for persons.

### E3

- Location: §3 Participants
- Quote: "Obtaining informed consent from participants and ensuring that any individual’s participation in research is completely voluntary at every stage."
- Key point: Obtain informed consent from participants and make sure participation is completely voluntary at every stage.

### E4

- Location: §3 Participants
- Quote: "Research participants have the right to control whether their actions are recorded in such a way that they can be connected with their personal identity."
- Key point: Participants have the right to decide whether recordings of what they say and do can be connected with their personal identity.

### E5

- Location: §3 Participants
- Quote: "Considering carefully whether some kind of compensation to participants is appropriate, be it remuneration for time and effort, formal recognition of their contribution to the creation or documentation of knowledge"
- Key point: Consider carefully what compensation is appropriate for participants: payment, formal recognition of their contribution, and so on.

### E6

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "In many communities, responsibility for linguistic and cultural knowledge is viewed as collective, so that individual community members are not in a position to consent to share materials with outsiders"
- Key point: In many communities, linguistic and cultural knowledge is viewed as collective, so individual members are not in a position to consent alone to sharing materials with outsiders.

### E7

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "linguists should comply with a community’s wishes regarding access, archiving, and distribution of results."
- Key point: Follow the community's wishes on access, archiving, and distribution of results.

### E8

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "community involvement sought from the earliest stages of project planning."
- Key point: Involve the community from the earliest stages of project planning.

### E9

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "returning, wherever feasible, data and knowledge gained to the community for stewardship. Not all data or knowledge collected with the aid of linguists or on behalf of linguistic scholarship belongs to linguists."
- Key point: Wherever feasible, return the data and knowledge gained to the community for stewardship; not all data collected with a linguist's help belong to the linguist.

### E10

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "Considering carefully the impact of what is shared through publication, specifically whether this could affect the communities’ social status or relationship with political structures."
- Key point: Before publishing, consider whether what you share could affect the community's social status or its relations with political institutions.

### E11

- Location: §4 Colleagues and the discipline
- Quote: "Reporting results with rigor and integrity (including not fabricating or falsifying data, engaging in p-hacking, etc.)."
- Key point: Report results with rigor and integrity: no fabricating or falsifying data, no p-hacking.

### E12

- Location: §4 Colleagues and the discipline
- Quote: "Citing carefully in one’s work the original sources of ideas, descriptions, and data."
- Key point: Cite carefully the original sources of ideas, descriptions, and data.

### E13

- Location: §4 Colleagues and the discipline
- Quote: "Promoting, wherever appropriate and possible, data sharing, replication, and reproducibility, as well as archiving in repositories that are accessible."
- Key point: Where appropriate and possible, promote data sharing, replication, and reproducibility, and archive in accessible repositories.

### E14

- Location: §4 Students
- Quote: "Properly acknowledging any and all contributions of students and other collaborators to our research"
- Key point: Properly acknowledge all contributions of students and other collaborators to the research.

### E15

- Location: §3 Communities and the public
- Quote: "Considering carefully the use of technologies that they may be involved in developing, including their potential role in social and political decision-making processes"
- Key point: Those involved in developing language technologies should consider their role in social and political decisions.

### E16

- Location: §3 Participants
- Quote: "Anonymous observations of public behavior, which often cannot involve full knowledge of the potential consequences, should be thoroughly vetted with research ethics boards"
- Key point: Anonymous observation of public behavior should be thoroughly vetted by an ethics committee.

### E17

- Location: bottom of page
- Quote: "Approved July of 2019; updated with revised LSA Code of Conduct for Events in November 2023"
- Key point: Version information: adopted July 2019, updated November 2023.

### E18

- Location: section "Communities and the public"
- Quote: "In all cases where the community has an investment in or is affected by the output of language research, the aims of an investigation should be clearly discussed with the community and community involvement sought from the earliest stages of project planning."
- Key point: Whenever the community has a stake in the research output, make the aims of the research clear to the community and involve it from the earliest stages of project planning.

### E19

- Location: section "Communities and the public"
- Quote: "Ensuring, if so desired by the community, that the community is an integral part of the process of determining the research question(s), the ways of collecting data, and the analysis and dissemination of data."
- Key point: If the community so wishes, make it part of deciding the research questions, how data are collected, and how they are analyzed and disseminated.

### E20

- Location: section "Communities and the public"
- Quote: "linguists must try to determine whether there are individuals who can legitimately represent the community in working out the terms of research."
- Key point: Linguists must try to find out whether anyone can legitimately represent the community in working out the terms of research.

### E21

- Location: section "Participants"
- Quote: "Considering carefully whether some kind of compensation to participants is appropriate, be it remuneration for time and effort, formal recognition of their contribution to the creation or documentation of knowledge, use of participants’ knowledge and expertise to benefit them or their communities, and so on."
- Key point: Consider what compensation is appropriate: payment, formal recognition of contributions, using their knowledge to benefit them or their community, and so on.

### E22

- Location: section "Communities and the public"
- Quote: "Making all reasonable efforts to preserve original irreplaceable data and documentary material and returning, wherever feasible, data and knowledge gained to the community for stewardship."
- Key point: Make every reasonable effort to preserve irreplaceable original data and documentary material, and wherever feasible return data and knowledge to the community for stewardship.
