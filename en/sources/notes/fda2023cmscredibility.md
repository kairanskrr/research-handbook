# US FDA 2023: Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions

- Source: `fda2023cmscredibility`, [original (Internet Archive snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/20260513124224/https://www.fda.gov/media/154985/download)
- Version: Final guidance issued by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on November 17, 2023, for industry and FDA staff. The FDA website returns 404 to scripts, so this excerpt was read from an Internet Archive snapshot of 2026-05-13. Locations give the section number and title.

### E1

- Location: note after the cover (legal status of the guidance)
- Quote: "It does not establish any rights for any person and is not binding on FDA or the public."
- Key point: The guidance represents FDA's current thinking and is not binding on FDA or the public.

### E2

- Location: I. Introduction
- Quote: "For the purposes of this guidance, CM&S refers to first principles-based (e.g., physics-based or mechanistic) computational models, and not statistical or data-driven (e.g., machine learning or artificial intelligence-based) models."
- Key point: In this guidance, computational modeling and simulation (CM&S) means first-principles-based (physics-based or mechanistic) models, not statistical or data-driven models (such as machine learning or artificial intelligence models).

### E3

- Location: II. Background, use category 1
- Quote: "Computational models that simulate medical devices can be used to generate information supporting device safety and/or effectiveness (e.g., in silico durability assessment of an implantable stent)."
- Key point: One use is "in silico device testing": simulating a device with a model to generate information on safety or effectiveness, e.g. in silico durability assessment of an implanted stent.

### E4

- Location: II. Background, use category 3
- Quote: "In silico clinical trials are an emerging application of CM&S where device safety and/or effectiveness is evaluated using a ‘virtual cohort’ of simulated patients with anatomical and physiological variability representing the indicated patient population."
- Key point: Another emerging use is "in silico clinical trials": evaluating a device with a "virtual cohort" of simulated patients who vary in anatomy and physiology.

### E5

- Location: IV. Definitions, Credibility
- Quote: "the trust, established through the collection of evidence, in the predictive capability of a computational model for a context of use"
- Key point: Credibility is defined as the trust, established through the collection of evidence, in the predictive capability of a computational model for a context of use.

### E6

- Location: IV. Definitions, Context of use (COU)
- Quote: "a statement that defines the specific role and scope of the computational model used to address the question of interest"
- Key point: The context of use is defined as a statement of the specific role and scope of the computational model in addressing the question of interest.

### E7

- Location: VI.A.(3) Model risk
- Quote: "Model risk is assessed because the level of credibility of a model should be commensurate to the risk associated with using the model to address the question of interest." / "which considers model risk as a combination of two factors, model influence and decision consequence."
- Key point: How credible a model needs to be depends on the risk of using it to answer the question; model risk combines two factors: how much the model influences the decision, and how severe the consequences of a wrong decision would be.

### E8

- Location: VI.A.(3) Model risk
- Quote: "Model influence will be dependent on whether other information (e.g., bench or animal test results) is also provided in the regulatory submission to address the question of interest."
- Key point: If bench or animal test results are also provided to address the same question, the model's influence is lower.

### E9

- Location: VI.B Credibility evidence, opening
- Quote: "Verification is focused on the software implementation of a numerical algorithm to solve the underlying mathematical model."
- Key point: Verification focuses on whether the numerical algorithm that solves the underlying mathematical model is correctly implemented in software.

### E10

- Location: VI.B, (2) Model calibration evidence
- Quote: "This is not validation evidence because it is not testing of the final model against data independent of model development" / "Calibration evidence is weak in comparison to validation evidence."
- Key point: Comparing against the same data used to tune the parameters is not validation evidence, because the final model is not tested against data independent of model development; calibration evidence is weaker than validation evidence.

### E11

- Location: VI.B, (3) Bench test validation results
- Quote: "that ‘bench testing’ is a broad term that encompasses in vitro, cadaveric and other types of nonclinical testing"
- Key point: "Bench testing" is a broad term covering in vitro, cadaveric, and other nonclinical testing.

### E12

- Location: III. Scope
- Quote: "We recommend that manufacturers seek feedback on their specific use of CM&S through the Q-submission process."
- Key point: FDA recommends that manufacturers seek feedback on their specific use of computational modeling and simulation through the Q-submission (pre-submission) process.

### E13

- Location: IV. Definitions, Validation
- Quote: "the process of determining the degree to which a model or a simulation is an accurate representation of the real world"
- Key point: Validation is defined as the process of determining the degree to which a model or simulation is an accurate representation of the real world.
