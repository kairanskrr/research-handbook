# Graham 2009: Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule

- Source: `graham2009makers`, [Original](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)
- Version: Essay on Paul Graham's personal website, July 2009, accessed 2026-10-07. The essay has no subheadings, so locations describe the topic of the paragraph.

### E1

- Location: Main text, the paragraph introducing the two kinds of schedule
- Quote: "They generally prefer to use time in units of half a day at least. You can't write or program well in units of an hour. That's barely enough time to get started."
- Key point: Makers such as programmers and writers generally prefer to use time in units of at least half a day; you can't write or program well in units of an hour, which is barely enough to get started.

### E2

- Location: Main text, the "meetings are a disaster" paragraph
- Quote: "A single meeting can blow a whole afternoon, by breaking it into two pieces each too small to do anything hard in."
- Key point: A single meeting can blow a whole afternoon by breaking it into two pieces, each too small to do anything hard in.

### E3

- Location: Main text, the "one meeting can sometimes affect a whole day" paragraph
- Quote: "If I know the afternoon is going to be broken up, I'm slightly less likely to start something ambitious in the morning."
- Key point: If you know the afternoon will be broken up, you are less likely to start something ambitious in the morning.

### E4

- Location: Main text, the "office hours" paragraph
- Quote: "These chunks of time are at the end of my working day, and I wrote a signup program that ensures all the appointments within a given set of office hours are clustered at the end. Because they come at the end of my day these meetings are never an interruption."
- Key point: The author clusters his meetings at the end of the working day, so they never interrupt work.

### E5

- Location: Ending
- Quote: "We know we have to have some number of meetings. All we ask from those on the manager's schedule is that they understand the cost."
- Key point: Some meetings are unavoidable; all makers ask is that people on the manager's schedule understand the cost.
