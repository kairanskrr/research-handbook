# Ofosu & Posner 2023: Pre-Analysis Plans: An Early Stocktaking

- Source: `ofosu2023preanalysis`, [original PDF (LSE repository)](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/112747/)
- Version: Perspectives on Politics 21(1): 174–190, 2023 (published online 2021), open access (CC BY 4.0). Locations give sections.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We analyze a representative sample of 195 PAPs registered on the Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) and American Economic Association (AEA) registration platforms to assess whether PAPs registered in the early days of pre-registration (2011 [...] 2016)"
- Key point: The authors analyze a representative sample of 195 pre-analysis plans (PAPs) registered on the Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) and American Economic Association (AEA) registries (registered 2011–2016).

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Graduate students are now taught that registering a PAP is a de rigeur part of undertaking their research projects."
- Key point: The authors observe that graduate students are now taught that registering a pre-analysis plan is a required part of a research project.

### E3

- Location: Empirical Approach
- Quote: "The overwhelming majority of the 195 PAPs we coded were from field (63%), survey (27%), or lab (4%) experiments; observational studies comprised just 4% of our sample."
- Key point: In the sample, 63% were field experiments, 27% survey experiments, and 4% lab experiments; observational studies made up only 4%.

### E4

- Location: Empirical Approach
- Quote: "The survey was conducted in 2018"
- Key point: The authors' separate survey of researchers was conducted in 2018.

### E5

- Location: Empirical Approach
- Quote: "was sent to all affiliated researchers in the EGAP and Innovations for Poverty Action (IPA) research networks (N=664). We received 155 responses"
- Key point: The survey was sent to all 664 researchers in the EGAP and Innovations for Poverty Action (IPA) networks; 155 responded.

### E6

- Location: Do PAPs Reduce the Scope for Fishing?
- Quote: "In 44% of PAPs, the number of pre-specified control variables was judged to be unclear"
- Key point: In 44% of pre-analysis plans, it was unclear which control variables would be included.

### E7

- Location: Do PAPs Reduce the Scope for Fishing?
- Quote: "However, in the fourteen instances in our sample where deviations occurred, the change was noted in only one case."
- Key point: In the 14 papers in the sample whose models deviated from the plan, only 1 noted the change.

### E8

- Location: Do PAPs Reduce the Scope for Fishing?
- Quote: "Twenty-five percent of PAPs specified how they would deal with missing values or attrition; 13% specified how they would deal with noncompliance"
- Key point: Only 25% of plans specified how to handle missing values or attrition, and 13% how to handle noncompliance.

### E9

- Location: Do PAPs Reduce the Scope for Post-Hoc Hypothesis Adjustment?
- Quote: "the median paper in our sample neglected to report 25% of the hypotheses that had been pre-specified in the PAP."
- Key point: The median paper in the sample failed to report 25% of the hypotheses pre-specified in its plan.

### E10

- Location: Do PAPs Reduce the Scope for Post-Hoc Hypothesis Adjustment?
- Quote: "But authors that presented results based on hypotheses that were not pre-registered failed to mention this in 82% of cases."
- Key point: Among papers reporting hypotheses that were not preregistered, 82% did not mention this.

### E11

- Location: Objections to PAPs: Too Time Consuming
- Quote: "Eighty-eight percent of researchers in our potential PAP users' survey reported devoting a week or more to writing the PAP for a typical project"
- Key point: In the 2018 survey, 88% of researchers said writing the pre-analysis plan for a typical project took a week or more.

### E12

- Location: Objections to PAPs: Too Time Consuming
- Quote: "when there is a limited window of opportunity to initiate an experiment before an election takes place or a new policy comes into force--such delays can make it impossible to undertake the project at all"
- Key point: When an experiment must start before an election or before a new policy takes effect, the delay from writing a plan can make the project impossible.

### E13

- Location: Objections to PAPs: Too Time Consuming
- Quote: "64% agreed with the statement that "it takes a considerable amount of time, but it is worth it.""
- Key point: 64% of surveyed researchers agreed that "it takes a considerable amount of time, but it is worth it."

### E14

- Location: Objections to PAPs: Limit Flexibility and Scope for New Discoveries
- Quote: "The more common approach--and the approach we advocate--is to freely undertake exploratory investigations that go beyond the PAP, clearly labeling the results of such investigations in the paper as coming from analyses that were not pre-specified, with an explanation provided for why they were added."
- Key point: The authors advocate freely running exploratory analyses beyond the plan, but clearly labeling in the paper which ones were not pre-specified and explaining why they were added.
