# QAA 2020: Characteristics Statement: Doctoral Degree

- Source: `qaa2020doctoral`, [original PDF](https://www.qaa.ac.uk/docs/qaa/quality-code/doctoral-degree-characteristics-statement-2020.pdf)
- Version: Doctoral Degree Characteristics Statement, third edition, February 2020, by the Quality Assurance Agency for Higher Education (QAA, UK). Describes the characteristics of UK doctoral degrees; it is a reference to help institutions design programs, not a mandatory requirement for UK higher education providers. Locations give sections and page numbers.

### E1

- Location: About this Statement, p. 1
- Quote: "Characteristics Statements sit alongside these resources to help providers develop courses and refine curricula but are not part of the regulated requirements for higher education providers in the UK."
- Key point: Characteristics Statements help providers design and refine courses but are not part of the regulated requirements for UK higher education providers.

### E2

- Location: 2.1 Category 1: Subject specialist doctorates, p. 6
- Quote: "Practical work, such as in the creative and performing arts, may well form part of a candidate's output. The form of artefacts and outputs of a practical nature, sometimes involving multimedia, relates to the candidate's subject area rather than to the form of degree programme."
- Key point: In fields such as the creative and performing arts, practical work can form part of the doctoral output; the form of artifacts and practical outputs depends on the discipline, not on the type of degree program.

### E3

- Location: 2.1 Category 1: Subject specialist doctorates, p. 6
- Quote: "In the final assessment, candidates are assessed on their thesis, portfolio, artefact or composition (the latter two normally, and the portfolio sometimes, are accompanied by a critical commentary on the work), and by an oral examination. A minimum of two examiners are usually present at the oral examination, usually one internal and a minimum of one external to the higher education provider."
- Key point: The final assessment covers the thesis, portfolio, artifact, or composition (the last two normally, and the portfolio sometimes, accompanied by a critical commentary), plus an oral examination, usually with at least two examiners, typically one internal and at least one external.

### E4

- Location: 2.1 Category 3: Professional and practice-based (or practitioner) doctorates, p. 8
- Quote: "For example, in the performing arts, the output involves a written component, which complements the practice-based element (this may be shorter than the traditional PhD thesis, and includes both reflection and context), and one or more other artefacts, such as a novel (for creative writing), a portfolio of work (for art and design), or one or more performance pieces (for theatre studies, dance or music)."
- Key point: In the performing arts, for example, the output includes a written component complementing the practice (possibly shorter than a traditional PhD thesis, with reflection and contextualization) and one or more works: a novel (creative writing), a portfolio (art and design), or one or more performances (drama, dance, music).

### E5

- Location: 4.2 Submission, p. 15
- Quote: "In creative arts, as already mentioned, the 'thesis' may take the form of an artefact and a commentary, as is appropriate for the field of study. Most doctoral examiners are looking for work that is original in nature, makes a contribution to knowledge in the subject and is of peer-reviewed publishable quality in that subject: this may refer to elements of the thesis rather than the complete work."
- Key point: In the creative arts, the "thesis" can be an artefact plus a commentary; most examiners look for work that is original, contributes to knowledge in the discipline, and is of a standard worthy of peer-reviewed publication (which may apply to parts of the thesis).

### E6

- Location: 4.3 Final assessment, p. 15
- Quote: "Whether a candidate is being examined on the basis of a 'traditional' thesis, portfolio, artefact(s), clinical practice or other output, the body of work presented demonstrates the research question and provides a critical evaluation of the extent to which it has been addressed."
- Key point: Whether the submission is a traditional thesis, a portfolio, an artefact, or another output, the body of work as a whole must demonstrate the research question and critically evaluate how far it has been answered.
