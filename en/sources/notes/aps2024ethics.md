# APS 2024: APS Ethics Standards

- Source: `aps2024ethics`, [original](https://www.aps.org/about/governance/policies-procedures/ethics-standards)
- Version: Revised and adopted by the Council of the American Physical Society (APS) on December 16, 2024, effective January 1, 2025. Web version read 2026-10-06. It replaces the 2019 19.1 Guidelines on Ethics and the earlier 02.2 Guidelines for Professional Conduct.

### E1

- Location: Introduction and Rationale, Integrity
- Quote: "Integrity is foundational to science. Simply put, physicists must tell the truth."
- Key point: Integrity is foundational to science; put simply, physicists must tell the truth.

### E2

- Location: I. Research Results, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Research results should be recorded and maintained in a form that allows subsequent use, analysis, review, and reproduction to the maximum extent possible."
- Key point: Research results should be recorded and kept in a form that lets others later use, analyze, review, and reproduce them as far as possible.

### E3

- Location: I. Research Results, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Research data and products (such as protocols, computer code, and study parameters) should be retained for a reasonable period in accordance with journal, funder, and legal mandates."
- Key point: Research data and products (protocols, computer code, study parameters, etc.) should be retained for a reasonable period in line with journal, funder, and legal mandates.

### E4

- Location: II. Authorship, Ethics Standard
- Quote: "Authorship should be limited to, and should not exclude, those who have made a significant contribution to the concept, design, execution, interpretation, or writeup of the research study. Authors should be able to identify and attest to their specific contribution to the work."
- Key point: Authorship should be limited to, and should not exclude, those who made a significant contribution to the concept, design, execution, interpretation, or writeup; every author should be able to identify and attest to their specific contribution.

### E5

- Location: II. Authorship, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "To avoid disputes over attribution of authorship, it may be helpful to decide at the start of writing the paper who will be credited as authors and in what order, who will be acknowledged as contributors, and to validate the decisions with all members of the research team."
- Key point: To avoid authorship disputes, it can help to decide at the start of writing who will be authors, in what order, and who will be acknowledged, and to confirm this with everyone on the team.

### E6

- Location: II. Authorship, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "APS recognizes authorship agreements for large collaborations, so long as authorship is determined through a transparent process."
- Key point: APS recognizes authorship agreements in large collaborations, as long as authorship is determined through a transparent process.

### E7

- Location: II. Authorship, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "All collaborations should establish processes for ensuring the accuracy and validity of reported results."
- Key point: All collaborations should set up processes that ensure the accuracy and validity of reported results.

### E8

- Location: III. Redundant Publication, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Preprint servers such as arXiv are a valuable means for sharing new work. Many journals do not consider the submission of a manuscript to a preprint server as redundant publication, but it is the responsibility of the author to follow the relevant journal policies. When the manuscript has been published in a refereed journal, a link to the final document should be added to the preprint."
- Key point: Preprint servers such as arXiv are a valuable way to share new work; many journals do not treat posting a preprint as redundant publication, but authors must check the journal's policy themselves; once the paper is published, the preprint should link to the published version.

### E9

- Location: Treatment of Colleagues, III. Treatment of Supervisees
- Quote: "Merely providing funding is not sufficient for authorship but should be noted in the acknowledgements."
- Key point: Merely providing funding does not qualify for authorship; it should be noted in the acknowledgements.

### E10

- Location: I. Research Results, Ethics Standard
- Quote: "Fabrication and falsification do not include honest errors in scientific practice or differences in opinion about interpretation."
- Key point: Honest errors and differences of opinion about interpretation are not fabrication or falsification.

### E11

- Location: I. Research Results, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Authors are obliged to provide prompt retractions or corrections of errors in published works."
- Key point: Authors must promptly retract or correct errors in published work.

### E12

- Location: III. Redundant Publication, Ethics Standard
- Quote: "Authors should avoid artificially increasing the number of publications by fragmenting the publication of research reports."
- Key point: Do not fragment one study into several papers to artificially inflate your publication count.

### E13

- Location: III. Redundant Publication, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Simultaneous submissions of the same paper to multiple journals are not allowed."
- Key point: The same paper may not be submitted to several journals at once.

### E14

- Location: Responsibility to the Profession, IV. Social Media
- Quote: "Claims of impacts to science, technology, and society beyond those that have been rigorously established can be misleading and should be avoided or clearly identified as speculation."
- Key point: When presenting work to the public, claims of impact beyond what has been rigorously established can mislead; either avoid them or clearly label them as speculation.

### E15

- Location: III. Redundant Publication, Explanations and Recommendations
- Quote: "Previous publication of an abstract or abbreviated presentation as part of a meeting does not usually preclude subsequent submission of a longer, more complete version for publication, but full disclosure of the existence of these materials should be included at the time of submission."
- Key point: Having published an abstract or short presentation at a meeting does not usually preclude submitting a longer, fuller paper later, but you must disclose these materials at submission.
