# EGAP Methods Guide: 10 Things You Need to Know About Cluster Randomization

- Source: `egapclusterrandomization`, [original web page](https://methods.egap.org/guides/data-collection/cluster-randomization_en.html)
- Version: Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) methods guide by Jake Bowers, Ashlea Rundlett, Jasper Cooper, and Anna Wilke; accessed October 2026. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1 What clustering is
- Quote: "Cluster randomized experiments allocate treatments to groups, but measure outcomes at the level of the individuals that compose the groups."
- Key point: Cluster randomized experiments allocate treatments to groups but measure outcomes at the level of the individuals in those groups.

### E2

- Location: §2 Why clustering can matter I: information reduction
- Quote: "If 200 observations arose from 10 clusters with 20 individuals within each cluster, where ICC = .5, such that 50% of the variation could be attributed to cluster-to-cluster differences (and not to differences within a cluster), Kish’s formula would suggest that we have an effective sample size of about 19 observations, instead of 200."
- Key point: With 200 observations from 10 clusters of 20 individuals and an intra-cluster correlation (ICC) of 0.5, the effective sample size is only about 19, not 200.

### E3

- Location: §3 What to do about information reduction
- Quote: "When we ignore the clustered-assignment, the standard error is too small: we are over-confident about the amount of information provided to us by the experiment."
- Key point: If the analysis ignores clustered assignment, the standard error is too small and overstates the information the experiment provides.
