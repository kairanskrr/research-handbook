# Greenberg & Buxton 2008: Usability Evaluation Considered Harmful (Some of the Time)

- Source: `greenberg2008usability`, [Author's website version](https://www.billbuxton.com/usabilityHarmful.pdf)
- Version: CHI 2008 proceedings, pp. 111–120, DOI 10.1145/1357054.1357074; PDF from the personal website of the second author, Bill Buxton, accessed 2026-10-08 and extracted with pdftotext. Locations are given as section names. This is an opinion paper; the CHI 2026 reviewer guide lists it as background reading for the debate on what makes a good CHI paper.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The choice of evaluation methodology – if any – must arise from and be appropriate for the actual problem or research question under consideration."
- Key point: The choice of evaluation method, if any, must arise from and fit the actual problem or research question.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "If done during early stage design, it can mute creative ideas that do not conform to current interface norms."
- Key point: Usability evaluation early in design can mute creative ideas that do not conform to current interface norms.

### E3

- Location: Usability Evaluation as Weak Science › Existence Proofs Instead of Risky Hypothesis Testing
- Quote: "In contrast, the existence proof as used in HCI is confirmative hypothesis testing, for the evaluator is seeking confirmatory evidence. This safe test produces only weak validations of an interface technique."
- Key point: The "existence proof" common in HCI (finding one setting where a new technique is better) seeks confirmatory evidence and gives only weak validation.

### E4

- Location: What To Do (fourth item)
- Quote: "For really novel innovations, existence proofs are likely appropriate. For mainstream systems or modest variations of established ideas, we should likely favor risky hypothesis testing."
- Key point: For truly novel innovations, existence proofs may be appropriate; for mainstream systems or modest variations of established ideas, favor risky hypothesis testing, that is, a serious attempt to refute the idea.

### E5

- Location: Usability Evaluation as Weak Science › Existence Proofs Instead of Risky Hypothesis Testing
- Quote: "Rigorous science also demands replication, and the same should be true in CHI"
- Key point: Rigorous science demands replication, and HCI should be no exception.

### E6

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Clearly, usability evaluation is valuable for many situations, as it often helps validate both research ideas and products at varying stages in its lifecycle." / "We believe that the community should continue to evaluate usability for many but not all interface development situations."
- Key point: The authors agree that usability evaluation is valuable in many situations and that the community should keep doing it in many, but not all, cases.

### E7

- Location: Related Work
- Quote: "Shumin Zhai challenged his position by saying that in spite" / "of the concerns, our evaluation methods are better than doing nothing at all"
- Key point: Shumin Zhai pushed back against the critique of evaluation: whatever the concerns, our evaluation methods are better than doing nothing at all.

### E8

- Location: What To Do
- Quote: "ACM UIST emphasizes novel systems, interaction techniques, and algorithms while evaluation is desired, it is not required if the design is inspiring and well argued"
- Key point: As the authors described it in 2008, UIST emphasizes novel systems, interaction techniques, and algorithms; evaluation is desired but not required if the design is inspiring and well argued.
