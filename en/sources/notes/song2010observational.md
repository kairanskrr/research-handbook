# Song & Chung 2010: Observational Studies: Cohort and Case-Control Studies

- Source: `song2010observational`, [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2998589/)
- Version: Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery 126(6): 2234-2242, 2010 (author manuscript on PMC). An introductory methods article for plastic surgeons, with examples from surgery.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "in an observational study, the investigator does not intervene and rather simply “observes”"
- Key point: In an observational study, the researcher does not intervene but simply "observes."

### E2

- Location: COHORT STUDIES
- Quote: "Cohort studies are particularly advantageous for examining rare exposures because subjects are selected by their exposure status. Additionally, the investigator can examine multiple outcomes simultaneously. Disadvantages include the need for a large sample size and the potentially long follow-up duration of the study design resulting in a costly endeavor."
- Key point: Cohort studies select people by exposure, are especially suited to studying rare exposures, and can look at several outcomes at once; their drawbacks are large sample sizes, possibly long follow-up, and high cost.

### E3

- Location: CASE-CONTROL STUDIES, Study Design; Table 4
- Quote: "Case-control studies are well suited to investigate rare outcomes or outcomes with a long latency period because subjects are selected from the outset by their outcome status." / "Rates of disease in exposed and unexposed individuals cannot be determined"
- Key point: Case-control studies select people by outcome from the start, which suits rare outcomes or outcomes with long latency; but they cannot give incidence rates for the exposed and unexposed groups.

### E4

- Location: COHORT STUDIES (distinction from case series)
- Quote: "Yet, unless a second comparative group serving as a control is present, these studies are defined as case-series."
- Key point: A study that follows only one group of people, without a second comparison group, is a case series, not a cohort study.

### E5

- Location: Methodological Issues, Selection of Subjects in Cohort Studies
- Quote: "A critical characteristic of subject selection is to have both the exposed and unexposed groups be selected from the same source population" / "Subjects who are not at risk for developing the outcome should be excluded from the study."
- Key point: The key to selecting people for a cohort study: the exposed and unexposed groups should come from the same source population, and people who cannot develop the outcome should be excluded.

### E6

- Location: Attrition Bias (Loss to follow-up)
- Quote: "Any systematic differences related to the outcome or exposure of risk factors between those who drop out and those who stay in the study must be examined, if possible, by comparing individuals who remain in the study and those who were loss to follow-up or dropped out."
- Key point: Wherever possible, compare those who remain with those lost to follow-up, to check for systematic differences in outcome or exposure.

### E7

- Location: Selection of Cases
- Quote: "In a case-control study, it is imperative that the investigator has explicitly defined inclusion and exclusion criteria prior to the selection of cases." / "most likely cases from the hospital sample will represent a more severe form of the disease than those in the community."
- Key point: Inclusion and exclusion criteria must be clearly defined before cases are selected; cases selected from hospitals tend to be more severe than cases in the community.

### E8

- Location: Selection of Controls
- Quote: "The precision of the findings may improve by having up to about three or four controls per case."
- Key point: Matching each case with about three or four controls may improve the precision of the results.

### E9

- Location: Bias in Case-Control Studies
- Quote: "A good study design will implement a standardized interview in a non-judgemental atmosphere with well-trained interviewers to reduce interviewer bias."
- Key point: Standardized interviews by trained interviewers in a nonjudgmental atmosphere can reduce interviewer bias.

### E10

- Location: COHORT STUDIES (retrospective cohort)
- Quote: "The primary disadvantage of this study design is the limited control the investigator has over data collection. The existing data may be incomplete, inaccurate, or inconsistently measured between subjects."
- Key point: The main drawback of a retrospective cohort is that the researcher has little control over data collection; existing data may be incomplete, inaccurate, or measured inconsistently across people.
