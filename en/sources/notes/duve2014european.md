# Duve 2014: European Legal History – Concepts, Methods, Challenges

- Source: `duve2014european`, [Original](https://www.lhlt.mpg.de/1009830/gplh_1_duve.pdf)
- Version: In Thomas Duve (ed.), Entanglements in Legal History: Conceptual Approaches (Global Perspectives on Legal History, vol. 1, open access, Max Planck Institute for European Legal History, Frankfurt, Germany, 2014, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 DE), pp. 29–66, by Thomas Duve (the institute's director). PDF downloaded from the institute's website on 2026-10-08. Locations give page numbers in the book. These are the author's arguments, addressed to research on European legal history.

### E1

- Location: p. 56
- Quote: "A first and crucial starting point is to gain more clarity about the problem of the formation of `Legal Spaces'." / "These have to be the result, not the constraint of our research."
- Key point: The "legal space" a study covers (its geographic scope) should be a result of the research, not a constraint set in advance.

### E2

- Location: pp. 56–57
- Quote: "A second starting point is that we need critical reflection on the concept of `law' that we are employing in order to structure our analysis." / "it is quite useless to compare legal traditions taking our own past's concepts and applying them to other areas, leading us to the conclusion that outside world is different."
- Key point: Reflect critically on the concept of "law" you use to structure the analysis; applying the concepts of your own past to other regions only shows that "things are different elsewhere," which is useless.

### E3

- Location: p. 60
- Quote: "writing legal history in a global perspective implies a certain change of habits, reflection on method and theory, and solid work on sources with the corresponding skills and knowledge in different areas."
- Key point: Writing legal history from a global perspective means changing habits, reflecting on method and theory, and doing solid work on sources with the corresponding skills and knowledge.
