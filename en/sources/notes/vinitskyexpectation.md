# Vinitsky: Expectation Setting

- Source: `vinitskyexpectation`, [original](https://www.eugenevinitsky.com/posts/expectation-setting)
- Version: Article on the author's home page, year to be confirmed, accessed 2026-10-05. An advisor's statement of expectations for students in his own group; locations give the web page subheading.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "It is quite hard to understand what a successful first year or two of graduate school looks like. This document is intended to help you set a standard so that you are neither too hard on yourself, stressed out about meeting some imaginary expectation that you could not possibly achieve, nor too easy on yourself because you misunderstand how much hard work is required."
- Key point: New students find it hard to know what doing well looks like; writing down expectations helps students neither stress over imagined standards nor slack off by misjudging the workload.

### E2

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "On the other hand, if you're struggling to hit these expectations we should have a chat to figure out what's going on and find a way to help you!"
- Key point: If you are struggling to meet the expectations, talk with your advisor to figure out what is going on and find a way forward together.

### E3

- Location: Year one / Meetings
- Quote: "I can’t read your mind and it’s not super-productive for me to sit and read your research notebook with you so it’d be good if you have your research in some kind of digestible form"
- Key point: Your advisor can't read your mind, and sitting through your research notebook together in a meeting is not productive; put your progress in a digestible form, such as a document sent to your advisor a day in advance.

### E4

- Location: Year Three
- Quote: "By this point you're setting the direction, not me."
- Key point: By the third year, the student, not the advisor, should mainly be setting the research direction.
