# Herremans 2020: How to be a successful PhD student?

- Source: `herremans2020successful`, [Original](https://medium.com/data-science/how-to-be-a-successful-phd-student-3f3a4eb48be8)
- Version: Medium (Towards Data Science) article, 2020-04-13; read via the r.jina.ai reader on 2026-10-05. The body is a set of unnumbered tips; locations give the opening words of the tip.

### E1

- Location: Tip "Avoid sending this all too common email..."
- Quote: "Always alert all co-authors on manuscripts well in advance before submitting them. While supervisors love receiving news that a new (draft) paper is ready, a better approach would be to share conference manuscripts at least two weeks in advance so that your senior co-authors can plan their time."
- Key point: Alert all co-authors well before submitting; share conference manuscripts at least two weeks in advance so senior co-authors can plan their time.

### E2

- Location: Tip "As a junior member of the team, pair up..."
- Quote: "Communicate very regularly with this person, and report on larger milestones with your supervisor, so as to maintain a proper research direction."
- Key point: A newcomer can pair up with a senior team member (ideally a postdoc), communicate with them very regularly, and report larger milestones to the supervisor.

### E3

- Location: Tip "Thoroughly proofread your manuscript..."
- Quote: "Thoroughly proofread your manuscript before sending it to a supervisor or co-author."
- Key point: Proofread your manuscript thoroughly before sending it to a supervisor or co-author, so they can spend their time on research feedback.

### E4

- Location: Tip "All authors listed on a manuscript..."
- Quote: "All authors listed on a manuscript need to have made a significant contribution to the work. This could be through coding, ideation, editing, advising, among others. Do not just include friends as co-authors. In addition, everyone needs to have read the final version of the manuscript before submitting."
- Key point: Every listed author must have made a significant contribution; don't add friends as co-authors, and everyone must read the final version before submission.

### E5

- Location: Tip "If your manuscript contains software code..."
- Quote: "Labs should be collaborative, not competitive."
- Key point: Labs should be collaborative, not competitive, for example by sharing code with labmates.

### E6

- Location: Tip "Own your project."
- Quote: "Own your project. When you meet with your supervisor, show up with all tasks completed, and ideally, the next step in mind or already started. [...] Do not just show up expecting your supervisor to give you small pre-chewed tasks."
- Key point: Own your project: show up to supervisor meetings with all tasks done and ideally the next step in mind or already started, instead of waiting for small pre-chewed tasks.
