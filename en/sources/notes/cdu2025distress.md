# Charles Darwin University 2025: Distress Protocol (template)

- Source: `cdu2025distress`, [Original](https://www.cdu.edu.au/files/2025-09/Distress%20Protocol%20Template.docx)
- Version: Distress protocol template published by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Charles Darwin University, Australia (Word file, uploaded September 2025); the template states it was originally adapted from a 2009 paper by Draucker, Martsolf, and Poole and from a template of the Aboriginal Health and Medical Research Council's ethics committee. Read 2026-10-10.

### E1

- Location: 1. How will the risk be identified by the researcher?
- Quote: "Is visibly upset or distressed (e.g., crying, teary, or any other noticeable change in behaviour ranging from relaxed to uncomfortable)."
- Key point: Watch for participants who are visibly upset or distressed, for example crying, teary, or shifting from relaxed to uncomfortable.

### E2

- Location: 2. Distress Response, a) Immediate action
- Quote: "Verbally acknowledge that the participant appears upset and ask them to confirm their capacity to continue." / "If the participant confirms they are triggered/upset, the researcher will offer to take a break from the interview." / "If the participant confirms they would like a break, the researcher will also remind the participant the interview can also cease." / "If the participant confirms their capacity to continue, the researcher will offer to not discuss the particular triggering subject."
- Key point: Say out loud that the participant seems upset and ask whether they can continue; if they confirm they are upset, offer a break; during the break, remind them they can also end the interview; if they can continue, offer to stop discussing the distressing topic.

### E3

- Location: 2. Distress Response, b) Referrals
- Quote: "Refer participants to appropriate community-based support services, mental health professionals, or crisis support lines."
- Key point: Refer participants to appropriate community support services, mental health professionals, or crisis lines.

### E4

- Location: 3. What will the researcher do if the interview is ended early?; 4. What will the researcher do if the interview stops at the normal stage?
- Quote: "The researcher will cease the interview and will follow up with the participant via email to check they are no longer distressed." / "Ask the participant at the end of the interview how they are feeling." / "Follow up with the participant later that day/following morning."
- Key point: If the interview ends early, follow up by email to check the participant is no longer distressed; if it ends normally, ask at the end how they are feeling and contact them again later that day or the next morning.

### E5

- Location: 6. Documentation
- Quote: "Document all instances of distress: Maintain confidential records of all instances of distress, including the date, time, nature of the distress, and the response provided."
- Key point: Keep a confidential record of every instance of distress: date, time, nature of the distress, and your response.

### E6

- Location: Background; source note at the end
- Quote: "This protocol outlines procedures for addressing distress experienced by participants, researchers, and/or community members involved in research projects." / "The template was initially developed from ‘Developing Distress Protocols for Research on Sensitive Topics’ by Claire Burke Draucker, Donna S. Martsolf, and Candice Poole"
- Key point: The template covers participants, researchers, and community members in research projects generally, not oral history specifically; it was initially adapted from Draucker, Martsolf, and Poole's "Developing Distress Protocols for Research on Sensitive Topics."
