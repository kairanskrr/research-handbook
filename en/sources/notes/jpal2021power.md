# J-PAL research resource: Power calculations

- Source: `jpal2021power`, [original web page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/power-calculations)
- Version: Research resource page of the Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab (J-PAL) by Sabhya Gupta and Sarah Kopper, updated August 2021; accessed October 2026. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Essentials
- Quote: "Minimum detectable effect (MDE): The smallest effect that, if true, has (1-β)% chance of producing an estimate that is statistically significant at the α% level (Bloom 1995). In other words, the MDE is the effect size below which we may not be able to distinguish that the effect is different from zero, even if it is."
- Key point: The minimum detectable effect (MDE) is the smallest effect that, if true, the study has a good chance of detecting as statistically significant; smaller effects may go undetected even if they exist.

### E2

- Location: Introduction: Relationship between power and power components
- Quote: "For example, if only half of the treatment group takes up the treatment, while no one in the control group does" [...] "the necessary sample size to detect a given effect size would be four times larger than with perfect compliance."
- Key point: If only half the treatment group takes up the treatment and no one in the control group does, detecting the same effect size requires four times the sample needed under perfect compliance.

### E3

- Location: Introduction: Relationship between power and power components
- Quote: "Attrition can also reduce power (or, conversely, increase the MDE) since the study sample is smaller than previously intended."
- Key point: Attrition also reduces power, because the actual sample is smaller than planned.

### E4

- Location: Clustered designs: Power equations for clustered designs
- Quote: "and an ICC of 0.07, the standard error of the treatment effect estimator (the square root term) doubles. As a result, the MDE doubles, while the required sample size quadruples."
- Key point: With 50 people per cluster and an intracluster correlation (ICC) of only 0.07, the standard error doubles, the MDE doubles, and the required sample size quadruples.

### E5

- Location: Practical tips: Gathering inputs
- Quote: "Note that published studies evaluating similar interventions will typically provide an optimistic estimate of a reasonable effect size."
- Key point: Published studies of similar interventions typically give an optimistic estimate of the effect size.

### E6

- Location: Practical tips: Gathering inputs
- Quote: "These standards are based on social psychology lab experiments among undergraduates, however, and have since been argued not to be realistic in real-world settings for some sectors and regions"
- Key point: Cohen's "small, medium, large" benchmarks come from social psychology lab experiments with undergraduates and have been argued to be unrealistic in real-world settings for some sectors and regions.

### E7

- Location: Practical tips: Initial calculations
- Quote: "Initial calculations should focus on whether the study may be feasible. After a proposed project passes a basic test of feasibility, power calculations can be refined and updated with new data."
- Key point: Initial power calculations should answer whether the study is feasible at all; once it passes that test, refine them with new data.

### E8

- Location: Practical tips: Gathering inputs
- Quote: "In clustered designs, while the addition of more clusters will in general increase power more than the addition of more units to existing clusters, the former is typically more expensive to do."
- Key point: In clustered designs, adding clusters generally increases power more than adding units to existing clusters, but it usually costs more.
