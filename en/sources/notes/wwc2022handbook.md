# What Works Clearinghouse 2022: What Works Clearinghouse Procedures and Standards Handbook, Version 5.0

- Source: `wwc2022handbook`, [original PDF](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/referenceresources/Final_WWC-HandbookVer5_0-0-508.pdf)
- Version: WWC 2022008REV, released August 2022 and revised December 2022. The review handbook of the What Works Clearinghouse (WWC), part of the Institute of Education Sciences (IES) at the US Department of Education; in the public domain. Locations give the chapter heading.

### E1

- Location: Chapter I (introduction to the WWC)
- Quote: "addresses the need for credible, succinct information by identifying existing research in education, assessing the quality of this research, and summarizing and disseminating the evidence from studies that meet WWC standards."
- Key point: The WWC identifies existing education research, assesses its quality, and summarizes and disseminates evidence from studies that meet WWC standards.

### E2

- Location: Phase 3: Synthesize and report results
- Quote: "Based on the WWC's confidence in the strength of the research design and execution of the design, eligible findings from the study will receive one of three research ratings: Meets WWC Standards Without Reservations, Meets WWC Standards With Reservations, or Does Not Meet WWC Standards."
- Key point: Based on the strength of the research design and the quality of its execution, findings receive one of three ratings: Meets WWC Standards Without Reservations, Meets WWC Standards With Reservations, or Does Not Meet WWC Standards.

### E3

- Location: Table 3. What Works Clearinghouse research ratings
- Quote: "This rating is reserved for findings based on a strong research design that is well-executed. This rating therefore provides the highest degree of confidence that the intervention caused the observed effect."
- Key point: The top rating is reserved for findings from a strong, well-executed design, giving the highest confidence that the intervention caused the observed effect.

### E4

- Location: Table 3
- Quote: "Because of natural limitations in research designs or because of circumstances around execution of a design, findings that receive this rating do not sufficiently rule out that something other than the intervention caused the observed effect."
- Key point: Findings rated "With Reservations" do not sufficiently rule out that something other than the intervention caused the effect, because of natural limitations of the design or circumstances in its execution.

### E5

- Location: Study eligibility (research design requirements)
- Quote: "The study must use one of four research designs: randomized controlled trial (RCT), including RCTs that estimate a complier average causal effect (CACE); quasiexperimental design (QED); regression discontinuity design (RDD); or single-case design (SCD)."
- Key point: To be reviewed by the WWC, a study must use one of four designs: randomized controlled trial (RCT), quasi-experimental design (QED), regression discontinuity design (RDD), or single-case design.

### E6

- Location: Baseline equivalence
- Quote: "When a study is a QED, study authors do not have this level of control over assignment to conditions. For this reason, authors of QEDs must demonstrate baseline equivalence of the intervention and comparison conditions on relevant covariates, such as outcome pretest, and if needed address differences between conditions by applying an acceptable statistical adjustment."
- Key point: Because quasi-experimental studies do not control assignment, authors must show that the intervention and comparison groups were equivalent before the intervention (for example, on pretest scores), adjusting for differences with acceptable statistical methods where needed.

### E7

- Location: Baseline equivalence
- Quote: "Therefore, the highest possible WWC research rating for QEDs, and for RCTs and RDDs with a high risk of bias due to compositional change, is Meets WWC Standards With Reservations."
- Key point: Quasi-experimental designs, and RCTs and RDDs with a high risk of bias from compositional change, can receive at most "Meets WWC Standards With Reservations."

### E8

- Location: Attrition
- Quote: "Attrition refers to the lack of outcome data for participants who were assigned to either the intervention or the comparison group."
- Key point: Attrition is the lack of outcome data for participants assigned to the intervention or comparison group.

### E9

- Location: Attrition
- Quote: "Participants are not available when outcome measurements were assessed. For example, a student might be absent the day that a posttest was given."
- Key point: One source of attrition: participants are not available when outcomes are measured, for example a student who is absent on the day of the posttest.

### E10

- Location: Attrition
- Quote: "High attrition could signal that the participants for whom outcomes are measured at the end of the study are too different from participants who were initially included in the study."
- Key point: High attrition can signal that the participants measured at the end are too different from those initially included in the study.

### E11

- Location: Confounding factors
- Quote: "Intervention or comparison group contains a single unit, such as teacher, classroom, school, or district (also known as an N = 1 confounding factor)."
- Key point: An intervention or comparison group that contains a single teacher, classroom, school, or district constitutes an "N = 1 confounding factor."

### E12

- Location: Confounding factors
- Quote: "However, if the study is conducted in one school but students within the school are assigned to intervention and comparison conditions, one school will not be a confounding factor because it is aligned with both study's conditions."
- Key point: If a study takes place in one school but students within it are assigned to intervention and comparison conditions, the school is not a confounding factor, because it is present in both groups.

### E13

- Location: Confounding factors
- Quote: "For instance, if an intervention group of students in grade 3 and a comparison group of students in grade 3 are observed during different academic years, then time could be a confounding factor."
- Key point: For example, if the intervention and comparison groups are third graders from different school years, time could be a confounding factor.

### E14

- Location: Major technical and procedural changes between versions 5.0 and 4.1
- Quote: "This procedure reflects the WWC's concern that potentially meaningful differences in effect sizes can be obtained from measures created by intervention developers or study authors, and that these measures may not be as informative to policymakers and practitioners as independent measures."
- Key point: The WWC is concerned that tests created by intervention developers or study authors can yield meaningfully different effect sizes and may be less useful to decision makers and practitioners than independent measures.

### E15

- Location: Chapter III
- Quote: "Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and quasi-experimental designs (QEDs) are the most common research designs reviewed by the WWC. Some of these studies assign individuals to intervention and comparison groups, whereas others assign clusters of individuals, such as entire schools of students."
- Key point: RCTs and quasi-experimental designs are the designs the WWC reviews most often; some assign individuals to groups, others assign clusters (such as whole schools).

### E16

- Location: WWC research ratings and the Standards for Excellence in Education Research
- Quote: "WWC research ratings focus primarily on the validity of education study findings for causal inferences."
- Key point: WWC ratings focus mainly on whether study findings support causal inference.
