# Scott, Wenderoth & Doherty 2020: Design-Based Research: A Methodology to Extend and Enrich Biology Education Research

- Source: `scott2020design`, [original (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8711803/), DOI [10.1187/cbe.19-11-0245](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-11-0245)
- Version: CBE-Life Sciences Education 19(3): es11, 2020; open access, full text on PubMed Central. Locations give subheadings in the text.

### E1

- Location: What Is Design-Based Research?
- Quote: "Consequently, investigators using design-based research approach educational inquiry much as an engineer develops a new product: First, the researchers identify a problem that needs to be addressed (e.g., a particular learning challenge that students face)."
- Key point: Design-based research approaches educational inquiry the way an engineer develops a new product: first identify a problem to solve, such as a particular learning difficulty students face.

### E2

- Location: What Is Design-Based Research?
- Quote: "Though we have portrayed four discrete phases to design-based research, there is often overlap of the phases as the research progresses (e.g., testing and evaluating can occur simultaneously)."
- Key point: The authors draw one cycle as four phases (design, test, evaluate, reflect), but in practice the phases often overlap; for example, testing and evaluating can happen at the same time.

### E3

- Location: What Is Design-Based Research?
- Quote: "3) Design-based research should generate design principles that guide the development and implementation of future instructional tools"
- Key point: The authors relay the third of Sandoval's (2014) "epistemic commitments": the research should produce design principles that guide the development and implementation of future instructional tools.

### E4

- Location: What Is Design-Based Research?
- Quote: "4) Design-based research should be enacted using extended, iterative teaching experiments in classrooms."
- Key point: The fourth commitment: the research should be carried out through extended, iterative teaching experiments in classrooms.

### E5

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "In design-based research, both researchers and instructors are engaged in all stages of the research from conception to reflection"
- Key point: In design-based research, researchers and instructors are both involved in every stage, from conception to reflection.

### E6

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "In this case, if the research questions being investigated produce generalizable results that have the potential to impact teaching broadly, then this is consistent with a design-based research approach"
- Key point: When the researcher is also the instructor, the work fits design-based research if the research questions produce generalizable results that could influence teaching broadly.

### E7

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "However, when the research questions are self-reflective about how a researcher/instructor can improve his or her own classroom practices, this aligns more closely with "action research,""
- Key point: If the research questions reflect on "how can I improve my own classroom," the work is closer to action research.

### E8

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "This involves collecting data from multiple sources as the research progresses, including how the instructional tools were implemented, aspects of the implementation process that failed to go as planned, and how the instructional tools or implementation process was modified."
- Key point: Collect data from multiple sources as the research proceeds, including how the tools were actually implemented, what did not go as planned, and how the tools or implementation were modified.

### E9

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "In experimental research, the intervention is fixed throughout the experimental period, with any revisions occurring only after the experiment has concluded."
- Key point: In experimental research, the intervention is fixed throughout the experiment, and revisions come only after it ends.

### E10

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "By contrast, design-based research takes a more flexible approach that allows instructional tools to be modified in situ as they are being implemented"
- Key point: Design-based research is more flexible and allows instructional tools to be modified in situ during implementation.

### E11

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "According to Sandoval (2014) , "Design research, as a means of uncovering causal processes, is oriented not to finding effects but to finding functions , to understanding how desired (and undesired) effects arise through interactions in a designed environment" (p. 30)."
- Key point: The authors quote Sandoval (2014): design research aims not at finding effects but at finding functions, understanding how desired (and undesired) effects arise in a designed environment.

### E12

- Location: Identifying the Learning Problem
- Quote: "The process of identifying a learning problem usually emerges from a researcher's own experiences (in or outside a classroom) or from previous research that has been described in the literature"
- Key point: The learning problem usually comes from the researcher's own experience or from previous research in the literature.

### E13

- Location: Identifying the Learning Problem
- Quote: "To remain true to Sandoval's first epistemic commitment, a learning problem must advance a theory of learning"
- Key point: Under Sandoval's first commitment, the chosen learning problem must advance a theory of learning.

### E14

- Location: Design-Based Research Cycle 1, Phase 1: Designing Instructional Tools
- Quote: "Indeed, this alignment between theory, the nature of the instructional tools, and the ways students are assessed is central to ensuring rigorous design-based research"
- Key point: Alignment between theory, the instructional tools, and the ways students are assessed is central to rigorous design-based research.

### E15

- Location: Phase 2: Testing the Instructional Tools
- Quote: "During this phase, if necessary, the design team may make adjustments to the tools as they are being used to account for these unanticipated conditions"
- Key point: During testing, the design team may adjust the tools as they are used, if necessary, to deal with unanticipated conditions.

### E16

- Location: Phase 3: Evaluating the Instructional Tools
- Quote: "This can be done using products produced by students (e.g., homework, lab reports), attitudinal gains measured with surveys, participation rates in activities, interview testimonials, classroom discourse practices, and formative assessment or exam data"
- Key point: Evaluation can use student products (homework, lab reports), attitude gains measured by surveys, participation rates, interviews, classroom discourse, and formative assessment or exam data.

### E17

- Location: Phase 3: Evaluating the Instructional Tools
- Quote: "Regardless of the source, evidence must be in a form that supports a systematic analysis that could be scrutinized by other researchers"
- Key point: Whatever its source, evidence must be in a form that supports systematic analysis that other researchers can scrutinize.

### E18

- Location: Phase 4: Reflecting on the Instructional Tools and Their Implementation
- Quote: "How was the theory underpinning the research advanced by the research endeavor (theoretical outcome)? Did the instructional tools support student learning about the learning problem (practical outcome)? What were the critical features of the design solution that supported student learning (design principles)?"
- Key point: Retrospective analysis answers three questions: How did the research advance the theory (theoretical outcome)? Did the tools support student learning (practical outcome)? Which critical design features made it work (design principles)?

### E19

- Location: Practical Outcome (Epistemic Commitment 2)
- Quote: "When we reviewed notes of the Spring 2017 implementation scheme, we saw that the pretest was due at the end of the first day of class after students had been exposed to ion flux ideas in class and in a reading/video assignment about ion flow, which may be one reason for the students' high performance on the pretest."
- Key point: Reviewing their notes on the Spring 2017 implementation, the authors found the pretest closed after the first day of class, when students had already met the content, which may partly explain the unusually high pretest scores.

### E20

- Location: Practical Outcome (Epistemic Commitment 2)
- Quote: "It also indicated we needed to close pretests before the first day of class for a more accurate measure of students' incoming ideas and the effectiveness of the instructional tools employed at the beginning of the unit."
- Key point: This told them to close the pretest before the first class in the next cycle.

### E21

- Location: Design Principles (Epistemic Commitment 3)
- Quote: "Therefore, design-based research should produce design principles that describe characteristics of learning environments that researchers and instructors can use to develop instructional tools specific to their local contexts"
- Key point: Design principles should describe features of learning environments that other researchers and instructors can use to develop tools suited to their own context.

### E22

- Location: Design Principles (Epistemic Commitment 3)
- Quote: "Consequently, the design principles must balance specificity with adaptability so they can be used broadly to inform instruction"
- Key point: Design principles must balance specificity with adaptability so they can inform teaching broadly.

### E23

- Location: Design Principles (Epistemic Commitment 3)
- Quote: "2) Instructional tools should explicitly articulate the principle of interest, with specific guidance on how that principle is applied in context."
- Key point: An example of a design principle from the authors' first cycle: instructional tools should state the principle of interest explicitly, with specific guidance on how to apply it in context.

### E24

- Location: Collaborations Can Be Difficult to Maintain
- Quote: "Moreover, the iterative nature of design-based research requires these collaborations to persist for an extended period of time. Attending to these challenges is an important part of forming the design team and identifying the different roles researchers and instructors will play in the research."
- Key point: The iterative nature of the work requires collaborations to last a long time; face these challenges when forming the team, and make the roles of researchers and instructors clear.

### E25

- Location: Design-Based Research Experiments Are Resource Intensive
- Quote: "Because of the large variety of data streams that could be collected in a design-based research experiment—and the resources required to collect and analyze them—it is critical that the research team identify a priori how specific data streams, and the methods of their analysis, will provide the evidence necessary to address the theoretical and practical objectives of the research"
- Key point: Because many kinds of data could be collected, at high cost, the team must decide in advance which data and which analysis methods will provide evidence for the theoretical and practical goals.

### E26

- Location: Concerns with Experimental Rigor
- Quote: "One way design-based researchers address this concern is by "specifying theoretically salient features of a learning environment design and mapping out how they are predicted to work together to produce desired outcomes" ( Sandoval, 2014 , p. 19)."
- Key point: One way to address concerns about rigor (citing Sandoval's 2014 "conjecture mapping" idea): specify the theoretically salient features of the design and map out how they are predicted to work together to produce the desired outcomes.

### E27

- Location: Concerns with Experimental Rigor
- Quote: "Through this process, researchers explicitly show before they begin the work how their theory of learning is embodied in the instructional tools to be tested, the specific data the tools will produce for analysis, and what outcomes will be taken as evidence for success."
- Key point: This way, before starting, researchers state how their theory of learning is embodied in the tools, what data the tools will produce for analysis, and what results would count as evidence of success.

### E28

- Location: Design-Based Research Experiments Are Resource Intensive
- Quote: "The focus of design-based research on studying learning ecologies to uncover mechanisms of learning requires that researchers collect multiple data streams through time, which often necessitates significant temporal and financial resources"
- Key point: Uncovering learning mechanisms requires collecting multiple data streams over time, often at considerable cost in time and money.

### E29

- Location: Design-Based Research Fosters Collaborations across Disciplines
- Quote: "It also gives instructors agency to make informed adjustments to the instructional tools during implementation that improve their practical applications while remaining true to the goals of the research"
- Key point: Making instructors part of the team lets them make informed adjustments during implementation that improve practical use while staying true to the research goals.

### E30

- Location: How Is Design-Based Research Different from an Experimental Approach?
- Quote: "In an experimental approach, the researcher is responsible for making all the decisions about how the experiment will be implemented and analyzed, while the instructor facilitates the experimental treatments."
- Key point: In experimental research, the researcher decides how the experiment is implemented and analyzed, and the instructor only delivers the treatments.

### E31

- Location: What Is Design-Based Research?
- Quote: "This leads to another research cycle of designing, testing, evaluating, and reflecting to refine the instructional tools in support of student learning."
- Key point: After one cycle comes another round of design, testing, evaluation, and reflection to refine the instructional tools.
