# Benchimol et al. 2015: The REporting of studies Conducted using Observational Routinely-collected health Data (RECORD) Statement

- Source: `benchimol2015record`, [PLOS Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001885) (CC BY)
- Version: PLoS Med 12(10): e1001885, 2015. Locations give subheadings or checklist item numbers.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Routinely collected health data are defined as data collected without specific a priori research questions developed prior to utilization for research"
- Key point: Routinely collected health data are data collected without specific a priori research questions and only later used for research.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "These data sources could include provision of broad resources for research (e.g., disease registries), clinical management (e.g., primary care databases), health system planning (e.g., health administrative data), documentation of clinical care (e.g., electronic health record data repositories), or epidemiological surveillance (e.g., cancer registries and public health reporting data)."
- Key point: Such data include disease registries, primary care databases, health administrative data, electronic health record repositories, cancer registries, and public health reporting data.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "RECORD was created as an extension to the STROBE statement to address reporting items specific to observational studies using routinely collected health data. RECORD consists of a checklist of 13 items"
- Key point: RECORD is an extension of STROBE for observational studies using routinely collected health data; its checklist has 13 items.

### E4

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Reporting deficiencies include inadequate or missing information concerning coding of exposures and outcomes as well as details of linkage rates of different data sources."
- Key point: Common reporting deficiencies in such studies: unclear coding of exposures and outcomes, and missing linkage rates between data sources.

### E5

- Location: RECORD item 7.1
- Quote: "A complete list of codes and algorithms used to classify exposures, outcomes, confounders, and effect modifiers should be provided."
- Key point: Provide a complete list of the codes and algorithms used to classify exposures, outcomes, confounders, and effect modifiers.

### E6

- Location: RECORD item 6.2
- Quote: "Any validation studies of the codes or algorithms used to select the population should be referenced."
- Key point: Reference any validation studies of the codes or algorithms used to select the population.

### E7

- Location: explanation for the Introduction section
- Quote: "Authors also should indicate whether their hypotheses were generated before or after data analysis."
- Key point: Authors should state whether their hypotheses were generated before or after data analysis.

### E8

- Location: RECORD item 19.1
- Quote: "Discuss the implications of using data that were not created or collected to answer the specific research question(s). Include discussion of misclassification bias, unmeasured confounding, missing data, and changing eligibility over time"
- Key point: The discussion should address the implications of using data not collected for this question, including misclassification bias, unmeasured confounding, missing data, and changing eligibility over time.

### E9

- Location: explanation for the Discussion (Limitations) section
- Quote: "Administrative data are particularly subject to errors in upcoding or opportunistic coding. For example, when hospital reimbursement is based on complexity of the case mix, hospitals may maximize reimbursement by liberally applying more complex disease codes to patient records"
- Key point: Administrative data are especially prone to upcoding: when hospital reimbursement depends on case-mix complexity, hospitals may liberally apply more complex disease codes to maximize reimbursement.

### E10

- Location: explanation for the Discussion (Limitations) section
- Quote: "In addition, changes in coding strategies may impact validity or consistency of data."
- Key point: Changes in coding practice can affect the validity and consistency of the data.

### E11

- Location: RECORD item 12.3
- Quote: "State whether the study included person-level, institutional-level, or other data linkage across two or more databases. Linkage techniques and methods used to evaluate linkage quality should be provided."
- Key point: If two or more databases were linked, state at what level (person, institution, or other), the linkage method, and how linkage quality was evaluated.

### E12

- Location: RECORD item 22.1
- Quote: "Authors should provide information on how to access any supplemental information such as the study protocol, raw data, or programming code."
- Key point: Explain how to access supplemental material such as the study protocol, raw data, or programming code.

### E13

- Location: explanation for the Methods (Setting) section
- Quote: "information should be provided to allow the reader to understand the content and validity of the database and the original reasons why the data were collected."
- Key point: Give readers enough to understand the content and validity of the database and why the data were originally collected.

### E14

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "the RECORD guidelines are not designed to recommend the methods used to conduct research, but rather to improve its reporting"
- Key point: RECORD does not prescribe how to conduct research; it only aims to improve reporting.

### E15

- Location: explanation for the Methods (Participants) section
- Quote: "Although the risk of misclassification is amplified in studies using databases containing large populations, such studies offer an opportunity to study rare or uncommon diseases"
- Key point: Databases covering large populations amplify the risk of misclassification, but they also make it possible to study rare diseases.

### E16

- Location: RECORD item 6.3
- Quote: "If the study involved linkage of databases, consider use of a flow diagram or other graphical display to demonstrate the data linkage process, including the number of individuals with linked data at each stage."
- Key point: If databases were linked, consider a flow diagram showing the linkage process and the number of people with linked data at each stage.

### E17

- Location: RECORD items 12.1 and 12.2
- Quote: "Authors should describe the extent to which the investigators had access to the database used to create the study population. RECORD ITEM 12.2: Authors should provide information on the data cleaning methods used in the study."
- Key point: Describe how far the investigators had access to the database used to create the study population, and describe the data cleaning methods.

### E18

- Location: RECORD items 12.1 and 12.2, Explanation
- Quote: "Errors can occur if data analysts unfamiliar with the nuances of cohort creation or study aims create the study cohorts."
- Key point: Errors can occur if data analysts unfamiliar with the nuances of cohort creation or the study aims create the cohorts.

### E19

- Location: RECORD item 13.1
- Quote: "Describe in detail the selection of the persons included in the study (i.e., study population selection), including filtering based on data quality, data availability and linkage."
- Key point: Describe in detail how the study population was selected, including filtering based on data quality, data availability, and linkage.
