# American Historical Association 2021: Guidelines for Acknowledgment of Collaborators

- Source: `aha2021collaborators`, [original (Internet Archive snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/20240601010410/https://www.historians.org/jobs-and-professional-development/statements-standards-and-guidelines-of-the-discipline/guidelines-for-acknowledgment-of-collaborators)
- Version: 2021 guidelines of the American Historical Association (AHA). The website returned 403 to scripted access, so these excerpts come from the Internet Archive snapshot of 2024-06-01 (old URL `historians.org/jobs-and-professional-development/statements-standards-and-guidelines-of-the-discipline/guidelines-for-acknowledgment-of-collaborators`). The guidelines are a single paragraph.

### E1

- Location: main text
- Quote: "At a time when more historians are working collaboratively to produce monographs, scholarly editions, articles, exhibitions, websites, textbooks and pedagogical materials, and other genres of scholarship, the AHA affirms the importance of fully and publicly recognizing and crediting the work of all contributors."
- Key point: More and more historians collaborate on monographs, scholarly editions, articles, exhibitions, websites, and textbooks; the AHA stresses full and public acknowledgment of every contributor.

### E2

- Location: main text
- Quote: "For example, depending on the project, it might be appropriate for contributors to be listed in an order corresponding to their degree of contribution, or for them to be listed in alphabetical order."
- Key point: There is no single rule for the order of names; it depends on the project: contributors may be listed by degree of contribution or alphabetically.
