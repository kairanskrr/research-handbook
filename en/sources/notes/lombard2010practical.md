# Lombard, Snyder-Duch & Bracken 2010: Practical Resources for Assessing and Reporting Intercoder Reliability in Content Analysis Research Projects

- Source: `lombard2010practical`, [web page](http://matthewlombard.com/reliability/)
- Version: the authors' public online guide, last updated June 1, 2010; a companion resource to the three authors' 2002 paper in Human Communication Research (Lombard, Snyder-Duch & Bracken 2002), including a revised version of that paper's guidelines. Locations give the section numbers of the web page.

### E1

- Location: 1. What is this site about, who created it, and why?
- Quote: "reported a study that characterized the assessment and reporting of reliability in 200 studies in the mass communication literature between 1994 and 1998." [...] "we found that only 69% (n = 137) of the articles contained any report of intercoder reliability"
- Key point: The authors' 2002 study examined 200 studies in the mass communication literature from 1994 to 1998; only 69% (137) reported intercoder reliability.

### E2

- Location: 2. What is intercoder reliability?
- Quote: "Intercoder reliability is the widely used term for the extent to which independent coders evaluate a characteristic of a message or artifact and reach the same conclusion."
- Key point: Intercoder reliability is the extent to which independent coders evaluating a characteristic of a message reach the same conclusion.

### E3

- Location: 3. Why should content analysis researchers care about intercoder reliability?
- Quote: "It is widely acknowledged that intercoder reliability is a critical component of content analysis, and that although it does not insure validity, when it is not established properly, the data and interpretations of the data can not be considered valid."
- Key point: Intercoder reliability is a critical component of content analysis; it does not ensure validity, but without properly established reliability the data and interpretations cannot be considered valid.

### E4

- Location: 4. How should content analysis researchers properly assess and report intercoder reliability?, step 1
- Quote: "If percent agreement is selected (and this is not recommended), use a second index that accounts for agreement expected by chance."
- Key point: Percent agreement is not recommended; if you use it, add a second index that accounts for agreement expected by chance.

### E5

- Location: Section 4, step 1
- Quote: "Note that the selection of index or indices must precede data collection and evaluation of intercoder reliability."
- Key point: The reliability index must be chosen before data collection and reliability assessment.

### E6

- Location: Section 4, step 3
- Quote: "Coefficients of .90 or greater are nearly always acceptable, .80 or greater is acceptable in most situations, and .70 may be appropriate in some exploratory studies for some indices."
- Key point: Coefficients of .90 or greater are nearly always acceptable, .80 or greater in most situations, and .70 may be appropriate for some indices in some exploratory studies.

### E7

- Location: Section 4, step 5
- Quote: "Coding must be done independently and without consultation or guidance; if possible, the researcher should not be a coder."
- Key point: Coding for reliability tests must be done independently, without consultation or guidance; if possible, the researcher should not be a coder.

### E8

- Location: Section 4, step 5
- Quote: "The size of this sample can vary depending on the project but a good rule of thumb is 30 units"
- Key point: A good rule of thumb for the pilot test sample is 30 units.

### E9

- Location: Section 4, step 6
- Quote: "it should not be less than 50 units or 10% of the full sample, and it rarely will need to be greater than 300 units"
- Key point: The formal reliability sample should be no less than 50 units or 10% of the full sample, and rarely needs to exceed 300 units.

### E10

- Location: Section 4, step 8
- Quote: "Provide this minimum information: The size of and the method used to create the reliability sample, along with a justification of that method." [...] "The intercoder reliability level for each variable, for each index selected." [...] "How disagreements in the reliability coding were resolved in the full sample."
- Key point: At a minimum, report the size of the reliability sample, how it was drawn and why; the reliability value for each variable and each index; how disagreements were resolved; and so on.

### E11

- Location: Section 4
- Quote: "Do NOT use only percent agreement to calculate reliability."
- Key point: Do not use only percent agreement to calculate reliability.

### E12

- Location: Section 4
- Quote: "Do NOT use overall reliability across variables, rather than reliability levels for each variable, as a standard for evaluating the reliability of the instrument."
- Key point: Do not evaluate the coding instrument with overall reliability across all variables; report reliability for each variable.

### E13

- Location: Section 4
- Quote: "Do NOT use Cronbach's alpha, Pearson's r, or other correlation-based indices that standardize coder values and only measure covariation."
- Key point: Do not use correlation-based indices such as Cronbach's alpha or Pearson's r; they only measure whether coders' scores covary, not whether the codes are the same.

### E14

- Location: 5. Which measure(s) of intercoder reliability should researchers use?
- Quote: "there is no consensus on a single, "best" one."
- Key point: There is no consensus on a single "best" reliability index.

### E15

- Location: Section 5
- Quote: "Krippendorff's alpha is well regarded and very flexible (it can be used with multiple coders, accounts for different sample sizes and missing data, and can be used for ordinal, interval and ratio level variables)"
- Key point: Krippendorff's alpha is well regarded and very flexible: it works with multiple coders, handles different sample sizes and missing data, and can be used with ordinal and numeric variables.

### E16

- Location: Section 4, step 4
- Quote: "assess reliability informally with a small number of units which ideally are not part of the full sample (or census) of units to be coded, and refine the instrument and coding instructions until the informal assessment suggests an adequate level of agreement."
- Key point: When training coders, assess reliability informally on a small number of units (ideally not part of the full sample), refining the codebook and instructions repeatedly until agreement is adequate.
