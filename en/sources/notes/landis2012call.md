# Landis et al. 2012: A call for transparent reporting to optimize the predictive value of preclinical research

- Source: `landis2012call`, [full text (PubMed Central author manuscript)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3511845/)
- Version: Nature 490(7419):187–191, 2012. Recommendations from a June 2012 workshop of the US National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS). Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The main workshop recommendation is that at a minimum studies should report on sample-size estimation, whether and how animals were randomized, whether investigators were blind to the treatment, and the handling of data."
- Key point: The main workshop recommendation: at a minimum, animal studies should report how sample size was estimated, whether and how animals were randomized, whether investigators were blinded to treatment, and how the data were handled.

### E2

- Location: Widespread deficiencies in methods reporting
- Quote: "A review of 100 articles published in Cancer Research in 2010 revealed that only 28% of papers reported that animals were randomly allocated to treatment groups, just 2% of papers reported that observers were blinded to treatment, and none stated the methods used to determine the number of animals per group"
- Key point: A review of 100 articles published in Cancer Research in 2010: 28% reported random allocation, 2% reported blinded observers, and none explained how the number of animals per group was determined.

### E3

- Location: Widespread deficiencies in methods reporting
- Quote: "Problems related to inadequate study design surfaced early in the stroke research community, as investigators tried to understand why multiple clinical trials based on positive results in animal studies ultimately failed. Part of the problem is, of course, that no animal model can fully reproduce all the features of human stroke."
- Key point: The stroke field noticed the problem early, when multiple clinical trials based on positive animal results failed. One reason is that no animal model fully reproduces human stroke.

### E4

- Location: Widespread deficiencies in methods reporting
- Quote: "publications that included information on randomization, concealment of group allocation, or blinded assessment of outcomes reported significantly smaller effect sizes of NXY-059 in comparison to studies lacking this information"
- Key point: In a systematic review and meta-analysis of animal stroke studies of the drug NXY-059, publications that reported randomization, allocation concealment, or blinded outcome assessment reported significantly smaller effect sizes than those that did not.

### E5

- Location: Widespread deficiencies in methods reporting
- Quote: "In certain cases, a series of poorly designed studies, obscured by deficient reporting, may, in aggregate, serve erroneously as the scientific rationale for large, expensive and ultimately unsuccessful clinical trials. Such trials may unnecessarily expose patients to potentially harmful agents"
- Key point: A series of poorly designed studies, obscured by deficient reporting, may in aggregate serve erroneously as the rationale for large, expensive clinical trials that fail, needlessly exposing patients to potentially harmful drugs.

### E6

- Location: Box 1, Blinding
- Quote: "Allocation concealment: the investigator should be unaware of the group to which the next animal taken from a cage will be allocated."
- Key point: Allocation concealment: the investigator should not know which group the next animal taken from a cage will be allocated to.

### E7

- Location: Randomization and blinding
- Quote: "Attention to these details will avoid mistaking batch effects for treatment effects (for example, dividing samples from a large study into multiple lots, which are then processed separately)."
- Key point: Sample processing and data collection should also be randomized or blocked, so that batch effects are not mistaken for treatment effects.

### E8

- Location: Sample-size estimation
- Quote: "It is unethical, however, to perform underpowered experiments with insufficient numbers of animals that have little prospect of detecting meaningful differences between groups."
- Key point: Underpowered experiments with too few animals to have much prospect of detecting meaningful differences are also unethical.

### E9

- Location: Interim data analysis
- Quote: "If the results are statistically significant in favour of the working hypothesis, the study is terminated and a paper is written. If the results look ‘promising’ but are not statistically significant, additional data are collected." / "Therefore, sample size and rules for stopping data collection should be defined in advance and properly reported."
- Key point: Stopping when results are significant and adding data when they are not produces many false positives; the sample size and the rule for stopping data collection should be fixed in advance and reported.

### E10

- Location: Ad hoc exclusion of data
- Quote: "For example, a treatment may appear effective if it kills off the weakest or most severely affected animals whose fates are then not reported."
- Key point: Differential attrition across groups causes bias: a treatment that kills off the weakest animals, unreported, may appear effective.

### E11

- Location: Retrospective primary end-point selection
- Quote: "False-positive conclusions arising from such practices can be avoided by specifying a primary end point before the study is undertaken, the time(s) at which the end point will be assessed, and the method(s) of analysis."
- Key point: Specifying the primary end point, the time of assessment, and the analysis method before the study starts avoids false positives from choosing end points after the fact.

### E12

- Location: An important note about exploratory experiments
- Quote: "Such exploratory testing is frequently conducted using a small sample size, does not have a primary outcome and is often unblinded. However, because such experiments are likely to be subject to many of the limitations described above, they should be viewed as hypothesis-generating experiments and interpreted as such."
- Key point: Early exploratory experiments often have small samples, no primary outcome, and no blinding; they should be interpreted only as hypothesis-generating.

### E13

- Location: The path to implementation
- Quote: "There is a widely accepted belief that the scientific community, promotions committees, funding agencies and journals favour positive outcomes, an impression that can lead to bias"
- Key point: There is a widely accepted belief that the scientific community, promotions committees, funding agencies, and journals favor positive outcomes, which leads to bias.

### E14

- Location: The path to implementation
- Quote: "although few animal studies report on randomization, blinding or sample-size determination, most describe compliance with animal regulations, which is required by journals"
- Key point: Few animal studies report randomization, blinding, or sample-size determination, yet most describe compliance with animal regulations, because journals require it.

### E15

- Location: Small effect sizes
- Quote: "reporting how often the particular experiment was performed as a means to control for a general tendency to publish only the best results would strengthen the validity of experimental results."
- Key point: Reporting how often a particular experiment was performed counters the tendency to publish only the best results.

### E16

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The US National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke convened major stakeholders in June 2012 to discuss how to improve the methodological reporting of animal studies in grant applications and publications."
- Key point: The recommendations come from a workshop the US National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke convened in June 2012 to discuss improving the reporting of animal research methods in grant applications and papers.

### E17

- Location: An important note about exploratory experiments
- Quote: "Potential discoveries arising from the exploratory phase of the research should be supported by follow-up, hypothesis-testing experiments that take into consideration and adequately report on the core standards detailed above"
- Key point: Discoveries from the exploratory phase should be supported by follow-up hypothesis-testing experiments designed and reported according to the core standards.
