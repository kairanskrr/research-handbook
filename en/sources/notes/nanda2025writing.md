# Nanda 2025: Highly Opinionated Advice on How to Write ML Papers

- Source: `nanda2025writing`, [original](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers)
- Version: AI Alignment Forum article, 2025. Web page; locations give section headings. The original is about machine learning papers; this chapter takes only the parts that hold across fields.

### E1

- Location: TL;DR
- Quote: "A narrative is fundamentally about a contribution to our body of knowledge: one to three specific novel claims that fit within a cohesive theme"
- Key point: A paper's narrative is its contribution to knowledge: one to three specific, novel claims that fit within a cohesive theme.

### E2

- Location: The Essence of a Paper / Crafting a Narrative
- Quote: "Readers will rarely take away more than a few sentences of content. Choose those sentences carefully."
- Key point: Readers rarely take away more than a few sentences, so compress your research into a few claims and deliberately drop the remaining details or move them to an appendix.

### E3

- Location: The Essence of a Paper / Crafting a Narrative
- Quote: "Generally, stronger statements make for more interesting papers, but require higher standards of evidence - resist the temptation to overclaim for clicks!"
- Key point: Stronger claims make more interesting papers but require higher standards of evidence; match the strength of your statement to your evidence (existence, generality, hedged, scoped) and don't overclaim.

### E4

- Location: The Essence of a Paper / Rigorous Supporting Evidence
- Quote: "Assume you've made a mistake - what is that mistake?"
- Key point: Actively attack your own narrative: assume you've made a mistake and find it; don't ignore results that contradict your narrative, and discuss limitations fully.

### E5

- Location: The Writing Process: Compress then Iteratively Expand
- Quote: "A key challenge in paper writing is the illusion of transparency - you have spent months steeped in the context of this research project."
- Key point: You know the project's context so well that you easily assume readers do too (the "illusion of transparency"); get feedback from as many people as you can.

### E6

- Location: TL;DR
- Quote: "Write iteratively: Write abstract -> bullet point outline -> introduction -> first full draft -> repeat"
- Key point: Write iteratively: abstract -> bullet-point outline -> introduction -> full first draft -> repeat, reflecting and getting feedback after each stage.

### E7

- Location: The Essence of a Paper / Novelty
- Quote: "be extremely clear about what is and is not novel, especially in the introduction and related work"
- Key point: In the introduction and related work, be very clear about what is and is not novel, and cite the most relevant work to show the difference.

### E8

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "the process of writing forces you to clarify your own thinking in ways that often reveal gaps or new insights"
- Key point: Writing forces you to clarify your thinking and often reveals gaps or new insights.

### E9

- Location: The Writing Process / Iteratively Expand
- Quote: "A good outline is tight and minimal - every part of it should have a clear role in the overall narrative."
- Key point: A good outline is tight and minimal; every part needs a clear role in the narrative. If you can't say what would go wrong without it, cut it.
