# Bergman & Danheiser 2016: Reproducibility in Chemical Research

- Source: `bergman2016reproducibility`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201606591)
- Version: Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2016, 55, 12548-12549, Editorial. Free to read on the publisher's website; this excerpt uses the publisher's PDF saved by the Internet Archive on 2025-05-31. The two authors are chemistry professors at UC Berkeley and MIT; Danheiser was then editor-in-chief of Organic Syntheses.

### E1

- Location: Sources of Irreproducibility, p. 12548
- Quote: "While a complete failure to repeat published results rarely occurs in chemistry, it is not unusual for chemists to encounter difficulty in exactly replicating reaction yields, selectivities, and other data reported in publications."
- Key point: Complete failure to repeat published results is rare in chemistry, but difficulty exactly replicating reported yields, selectivities, and other data is not unusual.

### E2

- Location: Sources of Irreproducibility, p. 12548
- Quote: "Instances of irreproducibility such as these are all too common, especially in synthetic chemistry."
- Key point: Such irreproducibility is all too common, especially in synthetic chemistry.

### E3

- Location: Sources of Irreproducibility, p. 12548
- Quote: "the editorial staff of the journal Organic Letters, who have found that 2–3 % of submitted manuscripts include evidence for the manual removal of peaks in NMR spectra"
- Key point: The editors of Organic Letters found evidence of manual removal of peaks from NMR spectra in 2-3% of submitted manuscripts.

### E4

- Location: Sources of Irreproducibility, p. 12548
- Quote: "Related violations of appropriate conduct include discarding data points that are not consistent with desired results, and only reporting the best yield or selectivity for a synthetic reaction."
- Key point: Related violations include discarding data points inconsistent with the desired results and reporting only the best yield or selectivity.

### E5

- Location: Replicating Valid Results, p. 12549
- Quote: "During the period 2010–2016, 7.5 % of the articles submitted to Organic Syntheses were rejected because the yield and/or selectivity reported by the original authors could not be reproduced within a reasonable range in the laboratory of one of the editors." / "The fact that results in 1 in 13 articles proved to not be reproducible even with all of these advantages underscores the challenges associated with reproducibility in synthetic chemistry."
- Key point: From 2010 to 2016, 7.5% of articles submitted to Organic Syntheses were rejected because an editor's lab could not reproduce the reported yield or selectivity within a reasonable range; even with detailed procedures and the option to consult the authors, about 1 in 13 articles could not be reproduced.

### E6

- Location: Replicating Valid Results, p. 12549
- Quote: "we believe that the majority of reproducibility problems encountered by chemists involve bona fide results, that is, results that were indeed actually obtained by the original authors."
- Key point: The authors believe most reproducibility problems chemists encounter involve bona fide results that the original authors really did obtain.

### E7

- Location: Recommendations, p. 12549
- Quote: "PIs should stay in close communication with co-workers, through regular face-to-face research talks in which primary data and laboratory notebooks are examined."
- Key point: Principal investigators (PIs) should stay in close contact with co-workers through regular face-to-face research talks that examine primary data and lab notebooks.

### E8

- Location: Recommendations, p. 12549
- Quote: "in the case of a report on new synthetic chemistry, a co-worker without experience in the area might be assigned to repeat a representative example based only on the supporting information."
- Key point: For a report on new synthetic chemistry, a co-worker with no experience in the area can be asked to repeat a representative example using only the supporting information.

### E9

- Location: Recommendations, p. 12549
- Quote: "While raising financial support is a critical and important contribution to a project, co-authorship should also require direct participation in the study and significant intellectual contribution to the work."
- Key point: Raising funding is important, but co-authorship should also require direct participation in the study and significant intellectual contribution.

### E10

- Location: Recommendations, p. 12549
- Quote: "all co-authors are responsible for published results, including the PI."
- Key point: All co-authors, including the PI, are responsible for published results.

### E11

- Location: Recommendations, p. 12549
- Quote: "PIs who train their co-workers (and themselves!) to be skeptical of results, especially those that they want to believe are correct, are good role models."
- Key point: Good PIs train their co-workers (and themselves) to be skeptical of results, especially ones they want to be true.

### E12

- Location: Unconscious Investigator Bias, p. 12548
- Quote: "we believe that this problem exists in the "harder" sciences as well."
- Key point: The authors believe researchers' unconscious bias exists in "harder" sciences such as chemistry too.
