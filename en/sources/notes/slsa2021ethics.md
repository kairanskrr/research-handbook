# Socio-Legal Studies Association 2021: Statement of Principles of Ethical Research Practice

- Source: `slsa2021ethics`, [original PDF](https://slsa.ac.uk/images/slsadownloads/SLSA_Board_2021/SLSA_Ethics_Statement_-_September_2021.pdf) (the association's website has been redesigned and the original link is dead; read via an Internet Archive snapshot of 2025-01-23)
- Version: Research ethics principles of the Socio-Legal Studies Association (SLSA, the UK organization of scholars who study how law works in society), adopted in 2001 and revised in 2009 and 2021. Locations give clause numbers.

### E1

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "this Ethics Statement seeks not to impose a single model of ethical practice"
- Key point: The statement does not impose a single model of ethics; it offers general principles that members should consider in their research.

### E2

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "as the SLSA does not award ethical approval, it has no legal responsibility where SLSA members have acted unethically."
- Key point: The association itself does not carry out ethics approval.

### E3

- Location: 7.5.1 (Gatekeepers)
- Quote: "In these situations, members should adhere to the principle of obtaining informed consent directly from research participants to whom access is required, while at the same time taking account of the gatekeeper's interest."
- Key point: When you gain access to a research site through a "gatekeeper" (such as the head of a court or law firm), you still need to obtain informed consent directly from each participant.

### E4

- Location: 8.1.1 (Confidentiality)
- Quote: "data about or from a research participant, for instance statements made by an interviewee, are to be treated as confidential and, in the case of attributable statements, should not be attributed to the interviewee without their permission."
- Key point: What interviewees say must be kept confidential; without their permission, do not attribute identifiable remarks to them.

### E5

- Location: 8.1.2
- Quote: "Socio-legal researchers should not give unrealistic guarantees of confidentiality and anonymity."
- Key point: Do not give unrealistic guarantees of confidentiality and anonymity.

### E6

- Location: 8.1.2
- Quote: "In drafting and concluding confidentiality agreements researchers should consider that in some cases they may be subject to legal requirements to disclose information obtained during the course of the research which pertains to serious criminal offences being committed (see below principle 8.3)."
- Key point: When making confidentiality agreements, keep in mind that researchers may, in some circumstances, have a legal duty to disclose information about serious crimes.

### E7

- Location: 8.2.2
- Quote: "Where potential informants and research participants possess a combination of attributes which make them readily identifiable it may be difficult to disguise their identity without introducing an unacceptably large measure of distortion into the data."
- Key point: When a combination of a participant's characteristics makes them easy to identify, it is hard to conceal their identity without seriously distorting the data.

### E8

- Location: 8.2.2
- Quote: "In circumstances where it is difficult to protect the anonymity of informants and research participants, they should be informed of this fact in advance where possible."
- Key point: When anonymity is hard to guarantee, tell participants so in advance wherever possible.

### E9

- Location: 8.3.1
- Quote: "Socio-legal researchers should be aware of the legal status of researcher/subject confidentiality, where the relevant jurisdiction(s) provide clear rules on this, including those where fieldwork is being carried out."
- Key point: Find out the legal status of confidentiality between researcher and interviewee in the relevant jurisdictions (including where the fieldwork takes place).

### E10

- Location: 8.3.1
- Quote: "the socio-legal researcher may be required to give evidence or reveal documents, which may make it impossible for socio-legal researchers to keep certain information confidential without breaking the law."
- Key point: Researchers may be required to give evidence in court or hand over documents, in which case some information cannot be kept confidential without breaking the law.

### E11

- Location: 6.3
- Quote: "In some cases, where the public interest suggests otherwise and particularly where power is being abused by those being researched, obligations of trust and protection, for instance, through protecting the anonymity of research participants, may weigh less heavily."
- Key point: Where the public interest is involved, such as abuse of power by those being studied, obligations like protecting anonymity may carry less weight.

### E12

- Location: 6.3
- Quote: "Nevertheless, these obligations should not be discarded lightly"
- Key point: But these obligations should not be set aside lightly.

### E13

- Location: 8.6.1 (Data collection and storage)
- Quote: "Researchers should ensure the security of their data at all stages of research and that their data is managed in line with the data management policies of their institution."
- Key point: Keep data secure throughout the research, and manage it according to your institution's data management policy.
