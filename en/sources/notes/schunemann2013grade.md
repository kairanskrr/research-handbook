# Schünemann et al. (eds.) 2013: GRADE Handbook

- Source: `schunemann2013grade`, [online edition](https://gdt.gradepro.org/app/handbook/handbook.html)
- Version: Handbook for grading the quality of evidence and the strength of recommendations using the GRADE approach, updated October 2013, GRADE Working Group, free to read online. The handbook uses the word "quality"; later literature mostly says "certainty."

### E1

- Location: §5, Table 5.1
- Quote: "High We are very confident that the true effect lies close to that of the estimate of the effect." / "Low Our confidence in the effect estimate is limited: The true effect may be substantially different from the estimate of the effect." / "Very Low We have very little confidence in the effect estimate: The true effect is likely to be substantially different from the estimate of effect"
- Key point: High = very confident the true effect lies close to the estimate; low = limited confidence, the true effect may be substantially different from the estimate; very low = very little confidence, the true effect is likely to be substantially different. ("Moderate" lies in between.)

### E2

- Location: §5.1
- Quote: "The GRADE approach to rating the quality of evidence begins with the study design (trials or observational studies) and then addresses five reasons to possibly rate down the quality of evidence and three to possibly rate up the quality."
- Key point: GRADE starts from study design (trials or observational studies), then considers five reasons to rate the evidence down and three to rate it up.

### E3

- Location: §5, after Table 5.1
- Quote: "Quality of evidence is a continuum; any discrete categorisation involves some degree of arbitrariness."
- Key point: Quality of evidence is a continuum; any division into categories involves some arbitrariness.

### E4

- Location: §5.3.1
- Quote: "When outcomes are subjective it is important to be cautious when considering upgrading because of observed large effects. This is especially true when outcome assessors were aware which group study subjects belonged to (i.e. were not blinded)."
- Key point: When outcomes are subjective, especially when assessors knew the group assignment (no blinding), be cautious about upgrading because of a large effect.
