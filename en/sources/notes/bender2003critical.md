# Bender 2003: Critical Reading

- Source: `bender2003critical`, [original](https://faculty.washington.edu/ebender/critical_reading.pdf)
- Version: Handout, 2003-10-17; local PDF `papers/pdf/bender2003critical.pdf` (the PDF fonts cannot be extracted as text, so the text was transcribed verbatim from page images)

### E1

- Location: p. 1, para. 1
- Quote: "Critical reading is the process of actively engaging with a text as you read it, going beyond simply following the author's arguments or explanation. It involves examining the underlying assumptions not made explicit in the text, both in the initial framing of the issue and in the reasoning from evidence to conclusions."
- Key point: Critical reading means actively engaging with a text, going beyond following the author's argument to examine unstated assumptions, both in how the issue is framed and in the reasoning from evidence to conclusions.

### E2

- Location: p. 1, para. 2
- Quote: "Critical reading is not criticizing. You can read a text critically and come out the other end liking it or disliking it. In either case, you'll have a better idea of why."
- Key point: Critical reading is not criticizing; you can end up liking or disliking a text, but either way you will know better why.

### E3

- Location: p. 1, "For articles reporting on research"
- Quote: "1. What is the research question that the article asks? 2. What assumptions/presuppositions are involved in asking/framing the question the way the authors do? 3. How were the data gathered? 4. What potential for bias was there in the gathering of the data?"
- Key point: When reading a research article, ask: What is the research question? What assumptions does framing the question this way involve? How were the data gathered? What potential for bias was there in gathering them?

### E4

- Location: p. 1, "For articles reporting on research"
- Quote: "6. What are the conclusions presented in the article? 7. What assumptions/presuppositions are involved in drawing those conclusions from that evidence?"
- Key point: Also ask: What are the conclusions? What assumptions are involved in drawing those conclusions from that evidence?

### E5

- Location: p. 1, para. 3
- Quote: "In answering these questions as you read, keep in mind who the author and intended audience are. [...] [NB: These questions were developed in the context of a sociolinguistics course, but I believe that they have broader applicability.]"
- Key point: Keep in mind who the author and intended audience are; the questions were developed for a sociolinguistics course, but the author believes they apply more broadly.
