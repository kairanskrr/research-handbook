# Tetley 1999: Mixed jurisdictions: common law vs civil law (codified and uncodified) (Part I)

- Source: `tetley1999mixed`, [original PDF](https://unidroit.org/english/publications/review/articles/1999-3-tetley1-e.pdf)
- Version: Uniform Law Review / Revue de droit uniforme, vol. 4, no. 3 (1999), DOI 10.1093/ulr/4.3.591, starting at p. 591; PDF published by the International Institute for the Unification of Private Law (UNIDROIT) and available on its website, accessed 2026-10-11. The author, William Tetley, was then a professor of law at McGill University in Canada. Locations give subsection numbers in Part V of the article. In comparing the two legal traditions the author mainly uses French civil law as the example; it is a 1999 generalization.

### E1

- Location: §V.1 Order of priority: jurisprudence and doctrine
- Quote: "A major difference between the civil law and common law is that priority in civil law is given to doctrine (including the codifiers' reports) over jurisprudence, while the opposite is true in the common law."
- Key point: A major difference between the two legal traditions: civil law places doctrine (including the reports of the codes' drafters) before jurisprudence (case law), while common law does the opposite.

### E2

- Location: §V.4 Jurisprudence: function
- Quote: "Common law jurisprudence sets out a new specific rule to a new specific set of facts and provides the principal source of law, while civil law jurisprudence applies general principles, and is only a secondary source of law of explanation."
- Key point: In common law, case law lays down new specific rules for new specific facts and is a primary source of law; in civil law, case law applies general principles and is only a secondary, interpretive source of law.

### E3

- Location: §V.5 Stare decisis and footnote 115
- Quote: "The English doctrine of stare decisis compels lower courts to follow decisions rendered in higher courts" / "Stare decisis is unknown to civil law, where judgments rendered by judges only enjoy the "authority of reason"." / "In practice, however, the Cour de cassation is feared by judges of lower courts."
- Key point: The English doctrine of stare decisis requires lower courts to follow the decisions of higher courts; civil law has no such doctrine, and decisions carry only "the authority of reason." The author adds in a footnote that in practice lower-court judges are very wary of the (French) Cour de cassation (the main text does not adopt this footnote).

### E4

- Location: §V.7 Statutes: functions
- Quote: "Civil law codes provide the core of the law" / "general principles are systematically and exhaustively exposed in codes"
- Key point: In civil law, the code is the core of the law, and general principles are set out in it systematically and comprehensively.

### E5

- Location: §V.9 Interpretation of laws
- Quote: "In civil law jurisdictions, the first step in interpreting an ambiguous law, according to MAZEAUD, is to discover the intention of the legislator by examining the legislation as a whole, including the "travaux préparatoires", as well as the provisions more immediately surrounding the obscure text."
- Key point: In civil law, according to Mazeaud, the first step in interpreting an unclear law is to examine the legislation as a whole (including the preparatory legislative materials and the provisions around the unclear article) to find the legislator's intent.

### E6

- Location: §V.2 Doctrine: functions
- Quote: "The civil law doctrine's function is "to draw from this disorganised mass [cases, books and legal dictionaries] the rules and the principles which will clarify and purge the subject of impure elements, and thus provide both the practice and the courts with a guide for the solution of particular cases in the future.""
- Key point: The role of doctrine in civil law (citing David and Brierley): to distill rules and principles from the scattered materials of case law, books, and legal dictionaries, and to give practitioners and courts guidance for resolving specific cases in the future.
