# Nielsen 2004: Principles of Effective Research

- Source: `nielsen2004principles`, [Internet Archive snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20250105211800/https://michaelnielsen.org/blog/principles-of-effective-research/) (the original URL returned 404 in October 2026)
- Version: technical note, July 2004. Locations give the page's section headings.

### E1

- Location: Self-discipline
- Quote: "The first factor is having clarity about what one wants to achieve, why one wants to achieve it, and how to go about achieving it. [...] Conversely, I think the main cause of aimlessness and procrastination is when you lack clarity on one or more of these points."
- Key point: The first factor in self-discipline is clarity about what you want to achieve, why, and how; procrastination and aimlessness mostly come from a lack of clarity on one of these.

### E2

- Location: Self-discipline
- Quote: "He was shocked to discover that after factoring in all the other activities he engaged in each day – interruptions, email, surfing the net, the phone, fruitless meetings, chatting with friends, and so on – he was averaging only half an hour of research per day."
- Key point: One researcher timed his research with a stopwatch and found that, after subtracting interruptions, he averaged only half an hour of research a day. Honest time records are the basis for change.

### E3

- Location: The skills of the problem-solver
- Quote: "For the most part, it’s better to be doing something, rather than nothing, provided, of course, that you set time aside frequently for reflection and reconsideration of your goals."
- Key point: Most of the time it is better to be doing something than nothing, provided you frequently set aside time to reflect and reassess your goals.

### E4

- Location: The skills of the problem-solver
- Quote: "One of the most common mistakes made by researchers is to hold on very closely to a particular problem formulation. [...] if you can find a related problem, or reformulate a problem in a way that permits you to move forward, that is progress."
- Key point: A common mistake is to hold on too closely to one formulation of a problem; finding a related problem or a reformulation that lets you move forward counts as progress.

### E5

- Location: Self-discipline
- Quote: "Researchers are typically under little immediate social pressure to produce research results. [...] The key is to be accountable to other people. Some simple ways of achieving such accountability are to take on students, to collaborate with colleagues, or to set up mentoring relationships with colleagues."
- Key point: Researchers are usually under little immediate pressure to produce results; a key to self-discipline is being accountable to other people, which collaborating with colleagues and mentoring relationships can provide.

### E6

- Location: opening
- Quote: "We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act but a habit."
- Key point: The article's starting point: excellence is not an act but a habit.

### E7

- Location: Integrating research into the rest of your life
- Quote: "Motivation and desire alone are not enough. You also need to have the rest of your life in order to be an effective researcher. Make sure you’re fit. Look after your health."
- Key point: Motivation alone is not enough; you also need the rest of your life in order: stay fit, look after your family, and enjoy life.

### E8

- Location: Integrating research into the rest of your life
- Quote: "while the neglect paid short-term dividends in increased productivity, over the total period of writing the book I believe it probably cost me productivity"
- Key point: The author once neglected his health and relationships to write a book; it raised productivity in the short term but probably cost him productivity over the whole period.

### E9

- Location: Self-discipline
- Quote: "It is a tempting but ultimately counterproductive fallacy to believe that self-discipline is merely a matter of will" / "The key is to be accountable to other people."
- Key point: Self-discipline is not merely a matter of will; a key is making yourself accountable to other people, e.g., by supervising students, collaborating, or building mentoring relationships.

### E10

- Location: Aspects of research: self-development and the creative process
- Quote: "However, some people end up obsessed with writing as many papers as possible, as quickly as possible. While the short-term rewards of this are attractive (jobs, grants, reputation and prizes), the long-term costs are significant. In particular, it can lead to stagnation, and plateauing as a researcher."
- Key point: Obsessing over publishing as many papers as quickly as possible has attractive short-term rewards but leads to stagnation in the long run. Balance output with developing yourself.

### E11

- Location: Developing research strengths
- Quote: "you want to develop unique combinations of abilities" / "The few who do put in this effort often achieve spectacular results."
- Key point: Develop a unique combination of abilities; when a new field merges two old ones, the few who systematically learn the other field often achieve spectacular results.

### E12

- Location: Developing research strengths
- Quote: "You are almost certainly better off reading deeply in the ten most important papers of a research field than you are skimming the top five hundred."
- Key point: Reading the ten most important papers in a field deeply is almost certainly more useful than skimming the top five hundred.

### E13

- Location: Develop a high-quality research environment
- Quote: "improving your environment is something anyone can do; students, in particular, often underestimate the magnitude of the changes they can bring about."
- Key point: Anyone can improve their research environment, e.g., by organizing seminars or reading groups; students often underestimate the changes they can bring about.

### E14

- Location: Vision
- Quote: "A good vision answers questions like: What sort of researcher would I like to become?"
- Key point: A good long-term vision answers questions such as "What sort of researcher would I like to become?"

### E15

- Location: Developing research strengths
- Quote: "(a) quickly skimming a great deal of work, to keep track of what is known, and what sort of problems people are thinking about, and (b) based on that skimming, picking a dozen or so papers each year to read deeply"
- Key point: How to keep up with a field: skim a great deal quickly to track what is known and what problems people are thinking about, then pick a dozen or so papers a year to read deeply.
