# Einav & Yariv 2006: What's in a Surname? The Effects of Surname Initials on Academic Success

- Source: `einav2006surname`, [PDF on the author's homepage](https://web.stanford.edu/~leinav/pubs/JEP2006.pdf)
- Version: Journal of Economic Perspectives 20(1), pp. 175–188 (journal typeset version, publicly available on the author's homepage).

### E1

- Location: p. 175 (introduction)
- Quote: "We suspect the "alphabetical discrimination" reported in this paper is linked to the norm in the economics profession prescribing alphabetical ordering of credits on coauthored publications."
- Key point: The norm in economics is to list coauthors alphabetically by surname.

### E2

- Location: p. 175 (introduction)
- Quote: "Faculty with earlier surname initials are significantly more likely to receive tenure at top ten economics departments, are significantly more likely to become fellows of the Econometric Society"
- Key point: In the top ten economics departments, faculty with earlier surname initials are more likely to receive tenure and to become Fellows of the Econometric Society.

### E3

- Location: p. 182, footnote 8
- Quote: "Psychology is one of the closest disciplines to economics, but it follows the convention of listing coauthors by contribution, rather than according to their alphabetical placement." / 第 175 页："We find no relationship between alphabetical placement and tenure status in psychology."
- Key point: Psychology lists coauthors by contribution rather than alphabetically; using it as a comparison, the authors find no relationship between surname position and tenure in psychology.

### E4

- Location: p. 184, section "Do Economists Respond to Alphabetical Bias?"
- Quote: "About half of the papers over this time are multiauthored, and in 88 percent of the multiauthored papers, the authors are ordered alphabetically."
- Key point: From 1980 to 2002, about half of the papers in the top five journals were multiauthored, and 88% of those listed authors alphabetically.

### E5

- Location: p. 176 (introduction)
- Quote: "authors with surname initials that are placed later in the alphabet are significantly less likely to participate in four- and five-author projects. Furthermore, such authors are also more likely to deviate from the accepted norm, and to write papers in which credits do not follow the alphabetical ordering."
- Key point: Authors with surnames later in the alphabet are less likely to join four- and five-author projects and more likely to use non-alphabetical ordering.
