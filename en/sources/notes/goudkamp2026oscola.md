# Goudkamp (ed.) 2026: OSCOLA, 5th edition

- Source: `goudkamp2026oscola`, [Original PDF (University of Oxford Faculty of Law website, free)](https://www.law.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/OSCOLA%205.pdf)
- Version: OSCOLA (Oxford University Standard for Citation of Legal Authorities), 5th edition, Hart Publishing 2026, edited by James Goudkamp; the PDF is free on the Oxford Faculty of Law website under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 2.0 England and Wales license. The previous (4th) edition was from 2012. Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "There are two golden rules for the citation of legal authorities. One is consistency. The other is consideration for the reader."
- Key point: There are two golden rules for legal citation: consistency and consideration for the reader.

### E2

- Location: §1.1 Citations and footnotes
- Quote: "When citing a source, place the reference in a footnote."
- Key point: OSCOLA is a footnote style: when citing a source, put the reference in a footnote.

### E3

- Location: §1.1.1 Citing cases
- Quote: "The inclusion of pinpoints is essential (particularly when quoting) except when reference is being made to a case as a whole"
- Key point: Pinpoints (the page or paragraph of the judgment) are essential, especially when quoting, unless you refer to a case as a whole.

### E4

- Location: §1.1.4 Order of sources in footnotes
- Quote: "if citing primary and secondary sources for a single proposition, cite the primary sources before the secondary ones."
- Key point: When citing primary and secondary sources for the same proposition, cite the primary sources first.

### E5

- Location: §1.2.1 Subsequent citations
- Quote: "`ibid' should not be used."
- Key point: The 5th edition no longer uses "ibid."

### E6

- Location: §1.4 Foreign sources
- Quote: "When referring to foreign materials, cite primary sources as in their home jurisdiction, with the exception that full stops in abbreviations should be dropped."
- Key point: When citing foreign materials, cite primary sources as in their home jurisdiction (only dropping full stops in abbreviations).

### E7

- Location: §1.1.1 Citing cases
- Quote: "If the case is reported, the reference to the best report should be supplied after the medium neutral citation (section 2.1.4)."
- Key point: If a case is reported, give the best report after the neutral citation.

### E8

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Second, we have not laid down rules that deal with every single issue that arises in connection with legal citation."
- Key point: OSCOLA does not lay down rules for every citation issue; rare questions are left to the user's common sense.

### E9

- Location: OSCOLA introduction page on the official website (https://www.law.ox.ac.uk/oscola , accessed 2026-10-06)
- Quote: "It is widely used in law schools and by journal and book publishers in the UK and beyond."
- Key point: OSCOLA is widely used by law schools, journals, and book publishers in the UK and beyond.
