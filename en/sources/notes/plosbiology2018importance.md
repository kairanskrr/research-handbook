# PLOS Biology Editors 2018: The importance of being second

- Source: `plosbiology2018importance`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.2005203)
- Version: PLoS Biology 16(1): e2005203, January 29, 2018, editorial, open access (CC BY). This is one journal's (PLOS Biology's) policy, not a general rule across journals. Read 2026-10-08.

### E1

- Location: Paragraph 1
- Quote: "we are formalizing a policy whereby manuscripts that confirm or extend a recently published study ("scooped" manuscripts, also referred to as complementary) are eligible for consideration at PLOS Biology."
- Key point: PLOS Biology formalized a policy that manuscripts confirming or extending a recently published study ("scooped" manuscripts, also called complementary) are eligible for consideration.

### E2

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "Being scooped is loosely defined as when two independent groups studying the same system produce the same or similar results, and one group publishes their work first. Being scooped is often considered to devalue the second, complementary study; many journals will reject it citing lack of novelty."
- Key point: Being scooped roughly means two independent groups get the same or similar results and one publishes first; the later study is often considered devalued, and many journals reject it for lack of novelty.

### E3

- Location: Paragraph 2
- Quote: "we consider that two papers from two groups independently identifying the same phenomenon in parallel increase the confidence in the results of the work."
- Key point: The journal considers that two groups independently finding the same phenomenon increases confidence in the results.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph 5
- Quote: "authors of a complementary study have six months from the publication or posting (to a preprint server) of the first article to submit their manuscript to PLOS Biology. We hope that authors will use these six months to fully support and potentially extend the results of the first article."
- Key point: At this journal, authors of a complementary study have six months from the publication or preprint posting of the first article to submit; the editors hope authors will use that time to fully support and, where possible, extend the first article's results.

### E5

- Location: Paragraph 5
- Quote: "All submissions must still meet our editorial requirements for depth of study and potential impact."
- Key point: Scooped manuscripts must still meet the journal's requirements for depth of study and potential impact.
