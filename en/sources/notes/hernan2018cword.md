# Hernán 2018: The C-Word: Scientific Euphemisms Do Not Improve Causal Inference From Observational Data

- Source: `hernan2018cword`, [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5888052/)
- Version: American Journal of Public Health 108(5): 616–619, 2018. A commentary representing the author's personal views.

### E1

- Location: Opening of the main text
- Quote: "Many journal editors request authors to avoid causal language"
- Key point: Many journal editors ask authors to avoid causal language.

### E2

- Location: Opening of the main text
- Quote: "The proscription against the C-word is harmful to science because causal inference is a core task of science, regardless of whether the study is randomized or nonrandomized."
- Key point: The author argues that banning the "C-word" harms science, because causal inference is a core task of science whether or not the study is randomized.

### E3

- Location: OF COURSE "ASSOCIATION IS NOT CAUSATION"
- Quote: "The risk ratio of 0.8 does not imply that drinking a glass of wine every day lowers the risk of heart disease by 20%."
- Key point: An observed risk ratio of 0.8 does not mean that drinking a glass of wine every day lowers the risk of heart disease by 20%.

### E4

- Location: OF COURSE "ASSOCIATION IS NOT CAUSATION"
- Quote: "It is possible that the kind of people who drink a glass of wine per day would have a lower risk of heart disease even if they didn’t drink wine because, for example, they have high enough incomes to buy, besides wine, nutritious food and to take time off to exercise, or have better access to preventive health care."
- Key point: People who drink a glass of wine a day might have a lower risk of heart disease even without the wine, because, for example, they have higher incomes, eat better, have time to exercise, or have better access to preventive care.

### E5

- Location: BETTER CAUSAL QUESTIONS
- Quote: "a causal analysis in observational data can be viewed as an attempt to emulate a hypothetical trial—the target trial."
- Key point: A causal analysis of observational data can be seen as an attempt to emulate a hypothetical trial, the "target trial."

### E6

- Location: BETTER CAUSAL QUESTIONS
- Quote: "A key advantage of specifying the target trial is that it forces investigators to consider the intervention of interest and the time period during which it takes place. For example, a target trial could assign people to 10 years of daily wine drinking from age 55 years, or to 20 years of wine drinking from age 30 years. Each of these target trials answers a different causal question"
- Key point: Specifying the target trial forces you to consider the intervention of interest and when it takes place. For example, "daily wine for 10 years from age 55" and "wine for 20 years from age 30" are different target trials answering different causal questions.

### E7

- Location: BETTER CONFOUNDING ADJUSTMENT
- Quote: "If the goal of the observational analysis is causal, adjustment for confounding is generally necessary." / "if the goal of the observational analysis is purely associational, no adjustment for confounding is necessary."
- Key point: If the goal is causal, adjustment for confounding is generally necessary; if the goal is purely associational, it is not.

### E8

- Location: BETTER CONFOUNDING ADJUSTMENT
- Quote: "Automatic variable selection procedures may work for prediction, but not necessarily for causal inference."
- Key point: Automatic variable selection may work for prediction but not necessarily for causal inference.

### E9

- Location: CONFLATING THE MEANS AND THE ENDS
- Quote: "The only part of the article in which the term “causal effect” has no place is the Results section, which should present the findings without trying to interpret them."
- Key point: The only part of a paper where "causal effect" has no place is the Results section, which should present findings without interpreting them.

### E10

- Location: BETTER CONFOUNDING ADJUSTMENT
- Quote: "there is no guarantee that a causal model incorporates all the confounders and therefore there is no guarantee that the parameter estimate for treatment can be causally interpreted, even approximately."
- Key point: There is no guarantee that a model includes all confounders, so no guarantee that the estimate can be interpreted causally.

### E11

- Location: CONFLATING THE MEANS AND THE ENDS
- Quote: "such a trial is considered unethical and hence forbidden. Unfortunately, that means that observational analyses become our best chance to quantify the long-term causal effect of wine on chronic diseases."
- Key point: A trial assigning people to drink wine for years would be unethical, so observational analyses are the best chance to estimate such long-term causal effects.
