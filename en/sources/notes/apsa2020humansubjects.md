# American Political Science Association 2020: Principles and Guidance for Human Subjects Research

- Source: `apsa2020humansubjects`, [original PDF](https://www.apsanet.org/Portals/54/diversity%20and%20inclusion%20prgms/Ethics/Final_Principles%20with%20Guidance%20with%20intro.pdf)
- Version: Adopted by the APSA Council on April 4, 2020; 12 principles, each with Guidance. Free PDF on the APSA website. Locations give the principle number and subheading.

### E1

- Location: introductory note
- Quote: "On April 4, 2020, at the 2020 Spring Council Meeting, the APSA Council approved and adopted the following Principles and Guidance for Human Subjects Research."
- Key point: These principles were adopted by the APSA Council on April 4, 2020.

### E2

- Location: Preamble
- Quote: "These principles are not intended to be rules, requirements, or prohibitions, and they are not presented as a checklist for ethical research."
- Key point: The principles are not rules, requirements, or prohibitions, and not a checklist for ethical research.

### E3

- Location: General Principles, 2
- Quote: "Political science researchers have an individual responsibility to consider the ethics of their research related activities and cannot outsource ethical reflection to review boards, other institutional bodies, or regulatory agencies."
- Key point: Researchers are personally responsible for the ethics of their research and cannot "outsource" ethical reflection to review boards or regulators.

### E4

- Location: Power, 4b
- Quote: "When conducting research with powerful parties, including some public officials, other actors, institutions, and corporations, covert or deceptive research with more than minimal harm may sometimes be ethically permissible."
- Key point: When studying powerful parties (some public officials, institutions, corporations), covert or deceptive research with more than minimal harm may sometimes be ethically permissible.

### E5

- Location: Guidance (Power)
- Quote: "The new regulations no longer offer an exemption for research on elected and public officials."
- Key point: The new US regulations in effect since January 2019 no longer exempt research on elected and public officials.

### E6

- Location: Consent, 5
- Quote: "Political science researchers should generally seek informed consent from individuals who are directly engaged by the research process, especially [...] research involves more than minimal risk of harm or if it is plausible to expect that engaged individuals would withhold consent if consent were sought"
- Key point: Researchers should generally seek informed consent from people directly engaged by the research, especially when the risk is more than minimal or when those people would plausibly refuse if asked.

### E7

- Location: Guidance (Harm)
- Quote: "a breach of confidentiality that reveals the identity of a dissident or her associates who were not studied;"
- Key point: Assessing harm includes harm from disseminating results, e.g., a breach of confidentiality that reveals the identity of a dissident or of associates who were not studied.

### E8

- Location: Confidentiality, 9d
- Quote: "If research materials are shared, researchers should ensure that the material is sufficiently redacted so that even well-informed parties cannot infer the identity of any person to whom specific statements or information can be attributed."
- Key point: If research materials are shared, redact them enough that even well-informed parties cannot infer who said what.

### E9

- Location: Guidance (Confidentiality)
- Quote: "Even if an editor requires data sharing as a condition of publication, this does not release the researcher from the ethical obligation to protect the confidentiality of their participants."
- Key point: Even if an editor requires data sharing as a condition of publication, the researcher's ethical obligation to protect participants' confidentiality still stands.

### E10

- Location: Guidance (Confidentiality)
- Quote: "Threats to confidentiality can come in many forms, including carelessness, data sharing, cybersecurity failures, freedom of information (FOIA) requests, subpoenas, and mandatory reporting."
- Key point: Threats to confidentiality include carelessness, data sharing, cybersecurity failures, freedom of information (FOIA) requests, subpoenas, and mandatory reporting.

### E11

- Location: Impact, 10
- Quote: "In general, political science researchers should not compromise the integrity of political processes for research purposes without the consent of individuals that are directly engaged by the research process."
- Key point: In general, researchers should not compromise the integrity of political processes for research purposes without the consent of those directly engaged.

### E12

- Location: Guidance (Impact), Minimal Risks
- Quote: "In democratic systems with widespread protections of political rights, such petition signing likely entails minimal risk of harm. However, in authoritarian systems, petition signers may plausibly be viewed as unwelcome critics of the government and might be targeted for reprisal."
- Key point: Asking citizens to sign a petition carries minimal risk in a democracy with protected political rights; in an authoritarian system, signers may be seen as critics and targeted for reprisal.

### E13

- Location: Guidance (Impact), Minimal Risks
- Quote: "An example of a study in which consent is not sought (and plausibly cannot be sought) but plausibly involves minimal risk, is one in which researchers place Facebook ads reproducing campaign advertising. Such interventions are minimal risk to individuals if they are similar to what individuals encounter in their usual lives and they are of minimal social risk if they are not done at a scale liable to alter electoral outcomes and do not inject false or misleading information into political processes."
- Key point: Example of Facebook ads reproducing campaign advertising: the intervention is minimal risk to individuals if it resembles what they normally see, and minimal risk to society only if it is too small to change an election outcome and injects no false information into the political process.

### E14

- Location: Guidance (Impact), Third parties
- Quote: "collaborations do not transfer all ethical responsibility to the third party."
- Key point: Collaborating with governments, electoral bodies, or other third parties does not transfer all ethical responsibility to them.

### E15

- Location: Guidance (Impact), Approvals
- Quote: "Approval from electoral or other authorities can help provide justification but does not obviate researchers from all ethical considerations."
- Key point: Approval from electoral or other authorities can help justify a study but does not relieve researchers of all ethical considerations.

### E16

- Location: Impact, 10d
- Quote: "In publications and presentations, researchers should report likely impacts on the experiences of participants, any impacts on broader political processes, and whether and from whom they sought consent."
- Key point: Publications should report likely impacts on participants, impacts on broader political processes, and whether and from whom consent was sought.

### E17

- Location: Laws, Regulations, and Prospective Review, 11
- Quote: "When a research project is conducted in more than one jurisdiction, or when a scholar is based in one jurisdiction and conducts research in another, the researcher should be aware of relevant laws and regulations in each setting."
- Key point: When research spans several jurisdictions, or the researcher is based in one and works in another, the researcher should know the relevant laws and regulations in each.

### E18

- Location: Guidance (Trauma)
- Quote: "Trauma may be more likely when research involves war or sexual violence, but trauma may emerge in a wide range of research settings."
- Key point: Trauma is more likely in research on war or sexual violence, but it can arise in many other research settings too.

### E19

- Location: Confidentiality, 9b
- Quote: "In some cases, it might be appropriate for researchers to not promise confidentiality (for example, research on powerful elites or oral histories in which the participants prefer to be identified). In these cases, researchers should clearly communicate the lack of confidentiality to the participants"
- Key point: Sometimes it is appropriate not to promise confidentiality, e.g., research on powerful elites or oral histories where participants want to be named; then make the lack of confidentiality clear to participants.

### E20

- Location: Guidance (Impact), Impact arising from use of research
- Quote: "there is generally no concern or harm if a book reaches conclusions unfavorable to a policy or political actor and these conclusions affect opinion and votes."
- Key point: Conclusions unfavorable to a policy or political actor that then shift opinion and votes are generally not the kind of harm this principle is about.

### E21

- Location: Impact, 10a
- Quote: "Relevant considerations include when the research presents minimal risk of negatively affecting individual experience or impacting political outcomes, when researchers are studying powerful actors [...] or institutions, or when deception or misrepresentation is otherwise justifiable."
- Key point: Whether research that affects political processes without consent may be appropriate depends on whether the risk is minimal, whether the subjects are powerful actors or institutions, and whether the deception is otherwise justifiable.
