# Sociological Science: review process, reproducibility policy, and journal information (web pages)

- Source: `sociologicalsciencepolicy`, [Editorial and Review Process](https://sociologicalscience.com/for-authors/editorial-and-review-process/), [Reproducibility Policy](https://sociologicalscience.com/for-authors/reproducibility-policy/), [Journal Information](https://sociologicalscience.com/about/journal-information/)
- Version: Three pages from the website of the open-access sociology journal Sociological Science (published by the nonprofit Society for Sociological Science), accessed October 6, 2026. This journal deliberately departs from traditional peer review, and its description of "the dominant practice of sociology journals" is the journal's own view. Locations give page names and sections.

### E1

- Location: Editorial and Review Process, opening
- Quote: "The dominant model de facto requires a majority (or sometimes even a unanimity) of reviewers to “vote” in favor of a paper before it will be published. Reviewers – who have little if any accountability to the journal or the author – can wield enormous influence over both the fate and content of papers. This practice has led to a bias toward errors of omission, to multiple rounds of revisions, and to ever-increasing review times."
- Key point: The journal argues that the dominant model in sociology journals in effect requires a majority (sometimes all) of reviewers to "vote" for publication, giving reviewers great influence over a paper's fate and content, which leads to multiple rounds of revision and ever-longer review times.

### E2

- Location: Editorial and Review Process, opening
- Quote: "Authors will never be asked to “revise and resubmit” their papers; after the initial review, all subsequent decisions will be made by the Editorial Staff without soliciting further reviews." / "Finally, authors will be notified of the journal’s editorial decision within 30 days of submission."
- Key point: The journal never asks for "revise and resubmit"; after the initial review, the editor decides without sending the paper out again, and a decision is given within 30 days of submission.

### E3

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, opening
- Quote: "Starting with submissions received after April 1, 2023, authors of articles relying on statistical or computational methods will be required to deposit replication packages as a condition of publication in Sociological Science. Replication packages must contain both the statistical code and — when legally and ethically possible — the data required to fully reproduce the reported results."
- Key point: For submissions received after April 1, 2023, papers that use statistical or computational methods must deposit a replication package to be published; it must contain the statistical code and, where legally and ethically permissible, the data needed to fully reproduce the results.

### E4

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, opening
- Quote: "Under many legitimate circumstances, data cannot legally or ethically be made available to readers. When authors cannot make their data available, they must explain why in the main text of the paper. In such cases, making code and other materials available is still required, unless doing so would violate legal or ethical constraints."
- Key point: There are many legitimate situations in which data cannot be made public; in that case the reasons must be explained in the text, and the code and other materials must still be made public (unless this would violate legal or ethical constraints).

### E5

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, opening
- Quote: "Researchers using qualitative data, such as interviews or participant observation data, are not required to submit a replication package. We encourage authors to make qualitative data available when possible, and urge them to consider whether materials such as interview protocols or coding schemes can be shared."
- Key point: Studies using qualitative data such as interviews or participant observation are not required to submit a replication package; authors are encouraged to share qualitative data where possible and to consider sharing materials such as interview guides and coding schemes.

### E6

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, FAQ: What if authors are analyzing publicly available datasets?
- Quote: "Alternatively, replication packages must include (a) a link to the official web site where the data set(s) are stored; (b) the release version and/or release date of the data the researcher used, in case the data producers make corrections later; and (c) the code necessary to extract the variables (since users often rename variables at the same time as they extract)."
- Key point: When analyzing a public dataset without including the original data, the replication package must include the official data URL, the release version or date of the data used (in case the provider later revises it), and the code that extracts the variables.

### E7

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, FAQ: Does this policy require researchers to document their results in a specific way?
- Quote: "At the bare minimum, we strongly recommend that documentation clearly demarcate which sections of code correspond to which specific tables and figures presented in the paper."
- Key point: At a minimum, document which code corresponds to which table and figure in the paper.

### E8

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, Policy on Findings from Experimental Data, (2) Pre-registration
- Quote: "Sociological Science requires authors of papers that include originally collected experimental data and present findings from those experiments as testing hypotheses (i.e., as “confirmatory” rather than “exploratory” research) to indicate explicitly whether those experiments were pre-registered."
- Key point: Papers that collect their own experimental data and present the results as hypothesis tests (confirmatory rather than exploratory) must state explicitly whether the experiment was preregistered.

### E9

- Location: Reproducibility Policy, FAQ: Do reviewers or editors check the accuracy of the replication packages?
- Quote: "No. The existence of a replication package does not imply that the package has been vetted."
- Key point: The journal does not check whether replication packages actually reproduce the results; having a replication package does not mean it has been vetted.

### E10

- Location: Journal Information, Principles
- Quote: "Second, since the ratio of novel insights to the number of written words should be high, Sociological Science discourages excessive citations to prior work, needless literature reviews, and tangential discussions."
- Key point: The journal wants a high ratio of new insight to word count, and discourages excessive citation, unnecessary literature reviews, and tangential discussion.

### E11

- Location: Journal Information, Principles
- Quote: "Science advances through a dialogue between theory and evidence. Neither should be privileged in the editorial process. In Sociological Science, papers that primarily identify or resolve empirical puzzles have equal standing with papers that primarily address conceptual puzzles."
- Key point: Science advances through a dialogue between theory and evidence, and neither should be privileged; papers that mainly address empirical puzzles and papers that mainly address conceptual puzzles have equal standing.
