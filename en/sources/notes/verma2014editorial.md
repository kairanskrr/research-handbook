# Verma 2014: Editorial Expression of Concern: Experimental evidence of massive-scale emotional contagion through social networks

- Source: `verma2014editorial`, [full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115552/)
- Version: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS) 111(29), p. 10779, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1412583111. An Editorial Expression of Concern issued by Editor-in-Chief Inder M. Verma about the Kramer et al. 2014 Facebook emotional contagion experiment. The text is a single paragraph, so the location is given as "Main text."

### E1

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "Questions have been raised about the principles of informed consent and opportunity to opt out in connection with the research in this paper."
- Key point: The research was questioned on the principles of informed consent and giving participants the chance to opt out.

### E2

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "Because this experiment was conducted by Facebook, Inc. for internal purposes, the Cornell University IRB [Institutional Review Board] determined that the project did not fall under Cornell's Human Research Protection Program."
- Key point: According to the authors, because Facebook ran the experiment for internal purposes, Cornell University's institutional review board decided the project did not fall under Cornell's Human Research Protection Program.

### E3

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "Adherence to the Common Rule is PNAS policy, but as a private company Facebook was under no obligation to conform to the provisions of the Common Rule when it collected the data used by the authors, and the Common Rule does not preclude their use of the data."
- Key point: Following the Common Rule, the US rule for protecting human research subjects, is PNAS policy, but Facebook as a private company was not obliged to follow it when collecting the data.

### E4

- Location: Main text
- Quote: "It is nevertheless a matter of concern that the collection of the data by Facebook may have involved practices that were not fully consistent with the principles of obtaining informed consent and allowing participants to opt out."
- Key point: Even so, the journal is concerned that Facebook's data collection may not have fully respected the principles of obtaining informed consent and allowing participants to opt out.
