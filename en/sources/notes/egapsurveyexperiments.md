# EGAP Methods Guide: 10 Things to Know About Survey Experiments

- Source: `egapsurveyexperiments`, [original web page](https://methods.egap.org/guides/data-collection/survey-experiments_en.html)
- Version: Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) methods guide, attributed to Christopher Grady in the page metadata; accessed October 2026. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1 What is a survey experiment
- Quote: "A survey experiment is an experiment conducted within a survey."
- Key point: A survey experiment is an experiment conducted within a survey.

### E2

- Location: §2 Why do a survey experiment
- Quote: "Other preferences, such as racist attitudes and illegal behaviors, may be sensitive — preferences with which respondents do not want to be publicly associated. Direct questioning techniques may thus understate the prevalence of these preferences."
- Key point: For sensitive topics such as racist attitudes or illegal behavior, asking directly underestimates how common they are.

### E3

- Location: §3 Conjoint experiments
- Quote: "In a typical conjoint experiment, researchers repeatedly ask respondents to choose between two distinct options and randomly vary the characteristics of these two options."
- Key point: Conjoint experiments repeatedly ask respondents to choose between two options whose characteristics are randomly varied.

### E4

- Location: §5 Endorsement experiments
- Quote: "Because endorsement experiments preempt the need for respondents to self-report their support for a controversial object, they are especially useful in politically sensitive contexts."
- Key point: Endorsement experiments do not require respondents to state their support for a controversial object directly, which makes them especially useful in politically sensitive settings.

### E5

- Location: §6 List Experiments
- Quote: "List experiments (also known as the item count technique) measure a sensitive attitude or behavior when researchers expect respondents to falsify it if it is solicited using a direct question."
- Key point: List experiments (the item count technique) measure sensitive attitudes or behaviors that respondents would likely misreport if asked directly.

### E6

- Location: §9 Considerations
- Quote: "researchers need to be careful about survey satisficing, which occurs when respondents put in minimal effort to understand and answer a survey question."
- Key point: Watch out for survey satisficing, where respondents put in minimal effort to understand and answer the questions.

### E7

- Location: §10 Limitations
- Quote: "When preferences are measured — and treatments are delivered — in a survey setting, there is no guarantee that the survey-experimental findings will translate into the real world."
- Key point: Findings measured in a survey setting are not guaranteed to translate to the real world.
