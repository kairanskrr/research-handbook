# Harris 1989: Principles of Archaeological Stratigraphy (2nd edition)

- Source: `harris1989principles`, [Free download page on the author's website](https://harrismatrix.com/download/); [PDF](https://harrismatrix.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Principles_of_Archaeological_Stratigraphy.-2nd-edition.pdf)
- Version: By Edward C. Harris; first edition 1979, 2nd edition 1989 (Academic Press). The author bought back the rights in 2004 and offers the book as a free download on his website. The origin of the Harris Matrix. Locations are given as chapter titles.

### E1

- Location: Chapter 6, Deposits as units of stratification
- Quote: "Archaeological stratification, on this account, is an irreversible process. Once a unit of stratification - either a layer or interface - is formed, it is thereafter subject only to alteration and decay: it cannot be made again."
- Key point: Archaeological stratification is irreversible; once a unit of stratification (a layer or an interface) is formed, it can only be altered or decay, never made again.

### E2

- Location: Chapter 5, The laws of archaeological stratigraphy, Law of Superposition
- Quote: "In a series of layers and interfacial features, as originally created, the upper units of stratification are younger and the lower are older, for each must have been deposited on, or created by the removal of, a pre-existing mass of archaeological stratification."
- Key point: The law of superposition: in layers as originally created, the upper units are younger and the lower units older.

### E3

- Location: Chapter 12, An outline for stratigraphic recording on excavations
- Quote: "In the presence of obvious stratification, the stratigraphic method of excavation must be used."
- Key point: Where there is obvious stratification, you must excavate stratigraphically (not by arbitrary fixed depths).

### E4

- Location: Chapter 3, Techniques of archaeological excavation
- Quote: "By imposing the arbitrary strategy of excavation on sites with clear stratification, archaeologists destroy the primary data they seek"
- Key point: Excavating by arbitrary levels on a site with clear stratification destroys the very primary data you are looking for.

### E5

- Location: Chapter 12
- Quote: "Starting from the latest units and working downwards to the earlier units of stratification, all the units must be numbered." / "It is sufficient to have only one series of numbers in the records."
- Key point: Working from the latest units down to the earlier ones, number every unit; one series of numbers in the records is enough.

### E6

- Location: Chapter 12
- Quote: "These are most easily recorded on pre-printed sheets (e.g. Fig. 59). Three relationships will be sought: which units lie above, which lie below, and which units can be stratigraphically correlated."
- Key point: Record stratigraphic relationships on pre-printed sheets; look for three relationships: which units lie above, which lie below, and which can be stratigraphically correlated.

### E7

- Location: Chapter 12
- Quote: "Before the excavation of the actual unit has commenced, a plan should be made of its surface." / "On complex sites with many overlapping deposits, the single-layer plan should be used and every unit should be planned."
- Key point: Before excavating a unit, draw a plan of its surface; on complex sites with many overlapping deposits, use single-layer plans and plan every unit.

### E8

- Location: Chapter 9, Archaeological plans
- Quote: "If the site has little stratification, the composite plan is the first, and probably the last, choice. On complex sites, the single-layer plan is the basic requirement, from which composite plans can later be made."
- Key point: Sites with little stratification can use a composite plan; complex sites need single-layer plans as the basis, from which composite plans can later be made.

### E9

- Location: Chapter 5
- Quote: "Using this method during an excavation (Fig. 10), a sequence can be built up on paper as the work progresses."
- Key point: You can build up the stratigraphic sequence on paper as the excavation proceeds.

### E10

- Location: Chapter 11, Stratigraphic sequences and post-excavation analyses
- Quote: "Residual remains. These objects were made at a much earlier time than the formation of the layer in which they were found." / "Infiltrated remains. These objects were made at a later time than the formation of the deposit in which they were found"
- Key point: Residual remains are much older than the layer they are found in; infiltrated remains are later than the layer and got mixed in afterward.

### E11

- Location: Chapter 11
- Quote: "Whether they are indigenous, infiltrated or residual does not affect the recording of artefacts on archaeological excavations. In fact, they must all be recorded in the same manner, if their character is to be distinguished later."
- Key point: Whether finds are indigenous, infiltrated, or residual, they must all be recorded the same way in the field so they can be told apart later.

### E12

- Location: Chapter 11
- Quote: "it is the least old object (or objects) which must be nearest to the date of the layer itself; it gives in other words a terminus post quem, which means that the date of the layer must be after the date of the objects' manufacture" / "This axiom is based on the assumption that layers can be sealed from any later intrusions"
- Key point: The latest object in a layer is nearest to the layer's date and gives a terminus post quem: the layer must be later than that object's manufacture, assuming the layer has not been intruded upon later.

### E13

- Location: Chapter 6
- Quote: "The chronological date of a unit of stratification can never change its position in the stratigraphic sequence of a site"
- Key point: The calendar date of a unit of stratification can never change its position in the stratigraphic sequence.
