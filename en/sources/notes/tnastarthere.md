# The National Archives (UK): Start here (research getting-started pages)

- Source: `tnastarthere`, [original](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/start-here/)
- Version: Several pages under "Help with your research > Start here" on the website of The National Archives (UK, at Kew, London): How to use archives, What are archives?, What's online, What's not online. The pages are undated; accessed 2026-10-06. Published under the UK Open Government Licence v3.0. Locations give page titles.

### E1

- Location: What are archives?, Is an archive like a library?
- Quote: "Library books are arranged by subject and author, whereas information in archives is arranged according to the person or organisation that created it. This means that you will probably need to look at records from more than one source, or more than one archive, as you gather information."
- Key point: Libraries arrange books by subject and author, whereas archives are arranged by the person or organization that created them; so when gathering information you will probably need to look at records from several sources and several archives.

### E2

- Location: How to use archives, Start at home
- Quote: "If the archive has an online catalogue, take some time to find out how to use it. Don’t expect it to be like Google – a simple keyword search will not always work"
- Key point: Take time to learn the archive's online catalog; it is not like Google, and simple keyword searches may not work.

### E3

- Location: How to use archives, In an archive
- Quote: "Recognise both the limitations and potential of the archive – you may not find exactly what you are looking for, but you could find new sources you didn’t know existed"
- Key point: Archives have both limitations and potential: you may not find what you want, but you may discover new material you did not expect.

### E4

- Location: What's online
- Quote: "Many of our most popular records are available online. This is over 5% of what we have and this figure keeps growing."
- Key point: The records The National Archives has put online make up just over 5% of its holdings (the page's own wording, undated).

### E5

- Location: What's online
- Quote: "Other records that we hold have been digitised by commercial partners like Ancestry and Findmypast. You will need to visit their websites to view them – and there will usually be a charge."
- Key point: Some records were digitized by commercial companies and must be viewed on their websites, usually for a fee.

### E6

- Location: What's not online
- Quote: "Looking for records that are not online can be a time-consuming process and, as with any search in archival records, there is never a guarantee of success."
- Key point: Finding records that are not online is time-consuming; no archival search is guaranteed to succeed.

### E7

- Location: What's not online
- Quote: "You can also search records of other archives using filters in Discovery, our catalogue."
- Key point: The National Archives' catalog, Discovery, can also search records held by other archives.

### E8

- Location: What's not online
- Quote: "Remember to bring the correct ID, as you will need a reader’s ticket to see original documents"
- Key point: Viewing original documents requires a reader's ticket; bring the required identity documents when you go.
