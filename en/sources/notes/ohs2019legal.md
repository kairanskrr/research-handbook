# UK Oral History Society 2019: Is your oral history legal and ethical?

- Source: `ohs2019legal`, [main page](https://www.ohs.org.uk/advice/ethical-and-legal/), and subpages: [Preparation](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-preparation/), [First approach](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-1st-approach/), [During the interview](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-during-interview/), [After the interview](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-after/), [Archiving](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-archiving/)
- Version: Online guidance from the Oral History Society (OHS, UK); the main page reads "© Oral History Society, 2019" and it replaces earlier versions such as 2012. Applies to all four UK nations and UK law. Read 2026-10-06. Locations give subpage names.

### E1

- Location: Main page
- Quote: "This guide is intended as no more than guidance and does not constitute formal legal advice. If you need legal advice you should consult a solicitor."
- Key point: The guide is guidance only, not formal legal advice; if you need legal advice, consult a solicitor.

### E2

- Location: Preparation
- Quote: "Interviewers working in a UK institution of higher or further education, or whose interviewees are either employees or currently patients of the National Health Service (NHS) will be required to submit their research proposal to an ethics committee."
- Key point: Interviewers working in a UK higher or further education institution, or whose interviewees are National Health Service (NHS) employees or patients, must submit their research plans to an ethics committee.

### E3

- Location: Preparation, citing the responsibilities listed by the UK Data Archive
- Quote: "a duty of confidentiality (though not necessarily anonymity) towards informants and participants"
- Key point: You owe informants a duty of confidentiality, though not necessarily anonymity.

### E4

- Location: Preparation
- Quote: "archives which may be willing to accept the recordings should be contacted in advance to check what documentation is required"
- Key point: Contact archives that may accept the recordings in advance to check what documentation they require.

### E5

- Location: Preparation
- Quote: "OHS advice is to seek parental or carer consent for interviewing children under the age of sixteen, in addition to the consent of the child where they are aged thirteen or over."
- Key point: OHS advises getting parental or carer consent to interview children under 16, plus the child's own consent if they are 13 or older.

### E6

- Location: 1st approach
- Quote: "An Interview Participation Agreement should be completed before the interview begins" / "An Interview Recording Agreement which covers copyright and access conditions should be completed after the interview has been finished." / "but which should be shown to and explained to an interviewee before any recording begins"
- Key point: OHS uses two documents: an Interview Participation Agreement signed before the interview, and an Interview Recording Agreement covering copyright and access, signed after it; the Recording Agreement should be shown and explained to the interviewee before recording starts.

### E7

- Location: 1st approach
- Quote: "Without a signed Recording Agreement contacting people at a later date is usually very time-consuming and often impossible if interviewees or interviewers have died or moved away."
- Key point: Without a signed Recording Agreement, contacting people later is usually very time-consuming, and often impossible if interviewees or interviewers have died or moved away.

### E8

- Location: 1st approach
- Quote: "Both interviewer and interviewee have copyrights in the recording"
- Key point: (Under UK law) both interviewer and interviewee have copyrights in the recording.

### E9

- Location: During interview
- Quote: "If for any reason an interviewee asks that the interview be ended or broken off and resumed at some later date, that preference should be respected." / "it is often helpful before the interview begins to agree a time to finish the recording session, or perhaps to arrange to have a break."
- Key point: If an interviewee asks to end or break off the interview and resume later, respect that; agreeing on an end time or breaks beforehand often helps.

### E10

- Location: During interview
- Quote: "They should record these instances (with time codes) on an Interview Sensitivity Review Form as the interview is being summarised (or transcribed) and then subsequently bring any concerns to the attention of interviewees and project leaders so that everyone involved can weigh up the likely risks of disclosure involved."
- Key point: When summarizing or transcribing, log content that may be defamatory or disclose sensitive personal information about others, with time codes, on an Interview Sensitivity Review Form, then raise it with the interviewee and project lead to weigh the risks of release.

### E11

- Location: During interview
- Quote: "Interviewers, and project staff/volunteers processing interview data, should treat the content of the interview as confidential until a Recording Agreement or other access agreement has been finalised."
- Key point: Interviewers and staff processing interview data should keep the content confidential until a Recording Agreement or other access agreement is finalized.

### E12

- Location: After
- Quote: "an interviewee owns copyright in his or her recorded words spoken on the recording and that this can be retained or assigned (passed) to someone else"
- Key point: The interviewee owns copyright in their recorded words and can keep it or assign it to someone else.

### E13

- Location: After
- Quote: "Any closure or restrictions on access or use that the interviewee may wish to make should be clearly described (with time codes and with dates when any embargoes come to an end)."
- Key point: Describe any closure or access restrictions the interviewee requests clearly, with time codes and the dates embargoes end.

### E14

- Location: After
- Quote: "Interviewers or project managers may also like to offer interviewees the opportunity to check the summary or transcript though this is not always a universal or necessary practice."
- Key point: You may offer interviewees the chance to check the summary or transcript, but this is not a universal or necessary practice.

### E15

- Location: Archiving
- Quote: "Places of deposit tend to avoid the acquisition of oral history interviews which are not accompanied by documentation including provenance, availability for use, and copyright status"
- Key point: Places of deposit tend not to acquire oral history interviews that lack documentation of provenance, availability for use, and copyright status.

### E16

- Location: Archiving
- Quote: "For digitisation projects involving online web access to oral history interviews, as far as possible interviewees should be re-contacted to confirm their agreement to this kind of access."
- Key point: When digitizing interviews for online access, re-contact interviewees as far as possible to confirm they agree to this kind of access.

### E17

- Location: Archiving
- Quote: "Interviews placed on the internet should always be accompanied by terms and conditions for use."
- Key point: Interviews placed online should come with terms and conditions for use.

### E18

- Location: Archiving
- Quote: "Publicly-funded organisations and places of deposit should also be aware of Freedom of Information legislation."
- Key point: Publicly funded organizations and places of deposit should be aware of (UK) Freedom of Information legislation.
