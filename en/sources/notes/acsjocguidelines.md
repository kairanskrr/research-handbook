# The Journal of Organic Chemistry: Author Guidelines

- Source: `acsjocguidelines`, [original](https://researcher-resources.acs.org/publish/author_guidelines?coden=joceah)
- Version: author guidelines web page of the American Chemical Society's *The Journal of Organic Chemistry* (JOC), marked last updated 2026-10-05, accessed 2026-10-06. The page has no page numbers, so locations give subheadings. It represents this journal's requirements only.

### E1

- Location: Compound Characterization Data
- Quote: "For new compounds, evidence adequate to establish both identity and degree of purity (homogeneity) must be provided."
- Key point: For new compounds, evidence adequate to establish both identity (structure) and degree of purity must be provided.

### E2

- Location: Compound Characterization Data
- Quote: "JOC requires that purity be documented compound-by-compound, with copies of spectra or chromatograms, elemental analysis, or quantitative NMR or chromatographic integration data."
- Key point: JOC requires purity to be documented compound by compound, with spectra or chromatograms, elemental analysis, or quantitative NMR or chromatographic integration data.

### E3

- Location: NMR / identifying new compounds
- Quote: "Evidence for documenting the identity of new compounds should include both proton and carbon NMR data and either MS accurate mass (HRMS) or elemental analysis data."
- Key point: Identifying a new compound requires both proton and carbon NMR data, plus either high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) or elemental analysis.

### E4

- Location: Purity
- Quote: "When primarily synthetic work is reported, JOC does not require that a certain minimum level of purity be met for the reported compounds, but it does require that the purity level that has been attained be faithfully documented. When new or known synthesized compounds are the study materials for physical measurements or bioassays, a purity level of at least 95% needs to be documented."
- Key point: For primarily synthetic work, JOC sets no minimum purity but requires the purity achieved to be reported accurately; compounds submitted for physical measurements or biological testing must be shown to be at least 95% pure.

### E5

- Location: Purity
- Quote: "A narrow melting point range is not sufficient by itself to document the purity of a new compound. MS accurate mass (HRMS) data may be used to support a molecular formula assignment but cannot serve to document compound purity."
- Key point: A narrow melting point range alone is not sufficient to document the purity of a new compound; HRMS supports a molecular formula but does not prove purity.

### E6

- Location: Elemental Analysis and Accurate Mass Measurement
- Quote: "Elemental analysis Found values for carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen should be within 0.4% of the Calcd values for the proposed formula."
- Key point: Elemental analysis found values for carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen should be within 0.4% of the calculated values.

### E7

- Location: Experimental Section
- Quote: "The editors remind authors that it is unethical to modify reported data or spectra, for example to correct spectral baselines or remove solvent or impurity peaks."
- Key point: Modifying reported data or spectra (for example, correcting baselines or removing solvent or impurity peaks) is unethical.

### E8

- Location: Purity / NMR spectra
- Quote: "It is not acceptable to use peak-editing software or other means to suppress or obscure peaks arising from impurities"
- Key point: Using peak-editing software or other means to suppress or obscure impurity peaks is not acceptable.

### E9

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "All reported yields should represent weighed amounts of isolated and purified products and must be reported in the Experimental Section as both weights and percentages."
- Key point: Reported yields should be weighed amounts of isolated, purified products, given in the Experimental Section as both weights and percentages.

### E10

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "The Editors would like to point out that reported yields above 95%, isomer ratios above 200:1, and enantiomeric excesses above 99% typically aren't realistic without further explanation."
- Key point: The editors point out that yields above 95%, isomer ratios above 200:1, and enantiomeric excesses above 99% are typically not realistic without further explanation.

### E11

- Location: Experimental Section/Computational Methods
- Quote: "Authors have the flexibility to place the experimental content in the main text (Experimental Section), in the Supporting Information, or a combination of both as it best supports the manuscript, so long as the information is accurate and complete." / "These sections should describe methods in sufficient detail to permit repetition of the work by others."
- Key point: Experimental content may go in the main text (Experimental Section), the Supporting Information, or both, as long as it is accurate and complete; it must be detailed enough for others to repeat the work.

### E12

- Location: NMR (primary data)
- Quote: "Authors are requested to furnish primary NMR data files (FID files, acquisition data, processing parameters). All original primary NMR data supporting a submission should be retained and provided if requested."
- Key point: JOC asks authors to provide primary NMR data files (FID files, acquisition data, processing parameters); all original NMR data must be kept and provided on request.

### E13

- Location: Computational Data
- Quote: "The level of theory, basis set, and relevant input parameters should be identified along with the specific program used. The data should include Z-matrices or Cartesian coordinates, grid size (for DFT calculations), and computed total energies of target or optimized structures."
- Key point: The computational section should state the level of theory, basis set, input parameters, and program used, and give coordinates, grid size, and computed total energies.

### E14

- Location: Preprints (Editorial Policies section)
- Quote: "Authors are allowed to deposit an initial version of their manuscript in a preprint repository such as ChemRxiv, arXiv, bioRxiv, or the applicable repository for their discipline prior to submission. Authors must note any use of a preprint repository in the cover letter, include a link to the preprint"
- Key point: JOC allows authors to deposit an initial version of the manuscript in a preprint repository such as ChemRxiv before submission, but this must be stated in the cover letter with a link.

### E15

- Location: Preprints (Editorial Policies section)
- Quote: "Authors are discouraged from posting a preprint or a revised version of the manuscript as a preprint after the manuscript has been submitted to the journal without consulting with the Editor-in-Chief."
- Key point: JOC discourages posting a preprint, or a revised version as a preprint, after submission without first consulting the Editor-in-Chief.

### E16

- Location: Emphasis on Chemical Safety
- Quote: "Authors must emphasize any unexpected, new, and/or significant hazards or risks associated with the reported work. This information should be in the Experimental Section of a full article and included in the main text of a letter."
- Key point: Since 2021, ACS has included a safety requirement in its journals' author guidelines: authors must emphasize any unexpected, new, or significant hazards of the work, in the Experimental section of full papers or in the main text of communications.

### E17

- Location: Emphasis on Chemical Safety
- Quote: "In 2021, in support of ACS's core value of "Professionalism, Safety, and Ethics," ACS Publications added a Safety Considerations requirement to the Author Guidelines of every ACS journal"
- Key point: In 2021, ACS Publications added a "Safety Considerations" requirement to the author guidelines of every one of its journals.

### E18

- Location: Compound Characterization Data
- Quote: "If required data cannot be obtained (a compound is too insoluble to record a carbon NMR, or too unstable to obtain a good elemental analysis, etc.), the reason for the absence of the data should be noted in the Experimental Section"
- Key point: If required data cannot be obtained (for example, a compound is too insoluble to record a carbon NMR), the reason must be stated in the Experimental section.

### E19

- Location: Compound Characterization Data
- Quote: "Purity documentation must also be provided for known compounds whose preparation by a new or modified literature method is reported."
- Key point: Purity documentation is also required for known compounds prepared by a new or modified literature method.

### E20

- Location: Compound Characterization Data (elemental analysis and HRMS)
- Quote: "A new compound that is a branching point, a convergence point, or the final new compound in a synthetic scheme, needs elemental analysis or HRMS data regardless of whether the precursor or successor compounds are fully characterized or previously reported."
- Key point: New compounds that are branching points, convergence points, or the final new compound in a synthetic scheme must have elemental analysis or HRMS data.

### E21

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "When known compounds have been prepared, procedures that were reported in the Experimental Section or Supporting Information of a previous publication should be cited but not reported in detail unless they have been modified."
- Key point: For known compounds prepared by literature methods, cite the original publication rather than rewriting the procedure, unless it was modified.

### E22

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "Reactant, reagent, and catalyst quantities should be given in both weight and molar units. Reaction solvent volumes and reaction times should be reported."
- Key point: Give quantities of reactants, reagents, and catalysts in both weight and molar units; report solvent volumes and reaction times.

### E23

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "If instead a general synthesis procedure reporting only relative molar quantities (as equivalents) is presented, the relative solvent volume also needs to be reported (as the molarity of the limiting reactant or reagent in the reaction mixture)."
- Key point: When a general procedure gives only equivalents, it must also give the concentration (the molarity of the limiting reactant in the reaction mixture).

### E24

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "If the several examples were not all conducted at the same molar scale, the paragraphs describing the individual products should include, along with the yields, the weights and molar amounts of the limiting reactants"
- Key point: If examples were run at different scales, each product paragraph must give the weight and molar amount of the limiting reactant.

### E25

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "When chromatographically or spectroscopically determined conversions of starting material to product are presented in a table [...] a column heading or footnote should identify which quantity is being reported. The isolation and purification of the products for several representative examples should be reported in the Experimental Section, and the yields of isolated product for those examples should be included in the table."
- Key point: When a table gives conversions measured by chromatography or spectroscopy, a column heading or footnote must say which quantity is reported; isolated yields must also be given for several representative examples.

### E26

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "Manuscripts that illustrate a new or modified synthetic method with multiple examples conducted on a submillimolar scale should include one or more examples carried out on a larger scale (1 mmol) to demonstrate the practical utility of the method as a synthetic tool"
- Key point: If all examples of a new method were run on a submillimolar scale, at least one example at 1 mmol scale should be included.

### E27

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "When preparative chromatography is used for product purification, both the stationary phase and solvent should be identified." / "the mixture composition or gradient scheme should be individually reported for each compound."
- Key point: For purification by preparative chromatography, identify both the stationary phase and the solvent; when a series of compounds uses different mixtures, report each compound separately.

### E28

- Location: Synthesis Experiments
- Quote: "For reactions that require heating, identify the temperature and heat source (oil bath, heating mantle, etc.) or the model and manufacturer number if a device is used"
- Key point: For heated reactions, report the temperature and the heat source.

### E29

- Location: General Experimental Methods
- Quote: "Sources of reactants, reagents, and solvents should not be identified except for (1) starting compounds that are unusual or not widely available; (2) materials for which the author has reason to suspect that the source is critical to the outcome of an experiment; and (3) catalysts. In the latter two cases, available purity information should be reported."
- Key point: JOC generally does not want sources of reagents reported, except for unusual starting materials and for materials and catalysts whose source is suspected to affect the results; for the latter two, purity must also be given.

### E30

- Location: Scope of the Journal, (a)
- Quote: "Single or multistep synthetic methods manuscripts and total synthesis manuscripts are expected to demonstrate strategies, transformations, or shortened routes to target structures that show conceptual novelty, not merely the extension of previously reported chemistry to a different class of reaction substrates, reagents, or catalysts."
- Key point: JOC expects synthetic methods and total synthesis papers to show conceptual novelty in strategy, transformations, or routes, not just extend known chemistry to another class of substrates, reagents, or catalysts.

### E31

- Location: Experimental Section
- Quote: "The Experimental Section should describe methods in sufficient detail to permit repetition of the work by others."
- Key point: The Experimental Section should describe methods in enough detail for others to repeat the work.

### E32

- Location: Scope of the Journal, (b)
- Quote: "Manuscripts focusing on mechanistic studies (experimental or theoretical) should show methodological advances or provide novel insight into the course of chemical reactions, rather than only confirming previously established mechanisms."
- Key point: JOC expects mechanistic studies (experimental or theoretical) to show methodological advances or provide new insight into how reactions proceed; merely confirming a known mechanism is not enough.

### E33

- Location: Data Requirements, Computational Data
- Quote: "When computational results are included in a manuscript, complete details of methods and results, reported in sufficient detail to allow other researchers to repeat the computations, should be included either within the article in a Computational Methods section or as Supporting Information."
- Key point: When a paper includes computational results, methods and results must be reported completely, in enough detail for other researchers to reproduce the calculations, in a computational methods section of the main text or in the Supporting Information.
