# Linguistic Society of America 2010: Resolution Recognizing the Scholarly Merit of Language Documentation

- Source: `lsa2010documentation`, [full text](https://lsa.membershipsoftware.org//Files/Advocacy/201001_Resolution_Scholarly_Merit_Lang_Documentation.pdf)
- Version: adopted at the January 2010 annual meeting; ratified by member vote in May 2011

### E1

- Location: first Whereas clause
- Quote: "Whereas the practice of linguistic fieldwork is shifting to a more collaborative endeavor firmly based on ethical responsibilities to speech communities"
- Key point: Linguistic fieldwork is shifting toward collaboration firmly based on ethical responsibilities to speech communities.

### E2

- Location: second Whereas clause
- Quote: "the range of scholarly work to include not only grammars, dictionaries, and text collections, but also archives of primary data, electronic databases, corpora, critical editions of legacy materials, pedagogical works designed for the use of speech communities, software, websites, or other digital media"
- Key point: The range of scholarly work has expanded beyond grammars, dictionaries, and text collections to archives of primary data, corpora, teaching materials for communities, software, and more.

### E3

- Location: Resolution
- Quote: "Therefore the Linguistic Society of America supports the recognition of these materials as scholarly contributions to be given weight in the awarding of advanced degrees and in decisions on hiring, tenure, and promotion of faculty."
- Key point: The Linguistic Society of America supports giving these materials weight as scholarly contributions in degrees, hiring, tenure, and promotion.
