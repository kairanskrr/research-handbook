# Gagnier et al. 2013: The CARE guidelines: consensus-based clinical case reporting guideline development

- Source: `gagnier2013care`, [Journal of Medical Case Reports](https://doi.org/10.1186/1752-1947-7-223), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3844611/) (open access)
- Version: J Med Case Rep 2013;7:223. The same guideline was published simultaneously in several journals.

### E1

- Location: Background
- Quote: "Case reports may generate hypotheses for future clinical studies, prove useful in the evaluation of global convergences of systems-oriented approaches, and guide the individualization and personalization of treatments in clinical practice."
- Key point: Case reports can generate hypotheses for future clinical studies and guide the individualization of treatment.

### E2

- Location: Background
- Quote: "For example, our understanding of the relationship between thalidomide and congenital abnormalities and the use of propranolol for the treatment of infantile hemangiomas began with case reports."
- Key point: Our understanding of the link between thalidomide and congenital abnormalities, and the use of propranolol for infantile hemangiomas, both began with case reports.

### E3

- Location: Background
- Quote: "Written without the benefit of reporting guidelines, case reports often are insufficiently rigorous to be aggregated for data analysis, inform research design, or guide clinical practice."
- Key point: Written without reporting guidelines, case reports are often not rigorous enough to be aggregated for analysis or to guide research and clinical practice.

### E4

- Location: Results, informed consent
- Quote: "We believe that authors have an ethical duty to obtain informed consent from the patient to publish patient information in a case report."
- Key point: Authors have an ethical duty to obtain the patient's informed consent before publishing patient information in a case report.

### E5

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "This 13-item checklist provides a framework to satisfy the need for completeness and transparency for published case reports."
- Key point: CARE is a 13-item checklist for case reports.

### E6

- Location: Limitations
- Quote: "Second, we recognize that causality determinations are a challenge for case reports even when following reporting guidelines."
- Key point: Even when reporting guidelines are followed, determining causality is a challenge for case reports.

### E7

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "Unlike randomized controlled trials, case reports are individual reports related to the care of individual patients where the sample size is one."
- Key point: A case report is a report on the care of an individual patient; the sample size is one.
