# National Cancer Institute: Dietary Assessment Primer

- Source: `nci2025dietprimer`, [National Cancer Institute website](https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/dietary-assessment-primer/)
- Version: read four pages, "Key Concepts about Measurement Error," "Types of Measurement Error," "Effects of Measurement Error," and "Learn More about Misreporting," each marked as last updated December 29, 2025.

### E1

- Location: Key Concepts about Measurement Error
- Quote: "Because dietary intake can rarely be directly observed, we typically rely on self-report instruments, such as 24-hour dietary recalls (24HR), food records, food frequency questionnaires (FFQ), screeners"
- Key point: Dietary intake can rarely be observed directly, so we usually rely on self-report instruments: 24-hour dietary recalls, food records, food frequency questionnaires, and brief screeners.

### E2

- Location: Key Concepts about Measurement Error
- Quote: "In dietary assessment, we are often interested in assessing usual dietary intake (i.e., long-term average) rather than intake on a given day or other short-term time period (i.e., acute intake)."
- Key point: Dietary assessment is often interested in usual intake (the long-term average), not intake on a given day or over a short period.

### E3

- Location: Effects of Measurement Error, Regression Models
- Quote: "When a single dietary exposure is measured with within-person random error only (i.e., without systematic error), the estimate of the" [...] "association between the exposure and the health or disease outcome is always attenuated (i.e., biased toward the null)."
- Key point: When a single dietary exposure has only within-person random error, the estimated association between exposure and health outcome is always attenuated (biased toward no association).

### E4

- Location: Effects of Measurement Error, Regression Models
- Quote: "When a single dietary exposure contains both systematic and within-person random error, the estimate of the association with the outcome may be biased in either direction from the null, i.e., it may be attenuated or inflated."
- Key point: When there is both systematic and random error, the estimated association may be biased in either direction: attenuated or inflated.

### E5

- Location: Effects of Measurement Error, Regression Models
- Quote: "the estimated association between the main dietary exposure and the outcome may be attenuated or inflated because of possible" [...] "residual confounding with other dietary covariates also measured with error."
- Key point: When the model also includes other mismeasured dietary variables, the estimated association for the main dietary exposure may be attenuated or inflated because of residual confounding.

### E6

- Location: Learn More about Misreporting
- Quote: "Misreporting in self-report dietary assessment is considered to be unavoidable and can include both under- and overreporting."
- Key point: Misreporting in self-reported dietary assessment is considered unavoidable and includes both under- and overreporting.

### E7

- Location: Learn More about Misreporting
- Quote: "Alternatively, a sensitivity analysis can determine whether findings differ based on inclusion or exclusion of energy misreporters. Researchers should carefully review data and exercise caution in discarding data."
- Key point: A sensitivity analysis can check whether findings differ when energy misreporters are included or excluded; researchers should examine their data carefully and be cautious about removing data.

### E8

- Location: Learn More about Misreporting
- Quote: "Energy underreporting has been shown to be associated with various personal characteristics, but most consistently with body mass index."
- Key point: Energy underreporting is associated with various personal characteristics, most consistently with body mass index.

### E9

- Location: Effects of Measurement Error, Table 1
- Quote: "Averaging repeat observations, or statistical modeling if number of repeats is limited"
- Key point: Remedies for within-person random error: average repeated observations, or use statistical modeling when the number of repeats is limited.
