### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "In catalysis science, important catalyst performance metrics include activity, selectivity, and the deactivation profile, which enable comparisons between new and standard catalysts."
- Key point: In catalysis, the key performance metrics are activity, selectivity, and the deactivation profile, which allow new catalysts to be compared with standard ones.

- Key point: In industry, catalyst performance under standard conditions is often measured in banks of nearly identical bench-scale reactors and in pilot-scale reactors.

### E5

- Location: §2.2.1 Best Practices (Accurate Reaction Kinetics), p. 2595
- Quote: "It is essential that measurements of turnover frequencies (TOFs, or site-normalized catalytic reaction rates), turnover numbers (TONs, or numbers of turns of a catalytic cycle, equivalent to site-normalized catalytic productivities), and selectivities be accurate, reproducible, uncorrupted by mass or heat transport limitations, and cover wide ranges of conversions."
- Key point: Measurements of turnover frequencies (site-normalized reaction rates), turnover numbers, and selectivities must be accurate, reproducible, free of mass- and heat-transfer limitations, and cover a wide range of conversions.

### E6

- Location: §2.2.1, p. 2595
- Quote: "reported TOF values should be based on rate measurements rather than on conversion at a single time point, and the values should be checked by systematically varying the concentrations of all species present in the reactor."
- Key point: Reported turnover frequencies should be based on rate measurements, not on conversion at a single time point, and checked by systematically varying the concentrations of all species in the reactor.

### E7

- Location: §2.2 Heterogeneous Catalysis, p. 2595
- Quote: "(1) optimum reaction conditions for one catalyst may be markedly suboptimal for another; and (2) the nature and number of the active sites may evolve during the test, complicating normalization of parameters such as rates and yields."
- Key point: Comparing heterogeneous catalysts has two common difficulties: conditions optimal for one catalyst may be poor for another, and the nature and number of active sites may change during the test, which complicates normalizing rates and yields.

### E8

- Location: §2.2.1 (Standard Materials), p. 2595
- Quote: "Cooperation between research groups in a blind, round-robin series of measurements of reaction rates, site densities, and TOF values would be valuable to characterize simple benchmark catalytic materials and reactions, to identify subtleties in such measurements, and to determine realistic error bars."
- Key point: Blind round-robin measurements across research groups (the same samples measured in turn in each lab) help uncover subtle measurement problems and establish realistic error bars.

### E9

- Location: §2.2.1 (Catalyst Deactivation), pp. 2595–2596
- Quote: "Accelerated catalyst aging tests can be expeditious, but these tests are risky unless the causes of deactivation are known and extrapolations justified on the basis of experiments."
- Key point: Accelerated aging tests save time but are risky unless the causes of deactivation are known and the extrapolation is backed by experiments.

### E10

- Location: §2.3 Catalysis by Molecular and Supported Molecular Species, p. 2596
- Quote: "For example, a laboratory that tries to repeat a catalytic reaction described in the literature may find much less than the reported activity or none at all. Discrepancies may be caused by differences in reagents and protocols that are incompletely documented."
- Key point: A lab repeating a catalytic reaction from the literature may find far less than the reported activity, or none at all; the cause may be differences in reagents and protocols that were incompletely documented.

### E11

- Location: §2.3.1 (Kinetic Measurements), p. 2597
- Quote: "Selectivity must be reported with the corresponding conversion"
- Key point: Selectivity must be reported together with the corresponding conversion.

### E12

- Location: §2.3.1 (Kinetic Measurements), p. 2597
- Quote: "To avoid reporting nonintrinsic kinetics, the absence of mass/heat transfer and mixing effects must be verified."
- Key point: To avoid reporting nonintrinsic kinetics as intrinsic, you must verify that there are no mass-transfer, heat-transfer, or mixing effects.

### E13

- Location: §2.3.1 (Kinetic Measurements), p. 2597
- Quote: "Mass balance should be verified"
- Key point: The mass balance should be verified.

### E14

- Location: §2.3.1 (Specification of Methodology), p. 2597
- Quote: "When reporting quantities such as the TON or TOF, they should be accompanied by a clear statement about how values were obtained from the raw data. This statement must include a description of how the number of active sites was counted or estimated, using some transparent and reproducible method."
- Key point: When reporting turnover numbers or turnover frequencies, state clearly how they were obtained from the raw data, including the transparent, reproducible method used to count or estimate the number of active sites.

### E15

- Location: §2.3.1 (Selectivity), p. 2597
- Quote: "When the selectivities of several catalysts are compared, values should be measured at the same conversion."
- Key point: When comparing the selectivities of several catalysts, measure them at the same conversion.

### E16

- Location: §2.3.1 (Catalyst Deactivation), p. 2597
- Quote: "In particular, recycling studies in which product yield is measured solely at high conversion do not demonstrate catalyst stability."
- Key point: Recycling studies that measure product yield only at high conversion do not demonstrate catalyst stability.

### E17

- Location: §2.3.1 (Comparisons and Controls), p. 2597
- Quote: "When reporting data for a new catalyst, it is important to compare the performance with wellestablished catalysts and/or state-of-the-art catalysts measured under the same experimental conditions."
- Key point: When reporting a new catalyst, compare it with well-established or state-of-the-art catalysts under the same experimental conditions. (The original's "wellestablished" lost its hyphen in PDF text extraction.)

### E18

- Location: §2.3.1 (Comparisons and Controls), p. 2597
- Quote: "It is essential to perform appropriate control experiments without catalyst present to establish the significance of uncatalyzed reactions"
- Key point: Control experiments without catalyst are essential to establish how far the reaction proceeds uncatalyzed.

### E19

- Location: §2.1.1 Best Practices (Sharing Existing Results), p. 2593
- Quote: "Making the computational input and output files available online (in addition to the output results) would significantly enhance the reader's ability to reproduce the published results."
- Key point: Putting the computational input and output files online greatly improves readers' ability to reproduce the results.

### E20

- Location: §2.4.1 Best Practices (Standardized Reporting), p. 2598
- Quote: "Importantly, currents (or current densities) should be reported as a function of overpotential and normalized to several different catalyst parameters so that all researchers can better understand the catalyst performance."
- Key point: In electrocatalysis, report currents (or current densities) as a function of overpotential, normalized to several different catalyst parameters.
