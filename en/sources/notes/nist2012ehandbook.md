# NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, Chapter 2: Measurement Process Characterization

- Source: `nist2012ehandbook`, [original](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/mpc.htm)
- Version: online statistics handbook of the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) and SEMATECH, 2012 edition (DOI 10.18434/M32189), freely available on a US government website, accessed 2026-10-06. Locations give section numbers on the web page.

### E1

- Location: 2.3.1 Issues in calibration
- Quote: "The purpose of calibration is to eliminate or reduce bias in the user's measurement system relative to the reference base."
- Key point: The purpose of calibration is to eliminate or reduce bias in your measurement system relative to the reference base.

### E2

- Location: 2.1.2 What is a check standard?
- Quote: "Check standard methodology is a tool for collecting data on the measurement process to expose errors that afflict the process over time."
- Key point: The check standard method collects data on the measurement process itself to expose errors that arise over time.

### E3

- Location: 2.1.2
- Quote: "An artifact check standard must be close in material content and geometry to the test items that are measured in the workload."
- Key point: An artifact check standard must be close in material and geometry to the test items routinely measured.

### E4

- Location: 2.2.2 How are bias and variability controlled?
- Quote: "A change in the measurement on the check standard that persists at a constant level over several measurement sequences indicates possible:"
- Key point: A shift in the check standard's measurements that persists at a new level over several measurement sequences indicates a possible change in the reference standard, the check standard, or the measurement procedure.

### E5

- Location: 2.5.1 Issues
- Quote: "it is important for laboratory personnel who are approaching uncertainty analysis for the first time to be aware of the resources required and to carefully lay out a plan for data collection and analysis."
- Key point: Anyone doing uncertainty analysis for the first time should know the resources it takes and plan data collection and analysis carefully.

### E6

- Location: 2.5.2 Approach
- Quote: "random errors cannot be corrected, and biases can, theoretically at least, be corrected or eliminated from the measurement result."
- Key point: Random errors cannot be corrected; biases can, at least in theory, be corrected or eliminated.

### E7

- Location: 2.5.2, Basic ISO tenets
- Quote: "All biases are assumed to be corrected and any uncertainty is the uncertainty of the correction."
- Key point: Following the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) approach, all biases are assumed corrected, and what remains is the uncertainty of the correction itself.

### E8

- Location: 2.5.2, Rule of quadrature
- Quote: "All uncertainty components (standard deviations) are combined by root-sum-squares (quadrature) to arrive at a 'standard uncertainty'"
- Key point: All uncertainty components (expressed as standard deviations) are combined by root-sum-of-squares to give the standard uncertainty.

### E9

- Location: 2.5.2
- Quote: "In the computation of the final uncertainty it makes no difference how the components are classified because the ISO guidelines treat type A and type B evaluations in the same manner."
- Key point: When computing the final uncertainty, it makes no difference whether a component is Type A or Type B; both are treated the same way.

### E10

- Location: 2.5.4 Type B evaluations
- Quote: "Reference standards calibrated by another laboratory Physical constants used in the calculation of the reported value Environmental effects that cannot be sampled Possible configuration/geometry misalignment in the instrument Lack of resolution of the instrument"
- Key point: Common Type B sources: reference standards calibrated by another laboratory, physical constants used in the calculation, environmental effects that cannot be sampled, possible misalignment or geometric error of the instrument, and insufficient instrument resolution.

### E11

- Location: 2.5.4
- Quote: "The uncertainty will usually be reported as an expanded uncertainty, U , which is converted to the standard uncertainty," / "If the k factor is not known or documented, it is probably conservative to assume that k = 2 ."
- Key point: Calibration certificates and similar documents usually report an expanded uncertainty U, which must be divided by k to convert it to a standard uncertainty; if k is not stated, assuming k = 2 is probably conservative.

### E12

- Location: 2.5.4.1 Standard deviations from assumed distributions
- Quote: "The ISO guidelines do not allow worst-case estimates of bias to be added to the other components, but require they in some way be converted to equivalent standard deviations."
- Key point: A worst-case estimate of bias cannot simply be added to the other components; it must first be converted to an equivalent standard deviation.

### E13

- Location: 2.5.4.1
- Quote: "The uniform distribution leads to the most conservative estimate of uncertainty; i.e., it gives the largest standard deviation."
- Key point: Of the three assumed distributions (uniform, triangular, normal), the uniform distribution gives the largest standard deviation and is the most conservative.

### E14

- Location: 2.5.6 Uncertainty budgets and sensitivity coefficients
- Quote: "Uncertainty components are listed in a table along with their corresponding sensitivity coefficients, standard deviations and degrees of freedom."
- Key point: An uncertainty budget is a table listing each component with its sensitivity coefficient, standard deviation, and degrees of freedom.

### E15

- Location: 2.5.2
- Quote: "For large degrees of freedom, it is suggested to use k = 2 to approximate 95% coverage."
- Key point: For large degrees of freedom, use k = 2 to approximate 95% coverage.

### E16

- Location: 2.5.7.1 Degrees of freedom
- Quote: "This is particularly troublesome if there are large components of uncertainty with small degrees of freedom."
- Key point: If large uncertainty components have few degrees of freedom (e.g., only a few repeats), the degrees of freedom of the combined uncertainty become very problematic.

### E17

- Location: 2.1.1 What are the issues for characterization?
- Quote: "One-time tests and special tests or destructive tests are difficult to characterize."
- Key point: One-time tests, special tests, and destructive tests are difficult to characterize as measurement processes.
