# Nanda 2025: My Research Process: Key Mindsets - Truth-Seeking, Prioritisation, Moving Fast

- Source: `nanda2025mindsets`, [original](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/cbBwwm4jW6AZctymL)
- Version: AI Alignment Forum post, 2025-04-27, accessed 2026-10-05. Locations give the page's section headings.

### E1

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "You need to be ruthless about dropping less promising directions where necessary. But beware switching costs - if you switch all the time without exploring anything properly you’ll learn nothing!"
- Key point: Be ruthless about dropping less promising directions, but beware switching costs: if you switch all the time without exploring anything properly, you learn nothing.

### E2

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "I like to explicitly write goals down and regularly check in that they’re being achieved"
- Key point: Write your goals down explicitly and check regularly that you are making progress toward them.

### E3

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "Goal: What is the overall north star of the project? (generally measured in months) [...] Sub-goal: What is my current bit of the project working towards (measured in weeks) [...] Objective: What is the concrete short-term outcome I am aiming for right now (measured in days, e.g. 1 week)"
- Key point: Three levels of goals: the project's overall goal (measured in months), the current sub-goal (weeks), and the immediate objective (days).

### E4

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "I recommend actually writing a plan, and estimate how long each step will take, at least for the current research stage you’re in. You don’t need to take it very seriously, and you’ll totally deviate a ton."
- Key point: Write a plan and estimate how long each step will take, at least for your current research stage; you don't need to follow it rigidly, and deviating is normal, but writing it forces you to think the project through.

### E5

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "If you feel stuck, set a 5 minute timer and brainstorm possible things you could do!"
- Key point: When you feel stuck, set a 5-minute timer and brainstorm everything you could do.

### E6

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "Prioritising and executing are different mental modes and should not be done simultaneously."
- Key point: Prioritizing and executing are different mental modes and should not be done at the same time; set aside separate time for reflection and for execution.

### E7

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "Having a weekly review can be incredibly useful - where you zoom out and check in on what’s going on, any current issues, etc."
- Key point: Hold a weekly review: zoom out and check on progress and current issues.

### E8

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "What’s consumed the most time recently? [...] What’s blocked me? [...] What mistakes have I made, and how could I systematically change my approach so it doesn’t happen again in future?"
- Key point: Questions for a weekly review: What has consumed the most time recently? What has blocked me? What mistakes have I made, and how could I systematically change my approach so they don't recur?

### E9

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "Some get stuck in rabbit holes and need to get better at moving on. Others get caught in analysis paralysis and never do anything, because they’re always waiting for the (non-existent) perfect opportunity."
- Key point: Two opposite failure modes: some people get stuck in rabbit holes and won't move on; others get caught in analysis paralysis and never act. First figure out which way you lean.

### E10

- Location: Moving Fast
- Quote: "Definition: A feedback loop is the process from having an experiment idea and to results. Tight feedback loops are when the time taken is short."
- Key point: A feedback loop is the process from having an experiment idea to seeing results; the shorter that time, the tighter the loop.

### E11

- Location: Moving Fast
- Quote: "It is far better to do a quick and dirty experiment to get some preliminary signs of life than an extremely long and expensive experiment that will produce conclusive data but only after weeks of work."
- Key point: A quick and dirty experiment that gives preliminary signs of life is far better than spending weeks on an expensive experiment that produces a conclusive answer.

### E12

- Location: Moving Fast
- Quote: "Thus, a key question to ask yourself is: if this direction is doomed, how could I discover this as fast as humanly possible?"
- Key point: Fail fast: ask yourself, "If this direction is doomed, how could I discover that as fast as possible?"

### E13

- Location: Moving Fast
- Quote: "One thing that might be helpful is to log how you spend your time and then reflect on it, and ways you might be able to go faster next time."
- Key point: Log how you spend your time, then reflect on how you could go faster next time.

### E14

- Location: Moving Fast
- Quote: "It’s easy to e.g. be sloppy in the name of speed and introduce many bugs that cost you time in the long-run."
- Key point: The author acknowledges the cost of chasing speed: being sloppy in the name of speed introduces bugs that cost more time in the long run.

### E15

- Location: Prioritisation
- Quote: "But even then, feel free to deviate - mentors typically have good research priors, but you know way more about your specific problem than them, which can be enough to make better decisions than even a very senior researcher"
- Key point: Mentors usually have good research priors, but you know far more about your specific problem, which is sometimes enough to make a better call than a senior researcher.

### E16

- Location: Truth Seeking
- Quote: "Asking another researcher, especially a mentor is a great source of perspective"
- Key point: Talking to another researcher, especially a mentor, is a great source of perspective and of alternative explanations.
