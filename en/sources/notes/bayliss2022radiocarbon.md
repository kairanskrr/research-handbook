# Bayliss and Marshall 2022: Radiocarbon Dating and Chronological Modelling: Guidelines and Best Practice

- Source: `bayliss2022radiocarbon`, PDF on the Historic England website: https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/radiocarbon-dating-chronological-modelling/heag312-radiocarbon-dating/ (when the site returns 403 to scripts, use the Internet Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/20220926101250/https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/radiocarbon-dating-chronological-modelling/heag312-radiocarbon-dating/)
- Version: Written by Alex Bayliss and Peter Marshall, published by Historic England in September 2022. The document says it is aimed at archaeological projects in England and should be used together with specific advice from radiocarbon laboratories and modelling specialists. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: 3.2.2 Archaeological criteria for identifying suitable samples
- Quote: "The association between the datable material and the archaeological activity that is of interest is paramount (Waterbolk 1971)." / "The basis of this inference, and its security, must be specifically considered for every potential sample."
- Key point: The association between the datable material and the archaeological activity of interest is paramount; the basis and security of this inference must be considered specifically for every potential sample.

### E2

- Location: 3.2.2 Archaeological criteria for identifying suitable samples
- Quote: "Radiocarbon [...] dating does not date contexts, it dates samples. So, the calibrated radiocarbon dates can only be constrained using the stratigraphic sequence of contexts if the dated samples were freshly deposited in the contexts from which they were recovered."
- Key point: Radiocarbon dating dates samples, not contexts; the stratigraphic sequence can constrain the dates only if the samples were freshly deposited in the contexts they came from.

### E3

- Location: 3.2.2 Archaeological criteria for identifying suitable samples
- Quote: "The golden rule is that every potential sample should be considered residual unless there is a plausible argument showing that it was freshly deposited in the context from which it was recovered."
- Key point: The "golden rule": treat every potential sample as residual unless there is a plausible argument that it was freshly deposited in its context.

### E4

- Location: 3.2.2, Single-entity dating
- Quote: "This is a strategy that minimises the risk that the submitted sample will contain residual or reworked material, by dating material that certainly derives from a single organism (e.g. a single cereal grain)."
- Key point: Single-entity dating: date only material that certainly comes from a single organism (e.g., one cereal grain), minimizing the risk that the sample contains residual material.

### E5

- Location: 3.2.3 Scientific criteria for identifying suitable samples, Old-wood effect
- Quote: "By far the most common source of error of this type is the old-wood effect, where dates are obtained on wood or charcoal from a long-lived tree." / "the carbon from the centre of a 300-year-old oak tree will be 300 years old while that tree is still growing" / "twigs or the outer rings of the tree (a single years' growth is optimal, but the number of growth rings to bark should be recorded if a single growth-ring cannot be isolated)," / "if twigs are not available, samples have to be taken from short-lived species (e.g. Corylus avellana) or branch-wood, although in this case a wood-offset of a few decades cannot be ruled out"
- Key point: By far the most common source of such error is the old-wood effect, from dating wood or charcoal from long-lived trees; the carbon at the center of a 300-year-old oak is already 300 years old while the tree is still growing. Wood and charcoal samples should be twigs or the outer rings of the tree; failing that, use short-lived species (e.g., hazel) or branches, though an offset of decades still cannot be ruled out.

### E6

- Location: 3.3 Statistical simulation and sample selection
- Quote: "Sometimes suitable samples are not available, and the temptation to submit inferior material for dating should be resisted. Dates on such samples almost always mislead more than they inform, and hamper the understanding of past chronologies"
- Key point: When suitable samples are not available, resist the temptation to submit inferior material; such dates almost always mislead more than they inform.

- Quote: "It gives practical advice on the application of these methods within archaeological projects in England. It should be used in conjunction with advice given by radiocarbon laboratories and modelling specialists on specific projects"
- Key point: The guidelines give practical advice for archaeological projects in England and should be used together with advice from radiocarbon laboratories and modelling specialists on specific projects.

### E8

- Location: "Key facts: Bayesian Chronological Modelling" after 2.2.1-2.2.2
- Quote: "Statistical methods are required to handle relationships between dated samples. Bayesian statistics enable calibrated radiocarbon dates to be combined with other information we might have about a chronological problem, producing posterior beliefs that take account of all the evidence. Prior beliefs can be simply that all the dated samples are from a single site or associated with the same kind of pottery, but could include relative sequences provided by stratigraphy, seriation or the growth-rings in wood or charcoal."
- Key point: Statistical methods are needed to handle relationships between dated samples; Bayesian statistics combine calibrated radiocarbon dates with other information we have about the chronological problem, producing "posterior" beliefs that take account of all the evidence. "Prior" information can be as simple as "the samples all come from the same site," or can include the order given by stratigraphy, seriation, or tree rings.

### E9
