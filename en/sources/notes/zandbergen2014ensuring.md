# Zandbergen 2014: Ensuring Confidentiality of Geocoded Health Data: Assessing Geographic Masking Strategies for Individual-Level Data

- Source: `zandbergen2014ensuring`, [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4590956/), doi:10.1155/2014/567049
- Version: Advances in Medicine 2014:567049, a review under a CC BY license. The author is a geographer at the University of New Mexico, USA, discussing address geocoding, reverse geocoding, and geographic masking in the context of public health data. Locations give the section name.

### E1

- Location: Section 2, Individual-Level Data and Geocoding
- Quote: "This confirms that geocoding has become firmly established as an analytical tool in public health research"
- Key point: The author holds that geocoding (turning addresses into points on a map) has become an established analytical tool in public health research.

### E2

- Location: Section 2, Individual-Level Data and Geocoding
- Quote: "Geocoding is not error-free. Typically, a certain number of records do not geocode due to incomplete or incorrect information. Geocoded locations may also not be accurate due to incorrect reference information or errors in the geocoding process"
- Key point: Geocoding is not error-free: some records do not match because of incomplete or incorrect information, and matched locations may be inaccurate because of errors in the reference data or the geocoding process.

### E3

- Location: Section 3, Reverse Geocoding
- Quote: "Publishing a location as coordinates (e.g., latitude/longitude) means that these can be plotted on a map and then associated with an address. Publishing a map in paper or digital form also means that the locations can be associated with an address."
- Key point: Publishing coordinates or a map of points lets others link the points back to addresses.

### E4

- Location: Section 3, Reverse Geocoding
- Quote: "created a hypothetical map of geocoded patient addresses and were able to correctly identify 79% of the addresses using manual reverse geocoding techniques in GIS."
- Key point: A study the author cites created a hypothetical map of geocoded patient addresses and correctly identified 79% of the addresses through manual reverse geocoding in a geographic information system (GIS).

### E5

- Location: Section 5, Confidentiality Protection Strategies
- Quote: "Many spatial analytical techniques, such as general point-pattern analysis and cluster detection, are much less powerful or simply not possible using aggregated data."
- Key point: Once data are aggregated into areas, many methods such as point-pattern analysis and cluster detection become much less powerful or impossible.

### E6

- Location: Section 6, Ensuring Confidentiality Using Geographic Masking
- Quote: "Geographic masking is the process of altering the coordinates of point location data to limit the risk of reidentification upon release of the data."
- Key point: Geographic masking alters the coordinates of point data before release to limit the risk of reidentification.

### E7

- Location: Section 7, Different Approaches to Geographic Masking
- Quote: "Determining the amount of displacement necessary to achieve confidentiality has been addressed by several of the studies on geographic masking [56], but no universal guidelines have emerged. However, it is widely agreed upon that the amount of displacement should be inversely proportional to the local population density"
- Key point: There is no universal guideline for how much displacement is enough, but it is widely agreed that displacement should be inversely related to local population density (the sparser the population, the farther the shift).

### E8

- Location: Section 10, Effects of Masking on Spatial-Analytic Methods
- Quote: "Findings indicated a consistent tradeoff between the amount of perturbation and the accuracy of the analytical results"
- Key point: A study the author cites found a consistent tradeoff: the more perturbation, the less accurate the analytical results.

### E9

- Location: Section 7, Different Approaches to Geographic Masking
- Quote: "The earlier review of applications of geographic masking to real-world datasets has indicated that some studies do not mention the specific technique by name."
- Key point: Reviewing real-world applications, the author found that some studies do not name the masking technique they used.
