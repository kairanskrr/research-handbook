# Alvesson & Sandberg 2020: The Problematizing Review: A Counterpoint to Elsbach and Van Knippenberg's Argument for Integrative Reviews

- Source: `alvesson2020problematizing`, [accepted-manuscript PDF in the City St George's open repository (City Research Online)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/34063/1/JMS.review.article.Alvesson%26%20Sandberg.pdf) ([repository record](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/34063/), [publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1111/joms.12582))
- Version: Journal of Management Studies 57(6), 2020, pp. 1290–1304. Read the authors' accepted manuscript (Accepted Version) made public in their institutional repository, which states it may be used for personal research, study and non-profit purposes. The accepted manuscript has no journal page numbers, so locations give section headings.

> Why this source: a complement to `alvesson2013has`. It spells out the six principles of problematization in more detail (saying what each step involves), gives an example from leadership research, and lists criticisms the method may face, in the authors' own words.

### E1

- Location: AN ALTERNATIVE: THE PROBLEMATIZING REVIEW
- Quote: "the primary aim of the problematizing review is to re-evaluate existing understandings of phenomena, with a particular view to challenging and reimagining our current ways of thinking about them"
- Key point: The main aim of a problematizing review is to re-evaluate existing understandings of a phenomenon, challenging and reimagining how we currently think about it.

### E2

- Location: The ideal of reflexivity
- Quote: "In a problematizing review, the author is guided by a constant consideration and occasional questioning of assumptions, perspectives and vocabularies in order to come up with, test and possibly suggest alternative ideas and ways of thinking about a phenomenon or domain."
- Key point: The author of a problematizing review keeps attending to, and now and then questions, existing assumptions, perspectives and vocabularies in order to come up with, test and possibly propose another way of thinking about the phenomenon.

### E3

- Location: The ideal of reflexivity
- Quote: "For example, the overwhelming majority of the leadership literature proceeds from `the assumption that the employees sampled innately need or desire leadership' (Hunter et al., 2007, p. 436). However, one could see this need, desire or interest in leadership among employees as a false assumption, or as a more open issue, and even suggest that some people do not see themselves as followers and want to be spared leadership at work"
- Key point: Example: most leadership research assumes employees innately need or want leadership; problematization can treat this as a false assumption or an open question, and even suggest that some people do not see themselves as followers and would rather not be led at work.

### E4

- Location: Not accumulating but problematizing
- Quote: "Rather than undertaking a surface reading of a large volume of available studies, a deep reading of the foundational texts, and of a moderate number of representative texts of a field, enables the author to better identify, articulate and challenge problematic, taken-for-granted assumptions in a specific domain"
- Key point: Rather than skimming many studies, read a field's foundational texts and a moderate number of representative texts in depth; this makes it easier to identify, articulate and challenge taken-for-granted assumptions.

### E5

- Location: Not accumulating but problematizing
- Quote: "(1) identifying a domain of literature; (2) identifying and articulating assumptions underlying this domain; (3) evaluating them and focusing on more problematic or limiting elements; (4) developing an alternative assumption ground with the potential to become the start of a novel theoretical contribution; (5) considering it in relation to its audience (what is seen as new, credible and interesting); and (6) evaluating the alternative assumption ground"
- Key point: A more detailed version of the six steps: step 3 focuses on the more problematic or limiting assumptions; the alternative assumptions in step 4 should have the potential to start a new theoretical contribution; step 5 considers what the audience will see as new, credible and interesting.

### E6

- Location: Not accumulating but problematizing
- Quote: "The last of these is important in order to assess the possibility of having a new, counterintuitive conversation with others in the field that will inspire new ways of thinking on a subject"
- Key point: The last step, evaluating the alternative assumptions, matters because it tests whether you can open a new, counterintuitive conversation with others in the field that inspires new thinking on the subject.

### E7

- Location: Not accumulating but problematizing
- Quote: "Identifying or constructing a domain of literature provides a way in to picking some texts, but careful reading of these may inspire a revision of the literature domain that will be the final target of the research question."
- Key point: Choosing a literature domain is only an entry point for picking texts; careful reading may lead you to revise the domain, so the one your final research question targets may differ from where you started.

### E8

- Location: FINAL REMARKS
- Quote: "the problematizing review we have proposed may be criticized for several disadvantages and problems, such as for underutilizing a whole set of studies in a knowledge area targeted for a review publication; for focusing on underlying assumptions that are hard to detect; for allowing too much discretion to the researcher doing the review work; and for being too demanding."
- Key point: Limitations the authors acknowledge: it may underuse the many studies in a field, it focuses on assumptions that are hard to detect, it gives the researcher too much discretion, and it is very demanding.

### E9

- Location: FINAL REMARKS
- Quote: "We also acknowledge that there may be a problem if more energy goes into challenging assumptions than into working out and refining or testing well-founded and productive ideas" / "The pursuit of novelty and everyone wanting to develop their own theory is not unproblematic."
- Key point: It is also a problem if more effort goes into challenging assumptions than into refining and testing well-founded ideas; everyone chasing novelty and wanting their own theory has downsides too.

### E10

- Location: Less is more (end of the section)
- Quote: "The idea with the problematizing review is, of course, not to dismiss everything that has been done or to problematize for its own sake. It is also important to highlight high-quality studies and ideas."
- Key point: Problematization does not mean dismissing all earlier work or questioning for its own sake; it is also important to highlight high-quality studies and ideas.
