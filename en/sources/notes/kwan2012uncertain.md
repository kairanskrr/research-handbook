# Kwan 2012: The Uncertain Geographic Context Problem

- Source: `kwan2012uncertain`, [PDF (author's website)](http://meipokwan.org/Paper/Kwan_UGCoP_2012.pdf), doi:10.1080/00045608.2012.687349
- Version: Annals of the Association of American Geographers 102(5):958–968 (2012). We read the publisher's version PDF hosted on the author's personal website (Internet Archive snapshot). The author is the geographer Mei-Po Kwan; the paper starts from neighborhood-effects and environmental health research. Locations give page numbers.

### E1

- Location: pp. 958–959
- Quote: "results about these effects can be affected by the zoning scheme or geographic scale of the areal units used [...] This is the well-known modifiable areal unit problem (MAUP), which has received much attention to date."
- Key point: When studying an effect with data for areal units, results can be affected by the zoning scheme or geographic scale of the units; this is the modifiable areal unit problem (MAUP).

### E2

- Location: p. 959
- Quote: "findings about the effects of area-based attributes could be affected by how contextual units or neighborhoods are geographically delineated and the extent to which these areal units deviate from the “true causally relevant” geographic context"
- Key point: A further problem: findings can be affected by how contextual units such as "neighborhoods" are delineated and how far they deviate from the geographic context that actually matters.

### E3

- Location: p. 959
- Quote: "It arises because of the spatial uncertainty in the actual areas that exert contextual influences on the individuals being studied and the temporal uncertainty in the timing and duration in which individuals experienced these contextual influences."
- Key point: This "uncertain geographic context problem" (UGCoP) has two sources: uncertainty about which area actually exerts contextual influence on a person (spatial uncertainty), and about when and for how long the person is exposed (temporal uncertainty).

### E4

- Location: p. 959
- Quote: "It is perhaps a major reason why research findings concerning the effects of social and physical environments on health behaviors and outcomes are often inconsistent, given that past studies on the same issue (e.g., obesity) often used different contextual units"
- Key point: Studies of the same problem (e.g., obesity) often use different contextual units, which may be a major reason why findings on how environments affect health are often inconsistent.

### E5

- Location: p. 960
- Quote: "defined neighborhood around each participant’s home as a 1-km or 3-km circular zone (Berke et al. 2007), as a 1-km road network buffer (Frank et al. 2005)"
- Key point: Example: different studies defined the "neighborhood" as a 1-km or 3-km circular zone around the home, a 1-km road network buffer, and so on.

### E6

- Location: p. 963
- Quote: "an explicit statement about what contextual units will be used, how well they approximate the true geographic context, and what temporal attributes of individuals and contextual influences will be taken into account by these contextual units should be given."
- Key point: Studies should state explicitly which contextual units they use, how well these approximate the true geographic context, and what temporal factors they take into account.

### E7

- Location: pp. 963–964
- Quote: "the model with the best fit is not necessarily the one that uses the true geographic context. [...] This means that appropriateness of contextual units cannot be justified using model fit as a criterion."
- Key point: The best-fitting model does not necessarily use the true geographic context, so model fit cannot be used to show that the contextual units were chosen correctly.

### E8

- Location: p. 964
- Quote: "An activity space is the area containing all locations that an individual visits as a result of his or her daily activities and travel"
- Key point: An activity space is the area containing all the locations a person visits through daily activities and travel; the author suggests it may approximate the true geographic context better than administrative areas.

### E9

- Location: p. 966, note 2
- Quote: "A major concern with collecting GPS data in health research is participants’ privacy and data confidentiality, because it could be possible to identify a person’s identity through reverse geocoding if data are not handled carefully."
- Key point: A major concern with collecting GPS data is participants' privacy and data confidentiality: if handled carelessly, a person could be identified through reverse geocoding (finding an address from coordinates).

### E10

- Location: p. 966, note 2
- Quote: "Further, no maps or displays of the home or activity sites visited by participants or their daily paths can be printed or disseminated."
- Key point: In the author's example, a study of smokeless tobacco in Appalachia, no maps or displays of participants' homes, visited sites, or daily paths could be printed or disseminated.

### E11

- Location: p. 966, note 2
- Quote: "In countries without strict regulations and in situations where people provide information without knowing or agreeing to its use for research purposes (e.g., social networking Web sites such as Facebook Places, Twitter, and Foursquare), there might be no informed consent and human subject protection protocol and issues of privacy violation can be a serious concern."
- Key point: In countries without strict regulations, or where people provide information without knowing or agreeing to its research use (e.g., check-ins on social networking sites), there may be no informed consent or human subjects protection at all, and privacy violations could be serious.
