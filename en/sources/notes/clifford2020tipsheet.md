# Clifford: Tipsheet – Interviewing Elites (Duke Initiative on Survey Methodology)

- Source: `clifford2020tipsheet`, [Original](https://dism.duke.edu/files/2020/05/Tipsheet-Elite_Interviews.pdf)
- Version: A tipsheet from the Duke Initiative on Survey Methodology (DISM), Duke University, by Scott Clifford (DISM research assistant), 4 pages. The file is undated; the server shows it was uploaded in May 2020. Its references include Goldstein 2002, "Getting in the Door."

### E1

- Location: p. 1, Getting in the Door
- Quote: "Send an advance letter or email o Make it official o Give the respondent plenty of time to respond o Spell out basics of the research and the amount of time it will demand, but avoid giving too much detail about the nature of the research project. o Cover the ground rules (how the information will be recorded, used, etc.). o Keep it short and clear" / "Follow up with a phone call repeating this information" / "Don't be afraid to use respondents to gain access to other respondents"
- Key point: Send a formal advance letter or email and give the person plenty of time to respond; explain the basics of the research and how much time it will take, without too much detail about the study; explain the ground rules (how information will be recorded and used); keep it short and clear. Then follow up by phone and repeat this information. You can use respondents to reach other respondents.

### E2

- Location: p. 1, Getting in the Door
- Quote: "Ask for an intermediate length of time for interview (e.g., 20-45 minutes)."
- Key point: When requesting an interview, ask for an intermediate length of time, e.g., 20–45 minutes.
