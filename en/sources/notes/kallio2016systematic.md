# Kallio et al. 2016: Systematic methodological review: developing a framework for a qualitative semi-structured interview guide

- Source: `kallio2016systematic`, [author accepted manuscript (University of Salford repository)](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1402105/systematic-methodological-review-developing-a-framework-for-a-qualitative-semi-structured-interview-guide)
- Version: Journal of Advanced Nursing 72(12), 2954–2965; we read the author accepted manuscript (Accepted Article) in the Salford repository, via the Internet Archive.

### E1

- Location: Results, Formulating the preliminary semi-structured interview guide
- Quote: "A semi-structured interview guide consisted of two levels of questions: main themes and follow-up questions."
- Key point: A semi-structured interview guide has two levels of questions: main themes and follow-up questions.

### E2

- Location: Results, Formulating the preliminary semi-structured interview guide
- Quote: "After that the order of the main themes could move from the lighter ones to more emotional and in-depth ones"
- Key point: Main themes can start with lighter topics and move on to more emotional and in-depth ones.

### E3

- Location: Results, Formulating the preliminary semi-structured interview guide
- Quote: "Pre-designed follow-up questions could be beneficial in increasing the consistency of the subjects covered by interviews carried out by different interviewers" / "As a spontaneous follow-up question, the interviewer could ask participants to expand on some particular point that came up in the interview, by asking for more information"
- Key point: Pre-designed follow-up questions make the topics covered by different interviewers more consistent; spontaneous follow-ups can ask participants to expand on a particular point.

### E4

- Location: Results, Formulating the preliminary semi-structured interview guide
- Quote: "Examples of verbal probes included repeating the participant's point, expressing interest with verbal agreement" / "Non-verbal probing referred to remaining silent and allowing the participant to think aloud"
- Key point: Verbal probes include repeating the participant's point and expressing interest through verbal agreement; non-verbal probing means staying silent so the participant can think aloud.

### E5

- Location: Results, Pilot testing of the interview guide
- Quote: "the pilot test of the interview guide could be conducted using three different techniques: internal testing, expert assessment and field-testing."
- Key point: An interview guide can be piloted in three ways: internal testing within the research team, expert assessment, and field-testing with potential participants.

### E6

- Location: Results, Pilot testing of the interview guide
- Quote: "This technique could provide critical information about the interview guide in general, for instance removing ambiguities and inappropriate leading questions"
- Key point: Internal testing helps remove ambiguities and inappropriate leading questions.

### E7

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "Spontaneous follow-up questions can also create an ethical dilemma, as some ethical review boards want to assess every interview question in advance"
- Key point: Spontaneous follow-up questions can create an ethical dilemma, because some ethics review boards want to assess every interview question in advance.
