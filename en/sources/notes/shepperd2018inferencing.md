# Shepperd 2018: Inferencing into the void: problems with implicit populations

- Source: `shepperd2018inferencing`, [arXiv:1810.07392](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.07392)
- Version: A commentary on Falessi et al. 2018, published as one of the "four commentaries" in Feldt et al. 2018 in Empirical Software Engineering; arXiv preprint

### E1

- Location: §2 Summary of F++
- Quote: "By contrast, I, Carolyn Mair and Magne Jørgensen have recently completed a series of experiments with more than 400 professional participants [7]. Clearly the challenges vary, but equally clearly it's not impossible to conduct experiments with large sample sizes."
- Key point: The author and colleagues have run a series of experiments with more than 400 professionals; how hard it is to recruit professionals varies, but running large-sample experiments is not impossible.

### E2

- Location: §3 First principles
- Quote: "This informality also leads to an under-appreciation of the fact that in software engineering the population is not restricted to the human participants (be they professional or otherwise) but also tasks in particular settings."
- Key point: The "population" you generalize to includes not just participants but also the tasks in a given setting.

### E3

- Location: §4 Examples
- Quote: "The point from all three examples is that we should consider the representativeness of participants, tasks and setting. Equally important we need to be aware of how they interact."
- Key point: Consider the representativeness of participants, tasks, and settings together, as well as the interactions among them.

### E4

- Location: §5 Summary
- Quote: "there is clear value in using students especially for preliminary experiments. But if the target population is some class of professional, it is hard to see why, if practically possible, it would be disadvantageous to use professional participants. The likelihood that this might entail more effort should not be a reason for not undertaking such empirical work."
- Key point: Pilot experiments with students are valuable; but when the target population is a kind of professional, use professionals whenever feasible. "It is more work" should not be a reason not to.
