# American Political Science Association 2012: A Guide to Professional Ethics in Political Science (2nd ed., revised 2012)

- Source: `apsa2012ethics`, [original PDF](https://www.apsanet.org/Portals/54/APSA%20Files/publications/ethicsguideweb.pdf)
- Version: Prepared by the Committee on Professional Ethics, Rights and Freedoms of the American Political Science Association (APSA); 2nd edition, revised 2012; free PDF on the APSA website. Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §III.A, item 6
- Quote: "Researchers have an ethical obligation to facilitate the evaluation of their [...] knowledge claims through data access, production transparency, and analytic transparency so that their work can be tested or replicated."
- Key point: Researchers have an ethical obligation to let others evaluate and replicate their evidence-based knowledge claims through data access, production transparency, and analytic transparency.

### E2

- Location: §III.A, 6.1 Data access
- Quote: "If these are data they [...] themselves generated or collected, researchers should provide access to those data or explain why they cannot."
- Key point: For data they generated or collected themselves, researchers should provide access or explain why they cannot.

### E3

- Location: §III.A, 6.2 Production transparency
- Quote: "Researchers providing access to data they themselves generated or collected, should offer a full account of the procedures used to collect or generate the data."
- Key point: When providing access to their own data, researchers should give a full account of how the data were collected or generated.

### E4

- Location: §III.A, 6.3 Analytic Transparency
- Quote: "Researchers making evidence-based knowledge claims should provide a full account of how they draw their analytic conclusions from the data, i.e., clearly explicate the links connecting data to conclusions."
- Key point: Researchers should give a full account of how they drew their conclusions from the data, clearly explaining the links between data and conclusions.

### E5

- Location: §III.A, 6.4
- Quote: "Scholars may be exempted from Data Access and Production Transparency in order to (A) address well-founded privacy and confidentiality concerns, including abiding by relevant human subjects regulation; and/or (B) comply with relevant and applicable laws, including copyright."
- Key point: Data access and production transparency can be waived for well-founded privacy and confidentiality concerns (including human subjects regulations) or to comply with the law (including copyright).

### E6

- Location: §III.A, 6.6
- Quote: "scholars may postpone data access and production transparency for one year after publication of evidence-based knowledge claims relying on those data"
- Key point: Those who collected the data get first use: they may postpone data access by one year after publication.

### E7

- Location: §III.B, 10.1
- Quote: "Teachers cannot represent themselves as authors of independent student research; and research assistance, paid or unpaid, requires full acknowledgement."
- Key point: Teachers may not present themselves as authors of independent student research; research assistance, paid or unpaid, must be fully acknowledged.

### E8

- Location: §III.B, 10.2
- Quote: "As advisers, faculty members are not entitled to claim joint authorship with a student of a thesis or dissertation."
- Key point: Advisors are not entitled to claim joint authorship of a student's thesis or dissertation.
