# Staron 2025: Guidelines for Conducting Action Research Studies in Software Engineering

- Source: `staron2025guidelines`, [original](https://www.e-informatyka.pl/EISEJ/papers/2025/1/5/)
- Version: e-Informatica Software Engineering Journal 19(1), 250105, 2025, DOI 10.37190/e-Inf250105. Open access (the PDF footer states CC BY-SA 4.0). The author wrote the 2020 Springer book *Action Research in Software Engineering*, and this paper distills guidelines from it; the book is not open access and this project has not read it.

### E1

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "The action research addresses the challenges and dilemmas many software engineering researchers face" / "how to introduce and simultaneously study new technology."
- Key point: The problem action research addresses is how to introduce a new technology and study it at the same time.

### E2

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "Researchers must be more observers or participants but not executors of actions and interventions at the company." / "Experiments are driven by hypotheses and, therefore, prescribe controllability, which favors isolated, small (even toy) problems as the core of experimentation. Design science research focuses on the artifacts rather than improving the practice of the collaborating company."
- Key point: In a case study the researcher is an observer or participant, not the one carrying out an intervention in the company; experiments are hypothesis-driven, require control, and lean toward small isolated problems; design science research focuses on the artifact itself rather than on improving the partner company's practice.

### E3

- Location: §2.2 Existing action research guidelines (restating the canonical action research principles of Davison et al. 2004)
- Quote: "Did both the researcher and the client agree that CAR was the appropriate approach" / "Did the client make an explicit commitment to the project?" / "Were the roles and responsibilities of the researcher and client organization members specified explicitly?" / "Were project objectives and evaluation measures specified explicitly?" / "Were the data collection and analysis methods specified explicitly?"
- Key point: Principle of the researcher-client agreement: do both sides agree that action research suits the situation; has the client made an explicit commitment; are the roles and responsibilities of both sides, the project objectives and evaluation measures, and the data collection and analysis methods all written down explicitly.

### E4

- Location: §2.2 (restating Davison et al. 2004)
- Quote: "Did the researcher conduct an independent diagnosis of the organizational situation?" / "Were the planned actions based explicitly on the results of the diagnosis?" / "Was this reflection followed by an explicit decision on whether or not to proceed through an additional process cycle?" / "Did the client approve the planned actions before they were implemented?" / "Was the organization situation assessed comprehensively both before and after the intervention?"
- Key point: Check items from the cyclical process principle and the principle of change through action: did the researcher independently diagnose the organization's situation; are the planned actions explicitly based on the diagnosis; after reflection, was an explicit decision made on whether to go through another cycle; was the action approved by the client before it was carried out; was the organization's situation comprehensively evaluated both before and after the intervention.

### E5

- Location: §2.2
- Quote: "These guidelines also do not consider the necessity for researchers to be embedded in the organizations but often imply that the researchers are external and introduce a change in the organization. This is not how action research is done in software engineering" / "research must be embedded in the organization, as without it, the change is often abandoned."
- Key point: Staron argues that guidelines from information systems tend to assume the researcher is an outsider, whereas in software engineering the research must be embedded in the organization, or the changes are often abandoned.

### E6

- Location: §2.1 Studies in action research; Figure 2
- Quote: "The action research methodology presented in that book focuses on a five-step cyclic model, which is similar to the view of action research of Baskerville et al." / "This paper follows the same model, although the guidelines are applicable for the action research models with an arbitrary number of phases."
- Key point: Staron's book uses a five-step cycle model (Figure 2: diagnosing, action planning, action taking, evaluating, learning), which this paper follows, but the guidelines also apply to models with a different number of phases.

### E7

- Location: §3 Context of action research
- Quote: "In action research, the fact that the researchers are embedded in the organization means that they are either directly employed at the company or that they have the same access to the company as the employees"
- Key point: Being embedded in the organization means the researcher is either directly employed by the company or has the same access rights as employees.

### E8

- Location: §3.1 What constitutes action research
- Quote: "An example of such an intervention can be changing how the company writes its commit messages by adding the ID of the user story that is implemented" / "at least some research team members should have a research degree" / "at least some action team members should be practitioners from the host company."
- Key point: An example of an intervention: requiring commit messages to include the ID of the user story being implemented. The action team should include members with research training as well as practitioners from the company.

### E9

- Location: §3.2 Situations where action research is not appropriate
- Quote: "if we do not have access to the company and no company representative is part of the research team, we must choose another research methodology." / "When researchers cannot commit to the project for the long term, we should also choose another methodology." / "We should also not engage when we cannot contribute to the practice and we are after publications, not impacting the industry."
- Key point: Situations unsuited to action research: no access to the company and no company representative on the team; the researcher cannot commit for the long term; the goal is only to publish papers, with no way to contribute to practice.

### E10

- Location: §4.1 Project set-up, Guideline 2 and Guideline 3
- Quote: "The researchers should specify their research results, publications, and engagement expectations. The practitioners should be transparent about their expectations regarding resources, the ability to conduct interventions, and collaboration with academic partners." / "Be ready to pivot on your research methodology if the situation is not optimal for action research." / "In most cases, to get more familiar with the organization, it is better to start with a case study"
- Key point: Before starting, both sides should state their expectations: researchers state the research results they want, their publication plans, and how much they can commit; practitioners state their resources, whether interventions can be carried out, and what they expect from working with academics. Be prepared to switch research methods if the situation is unsuitable; in most cases it is better to start with a case study to get to know the organization.

### E11

- Location: §4.2 Host environment, Guideline 5 and Guideline 6
- Quote: "approach management first in hierarchical organizations, and start with the team in empowered organizations." / "The researchers must respect the rules, principles, and obligations of the host organization as if they were employed there." / "researchers must be able to generalize their results to such a level that does not harm the company and is transferable to other contexts."
- Key point: In hierarchical organizations, approach management first; in autonomous organizations, approach the team first. Researchers must follow the organization's rules and obligations just as employees do, and when publishing, generalize the results to a level that neither harms the company nor prevents transfer to other contexts.

### E12

- Location: §4.3 Interventions, Guideline 7
- Quote: "Every intervention should be planned, lead to observable effects, and properly evaluated." / "Quantitative effects can be collected by measuring products, processes, or designs, while qualitative effects can be observed through interviews, workshops, and post-mortem analyses." / "We must measure the baselines and conduct qualitative data collection before the intervention."
- Key point: Every intervention must be planned in advance, have an observable effect, and be carefully evaluated; quantitative effects come from measuring the product, process, or design, and qualitative effects from interviews, workshops, and retrospectives. Measure the baseline and collect qualitative data before the intervention.

### E13

- Location: §4.3, Guideline 8 and Guideline 9
- Quote: "Interventions in each cycle should be atomic." / "These disruptions are confounding factors, making it difficult to determine whether the observed results are due to the intervention or these other changes." / "Practitioners in the action team must be able to conduct the intervention." / "Practitioners should intervene within the scope of their work rather than attempting to change or advocate for the practices of other teams or organizations." / "Conducting a mock-up study or creating a pilot product to demonstrate a tool outside of current practices is not action research, as it does not introduce observable changes; it is merely an offline study."
- Key point: Keep each cycle's intervention as small and focused as possible; the longer an intervention runs, the more likely it is to be interrupted by other work, and these disturbances are confounding factors that make it impossible to tell whether an effect came from the intervention or from other changes. Practitioners on the action team must be able to carry out the intervention and do so within their own scope of work, not try to change other teams' practices. Running simulation studies or demonstrating prototypes outside existing practice is not action research.

### E14

- Location: §4.4 Planning, conducting and analyzing, Guideline 10 and Guideline 11
- Quote: "We must set the stop criteria and ensure we reach them." / "We need to know when the intervention was successful and when it was not." / "Every intervention must be compared to a baseline, so ensure that there is one."
- Key point: Set stopping criteria in advance, and know beforehand what counts as a successful or unsuccessful intervention; compare every intervention with the baseline.

### E15

- Location: §4.4, after Guideline 11
- Quote: "when we want to validate the effect of introducing modern code reviews, we should measure the performance of code reviews before this introduction in terms of speed, duration, and number of reviews." / "If pre-intervention data collection is necessary, we should be prepared to mitigate the Hawthorne effect"
- Key point: For example, to verify the effect of introducing modern code review, measure the speed, duration, and number of reviews before it is introduced; when collecting data before the intervention, be ready to deal with the Hawthorne effect (people change their behavior when they know they are being observed).

### E16

- Location: §4.4, Guideline 12
- Quote: "ensure that when the intervention is done, there are no confounders like reorganizations, new team onboarding, product changes, or other events that could affect the result. If they are, be prepared to document these events and study their effects or pivot"
- Key point: During the intervention, watch for events that could affect the results, such as reorganizations, new team members, or product changes; if they occur, record them and study their impact, or change course.

### E17

- Location: §4.4, Guideline 13
- Quote: "Design and plan interventions based on causes identified in the diagnosing phase." / "The action team can introduce new theories (e.g., from processes to products), change the scope of the cycle (e.g., from methods to tools), adjust the team (e.g., introduce new roles), or re-design the intervention so that it fits both the organization and the theory."
- Key point: Design the intervention based on the causes identified in the diagnosis phase; between cycles you can adjust the theory, scope, or team, or redesign the intervention.

### E18

- Location: §4.4, Guideline 14
- Quote: "Be ready to stop the intervention if it causes harm to the organization" / "We should remember that even negative or inconclusive results are of certain value to the research community."
- Key point: Be ready to stop at any time if the intervention harms the organization; negative or inconclusive results are also valuable to the research community.

### E19

- Location: §4.4, Guideline 15
- Quote: "Depending on the specific context and goals of the research, the process might conclude after two or three cycles or extend to ten or more cycles." / "they can get stuck in solving no longer important problems."
- Key point: Action research may end after two or three cycles or run for more than ten; a team that stays unchanged for a long time risks getting stuck on problems that no longer matter.

### E20

- Location: §4.5 Reporting, Guideline 18
- Quote: "Characterize the context of the study, including undertaken actions/interventions." / "If the action team has to choose whether to reveal the organization's name or its characteristics, the characteristics provide more value to other researchers."
- Key point: Describe the context of the study, including which interventions were made; if you must choose between disclosing the organization's name and disclosing its characteristics, the characteristics are more valuable to other researchers.

### E21

- Location: §4.6 Ethical considerations for action research studies
- Quote: "This can include private conversations, e-mails, or commit messages containing sensitive information." / "we should always consult the company's management and legal department before spreading information that could be sensitive" / "When presenting such results in a larger forum, it is essential to anonymize the data by removing names and identifying details of individuals or teams."
- Key point: Researchers may come across private conversations, emails, and commits containing sensitive information; consult company management and legal staff before disseminating potentially sensitive information, and remove names and identifying details of individuals or teams when presenting results more widely.

### E22

- Location: §4.6, Guideline 19 and Guideline 20
- Quote: "Both researchers and practitioners tend to generalize the results too quickly. The fact that an improvement worked for one organization does not guarantee it will work for another." / "When we specify the theory upfront, we use it to interpret the results, which helps avoid overinterpretation, speculation, or even the Hawthorne effect."
- Key point: Both researchers and practitioners tend to generalize results too quickly; what works in one organization does not necessarily work in another. Choosing a theory in advance and using it to explain the results helps avoid over-interpretation.

### E23

- Location: §5 Conclusions
- Quote: "Although these guidelines are probably relevant to industrial action research (in vivo action research), they have not yet been validated in that context."
- Key point: The author acknowledges that these guidelines have not yet been validated in industrial action research.
