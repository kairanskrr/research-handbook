# ACM CHI 2026: Papers call for submissions and author and reviewer guides

- Source: `chi2026papers`, [Call for papers](https://chi2026.acm.org/authors/papers/); on the same site, [Guide to a Successful Submission](https://chi2026.acm.org/guide-to-a-successful-submission/), [Papers Review Process](https://chi2026.acm.org/papers-review-process/), [Guide to Reviewing Papers](https://chi2026.acm.org/guide-to-reviewing-papers/), [Contributions to CHI](https://chi2026.acm.org/submission-guides/contributions-to-chi/), [CHI Anonymization Policy](https://chi2026.acm.org/chi-anonymization-policy/)
- Version: The six pages above on the website of ACM CHI 2026 (the ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, held in Barcelona in April 2026), read 2026-10-08. Locations are written "page name › subheading." They describe CHI 2026 only; other years and other human-computer interaction conferences may have different rules.

### E1

- Location: Papers › Message from the Papers Chairs
- Quote: "Acceptance is highly competitive: accepted papers will excel in originality, significance, validity, research quality, and presentation clarity."
- Key point: Acceptance is highly competitive; accepted papers excel in originality, significance, validity, research quality, and clarity of presentation.

### E2

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › opening
- Quote: "a large proportion of submissions, typically around 75%, get rejected."
- Key point: Typically around 75% of submissions are rejected.

### E3

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › The Review Process
- Quote: "This replaces the rebuttal process used in previous years, giving authors time to revise and resubmit their work for CHI 2026."
- Key point: CHI 2026 replaced the rebuttal phase used in earlier years with "revise and resubmit."

### E4

- Location: Papers Review Process › Round 1 Review Process
- Quote: "Authors that meet this minimum threshold will have 5 weeks to respond as they see fit." / "as many as 50% of R&R papers may be rejected during the second round of reviewing."
- Key point: Papers that pass the first-round threshold get 5 weeks to revise; up to half of the revise-and-resubmit papers may still be rejected in the second round.

### E5

- Location: Papers › Important Facts
- Quote: "Short Papers: Submissions of 5,000 words or less." / "Standard-Length Papers: Submissions between 5,000 and 12,000 words (about 7,000-8,000 words on average)." / "Submissions of more than 12,000 words will be desk-rejected if their length is not justified."
- Key point: Length is counted in words: short papers up to 5,000 words; standard papers 5,000–12,000 words (about 7,000–8,000 on average); papers over 12,000 words without justification are desk-rejected.

### E6

- Location: Papers › Important Facts
- Quote: "reviewers/ACs are NOT required to read the appendices and supplementary materials for review."
- Key point: Reviewers and associate chairs are not required to read appendices or supplementary material, so everything needed to understand the contribution must be in the main text.

### E7

- Location: Papers › Step 1. Write Your Paper › Research Quality
- Quote: "design papers will be judged as rigorous according to the expectations of the design community, not by the expectations of the computational interaction community"
- Key point: Rigor is judged by the norms of the paper's subcommunity; for example, design papers are judged by the design community's expectations, not the computational interaction community's.

### E8

- Location: Papers › Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects
- Quote: "Any research in submitted manuscripts that involves human subjects must go through the appropriate ethics review requirements that apply to the authors’ research environment." / "authors are asked to submit a short note to reviewers that provides this context."
- Key point: Research involving human participants must go through the ethics review required in the authors' research environment; since requirements differ by place, authors attach a short note for reviewers.

### E9

- Location: Papers › Policy on Use of Large Language Models
- Quote: "Text generated from a large-scale language model (LLM), such as ChatGPT, must be marked where such tools are used for purposes beyond editing the author’s own text"
- Key point: Text generated by a large language model must be marked wherever it is used for anything beyond editing the authors' own text.

### E10

- Location: Papers › Step 3. Select a Subcommittee
- Quote: "you can state a preference of up to two subcommittees whose mandates you believe your topic fits into."
- Key point: At submission you can choose up to two subcommittees.

### E11

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › The Review Form
- Quote: "Significance: why do the contribution and benefit matter, and how much?" / "Research quality: How clear are the authors’ processes? How rigorous is the research (for the subfield’s definition of rigor)?"
- Key point: The review form's criteria include significance, originality, research quality (by the subfield's definition of rigor), presentation clarity, and related work.

### E12

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Making a Significant Contribution
- Quote: "Of these, the contribution and its significance are the most important"
- Key point: Of these criteria, the contribution and its significance matter most.

### E13

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Ensuring High-Quality Research
- Quote: "For this reason it is risky to leave quality issues (for example, evaluating a design) until the last minute."
- Key point: Reviewers often reject papers over quality problems, so plan how you will demonstrate quality (for example, how you will evaluate a design) early, not at the last minute.

### E14

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Ensuring High-Quality Research
- Quote: "They often criticize authors for conducting studies without adequate theoretical basis" / "you should explain not only what you did, but also why you did it"
- Key point: Reviewers often criticize studies that lack an adequate theoretical basis or design choices that are not justified; explain not only what you did but also why.

### E15

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency
- Quote: "Contributions that are technology-oriented (e.g., a new technique or algorithm) and contributions that are quantitative studies (i.e., experiments with statistically analyzed results) are expected to be verifiable, reproducible"
- Key point: Technology-oriented contributions and quantitative studies are expected to be verifiable, replicable, and reproducible.

### E16

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency
- Quote: "Contributions that follow a qualitative research approach (i.e., which most of the time incorporate researchers’ interpretation as part of the method) should be transparent about the various decisions made; the positionality of the authors and/or researchers"
- Key point: Qualitative research should be transparent about the decisions made, the researchers' positionality, and the steps of study design and reporting.

### E17

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency
- Quote: "The authors should make explicit how the themes were identified or constructed from the data, and whether each conclusion was drawn from outstanding instances or general trends among participants."
- Key point: Make explicit how the themes were identified or constructed from the data, and whether each conclusion comes from outstanding instances or general trends among participants.

### E18

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency › Sharing research material
- Quote: "These resources are most reliably shared by posting to a publicly available open-access repository with a persistent identifier"
- Key point: Code, data, interview guides, and similar materials are most reliably shared in a public open-access repository with a persistent identifier.

### E19

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency › Sharing research material
- Quote: "In these cases, we advise you to share as much as possible and explicitly state in your paper why the rest cannot be shared."
- Key point: When sensitive data or proprietary code cannot be shared, share as much as possible and state in the paper why the rest cannot be shared.

### E20

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Transparency › Sharing research material
- Quote: "When research involves asking questions of human subjects, it is helpful for reviewers, and later readers, to know what questions (and with what wording) were asked."
- Key point: When research asks participants questions, readers should be able to see what was asked and in what wording.

### E21

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Describing the Work Clearly and Concisely
- Quote: "Remember that, unlike journal papers, CHI Papers are reviewed mostly on an ‘as-is’ basis"
- Key point: CHI papers are reviewed mostly as is; the revision period is short, and papers needing major changes are rejected.

### E22

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Systems, Tools, Architectures, and Infrastructure
- Quote: "(Note that a good systems paper does not necessarily require a user study, but it does require an appropriate and reasonable validation.)"
- Key point: A good systems paper does not necessarily need a user study, but it does need an appropriate and reasonable validation.

### E23

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Development or Refinement of Interface Artifacts or Techniques
- Quote: "Do you include a rigorous and convincing validation of the artifact/technique (e.g., empirical study, usability study, field study as appropriate), where you clearly show not only that incremental gains exist, but that the gains are of practical significance?"
- Key point: An interface artifact or technique needs a rigorous and convincing validation (empirical study, usability study, field study, etc.) that shows not only that gains exist but that they matter in practice.

### E24

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Understanding Users
- Quote: "Do you identify the population you are studying and account for the diversity (or lack thereof) of the population?"
- Key point: Studies that aim to understand users should identify the population studied and account for its diversity (or lack of it).

### E25

- Location: Guide to Reviewing Papers › Replicating Work
- Quote: "Constructive replication can be a significant contribution to human-computer interaction"
- Key point: Constructive replication can itself be a significant contribution to human-computer interaction.

### E26

- Location: Guide to Reviewing Papers › Prior Publication
- Quote: "non-archival venues, such as workshop presentations, posters, and CHI’s own Late-Breaking Work do not count as prior publications."
- Key point: Non-archival venues such as workshop presentations, posters, and CHI's own Late-Breaking Work do not count as prior publication.

### E27

- Location: CHI Anonymization Policy
- Quote: "Authors are required to cite their own work in the third person"
- Key point: Authors must cite their own earlier work in the third person.

### E28

- Location: CHI Anonymization Policy
- Quote: "CHI does not discourage non-archival publication of work prior to or during the review process, but recognizes that complete anonymization becomes more difficult in that context."
- Key point: CHI does not discourage posting work publicly (e.g., on arXiv) before or during review, but recognizes that this makes full anonymization harder.

### E29

- Location: CHI Anonymization Policy
- Quote: "too detailed descriptions of where user studies were conducted, where authors received IRB approvals, or figures that include faces of authors"
- Key point: Identifying details include overly detailed descriptions of where user studies were run, where IRB approval was obtained, and figures showing authors' faces.

### E30

- Location: Papers Review Process › Update for CHI 2026: Assisted Desk Reject (ADR) Process
- Quote: "Grossly insufficient data to support claims." / "A disproportionately small HCI contribution given the paper length."
- Key point: The new Assisted Desk Reject criteria at CHI 2026 include grossly insufficient data to support the claims and an HCI contribution that is disproportionately small for the paper's length.

### E31

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Maximizing Impact
- Quote: "You should keep a critical eye on who is and who is not included in your work and ensure that you make claims accordingly."
- Key point: Keep a critical eye on who is and is not included in your study, and scope your claims accordingly.

### E32

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Introduction and each subheading
- Quote: "Below you will find some of the types of contributions a paper can make to HCI, and the associated criteria that a reviewer may use to assess this type of contribution."
- Key point: CHI lists several contribution types, each with its own review criteria; the subheadings are, in order: development or refinement of interface artifacts or techniques, understanding users, systems and tools, methodology, theory, innovation and vision, argument, and validation and replication.

### E33

- Location: Papers › Step 3. Select a Subcommittee
- Quote: "CHI 2026 expects to receive over 5,000 submissions as of 2025."
- Key point: CHI 2026 expected over 5,000 submissions.

### E34

- Location: Papers › Your Paper at the Conference
- Quote: "Authors of accepted papers are required to present their work at CHI 2026"
- Key point: Authors of accepted papers must present their work at the conference.

### E35

- Location: Papers › Important Dates
- Quote: "Thursday, September 4, 2025 : Abstract/metadata deadline" / "Thursday, September 11, 2025 : Full paper deadline" / "Thursday, January 15, 2026 : Decision Notification"
- Key point: CHI 2026: abstracts and metadata due September 4, 2025, full papers September 11, decisions January 15, 2026.

### E36

- Location: Papers › Step 4. Complete Submission
- Quote: "Listed authors cannot be changed after the abstract/metadata deadline."
- Key point: The author list cannot be changed after the abstract and metadata deadline.

### E37

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Ensuring High-Quality Research
- Quote: "if you are providing an incremental improvement to a well-known technique, such as a new or refined type of menu capable of reducing errors in selecting menu items, you will probably run a careful and convincing experiment to measure error rates with this and other types of menu." / "If you have come up with a system to support a new way of collaborative writing, you may try to evaluate it in real-world conditions"
- Key point: How you demonstrate quality depends on the contribution type: an incremental improvement to a known technique (such as a new menu that reduces selection errors) usually calls for a careful experiment comparing error rates; a system supporting a new way of collaborative writing may be evaluated under realistic conditions.

### E38

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Development or Refinement of Interface Artifacts or Techniques
- Quote: "Do you describe the artifact or technique in sufficient detail for others to replicate it?"
- Key point: Describe an interface artifact or technique in enough detail for others to replicate it.

### E39

- Location: Contributions to CHI › Argument
- Quote: "Is the argument well-founded and well-argued? Is it supported by facts?" / "Does it incorporate other points of view and perspectives, and does it consider them in a balanced fashion?"
- Key point: Argument papers should be well founded, supported by facts, and consider other points of view in a balanced way.

### E40

- Location: Contributions to CHI › subheadings for each contribution type
- Quote: "Development or Refinement of Interface Artifacts or Techniques" / "Systems, Tools, Architectures, and Infrastructure" / "Innovation, Creativity, and Vision" / "Validation and Replication Validation and replication studies consolidate what the CHI field knows."
- Key point: The contribution types listed on the Contributions to CHI page include development or refinement of interface artifacts or techniques, understanding users, systems and tools (including architectures and infrastructure), methodology, theory, innovation and vision, argument, and validation and replication.

### E41

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › The Review Form
- Quote: "Originality: What new ideas or approaches are introduced?" / "Relevant previous work: Is prior work adequately reviewed?"
- Key point: The review form also asks about originality, clarity of presentation, and whether prior work is adequately reviewed.

### E42

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Ensuring High-Quality Research
- Quote: "Reviewers often cite problems with quality as the reason to reject a submission."
- Key point: Reviewers often cite problems with research quality as the reason to reject a paper.

### E43

- Location: Guide to a Successful Submission › Ensuring High-Quality Research
- Quote: "A further concern is lack of justification for design choices and not explaining why certain design features have been included."
- Key point: Another common reviewer concern is design choices made without justification.

### E44

- Location: Papers Review Process › Update for CHI 2026: Assisted Desk Reject (ADR) Process
- Quote: "CHI 2026 introduces an Assisted Desk Reject (ADR) step, collaboratively performed by SCs and ACs." / "ADR applies when a paper is out of scope or so far from acceptable as to make external reviews unnecessary."
- Key point: CHI 2026 introduced Assisted Desk Reject, performed jointly by subcommittee chairs and associate chairs, for papers that are out of scope or so far from acceptable that external reviews are unnecessary.

### E45

- Location: Guide to Reviewing Papers › References
- Quote: "the debate about what makes a good CHI paper has been going on as long as the CHI conference has existed" / "Greenberg, S. and Buxton, B. 2008. Usability evaluation considered harmful (some of the time)."
- Key point: The reviewing guide lists Greenberg & Buxton 2008 as background reading on the long debate over what makes a good CHI paper.
