# AEA: Disclosure Policy

- Source: `aea2026disclosure`, [original](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/policies/disclosure-policy)
- Version: web page accessed October 6, 2026. Applies to AEA journals.

### E1

- Location: Disclosure Principles
- Quote: "Every submitted article should state the sources of financial support for the particular research it describes. If none, that fact should be stated."
- Key point: Every submission should state the sources of financial support for the research; if there are none, that must be stated.

### E2

- Location: Disclosure Principles
- Quote: "Each author of a submitted article should identify each interested party from whom he or she has received significant financial support, summing to at least $10,000 in the past three years, in the form of consultant fees, retainers, grants and the like. The disclosure requirement also includes in-kind support, such as providing access to data."
- Key point: Each author must disclose every interested party from whom they received significant financial support (consulting fees, grants, etc.) summing to at least $10,000 over the past three years, including non-cash support such as data access.

### E3

- Location: Disclosure Principles
- Quote: "Each author must disclose if another party had the right to review the paper prior to its circulation."
- Key point: Each author must disclose whether another party had the right to review the paper before its circulation.

### E4

- Location: Instructions
- Quote: "Each (co)author should prepare a separate PDF document entitled “Disclosure Statement,” which must be provided with the manuscript."
- Key point: Each author prepares a separate PDF "Disclosure Statement" submitted with the manuscript.

### E5

- Location: Institutional Review Board (IRB) Approval
- Quote: "For any paper involving the collection of data on human subjects the author(s) must disclose whether they have obtained Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval; if no IRB approval was obtained, the reason should be stated."
- Key point: Papers collecting data on human subjects must disclose whether Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval was obtained, and if not, explain why.

### E6

- Location: Instructions
- Quote: "Failure to disclose relevant information at the submission stage may result in reversal of acceptance decisions."
- Key point: Failure to disclose relevant information at submission may result in reversal of acceptance decisions.
