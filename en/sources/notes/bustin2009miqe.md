# Bustin et al. 2009: The MIQE Guidelines: Minimum Information for Publication of Quantitative Real-Time PCR Experiments

- Source: `bustin2009miqe`, [Original web page](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/55/4/611/5631762), DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2008.112797
- Version: Clinical Chemistry 55(4):611-622, 2009. Europe PMC marks the full text on the publisher's site as "free after 12 months." The publisher's site returned 403 to scripts, so the full text was read from an Internet Archive snapshot (December 2019) of the journal's old URL (clinchem.aaccjnls.org/content/55/4/611). A revised MIQE 2.0 appeared in 2025 (see `bustin2025miqe`). Locations are given as section numbers.

### E1

- Location: Abstract, Background
- Quote: "Currently, a lack of consensus exists on how best to perform and interpret quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) experiments. The problem is exacerbated by a lack of sufficient experimental detail in many publications, which impedes a reader’s ability to evaluate critically the quality of the results presented or to repeat the experiments."
- Key point: There is no consensus on how to perform and interpret quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) experiments, and many papers give too little experimental detail for readers to judge the quality of the results or repeat the experiments.

### E2

- Location: Abstract, Content
- Quote: "MIQE is a set of guidelines that describe the minimum information necessary for evaluating qPCR experiments. Included is a checklist to accompany the initial submission of a manuscript to the publisher."
- Key point: MIQE sets out the minimum information needed to evaluate qPCR experiments and includes a checklist to submit with the manuscript.

### E3

- Location: Abstract, Content
- Quote: "Full disclosure of all reagents, sequences, and analysis methods is necessary to enable other investigators to reproduce results."
- Key point: For others to reproduce the results, all reagents, sequences, and analysis methods must be fully disclosed.

### E4

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Specifically, information about sample acquisition and handling, RNA quality and integrity, reverse-transcription details, PCR efficiencies, and analysis parameters are frequently omitted, whereas sample normalization is habitually carried out against single reference genes without adequate justification."
- Key point: Papers often omit sample acquisition and handling, RNA quality and integrity, reverse-transcription details, PCR efficiencies, and analysis parameters, while normalization is habitually done against a single reference gene without adequate justification.

### E5

- Location: 8.1 Normalization
- Quote: "Normalization against a single reference gene is not acceptable unless the investigators present clear evidence for the reviewers that confirms its invariant expression under the experimental conditions described. The optimal number and choice of reference genes must be experimentally determined and the method reported"
- Key point: Normalizing to a single reference gene is not acceptable unless clear evidence shows its expression is invariant under the experimental conditions; the number and choice of reference genes must be determined experimentally and the method reported.

### E6

- Location: 7.3 Controls and quantification calibrators
- Quote: "NTCs should be included on each plate or batch of samples, and conditions for data rejection be established."
- Key point: Include no-template controls on every plate or batch, and set the conditions for rejecting data in advance.

### E7

- Location: 2.1 Analytical sensitivity
- Quote: "Experimental results less than the theoretically possible LOD should never be reported. It also follows that results of “0” are meaningless and misleading."
- Key point: Never report results below the theoretical limit of detection; reporting "0" is meaningless and misleading.

### E8

- Location: Conclusions (note to Table 1)
- Quote: "Items deemed essential (E) are required to allow reviewers to evaluate the work and other investigators to reproduce it."
- Key point: Checklist items marked "essential" are those reviewers need to evaluate the work and other investigators need to reproduce it.

### E9

- Location: 2. Conceptual Considerations
- Quote: "It is also necessary to be aware that most quantitative RNA data are not absolute, but relative."
- Key point: Most quantitative RNA data are relative, not absolute.

### E10

- Location: 2. Conceptual Considerations
- Quote: "The reporting of results for nonvalidated commercial assays and assays that have been validated only in silico are strongly discouraged."
- Key point: Reporting results from nonvalidated commercial assays, or assays validated only in silico, is strongly discouraged.
