# Sutherland 1996: Technology and Courage

- Source: `sutherland1996courage`, [PDF hosted by a third party](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~ravenben/ucsb/courage-IS.pdf)
- Version: Sun Microsystems Laboratories Perspectives 96-1, April 1996. The cover states: "Unlimited copying without fee is permitted provided that the copies are not made nor distributed for direct commercial advantage" (as given in the locally extracted text).

### E1

- Location: §1.1 What is Courage?
- Quote: "Courage is what it takes to overcome fear. Fear is an emotion appropriate to perceived risk. Thus, to exhibit courage one must both perceive a risk and proceed in spite of it."
- Key point: Courage is what it takes to overcome fear; to show courage is to see the risk and still go forward.

### E2

- Location: §2.6 Encouragement in Business
- Quote: "If you don't fail regularly you are not trying hard enough things. The trouble, of course, is that it is emotionally much harder to restart after a failure because the risks seem clearer."
- Key point: If you are not failing regularly, you are not trying hard enough problems; the difficulty is that starting again after a failure is emotionally harder, because the risk is now clearer.

### E3

- Location: End of §2.2 Formal Mechanisms
- Quote: "My advice for a new graduate student seeking to get started in research is to join an ongoing research group." / "But it matters far less what a new student does than that he do something."
- Key point: Advice to new graduate students: join a research group that is already up and running; what you work on matters far less than getting started on something.

### E4

- Location: §2.2 Formal Mechanisms
- Quote: "Working together with colleagues reduces the labor required from each participant and makes the learning experience more pleasurable. Finally, working in a group reduces the perceived risk inherent in the new material."
- Key point: Learning together with peers lightens everyone's load, makes learning more enjoyable, and lowers the risk you feel when facing something new.

### E5

- Location: §2.5 In Academia, it's Hard to Stop
- Quote: "Academia seems to me deficient in mechanisms to help people stop old and stale projects."
- Key point: Academia lacks mechanisms to help people stop stale projects; stopping takes courage too.

### E6

- Location: §3.1 Courage to Start
- Quote: "Recognizing that it takes courage to get started may help in identifying the excuses you have as excuses, and not reasons."
- Key point: Recognizing that getting started itself takes courage helps you see that your reasons for procrastinating are just excuses.

### E7

- Location: §3.3 Overcoming Risks
- Quote: "A warm-up project is very helpful in getting any new research going. Do something fairly easy and carry it all the way through from beginning to end." / "a warm-up project not only teaches, but also encourages."
- Key point: When starting new research, first do a relatively easy warm-up project and carry it through from start to finish; it both teaches you things and gives you courage.

### E8

- Location: §3.4 Procedures
- Quote: "By breaking the task into smaller tasks through emphasizing that only the first dish need be washed or the first expense need be recorded, I reduce my estimate of risk."
- Key point: Breaking a big task into small steps ("wash the first dish") lowers your estimate of the risk and makes it easier to start.
