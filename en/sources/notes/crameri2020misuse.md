# Crameri, Shephard & Heron 2020: The misuse of colour in science communication

- Source: `crameri2020misuse`, [Original (Nature Communications, open access)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19160-7), doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7
- Version: Nature Communications 11:5444 (2020), CC BY 4.0. The authors are earth scientists writing about color in scientific figures across disciplines. Locations give paragraphs.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "colour maps that visually distort data through uneven colour gradients or are unreadable to those with colour-vision deficiency remain prevalent in science. These include, but are not limited to, rainbow-like and red–green colour maps."
- Key point: Color maps with uneven color gradients, or that people with color-vision deficiency cannot read, remain common in science; these include rainbow and red–green color maps.

### E2

- Location: Introduction, paragraph 2
- Quote: "when colours are used incorrectly, this can lead to the effective manipulation of data (e.g., by highlighting some data over others), the oversight of the needs of those with colour vision deficiencies, and the removal of meaning when printed in black and white"
- Key point: Using color incorrectly effectively manipulates the data (by highlighting some data over others), ignores readers with color-vision deficiency, and loses meaning when printed in black and white.

### E3

- Location: Introduction, paragraph 5
- Quote: "In rainbow colour maps, the yellow is the brightest colour and attracts the eye the most" / "Hence, such an arrangement of colours can unfairly highlight a particular section of the parameter space while obscuring other parts"
- Key point: In rainbow color maps, yellow is the brightest and most eye-catching color, yet it sits neither at the ends nor in the middle, so it unfairly highlights one part of the data range and obscures others.

### E4

- Location: Introduction (paragraph on color-vision deficiency)
- Quote: "Colour maps that include both red and green colours with similar lightness cannot be read by a large fraction of the readership" / "The general estimate is that worldwide 0.5% of women and 8% of men are subject to a colour-vision deficiency"
- Key point: A large fraction of readers cannot read red–green color maps of similar lightness; the general estimate is that 0.5% of women and 8% of men worldwide have a color-vision deficiency.

### E5

- Location: Introduction ("For the casual reader" paragraph)
- Quote: "Colour map choice is often made passively and is not subject to the same scrutiny as other data methodologies."
- Key point: Color maps are often chosen passively (software defaults) and do not get the scrutiny other data methods do.

### E6

- Location: Box 2 (sources of scientific color maps)
- Quote: "With the main aim being cartography, the online tool offers sequential, diverging and categorical (or in other words, qualitative) colour maps"
- Key point: ColorBrewer is a free color tool aimed at cartography, offering sequential, diverging, and categorical color maps.

### E7

- Location: Main text, "Scientific data should be displayed..." paragraph
- Quote: "A perceptually uniform colour map weights the same data variation equally all across the dataspace, while other colour maps (such as rainbow) interpret some small data variation to be more important than others"
- Key point: A perceptually uniform color map makes the same data variation look equally large across the whole range; rainbow and other maps exaggerate some small variations.
