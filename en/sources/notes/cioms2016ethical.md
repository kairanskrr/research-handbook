# CIOMS 2016: International Ethical Guidelines for Health-related Research Involving Humans

- Source: `cioms2016ethical`, [Original PDF](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf)
- Version: Prepared by the Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) in collaboration with the World Health Organization, 4th edition, 2016. Locations give the guideline number and subheading.

### E1

- Location: Preface, Scope of the 2016 version
- Quote: "The Working Group decided to broaden the scope of the 2002 Guidelines from “biomedical research” to “health-related research”."
- Key point: The 2016 edition broadened the scope from "biomedical research" to "health-related research."

### E2

- Location: Preface, Scope of the 2016 version
- Quote: "The current scope is confined to the classic activities that fall under health-related research with humans, such as observational research, clinical trials, biobanking and epidemiological studies."
- Key point: The current scope covers the classic forms of health-related research with humans, such as observational research, clinical trials, biobanking, and epidemiological studies.

### E3

- Location: Preface
- Quote: "the Working Group decided to merge the CIOMS Guidelines for Biomedical Research with the CIOMS Guidelines for Epidemiological Research."
- Key point: The 2016 edition merged the guidelines for biomedical research and the guidelines for epidemiological research into one document.

### E4

- Location: Guideline 1: Scientific and social value and respect for rights
- Quote: "The ethical justification for undertaking health-related research involving humans is its scientific and social value"
- Key point: The ethical justification for health-related research involving humans is its scientific and social value.

### E5

- Location: Guideline 1
- Quote: "Scientific and social value cannot legitimate subjecting study participants or host communities to mistreatment, or injustice."
- Key point: No amount of scientific and social value can justify mistreating participants or host communities.

### E6

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 4, Risks to groups
- Quote: "research results in certain fields (for example, epidemiology, genetics, and sociology) may present risks to the interests of communities, societies, families, or racially or ethnically defined groups."
- Key point: Research results in fields such as epidemiology, genetics, and sociology can harm the interests of communities, families, or racially or ethnically defined groups.

### E7

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 4, Risks to groups
- Quote: "Research results could therefore stigmatize a group or expose its members to discrimination."
- Key point: Research results can stigmatize a group or expose its members to discrimination.

### E8

- Location: Guideline 7: Community engagement
- Quote: "Researchers, sponsors, health authorities and relevant institutions should engage potential participants and communities in a meaningful participatory process that involves them in an early and sustained manner in the design, development, implementation, design of the informed consent process and monitoring of research, and in the dissemination of its results."
- Key point: Researchers and other parties should involve potential participants and communities early and continuously in designing and conducting the research, designing the informed consent process, monitoring, and disseminating results.

### E9

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 7
- Quote: "community engagement is a means of ensuring the relevance of proposed research to the affected community, as well as its acceptance by the community."
- Key point: Community engagement ensures the research is relevant to the community and accepted by it.

### E10

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 7
- Quote: "For instance, when community leaders are men only, researchers should actively include the views of women, as well."
- Key point: When community leaders are all men, researchers should actively seek out women's views as well.

### E11

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 7
- Quote: "The research protocol or other documents submitted to the research ethics committee should include a description of the plan for community engagement"
- Key point: The protocol submitted to the research ethics committee should describe the plan for community engagement.

### E12

- Location: Guideline 12: Collection, storage and use of data in health-related research
- Quote: "When data are used that were collected in the context of routine clinical care, an informed opt-out procedure must be used."
- Key point: When using data collected in routine clinical care, an informed opt-out procedure must be offered.

### E13

- Location: Guideline 12
- Quote: "1) the research would not be feasible or practicable to carry out without the waiver; and 2) the research has important social value; and 3) the research poses no more than minimal risks to participants or to the group to which the participant belongs."
- Key point: For data collected earlier for other purposes without consent for research, the ethics committee may waive individual informed consent if the research would be infeasible without the waiver, has important social value, and poses no more than minimal risk to participants and their group.

### E14

- Location: Guideline 12
- Quote: "Custodians of the data must arrange to protect the confidentiality of the information linked to the data, by sharing only anonymised or coded data with researchers"
- Key point: Data custodians must protect confidentiality by sharing only anonymized or coded data with researchers.

### E15

- Location: Guideline 12
- Quote: "Data from low-resource settings should only be collected and stored in collaboration with local health authorities."
- Key point: Data from low-resource settings should be collected and stored only in collaboration with local health authorities.

### E16

- Location: Commentary on Guideline 21: Cluster randomized trials
- Quote: "In this research design, groups of individuals (clusters), communities, hospitals, or units of a health facility are randomized to different interventions."
- Key point: A cluster randomized trial randomizes groups, communities, hospitals, or hospital units rather than individuals.

### E17

- Location: Guideline 21
- Quote: "determine whether requiring informed consent and allowing refusal to consent may invalidate or compromise the research results"
- Key point: Before a cluster randomized trial starts, determine whether requiring informed consent and allowing refusal would invalidate or compromise the results.

### E18

- Location: Preface, Scope of the 2016 version
- Quote: "The Working Group also acknowledged that there is no clear distinction between the ethics of social science research, behavioural studies, public health surveillance and the ethics of other research activities."
- Key point: The working group acknowledges that there is no clear line between the ethics of social science research, behavioral studies, and public health surveillance and the ethics of other research.
