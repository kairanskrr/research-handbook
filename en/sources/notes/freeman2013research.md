# Freeman 2013: How to do research

- Source: `freeman2013research`, [Original PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/billf/publications/How_To_Do_Research.pdf)
- Version: Handout, 2013-03-06, 3 pages. Page numbers follow the PDF page order.

### E1

- Location: p. 1
- Quote: "advice can go on a bumper sticker: “Slow down to speed up”."
- Key point: The first piece of advice: "Slow down to speed up."

### E2

- Location: p. 1
- Quote: "Take things slowly. Verify your assumptions. [...] As you do experiments, only change one thing at a time, so you know what the outcome of the experiment means."
- Key point: In research no one tells you the right answer, so take things slowly, verify your assumptions, and change only one thing per experiment so you know what the outcome means.

### E3

- Location: p. 1
- Quote: "Please don’t tell me “it doesn’t work”. [...] What I want to hear instead is something like, “I’ve narrowed down the problem to step B."
- Key point: Don't just say "it doesn't work"; say how far you have narrowed down the problem and which causes you have ruled out.

### E4

- Location: p. 1
- Quote: "It’s the simplest toy model that captures the main idea."
- Key point: Find the simplest toy model that captures the main idea and use it to build intuition.

### E5

- Location: p. 1
- Quote: "But we also need a rudder for steering–that’s stepping back periodically to make sure we’re working on the right thing."
- Key point: Besides working hard, you need a "rudder": step back periodically to make sure you are working on the right thing.

### E6

- Location: p. 3
- Quote: "Is there a simpler case we can make it work?"
- Key point: When something fails, ask: is there a simpler case where we can make it work?

### E7

- Location: p. 3
- Quote: "Progress can include: “I’ve shown why this doesn’t work”, [...] or “I spent the whole time reading because I know I have to understand this before I can make any progress.”"
- Key point: Progress is broad: showing why something doesn't work, simplifying the task until it runs, and reading to understand the problem all count.

### E8

- Location: p. 2
- Quote: "The strong student starts doing what the advisor has asked, sees that it doesn’t work, looks around within some epsilon ball of the original proposal to [...] what does work, and reports that solution."
- Key point: A weak student does what the advisor proposed and reports failure when it fails; a strong student, seeing it doesn't work, searches near the original proposal for something that does, and reports that solution.

### E9

- Location: p. 3
- Quote: "I love to hear about progress when I meet with students, but note that I have a very general notion of progress."
- Key point: The advisor has a very general notion of progress: explaining why something doesn't work, simplifying the problem, and reading to understand the background all count.

### E10

- Location: p. 3
- Quote: "Please don’t hide from me. Let’s talk. I like it when you track me down and insist that we talk"
- Key point: Don't hide from your advisor; even when the advisor is traveling, students are welcome to track them down and insist on talking.
