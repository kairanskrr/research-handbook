# Committee on Publication Ethics 2019: COPE Discussion Document: Authorship (2nd edition)

- Source: `cope2019authorship`, [Original](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.3.3)
- Version: 2nd edition, September 2019 (1st edition June 2014). The website returns 403 to scripts; this entry comes from the Internet Archive capture (2025-05-03) of the official PDF (publicationethics.org/files/COPE_DD_A4_Authorship_SEPT19_SCREEN_AW.pdf).

### E1

- Location: Guidance for editors, p. 7
- Quote: "When authorship disputes arise, submissions can be halted in the review or publication process until the dispute is resolved. Editors are not in a position to fairly adjudicate disputes, nor should they capitulate to threats and bullying by the authors involved in those disputes."
- Key point: When an authorship dispute arises, a submission can be halted in review or production until the dispute is resolved; editors are not in a position to adjudicate such disputes fairly.

### E2

- Location: Pre-publication authorship disputes, p. 8
- Quote: "When there is an unresolved question about authorship or competing interests in a submitted paper, the editor should delay the review and production processes until a satisfactory resolution of all unresolved issues has been reached."
- Key point: While an authorship question on a submitted paper is unresolved, the editor should delay review and production.

### E3

- Location: Post-publication authorship disputes, p. 9
- Quote: "Changes in authorship order, or addition or removal of an author require consent of all listed authors; editors should have in place some mechanism such as a requirement for all authors to agree in writing to the proposed change."
- Key point: Changing the author order, or adding or removing an author, after publication requires the consent of all listed authors, for example in writing.

### E4

- Location: Guidance for editors, p. 7
- Quote: "When a paper has already been published and an authorship dispute emerges, the editor has an obligation to correct the scientific record through an erratum, corrigendum, or, in extreme situations, an editorial expression of concern or retraction."
- Key point: When an authorship dispute arises over a published paper, the editor must correct the record through an erratum or corrigendum or, in extreme cases, an expression of concern or a retraction.

### E5

- Location: Considerations for academic and research institutions, p. 11
- Quote: "Individuals who may be vulnerable to harassment intimidation, threats or other forms of coercion, such as students and junior faculty, need an institutional environment in which they can bring their concerns about research and authorship to the administration without fear of retribution. Institutional offices of research integrity and grants administration are an important part of a system to educate new researchers and protect whistleblowers."
- Key point: Students and junior faculty need an institutional environment where they can raise research and authorship concerns with administrators without fear of retaliation; the institution's research integrity office is part of protecting whistleblowers.
