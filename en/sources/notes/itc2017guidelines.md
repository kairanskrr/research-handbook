# International Test Commission 2017: ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (Second Edition)

- Source: `itc2017guidelines`, [original PDF (International Test Commission website)](https://www.intestcom.org/files/guideline_test_adaptation_2ed.pdf)
- Version: Second edition, v2.4; free from the International Test Commission website. Locations give guideline numbers.

### E1

- Location: Summary
- Quote: "The 18 guidelines are organized into six categories to facilitate their use: Pre-condition (3), test development (5), confirmation (4), administration (2), scoring and interpretation (2), and documentation (2)."
- Key point: The 18 guidelines fall into six categories: pre-condition, test development, confirmation, administration, scoring and interpretation, and documentation.

### E2

- Location: PC-1 (1)
- Quote: "Obtain the necessary permission from the holder of the intellectual property rights relating to the test before carrying out any adaptation."
- Key point: Obtain permission from the holder of the test's intellectual property rights before adapting it.

### E3

- Location: PC-2 (2), Explanation
- Quote: "This guideline requires that what is assessed should be understood in the same way across language and cultural groups, and this is the foundation of valid cross-cultural comparisons."
- Key point: What is assessed must be understood the same way across language and cultural groups; this is the foundation of valid cross-cultural comparison.

### E4

- Location: TD-2 (5), Explanation
- Quote: "focusing on functional rather than on literal equivalence" / "both designs have flaws, and so rarely would these two designs provide sufficient evidence to validate a translated and adapted test." / "The design too often results in a target language version of the test which maximizes the ease of back translation, but sometimes produces a rather awkward target language version of the test."
- Key point: Translation aims at functional rather than literal equivalence; forward and back translation designs both have flaws and rarely provide enough evidence on their own to validate a translated test; relying on back translation alone often yields a version that back-translates well but reads awkwardly.

### E5

- Location: TD-2 (5), Explanation
- Quote: "In this approach, a third independent translator or an expert panel identifies and resolves any discrepancies between alternative forward translations, and reconciles them into a single version."
- Key point: Double translation with reconciliation: two independent forward translations, with a third independent translator or an expert panel identifying and resolving discrepancies and reconciling them into a single version.

### E6

- Location: TD-5 (8)
- Quote: "Collect pilot data on the adapted test to enable item analysis, reliability assessment and small-scale validity studies so that any necessary revisions to the adapted test can be made." / "an item analysis using at least a modest sample size (e.g., 100) can provide much-needed data about the functioning of particular test items."
- Key point: Collect pilot data on the adapted test for item analysis, reliability, and small-scale validity studies, then revise; an item analysis with a moderate sample (e.g., 100 people) already gives very useful information.

### E7

- Location: C-3 (11), Explanation
- Quote: "The norms, validity evidence and reliability evidence of a test in its source language version do not automatically apply to other possible adaptations of the test into different cultures and languages."
- Key point: The norms, validity evidence, and reliability evidence of the source-language version do not automatically apply to adaptations for other languages and cultures.

### E8

- Location: SSI-2 (16)
- Quote: "Only compare scores across populations when the level of invariance has been established on the scale on which scores are reported."
- Key point: Compare scores across populations only when the corresponding level of invariance has been established on the scale on which scores are reported.

### E9

- Location: Doc-1 (17)
- Quote: "Provide technical documentation of any changes, including an account of the evidence obtained to support equivalence, when a test is adapted for use in another population."
- Key point: When adapting a test, provide technical documentation of all changes and of the evidence supporting equivalence.
