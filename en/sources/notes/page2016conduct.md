# Page et al. 2016: Conduct, Oversight, and Ethical Considerations of Clinical Trials in Companion Animals with Cancer

- Source: `page2016conduct`, [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913608/)
- Version: Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine 30(2):527-535, 2016 (DOI 10.1111/jvim.13916), open access (CC BY-NC). Consensus report from a November 2014 workshop at the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) on clinical trials in companion animals with cancer. Locations give subheadings or tables.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "The ethical conduct and oversight of clinical research involving client‐owned companion animals require the proper management of concerns and expectations that may differ from both laboratory animal research oversight and clinical trial conduct in humans."
- Key point: Clinical research with client-owned companion animals raises ethical concerns that differ from both laboratory animal research and human clinical trials.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Although clear and established policy guidelines for the use and care of laboratory animals in research exist, many of these guidelines are limited in their direct applicability to client‐owned animals included in clinical trials."
- Key point: Established guidelines for laboratory animals exist, but many do not apply directly to client-owned animals enrolled in clinical trials.

### E3

- Location: Table 1, Guiding ethical principles
- Quote: "The consent process must be honest, thorough, and well‐communicated and the pet owner must have adequate time to consider participation without real or perceived coercion or conflict of interest from any member of the investigative team."
- Key point: The consent process must be honest, thorough, and well communicated; owners must have adequate time to decide, free of real or perceived coercion or conflict of interest from anyone on the study team.

### E4

- Location: Consenting Process – Best Practices And Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "Full disclosure of the study purpose, associated risks and benefits, study design and interventions required, funding source for the study, incentives to the owner, and conflict of interest (COI) statements from the study staff and investigators are required elements to address during the consent process. The recommended best practice for the consenting process is that it be managed by a neutral individual (nurse, nonsponsor supported veterinarian) with a witness present."
- Key point: Consent must fully disclose the study purpose, risks and benefits, design and required interventions, funding source, incentives to the owner, and conflicts of interest of the investigators. It is recommended that a neutral person (a nurse, or a veterinarian not supported by the sponsor) lead the process, with a witness present.

### E5

- Location: Consenting Process – Best Practices And Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "All financial subsidies or incentives should be carefully and fully described. A description of what is not subsidized is equally important."
- Key point: Describe all financial subsidies or incentives fully; describing what is not subsidized is equally important.

### E6

- Location: Study Design – Best Practices and Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "A potential ethical conflict occurs when an animal is placed at risk, real or perceived, in return for a therapeutic incentive. The owner is placed in a financially vulnerable position when study completion is required before receiving reimbursement for veterinary care."
- Key point: Placing an animal at risk in return for a therapeutic incentive is a potential ethical conflict; making reimbursement of care costs conditional on completing the study puts owners in a financially vulnerable position.

### E7

- Location: Study Design – Best Practices and Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "All patients enrolled in clinical trials, whether studied with or without a control group, must receive best supportive care."
- Key point: Every enrolled animal, with or without a control group, must receive best supportive care (treatment that relieves symptoms and maintains quality of life).

### E8

- Location: Study Design – Best Practices and Ethics Recommendations (endpoints)
- Quote: "In addition, euthanasia is a well‐known confounder for determination of overall survival in animal patients enrolled in clinical trials." / "Elective euthanasia, when a patient in a self‐supportive, clinically stable status is euthanized at the owner's request, must be considered carefully when examining outcome data from clinical trials."
- Key point: In animal clinical trials, euthanasia is a well-known confounder of overall survival; elective euthanasia at the owner's request while the patient is stable must be considered carefully when analyzing outcome data.

### E9

- Location: Study Design – Best Practices and Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "Commercially sponsored trials, donor‐sponsored trials, and hospital‐subsidized trials may not receive a scientific peer review separate from an evaluation by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC), the mandate of which does not always include such scientific merit review."
- Key point: Commercially sponsored, donor-sponsored, or hospital-subsidized trials may be reviewed only by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC), whose remit does not necessarily include scientific peer review.

### E10

- Location: Table 1, Guiding ethical principles
- Quote: "Results from all trials should be published to ensure reproducibility and avoid redundancy."
- Key point: Results from all trials should be published to ensure reproducibility and avoid redundancy.

### E11

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Clinical trials involving companion animals with naturally occurring cancers have been conducted for over 50 years with the intent of improving both animal and human health."
- Key point: Clinical trials in companion animals with naturally occurring cancers have been run for over 50 years, aiming to improve both animal and human health.

### E12

- Location: Consenting Process – Best Practices And Ethics Recommendations
- Quote: "The informed consent document should describe the aspects of the study in simple, concise, and easy‐to‐understand language."
- Key point: The informed consent document should describe the study in simple, concise, easy-to-understand language.

### E13

- Location: Clinical Trial Approval Policies – Best Practice Recommendations
- Quote: "Several institutions have adopted the model of a clinical review board (CRB) to evaluate the merit, feasibility, and compliance with ethical standards for clinical trials in client‐owned animals."
- Key point: Several institutions have set up clinical review boards (CRBs) to evaluate the merit, feasibility, and ethical compliance of clinical trials in client-owned animals.

### E14

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "A workshop bringing together a diverse group of stakeholders was convened at the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) headquarters in Washington, DC in November 2014 to confirm guiding ethical principles (Table 1) and develop ‘Best Practice Recommendations’ for clinical trial conduct and oversight in veterinary oncology."
- Key point: In November 2014, a workshop of diverse stakeholders met at American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) headquarters in Washington, DC, to confirm guiding ethical principles and draft best-practice recommendations for veterinary oncology clinical trials.
