# Carver 2010: Towards Reporting Guidelines for Experimental Replications: A Proposal

- Source: `carver2010towards`, [Original](https://carver.cs.ua.edu/ReplicationGuidelines.htm), local PDF `papers/pdf/carver2010towards.pdf`
- Version: Paper at RESER 2010 (the first Workshop on Replication in Empirical Software Engineering Research), 4 pages. Some items in the SIGSOFT standard for replication studies come from this paper.

### E1

- Location: §2.3
- Quote: "One of the main benefits of an experimental replication is that it provides researchers with the ability to confirm, refute, or deepen the conclusions drawn from an earlier study."
- Key point: The main value of a replication: it lets researchers confirm, refute, or deepen the conclusions of an earlier study.

### E2

- Location: §3.1.2, Level of interaction with original experimenters
- Quote: "If the replication is external (i.e. the original researchers are not involved), the level of interaction the replicators had with the original experimenter should be reported. This interaction could range from none (i.e. simply read the paper) to a lot (i.e. original experimenter acted as consultants)."
- Key point: An external replication should report how much the replicators interacted with the original authors, which can range from simply reading the paper to the original authors acting as consultants.

### E3

- Location: §1 Introduction
- Quote: "A major difference in the viewpoints taken by these two papers regards the level of interaction between replicating researchers and the original researchers. Without going through the whole debate here, there are legitimate issues on both sides."
- Key point: There are two opposing views on how much replicators and original authors should interact, and both sides have legitimate points.

### E4

- Location: §3.1.2, Changes to the original experiment
- Quote: "Any changes made to the design, participants, artifacts, procedures, data collected and/or analysis techniques should be discussed along with the motivation for the change."
- Key point: Report any change to the design, participants, artifacts, procedures, data collected, or analysis techniques, together with the reason for it.

### E5

- Location: §3.1.3
- Quote: "To make the comparison explicit, it is also important to have a section specifically devoted to comparing the results of the replication with the results of the original study."
- Key point: Devote a section specifically to comparing the replication's results with the original study's.

### E6

- Location: §3.1.3, Differences in results
- Quote: "results from the replication that did not coincide with the results from the original study. Authors should also discuss how changes made to the experimental design (Section 3.1.2) may have caused these differences."
- Key point: In that comparison, list the results that differ from the original study and discuss whether your changes may have caused the differences.

### E7

- Location: §3.1.4
- Quote: "By combining conclusions from the original study with conclusions from the replication, the authors should be able to provide insights that would not have been evident from either study individually."
- Key point: Finally, combine the conclusions of the original study and the replication to offer insights that neither study shows on its own.

### E8

- Location: §3.3 Adaptations for shorter papers
- Quote: "The recommendation is to provide all of the information, but with less detail. Comparisons between the original study and the replication can be summarized in tables rather than fully described in the text."
- Key point: When space is limited (as in a conference paper), still give every kind of information, just in less detail; comparisons with the original study can be summarized in tables.

### E9

- Location: §3.3 Adaptations for shorter papers
- Quote: "If information must be omitted, authors should ensure that the most important details, i.e. those details that relate to changes or differences between the original study and the replication, should be included in the report."
- Key point: If you must cut, the last thing to cut is the changes and differences relative to the original study.

### E10

- Location: §2.3
- Quote: "one interesting method use by two studies was to present the results of the replication and the results of the original study in a summary table for easy reference"
- Key point: Some replication papers put the replication's results and the original results side by side in one summary table for easy comparison.

### E11

- Location: §3.2
- Quote: "it is important for the author to place the results into the context of the entire family of studies. Conclusion should be drawn based on knowledge gained by analyzing the results of all studies."
- Key point: If the original study has already been replicated several times (a "family" of replications), place your results in the context of the whole family and draw conclusions from all the studies.
