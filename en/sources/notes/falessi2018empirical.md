# Falessi et al. 2018: Empirical Software Engineering Experts on the Use of Students and Professionals in Experiments

- Source: `falessi2018empirical`, [author version in the Brunel University repository](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/14575/1/Fulltext.pdf)
- Version: Empirical Software Engineering 23(1), 2018; author accepted manuscript

### E1

- Location: abstract
- Quote: "We, on the contrary, strongly believe that no population (students, professionals, or others) can be deemed better than another in absolute terms. [Conclusion] Using students as participants remains a valid simplification of reality needed in laboratory contexts."
- Key point: The authors hold that no population (students, professionals, or others) is better than another in absolute terms; in laboratory settings, using students as participants remains a valid and necessary simplification of reality.

### E2

- Location: §4.1.1 Misleading terminology
- Quote: "ST1 - Classifying experiment participants using a binary scale (students or professional) is an inappropriate approach."
- Key point: Classifying experiment participants on a binary scale (students or professionals) is inappropriate: the two overlap, and students may have work experience.

### E3

- Location: §4.1.2 Threats to validity trade-off
- Quote: "The experimental paradigm requires a trade-off between internal and external validity. It is infeasible to maximize both internal and external validity at the same time."
- Key point: Experiments require a trade-off between internal and external validity; both cannot be maximized at once.

### E4

- Location: §4.1.3 (convenience samples)
- Quote: "When experiments are conducted in a company, the participants are also a convenient sample because they are not selected randomly from the population of all developers and very often they are not even a random sample of the developers at the company."
- Key point: When experiments are run in a company, professionals are also a convenience sample: they are not randomly selected from all developers, and often not a random sample of the company's developers either.

### E5

- Location: §6.2 Added statements
- Quote: "ST9 - We should think about population and validity already before conducting the experiment, at the time when we are planning to use convenience sampling."
- Key point: Think about the population and validity before running the experiment, when you plan to use a convenience sample, rather than looking for justifications afterward. (100% agreement in the expert survey)

### E6

- Location: §6.2 Added statements
- Quote: "ST16 - Papers should provide a precise description of the reasons why the use of students as subjects in the study was appropriate under the specific circumstances of the experiment reported. On the other hand, reviewers should criticize the use of students not as a general statement valid for all experiments, but by detailing why it was inappropriate under the specific circumstances in the experiment presented."
- Key point: Papers should state precisely why using students was appropriate in the specific circumstances of the study; reviewers who object to students should likewise state precisely why it is inappropriate for this experiment, rather than objecting in general terms.

### E7

- Location: §4.2.1 Hard to get, even harder to improve
- Quote: "This means that in one case, only 33% of the committed participants finally showed up for the experiment. In this author's experience, "no shows" happen more often with professionals than with students."
- Key point: One author's experience: in one experiment, only 33% of the professionals who committed actually showed up; no-shows are more common among professionals than among students.

### E8

- Location: §7 Towards a characterization of experience
- Quote: "The R3 scheme characterizes Real, Relevant, and Recent experience, hence the name R3."
- Key point: The authors propose characterizing participants by Real, Relevant, and Recent experience (the R3 scheme) rather than just labeling them "students" or "professionals".

### E9

- Location: §9 Discussion
- Quote: "Thus, students are clearly neither always nor never suitable as proxies for professional developers; it depends on different contexts and with different types of population."
- Key point: Students are neither always nor never suitable proxies for professional developers; it depends on the context and the target population.
