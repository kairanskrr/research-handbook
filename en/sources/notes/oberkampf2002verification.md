# Oberkampf and Trucano 2002: Verification and Validation in Computational Fluid Dynamics

- Source: `oberkampf2002verification`, [original](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/793406)
- Version: Sandia National Laboratories report SAND2002-0529 (Unlimited Release, March 2002), freely available from the US Department of Energy's Office of Scientific and Technical Information (OSTI). Locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: 1.1 Background
- Quote: "Briefly, verification is the assessment of the accuracy of the solution to a computational model by comparison with known solutions. Validation is the assessment of the accuracy of a computational simulation by comparison with experimental data."
- Key point: Verification checks how accurately a computational model is solved by comparing with known solutions; validation checks how accurate the simulation is by comparing with experimental data.

### E2

- Location: 1.1 Background
- Quote: "Stated differently, verification is primarily a mathematics issue; validation is primarily a physics issue"
- Key point: Verification is primarily a mathematics issue; validation is primarily a physics issue.

### E3

- Location: 2.1 Development of Terminology for Verification and Validation
- Quote: "Validation: The process of determining the degree to which a model is an accurate representation of the real world from the perspective of the intended uses of the model."
- Key point: The definition adopted by the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA): validation is determining the degree to which a model is an accurate representation of the real world from the perspective of its intended uses.

### E4

- Location: 2.1 Development of Terminology for Verification and Validation
- Quote: "Thus, V&V activities can only assess the correctness or accuracy of the specific cases tested."
- Key point: Verification and validation (V&V) can only assess the correctness or accuracy of the specific cases tested.

### E5

- Location: 4.1 Fundamentals of Validation
- Quote: "In other words, calibration, or parameter estimation, is a response to obtain needed agreement between the computational results and the experimental data."
- Key point: Calibration (also called parameter estimation) is adjusting the model to obtain agreement between computational results and experimental data; the authors stress that it must be kept distinct from validation.

### E6

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments, Guideline 1
- Quote: "A validation experiment should be jointly designed by experimentalists, model developers, code developers, and code users working closely together throughout the program, from inception to documentation, with complete candor about the strengths and weaknesses of each approach."
- Key point: A validation experiment should be designed jointly, from start to finish, by experimentalists, model developers, code developers, and code users, each candid about the strengths and weaknesses of their approach.

### E7

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments, Guideline 4
- Quote: "The reason for this recommendation, of course, is that it is so common for CFD codes to be calibrated to the experimental measurements that many people do not recognize when they are calibrating versus validating"
- Key point: Calibrating computational fluid dynamics (CFD) codes to experimental measurements is so common that many people cannot tell whether they are calibrating or validating.

### E8

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments, Guideline 4
- Quote: "the CFD team should not be given the results initially. The CFD team should be given the complete details of the physical modeling parameters and the initial and boundary conditions of the experiment, exactly as it was conducted. That is, everything that is needed for the computationalists to compute solutions must be provided—but no more."
- Key point: After the experimental data are processed, do not give the results to the computational team at first; give them only the complete physical modeling parameters and initial and boundary conditions actually used in the experiment, everything needed for the computation and nothing more.

### E9

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments, Guideline 4
- Quote: "The computationalists must quantify the errors and uncertainties in the CFD solution and then present the results for comparison with the experimental data."
- Key point: The computationalists must first quantify the errors and uncertainties in their solution and only then present it for comparison with the experimental data.

### E10

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments (closing paragraph on estimating experimental uncertainty)
- Quote: "As a final comment on the estimation of experimental uncertainty, we recommend that the same validation experiment be conducted, if possible, in different facilities."
- Key point: If possible, conduct the same validation experiment in different facilities to reveal facility-related biases.

### E11

- Location: 4.3 Guidelines for Validation Experiments, Guideline 4
- Quote: "For example, a code being developed by university researchers need not, in our view, attain the level of experimental and computational independence recommended here."
- Key point: The authors hold that this strict independence between computation and experiment is meant for codes used in engineering analysis; codes developed by university researchers need not reach that level.
