# Mark, Gudith, Klocke 2008: The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress

- Source: `mark2008cost`, [PDF](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf)
- Version: CHI 2008 (ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems) short paper, PDF from the author's website, read 2026-10-07, extracted with pdftotext. Locations give the section name.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We found that context does not make a difference but surprisingly, people completed interrupted tasks in less time with no difference in quality. Our data suggests that people compensate for interruptions by working faster, but this comes at a price: experiencing more stress, higher frustration, time pressure and effort."
- Key point: People completed interrupted tasks in less time with no difference in quality, but they compensated by working faster, at the cost of more stress, frustration, time pressure, and effort. Whether the interruption was related to the task at hand made no difference.

### E2

- Location: Experimental Design and Procedure
- Quote: "Forty-eight subjects participated." / "We simulated an office environment in the lab and chose an email task, common in information work." / "Interruption frequency was set to two minutes"
- Key point: The experiment had 48 participants in a simulated office environment in the lab, answering email; interruptions came every two minutes.

### E3

- Location: Discussion and Conclusions
- Quote: "After only 20 minutes of interrupted performance people reported significantly higher stress, frustration, workload, effort, and pressure."
- Key point: After only 20 minutes of interrupted work, participants reported significantly higher stress, frustration, workload, effort, and pressure.

### E4

- Location: Discussion and Conclusions
- Quote: "Our results showed that any interruption introduces a change in work pattern and is not related to context per se."
- Key point: Any interruption changes the pattern of work, regardless of whether its content relates to the task at hand.
