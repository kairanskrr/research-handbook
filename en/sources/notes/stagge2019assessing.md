# Stagge et al. 2019: Assessing data availability and research reproducibility in hydrology and water resources

- Source: `stagge2019assessing`, [original](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201930)
- Version: Scientific Data 6: 190030, open access.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We used the tool to assess 360 of the 1,989 articles published by six hydrology and water resources journals in 2017."
- Key point: Using a survey tool they designed, the authors assessed 360 of the 1,989 papers published in 2017 in six hydrology and water resources journals.

### E2

- Location: Results
- Quote: "70.3% of the 360 sampled articles stated some materials were available, but we could only access 48.6% of those materials online" / "Only 5.6% of sampled articles made data, model/code, and directions publicly available while just 1.1% of sampled articles made artifacts available and were fully reproduced."
- Key point: Of the 360 sampled papers, 70.3% stated that materials were available, but only 48.6% of those materials could actually be obtained online; only 5.6% made data, models, or code available together with directions for use; and only 1.1% both made materials available and were fully reproduced.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "We estimated, with 95% confidence, that results might be reproduced for only 0.6% to 6.8% of all 1,989 articles."
- Key point: The authors estimate that between 0.6% and 6.8% (95% confidence interval) of the 1,989 papers have reproducible results.

### E4

- Location: Results, Reproducibility of Results
- Quote: "A common issue across cases where we did not generate results was that folder and file locations were hard-coded to work on the author’s computer."
- Key point: Among the cases where results could not be generated, a common problem was folder and file paths hard-coded so they only worked on the author's computer.

### E5

- Location: Discussion, Authors
- Quote: "our results showed that, for all journals, the number of sampled articles with code/data/software was consistently 2 to 3 times higher than the number of articles that provided directions"
- Key point: In all six journals, sampled papers that provided code, data, or software outnumbered those that also provided directions for use by a factor of 2 to 3.

### E6

- Location: Results, Reproducibility and Journal Policies
- Quote: "Stronger journal data availability policies and making open data commitments tend to produce higher rates of artifact availability and result reproducibility. However, there is significant variation among these journals"
- Key point: Journals with stronger data-availability policies tended to have higher rates of material availability and reproducibility, but there was wide variation among journals.

### E7

- Location: Results, Reproducibility and Journal Policies
- Quote: "HESS and WRR, which require data availability statements, had lower percentages of articles that made artifacts available and more papers that direct readers to the authors or third parties for data, models, or code"
- Key point: The two journals that required data availability statements (HESS and WRR) actually had lower proportions of papers with publicly available materials; more of their papers sent readers to the authors or third parties to request data, models, or code.

### E8

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "we reproduced results for only a small fraction of articles (1.6% of tested articles) using their available artifacts. We estimated, with 95% confidence, that results might be reproduced for only 0.6% to 6.8% of all 1,989 articles."
- Key point: Of the sampled papers, only 1.6% could be reproduced from the available materials; extrapolated to all 1,989 papers, the reproducible proportion lies between 0.6% and 6.8% (95% confidence interval).

### E9

- Location: Results
- Quote: "70.3% of the 360 sampled articles stated some materials were available, but we could only access 48.6% of those materials online"
- Key point: Of the 360 papers, 70.3% stated that materials were available, but only 48.6% of those could actually be obtained online.

### E10

- Location: Results
- Quote: "Hydrology and Earth Systems Sciences (HESS) and Water Resources Research (WRR) had high percentages of articles where materials were only available by first author request (38.5–40.2%)."
- Key point: Among the sampled 2017 papers in Hydrology and Earth System Sciences (HESS) and Water Resources Research (WRR), 38.5%-40.2% made their materials available only "upon request from the first author."

### E11

- Location: Results
- Quote: "Other cases pointed to general data gateways, like the USGS streamgauge network, with no further details, or required expensive or proprietary software."
- Key point: Other papers pointed only to a general data portal (such as the US Geological Survey's stream gauge network) without specifics, or relied on expensive commercial software.

### E12

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "for all journals, the number of sampled articles with code/data/software was consistently 2 to 3 times higher than the number of articles that provided directions"
- Key point: In all six journals, the number of sampled papers that provided code or data was 2 to 3 times the number that provided directions for use.

### E13

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "Authors can use the survey tool as a checklist to self-assess the availability of data, models, code, and directions and reproducibility of their work before submitting work for publication."
- Key point: Authors can use the survey as a checklist to check their own work before submission.

### E14

- Location: Discussion
- Quote: "Authors who bundled their code and data together in a single GitHub repository further allowed us to download the entire project, with a higher likelihood that code pointed to valid file directories."
- Key point: Bundling code and data in the same repository makes it easier for someone reproducing the work to get the whole project, and makes it more likely that paths will work.
