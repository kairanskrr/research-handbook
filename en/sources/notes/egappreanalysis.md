# EGAP Methods Guide: 10 Things to Know About Pre-Analysis Plans

- Source: `egappreanalysis`, [original web page](https://methods.egap.org/guides/planning/pap_en.html)
- Version: Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP) methods guide by Nuole (Lula) Chen and Christopher Grady; accessed October 2026. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1 What is a Pre-Analysis Plan (PAP)
- Quote: "A PAP is a document that formalizes and declares the design and analysis plan for your study. It is written before the analysis is conducted and is generally registered on a third-party website."
- Key point: A pre-analysis plan (PAP) is a document setting out the study's design and analysis plan, written before the analysis and generally registered on a third-party website.

### E2

- Location: §1
- Quote: "a PAP should, at a minimum, include the full list of hypotheses that you intend to test, how you will measure variables relevant to those hypotheses, and a verifiable time stamp."
- Key point: At a minimum, a pre-analysis plan should include the full list of hypotheses to be tested, how the relevant variables will be measured, and a verifiable timestamp.

### E3

- Location: §3 Hypotheses
- Quote: "with more than one hypothesis you will need to specify a procedure for handling multiple hypotheses in the inference criteria section of your PAP, either by correcting for multiple tests or by aggregating all hypotheses into an index or an omnibus test."
- Key point: With more than one hypothesis, specify in advance how you will handle multiple testing: either correct for it, or combine outcomes into an index or run an omnibus test.

### E4

- Location: §9 Why make a PAP?
- Quote: "But for experimental studies or other studies using original data collection, the researcher gets one chance to collect the data needed for the analysis."
- Key point: In experiments and other studies that collect original data, you get only one chance to collect the data, and writing a plan helps you think it through beforehand.

### E5

- Location: §9 Why make a PAP?
- Quote: "Exploratory analyses are okay! The point of a PAP is not to prevent these unanticipated analyses, but to formalize and explain the process that led to the analyses."
- Key point: Exploratory analyses are fine; the point of a plan is not to prevent unanticipated analyses but to document and explain the process that led to them.

### E6

- Location: §10 When and where do you register a PAP?
- Quote: "For experimental studies, the latest you should register the PAP is before final data are collected. For observational studies, the latest you should register the PAP is before any analyses are done, including looking at descriptive statistics. You can revise PAPs through an amendment at any time."
- Key point: Experimental studies should register at the latest before final data are collected; observational studies at the latest before any analysis (including looking at descriptive statistics); the plan can be updated later through amendments.
