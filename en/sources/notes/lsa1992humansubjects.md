# Linguistic Society of America 1992: Human Subjects in Linguistic Research

- Source: `lsa1992humansubjects`, [full text](https://www.lsadc.org/human_subjects_in_linguistic_research)
- Version: adopted by the Executive Committee of the Linguistic Society of America in May 1992

### E1

- Location: opening note
- Quote: "Members who do research with human subjects have expressed concern that the Federal Policy on the Protection of the Human Subjects does not always apply to linguistic research."
- Key point: The background of the statement: the US Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects does not always fit linguistic research.

### E2

- Location: main text, paragraph 1
- Quote: "Studies of a human language often depend upon a continuing relation with speakers of the language."
- Key point: Studying a human language often depends on a continuing relationship with its speakers.

### E3

- Location: main text, paragraph 3
- Quote: "Those who participate in such a work often do so with pride in their command of their language and may wish to be known for their contribution. Not to disclose their names would do them a disservice."
- Key point: Participants often take pride in their command of their language and may wish to be known for their contribution; withholding their names would do them a disservice.

### E4

- Location: main text, last paragraph
- Quote: "Such considerations make it difficult to apply general rules in a mechanical way."
- Key point: Such considerations make it difficult to apply general rules mechanically.

### E5

- Location: main text, paragraph 1
- Quote: "Studies of a human language often depend upon a continuing relation with speakers of the language. Such a relation comes to be defined as much by the speakers as by the linguist. Their patterns of life govern when work can be done."
- Key point: Language research often depends on a long-term relationship with speakers; that relationship is defined as much by the speakers as by the linguist, and when work can happen is set by the rhythm of their lives.
