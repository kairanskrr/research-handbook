# Varian 1997: How to Build an Economic Model in Your Spare Time

- Source: `varian1997build`, [original PDF](https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~hal/Papers/how.pdf)
- Version: Public version on the author's home page. Locations give the section number.

### E1

- Location: §1.1 Getting ideas
- Quote: "I think that you should look for your ideas outside the academic journals--in newspapers, in magazines, in conversations, and in TV and radio programs."
- Key point: The author suggests looking for ideas outside academic journals: in newspapers, magazines, conversations, and TV and radio programs.

### E2

- Location: §1.2 Is your idea worth pursuing?
- Quote: "The first test is to try to phrase your idea in a way that a non-economist can understand."
- Key point: The first test of an idea: can you phrase it in a way a non-economist can understand?

### E3

- Location: §1.3 Don't look at the literature too soon
- Quote: "Eventually you should do a thorough literature review, of course, but I think that you will do much better if you work on your idea for a few weeks before doing a systematic literature search."
- Key point: Eventually you should do a thorough literature review, but the author suggests working on your idea for a few weeks before searching the literature systematically.

### E4

- Location: §1.4 Building your model
- Quote: "Lucky for you, all economics models look pretty much the same. There are some economic agents. They make choices in order to advance their objectives. The choices have to satisfy various constraints so there's something that adjusts to make all these choices consistent."
- Key point: The author says economics models all look pretty much the same: there are economic agents who make choices to advance their objectives, the choices must satisfy constraints, and something adjusts to make the choices consistent.

### E5

- Location: §1.4 Building your model
- Quote: "Most students think that the next stage is to prove a theorem or run a regression. No! The next stage is to work an example." / "Write down the simplest possible model you can think of, and see if it still exhibits some interesting behavior. If it does, then make it even simpler."
- Key point: Work an example first instead of rushing to prove a theorem or run a regression; write down the simplest possible model you can think of, and if it still shows something interesting, simplify further.

### E6

- Location: §1.6 Making mistakes
- Quote: "This process--simplify to get the result, complexify to see how general it is--is a good way to understand your model."
- Key point: Simplify to get the result, then generalize to see how general it is; going back and forth like this is a good way to understand your model.

### E7

- Location: §1.8 Giving a seminar
- Quote: "the more you can talk [...] about your work, the better the final paper will be. This is because a talk forces you to get to the point."
- Key point: The more you talk about your work, the better the final paper will be, because a talk forces you to get to the point.

### E8

- Location: §1.8 Giving a seminar
- Quote: "When you are done with your talk you should take a few minutes to jot down some notes: what was difficult for people to understand? What questions did they ask? What suggestions did they make? What references did they give you?"
- Key point: Right after a talk, jot down notes: what people found hard to understand, what they asked, what they suggested, and what references they gave.

### E9

- Location: §1.11 When to stop
- Quote: "You can tell when your work is getting ready for publication by the reactions in the seminars: people stop asking questions."
- Key point: When people at seminars stop asking questions, your work is getting ready for publication.

### E10

- Location: §1.8 Giving a seminar / Controlling the audience
- Quote: "Spell out each aspect of your result in excruciating detail so no one can possibly misunderstand. When you do this you will certainly get questions like "Will this generalize to n agents?" or "Have you corrected for heteroskedasticity?""
- Key point: When presenting your first result, spell out every detail; someone will certainly ask questions on the spot, and the author suggests using this to build the audience's trust before moving on.
