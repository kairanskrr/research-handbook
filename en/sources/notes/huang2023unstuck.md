# Huang 2023: How to get unstuck? (tweet thread)

- Source: `huang2023unstuck`, [original tweet](https://twitter.com/jbhuang0604/status/1717381151508054029), [Thread Reader mirror](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1717381151508054029.html)
- Version: Tweet thread by Jia-Bin Huang, 2023-10-26, 11 tweets; read via the Thread Reader mirror on 2026-10-05. Locations give the subheadings in the tweets.

### E1

- Location: Tweet 1
- Quote: "In most research projects, ~99% of the time your experiments DO NOT work."
- Key point: In most research projects, the vast majority of experiments do not work.

### E2

- Location: *Show HOW it failed*
- Quote: "Here is the input and our expected results. But here are our current results."
- Key point: When asking others for help, show the input, the expected results, and the actual results, rather than just saying "it doesn't work."

### E3

- Location: *Simplify the tasks until it works*
- Quote: "Progressively make your tasks simpler until the tasks become so trivial that they must "work"."
- Key point: Make the task progressively simpler until it is so trivial it must work, then add difficulty back from there.

### E4

- Location: *Introduce privileged information*
- Quote: "Adding privileged information is a powerful trick to make progress."
- Key point: When you need to estimate both A and B, first assume one is known and solve only for the other; adding "privileged information" is a powerful trick for making progress.

### E5

- Location: *Debug the idea*
- Quote: "When you debug all your code/experiments and still find it not working, take a step back and debug the idea."
- Key point: If you have debugged all your code and experiments and it still does not work, step back and debug the idea itself.

### E6

- Location: *Iterate fast*
- Quote: "Find toy examples and proxy tasks so you can have a short turnaround time."
- Key point: Use toy examples and proxy tasks to shorten turnaround time; the faster you iterate, the more progress you make.

### E7

- Location: *Help your mentors help you*
- Quote: "Your mentors have more experience than you and are willing to help. But you have to help them help you."
- Key point: Your mentors have experience and are willing to help, but you have to help them help you: explain why you ran the experiment, how you ran it, and what you saw.

### E8

- Location: *Make your experiments easy to understand*
- Quote: "If you only verbally describe why/how/what you did, it's difficult for others to fully understand the process."
- Key point: A purely verbal description is hard for others to fully understand; draw the pipeline and write down the details.
