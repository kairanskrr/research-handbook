# SAA and ACRL/RBMS 2018: Guidelines for Primary Source Literacy

- Source: `saaacrl2018primary`, [original PDF](https://www2.archivists.org/sites/all/files/Guidelines%20for%20Primary%20Souce%20Literacy_AsApproved062018_1.pdf) ([standard's page](https://www2.archivists.org/standards/guidelines-for-primary-source-literacy))
- Version: Written by a joint task force of the Society of American Archivists (SAA) and the Rare Books and Manuscripts Section (RBMS) of the Association of College and Research Libraries (ACRL); approved by the ACRL Board in February 2018 and the SAA Council in June 2018. Free PDF from the official site, read 2026-10-06. Locations give section names (Introduction, Core Ideas, Learning Objectives numbers, Appendix 1 glossary entries). The document is written mainly for librarians, archivists, and instructors who teach the use of primary sources, but says students and researchers can also use it on their own.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "They require critical analysis due to their creators' intents and biases; the variety of contexts in which they have been created, preserved, and made accessible; and the gaps, absences, and silences that may exist in the materials."
- Key point: Primary sources need critical analysis for three reasons: their creators' intents and biases; the varied contexts in which they were created, preserved, and made accessible; and the gaps, absences, and silences they may contain.

### E2

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Discipline"
- Quote: "Disciplines often have specific views on the types of sources that should be used in research and what constitutes a primary source."
- Key point: Disciplines often have their own views on which types of sources to use and what counts as a primary source.

### E3

- Location: Core Ideas, Analytical Concepts
- Quote: "Interpretation of sources occurs on a continuum from the creation of the source to its utilization by the current user, and includes mediation by librarians, archivists, and database creators or designers."
- Key point: Interpretation of a source runs on a continuum from its creation to its use by today's reader, including mediation by librarians, archivists, and database designers.

### E4

- Location: Core Ideas, Theoretical Concepts
- Quote: "Users must seek to understand resulting silences and absences by critically considering what sources were never created, what sources may no longer exist, and what sources are collected, as well as communities' abilities to engage in these activities."
- Key point: To understand silences and absences in the archive, consider which sources were never created, which no longer exist, which were collected, and whether different communities had the ability to do these things.

### E5

- Location: Core Ideas, Theoretical Concepts
- Quote: "Collections in cultural heritage institutions reflect and reinforce societal power structures."
- Key point: Collections in cultural heritage institutions reflect and reinforce societal power structures.

### E6

- Location: Core Ideas, Theoretical Concepts
- Quote: "Collections and databases are always mediated in some way, and exhibits, digital collections, and guides or other access tools reflect the selection, reproduction, and presentation decisions of many individuals"
- Key point: Collections and databases are always mediated in some way; exhibits, digital collections, and guides or other access tools reflect many people's decisions about selection, reproduction, and presentation.

### E7

- Location: Core Ideas, Practical Considerations
- Quote: "They will need strategies for capturing and managing research data, including transcription, photography, and downloads."
- Key point: Users need strategies for capturing and managing research data, including transcription, photography, and downloads.

### E8

- Location: Learning Objectives 1.C, 1.D
- Quote: "Draw on primary sources to generate and refine research questions." / "Understand that research is an iterative process and that as primary sources are found and"
- Key point: Use primary sources to generate and refine research questions; research is iterative, and questions may change as sources are found and analyzed.

### E9

- Location: Learning Objectives 2.D
- Quote: "Understand that historical records may never have existed, may not have survived, or may not be collected and/or publicly accessible. Existing records may have been shaped by the selectivity and mediation of individuals such as collectors, archivists, librarians, donors, and/or publishers, potentially limiting the sources available for research."
- Key point: Historical records may never have existed, may not have survived, or may not be collected or publicly accessible; existing records may have been shaped by the selection and mediation of collectors, archivists, donors, publishers, and others.

### E10

- Location: Learning Objectives 2.C
- Quote: "Distinguish between catalogs, databases, and other online resources that contain information about sources, versus those that contain digital versions, originals, or copies of the sources themselves."
- Key point: Distinguish catalogs and databases that contain information about sources from resources that contain the sources themselves (digital versions, originals, or copies).

### E11

- Location: Learning Objectives 3.B
- Quote: "Identify and communicate information found in primary sources, including summarizing the content of the source and identifying and reporting key components such as how it was created, by whom, when, and what it is."
- Key point: When reading a primary source, be able to summarize its content and state how it was created, by whom, when, and what it is.

### E12

- Location: Learning Objectives 3.C
- Quote: "Understand that a primary source may exist in a variety of iterations, including excerpts, transcriptions, and translations, due to publication, copying, and other transformations."
- Key point: One primary source may exist in several iterations: excerpts, transcriptions, translations, and so on.

### E13

- Location: Learning Objectives 4.B
- Quote: "Critically evaluate the perspective of the creator(s) of a primary source, including tone, subjectivity, and biases, and consider how these relate to the original purpose(s) and audience(s) of the source."
- Key point: Evaluate the creator's perspective (tone, subjectivity, biases) and consider how it relates to the source's original purpose and audience.

### E14

- Location: Learning Objectives 4.D
- Quote: "As part of the analysis of available resources, identify, interrogate, and consider the reasons for silences, gaps, contradictions, or evidence of power relationships in the documentary record"
- Key point: When analyzing sources, identify and interrogate silences, gaps, contradictions, and evidence of power relationships in the documentary record, and consider their causes.

### E15

- Location: Learning Objectives 4.E
- Quote: "Factor physical and material elements into the interpretation of primary sources including the"
- Key point: Factor a primary source's physical and material features into its interpretation.

### E16

- Location: Learning Objectives 5.A
- Quote: "Examine and synthesize a variety of sources in order to construct, support, or dispute a research argument."
- Key point: Examine and synthesize a variety of sources to construct, support, or dispute a research argument.

### E17

- Location: Learning Objectives 5.C
- Quote: "Cite primary sources in accordance with appropriate citation style guidelines or according to repository practice and preferences (when possible)."
- Key point: Cite primary sources in an appropriate citation style and, when possible, according to the repository's practice and preferences.

### E18

- Location: Learning Objectives 5.B, 5.D
- Quote: "Use primary sources in a manner that respects privacy rights and cultural contexts." / "Adhere to copyright and privacy laws when incorporating primary source information in a research or creative project."
- Key point: Use primary sources in a way that respects privacy and cultural context, and comply with copyright and privacy law.

### E19

- Location: Core Ideas, Ethical Concepts
- Quote: "They must understand how these concepts affect their ability to use primary sources in their work, and they should responsibly consider how their scholarship may potentially affect the creators, donors, owners, and readers of these primary sources."
- Key point: Understand how law, privacy, donor agreements, copyright, and so on affect your use of sources, and consider responsibly how your scholarship may affect the sources' creators, donors, owners, and readers.

### E20

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Authenticity"
- Quote: "Authenticity alone does not automatically imply that the content of a record is reliable."
- Key point: A record being authentic does not mean its content is reliable.

### E21

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Silences"
- Quote: "Gaps or missing pieces in the historical record, often caused by those who were unable to write their own records, or whose records were not considered valuable or were suppressed by the dominant culture. Should be distinguished from merely lack of holdings in a particular repository."
- Key point: "Silences" are gaps in the historical record, often because some people could not create records, or their records were not valued or were suppressed; they should be distinguished from a particular repository simply not holding something.

### E22

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Research Question"
- Quote: "A good research question is answerable, arguable, and is not too broad or too narrow." / "The research question determines what information is needed, and may help the researcher define which sources are considered "primary" for a given project."
- Key point: A good research question is answerable, arguable, and neither too broad nor too narrow; it determines what information is needed and helps define which sources count as primary.

### E23

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Privacy"
- Quote: "Professionals may restrict or redact records because of applicable laws, institutional risk management decisions, donor agreements, or professional ethics."
- Key point: Archive and library professionals may restrict or redact records because of laws, institutional risk management, donor agreements, or professional ethics.

### E24

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Copyright"
- Quote: "Copyright law varies by country."
- Key point: Copyright law varies by country.

### E25

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Evidence"
- Quote: "The strength of supporting evidence and the approach by which it is gathered and applied to a claim impact the credibility of the claim"
- Key point: How strong the evidence is, and how it is gathered and applied to a claim, affect the claim's credibility.

### E26

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Public Domain"
- Quote: "Privacy and other ethical considerations should still be weighed when using materials in the public domain."
- Key point: Even when material is in the public domain (free of copyright restrictions), privacy and other ethical considerations still need weighing.

### E27

- Location: Appendix 1, entry "Mediation"
- Quote: "Librarians and archivists also provide mediation for documents through finding aids and catalog records, as well as their organization and arrangement of materials."
- Key point: Librarians and archivists also mediate between documents and users through finding aids, catalog records, and the way they organize and arrange materials.
