# Kozlowski 2022: On the Topic of Substrate Scope

- Source: `kozlowski2022substrate`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.orglett.2c03246)
- Version: Organic Letters 2022, 24, 7247–7249, an editorial. The author was then Editor-in-Chief of Organic Letters (Department of Chemistry, University of Pennsylvania). Free to read on the publisher's site; because the site blocks scripts, this excerpt reads the publisher's page as saved by the Internet Archive on 2025-07-07. The editorial states that its views are the author's own and not necessarily those of the American Chemical Society.

### E1

- Location: Opening
- Quote: "Increasingly, we have been seeing submissions with very large numbers of substrates (>50), and even a submission where the entire scope was relegated to the Supporting Information."
- Key point: The editors increasingly receive submissions with very large numbers of substrates (over 50), and even one with the entire scope relegated to the Supporting Information.

### E2

- Location: Opening (the author's Twitter poll of September 2022)
- Quote: "I engaged the organic chemistry community via Twitter on September 8 th of this year with a poll asking what a reasonable number of examples might be" / "there is clearly overwhelming support for <30 examples (88% of 1909 votes)."
- Key point: In the author's Twitter poll, 88% of 1,909 votes favored fewer than 30 examples.

### E3

- Location: Opening (conclusion of the poll)
- Quote: "The overwhelming conclusion is that a small scope is acceptable provided that it is diverse , and the more so the better."
- Key point: The conclusion: a small scope is acceptable provided it is diverse, and the more diverse the better.

### E4

- Location: Paragraph explaining Figure 2
- Quote: "The red substrates below the line would be far better choices for scope as they answer questions about sterics (2-methyl), functional group compatibility (4-hydroxy, 2-pyridyl, 3-pyridyl), and reactivity of the carbonyl (ketone, alkyl aldehyde, activated ketone)."
- Key point: Good substrates each answer a question: sterics, functional group compatibility, reactivity of the carbonyl, and so on.

### E5

- Location: Paragraph explaining Figure 2
- Quote: "Screening of additives is an alternative means of assessing compatibility with different functional groups that can also reduce the amount of work associated with development of a new reaction method."
- Key point: Additive screening is an alternative way to assess functional group compatibility and can also reduce the workload.

### E6

- Location: Drug-like Structures or Not?
- Quote: "I agree that such molecules can be helpful especially to our colleagues in pharma, but they should not be a prerequisite until such a time as they are freely available."
- Key point: Complex substrates such as drug molecules are helpful, but they should not be a prerequisite for publication until they are freely available.

### E7

- Location: Substrates That Do Not Work
- Quote: "For the reasons above, it is hard, as an author, to put lots of negative examples into an article because of fear that reviewers will not be positive."
- Key point: Authors find it hard to include many negative examples because they fear reviewers will react badly.

### E8

- Location: Substrates That Do Not Work
- Quote: "I recommend including selected examples of low-yielding or nonyielding reactions in either the manuscript text or tables to provide a general sense of what does and does not work. Additional tables of ALL unsuccessful substrates can be added to the Supporting Information."
- Key point: The author recommends including selected low-yielding or non-reacting examples in the text or tables to give a general sense of what does and does not work; tables of all unsuccessful substrates can go in the Supporting Information.

### E9

- Location: Reviewers
- Quote: "We ask reviewers to keep in mind the above principles as well (more is not always better; negative results are not always bad) when they review submissions for Organic Letters."
- Key point: Reviewers are asked to remember that more is not always better and negative results are not always bad.

### E10

- Location: Reviewers (a quoted reviewer comment)
- Quote: "Substrates should be chosen to highlight some important fact about a reaction, not just fill a table."
- Key point: Substrates should be chosen to highlight some important fact about a reaction, not just fill a table.

### E11

- Location: Reasonable Substrate Scope Examples
- Quote: "For methods discoveries, we generally recommend 10–20 diverse substrates including some that are unreactive or give low yields."
- Key point: For methods letters, Organic Letters generally recommends 10–20 diverse substrates, including some that are unreactive or give low yields.

### E12

- Location: Reasonable Substrate Scope Examples
- Quote: "Even so, there may be instances where additional substrates are desirable, so this recommendation is by no means meant to be definitive."
- Key point: This recommendation is not a hard rule; sometimes more substrates are warranted.

### E13

- Location: Reasonable Substrate Scope Examples
- Quote: "There may be instances where an extensive substrate scope in a Full Article does indeed add value in terms of showing reproducibility/robustness, delineating the subtle interplay of multiple modifications, and further providing data to drive machine learning efforts."
- Key point: In a full article, an extensive substrate scope can sometimes add value, for example by showing reproducibility and robustness or providing data for machine learning.

### E14

- Location: Substrates That Do Not Work (a quoted reader comment)
- Quote: "I want to know what happened with the obvious substrate that is missing (like if all your aryls only have meta or para substituents, what did ortho do?)."
- Key point: Readers want to know what happened with the obvious missing substrate; for example, if all aryl substituents are meta or para, what did ortho do?
