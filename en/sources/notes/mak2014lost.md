# Mak, Evaniew, and Ghert 2014: Lost in translation: animal models and clinical trials in cancer treatment

- Source: `mak2014lost`, [full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3902221/)
- Version: American Journal of Translational Research 6(2):114–118, 2014. A short narrative review (not a systematic review); most figures in it are cited from other literature. Locations give the subheading.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "However, the average rate of successful translation from animal models to clinical cancer trials is less than 8%."
- Key point: According to the authors, the average rate of successful translation from animal models to clinical cancer trials is less than 8%.

### E2

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Despite successful pre-clinical testing, 85% of early clinical trials for novel drugs fail; of those that survive through to phase III, only half become approved for clinical use"
- Key point: Figures the authors cite: despite successful preclinical testing, 85% of early clinical trials of new drugs fail, and of those that reach phase III, only half are approved.

### E3

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "Animal models are limited in their ability to mimic the extremely complex process of human carcinogenesis, physiology and progression."
- Key point: Animal models are limited in their ability to mimic the complex process of human carcinogenesis, physiology, and progression.

### E4

- Location: Limitations of animal models in cancer research
- Quote: "Indeed, animal studies seem to overestimate by about 30% the likelihood that a treatment will be effective because negative results are often unpublished"
- Key point: Research the authors cite suggests that animal studies overestimate the likelihood of efficacy by about 30%, because negative results often go unpublished.

### E5

- Location: Limitations of animal models in cancer research
- Quote: "The failure to translate from animals to humans is likely due in part to poor methodology and failure of the models to accurately mimic the human disease condition."
- Key point: The failure to translate from animals to humans is likely due partly to poor methodology and partly to models that do not accurately mimic the human disease.

### E6

- Location: Limitations of animal models in cancer research
- Quote: "It has been recommended that therapeutic agents should not only be evaluated in rodents, but also in higher animal species, and that randomization and outcomes assessor blinding should be performed. In addition, experiments should be designed in both genders and in different age groups of animals and all data, both positive and negative, should be published"
- Key point: Recommendations reported in the article: evaluate agents not only in rodents but also in higher species; randomize and blind outcome assessors; include animals of both sexes and different ages; publish both positive and negative data.

### E7

- Location: Notable examples of failed clinical cancer trials initiated due to successful animal models
- Quote: "In the first human clinical trials of TGN1412, the drug caused catastrophic systemic organ failure in patients, despite being administered at a sub-clinical dose that was 500 times lower than the dose found safe in animal studies"
- Key point: In its first human trials, TGN1412 caused catastrophic systemic organ failure, even though the dose was 500 times lower than the dose found safe in animal studies.

### E8

- Location: Notable examples of failed clinical cancer trials initiated due to successful animal models
- Quote: "IPI-926 treated mice with the advanced brain tumors gained a fivefold increase in survival" / "However, IPI-926 showed no effect compared to placebo in the human trial"
- Key point: IPI-926 gave mice with advanced brain tumors a fivefold increase in survival, but in human trials it showed no effect compared with placebo.

### E9

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Experimental discoveries are thought to begin at “the bench” with basic research, progress through pre-clinical animal studies, then show therapeutic efficacy in human clinical trials."
- Key point: The "bench to bedside" model: discoveries begin with basic research at the bench, move through preclinical animal studies, and then show therapeutic effect in human clinical trials.

### E10

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "Both the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and Health Canada require that animal tests be conducted before humans are exposed to a new molecular entity"
- Key point: Both the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and Health Canada require animal tests before humans are exposed to a new molecular entity.

### E11

- Location: Critical re-evaluation of animal models and alternative strategies
- Quote: "These include epidemiological studies, autopsies, in vitro studies, in silico computer modelling"
- Key point: Alternatives to preclinical animal studies include epidemiological studies, autopsies, in vitro studies, and in silico computer modeling (the article also mentions organs-on-chips and microfluidic chips).
