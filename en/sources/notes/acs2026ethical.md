# American Chemical Society 2026: Ethical Guidelines to Publication of Chemical Research

- Source: `acs2026ethical`, [original](https://researcher-resources.acs.org/publish/ethical_guidelines)
- Version: publication ethics guidelines set by the editors of the American Chemical Society (ACS) Publications Division, revised version effective June 15, 2026, accessed 2026-10-06. Applies to ACS journals.

### E1

- Location: B. Ethical Obligations of Authors, item 1
- Quote: "Data–defined as information collected or used to generate research conclusions–must be presented with sufficient detail and/or citation to reliable and accessible sources to allow a professional in the research field to replicate the study's findings."
- Key point: Data must be presented in enough detail (or with citations to reliable, accessible sources) that professionals in the field can repeat the results.

### E2

- Location: B, item 3
- Quote: "Data must be retained and made available for at least five years after publication, and authors are encouraged to deposit their data in a publicly accessible database when possible."
- Key point: Data must be retained and made available for at least five years after publication; authors are encouraged to deposit data in public databases where possible.

### E3

- Location: B, item 5
- Quote: "any unusual hazards associated with chemicals, equipment, or procedures used in an investigation must clearly be identified in the manuscript."
- Key point: Any unusual hazards associated with chemicals, equipment, or procedures used in the research must be clearly identified in the manuscript.

### E4

- Location: B, item 6
- Quote: "Authors must evaluate the potential for unintended dual-use or harmful applications of their research"
- Key point: Authors must evaluate the potential for unintended dual-use (civilian use that could also be misused) or harmful applications of their research.

### E5

- Location: B, item 7
- Quote: "Fragmenting research across multiple publications should be avoided."
- Key point: Fragmenting one study across multiple publications should be avoided.

### E6

- Location: B, item 9
- Quote: "Authors should submit a manuscript to only one journal at a time" / "Submitting a full paper that expands on a previously published brief report (such as a "communication" or "letter") is generally acceptable, but the earlier publication must be cited and disclosed to the editor at submission."
- Key point: Submit a manuscript to only one journal at a time; expanding a previously published brief report (a communication or letter) into a full paper is generally acceptable, but the earlier report must be cited and the editor informed at submission.

### E7

- Location: B, item 13 (authorship)
- Quote: "The co-authors of a manuscript should be all those persons who have made significant scientific contributions to the reported work and who accept shared responsibility and accountability for the integrity and accuracy of the results."
- Key point: Co-authors should be all those who made significant scientific contributions to the work and who accept shared responsibility for the integrity and accuracy of the results.

### E8

- Location: B, item 13 (authorship)
- Quote: "The submitting author is responsible for sharing a draft of the manuscript with each living co-author and obtaining their agreement to be listed as a co-author."
- Key point: The submitting author is responsible for sharing the manuscript with each living co-author and obtaining their agreement to be listed.

### E9

- Location: B, item 13 (authorship)
- Quote: "Authors may acknowledge individuals who contributed to the work but do not meet authorship criteria in the "Acknowledgments" section. This may include technical staff or data professionals."
- Key point: People who contributed but do not meet authorship criteria (such as technical staff) may be listed in the Acknowledgments.
