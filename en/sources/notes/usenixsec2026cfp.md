# USENIX Security '26: Call for Papers

- Source: `usenixsec2026cfp`, [original](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/call-for-papers)
- Version: USENIX Security Symposium 2026 call for papers (including the Ethics Guidelines section), accessed 2026-10-05. Locations give the web page subheading. Used only as an example of a top security conference.

### E1

- Location: Important Dates, Cycle 1
- Quote: "Paper submissions (including artifacts) due: Tuesday, August 26, 2025, 11:59 pm AoE [...] Early reject notification: Tuesday, October 7, 2025 [...] Rebuttal Period: November 6–13, 2025"
- Key point: Two submission cycles a year; each cycle has fixed dates such as the submission deadline, early reject notification, and a rebuttal period.

### E2

- Location: Submission Policies and Instructions, Executive Summary
- Quote: "All papers MUST have a discussion of research ethics."
- Key point: Every paper must include a discussion of research ethics.

### E3

- Location: Anonymous Submission
- Quote: "While submitted papers must be anonymous, authors may choose to give talks about their work, post a preprint of the paper online, disclose security vulnerabilities to vendors or the public, etc., during the review process."
- Key point: Submissions must be anonymous, but during review authors may still give talks, post a preprint, and disclose vulnerabilities to vendors or the public.

### E4

- Location: Anonymous Submission
- Quote: "When referring to your previous work, do so in the third person, as though it were written by someone else."
- Key point: In an anonymous submission, refer to your own previous work in the third person, as though someone else wrote it.

### E5

- Location: Ethics Guidelines, Disclosures
- Quote: "Publicly disclosing vulnerabilities before they have been privately disclosed to the responsible parties, and hence before they have been mitigated, can therefore expose people to negative outcomes."
- Key point: Disclosing vulnerabilities publicly before privately disclosing them to the responsible parties, and so before they are mitigated, can expose people to harm.

### E6

- Location: Ethics Guidelines, Disclosures
- Quote: "Clearly state to whom you have disclosed vulnerabilities and the outcomes of those disclosures, if any."
- Key point: The paper must state clearly to whom vulnerabilities were disclosed and with what outcomes.

### E7

- Location: Ethics Guidelines, Is IRB approval a sufficient alternative?
- Quote: "IRBs are not expected to understand computer security research well or to know about best practices and community norms in our field, and so IRB approval does not absolve researchers from considering ethical aspects of their work."
- Key point: Institutional review board (IRB) approval does not absolve researchers from considering ethical issues.

### E8

- Location: Ethics Guidelines
- Quote: "sometimes, the most ethical path is not to do the research or not to publish the research after it is complete."
- Key point: Sometimes the most ethical path is not to do the research, or not to publish it once it is done.

### E9

- Location: Ethics Guidelines, When should I start considering Ethics?
- Quote: "As early as possible in the research process."
- Key point: Consider ethics as early as possible.

### E10

- Location: Ethics Guidelines
- Quote: "Authors are strongly encouraged to familiarize themselves with The Menlo Report"
- Key point: The conference strongly encourages authors to familiarize themselves with The Menlo Report.

### E11

- Location: Simultaneous Submission and Plagiarism
- Quote: "Simultaneous submission of the same work to multiple venues, submission of previously published work, and plagiarism constitute dishonesty or fraud."
- Key point: Simultaneous submission to multiple venues, submitting previously published work, and plagiarism all count as dishonesty or fraud.

### E12

- Location: Submission Policies and Instructions, Executive Summary
- Quote: "Artifacts must be made available during the reviewing process. If they cannot be made available during review or after publication, the Open Science appendix must explain the reasoning."
- Key point: Artifacts must be available during review; if they cannot be, the Open Science appendix must explain why.

### E13

- Location: Simultaneous Submission and Plagiarism
- Quote: "This includes cases in which preliminary reviews from another venue indicate likely rejection but the paper is still considered under submission to said venue."
- Key point: Even if preliminary reviews at another venue indicate likely rejection, submitting here while the paper is still considered under submission there counts as simultaneous submission.

### E14

- Location: Summary of main changes from previous editions
- Quote: "USENIX Security '26 will no longer feature major revisions; papers will at most undergo a two-week shepherding process."
- Key point: From 2026 the conference no longer has "major revision" outcomes; papers undergo at most a two-week shepherding process.
