This is an open-access paper by the same two authors that directly defines gap-spotting and gives six principles for the problematization method; its content matches the two 2011 papers, which it also cites.

### E1

- Location: Abstract, p. 128
- Quote: "We contend that a primary reason behind this paradoxical shortage is the near total dominance of incremental gap-spotting research in management. This domination is even more paradoxical as it is well known that gap-spotting rarely leads to influential theories."
- Key point: The authors argue that the shortage of high-impact management research stems mainly from the near-total dominance of incremental gap-spotting research, which rarely produces influential theories.

### E2

- Location: Gap-Spotting: The Key Problem to the Paradoxical Shortage of Interesting Studies, p. 131
- Quote: "It is by identifying or constructing gaps in existing literature that most management researchers formulate their research questions and develop their theories." / "Such gap-spotting research means that the assumptions underlying existing literature for the most part remain unchallenged in the formulation of research questions."
- Key point: Gap-spotting means formulating research questions by identifying or constructing gaps in the existing literature; in doing so, the assumptions underlying that literature mostly go unchallenged.

### E3

- Location: Gap-Spotting: The Key Problem to the Paradoxical Shortage of Interesting Studies, p. 131
- Quote: "In gap-spotting, researchers refer positively or mildly critically to earlier studies with the purpose of `extend(ing) this literature' (Westphal and Khanna, 2003, p. 363), to `address this gap in the literature' (Musson and Tietze, 2004, p. 1301), to `fill this gap'"
- Key point: Typical gap-spotting phrases are "extend this literature", "address this gap in the literature" and "fill this gap", with a positive or mildly critical stance toward earlier studies.

### E4

- Location: Gap-Spotting: The Key Problem to the Paradoxical Shortage of Interesting Studies, pp. 131–132
- Quote: "Neither is it simply about finding a gap in a given body of literature. Instead, it often involves constructing gaps by bringing together different bodies of literature in complex and sometimes creative ways"
- Key point: Gap-spotting is not just finding a gap in one body of literature; it often means constructing a gap by combining different bodies of literature in complex and sometimes creative ways.

### E5

- Location: Gap-Spotting: The Key Problem to the Paradoxical Shortage of Interesting Studies, p. 132
- Quote: "Nor do we deny the possibility that gap-spotting research can lead to important contributions. However, because gap-spotting research does not deliberately and ambitiously question the assumptions underlying established literature, it rarely leads to the development of new high-ranking theories."
- Key point: The authors grant that gap-spotting can make important contributions, but because it does not deliberately question the assumptions of established literature, it rarely produces important new theories.

### E6

- Location: What Makes a Theory Interesting and Influential?, p. 131
- Quote: "In other words, if a theory doesn't challenge some of an audience's assumptions significantly, it is unlikely to receive attention and become influential – even if it has been extremely rigorously developed."
- Key point: A theory that does not significantly challenge some of its audience's assumptions is unlikely to get attention or become influential, however rigorously it was developed.

### E7

- Location: Gap-Spotting: The Key Problem to the Paradoxical Shortage of Interesting Studies, p. 131
- Quote: "It is important to note also that assumption-challenging research needs to be connected to established literature in order to be seen as meaningful"
- Key point: Assumption-challenging research also has to connect to established literature for others to see it as meaningful.

### E8

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, pp. 144–145
- Quote: "An alternative to this is to formulate research questions by problematizing some dominant assumptions in existing research (Davis, 1971)." / "`Real' problematization also involves questioning the assumptions underlying one's own meta-theoretical position."
- Key point: Problematization is the alternative to gap-spotting: you formulate research questions by questioning dominant assumptions in existing research, and real problematization also questions the assumptions behind your own position.

### E9

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, p. 145
- Quote: "This ready-made or pseudo `problematization' only reproduces the assumptions of the framework inspiring the researcher and is unlikely to lead to particularly novel and interesting ideas"
- Key point: Criticizing others from a favorite ready-made theoretical position is pseudo-problematization: it just reproduces the assumptions of your own framework and is unlikely to yield novel, interesting ideas.

### E10

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, p. 145
- Quote: "To be able to problematize assumptions through such a dialectical interrogation, the following methodological principles are central: (1) to identify a domain of literature; (2) to identify and articulate assumptions underlying this domain; (3) to evaluate them; (4) to develop an alternative assumption ground; (5) to consider it in relation to its audience; and (6) to evaluate the alternative assumption ground."
- Key point: The six steps of problematization: (1) identify a domain of literature; (2) identify and articulate its underlying assumptions; (3) evaluate them; (4) develop an alternative set of assumptions; (5) consider it in relation to the audience; (6) evaluate the alternative assumptions.

### E11

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, p. 145
- Quote: "Successful problematization is of course also very much a matter of creativity, intuition, reading inspiring texts that offer critical insights (but without being accepted as a new fixed framework), talking to other people, having specific experiences, or making observations that may trigger new thinking etc. Although we have no strong belief in rational, logical, or mechanistic procedures for problematization, we do think some structure can be helpful."
- Key point: The authors admit that problematization depends heavily on creativity, intuition, reading, talking with others and personal experience; the six steps are not a mechanical procedure, just some helpful structure.

### E12

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, p. 144
- Quote: "There are no guarantees, of course, and most research is almost per definition not capable of accomplishing something that is seen as really interesting, as this is something that clearly stands above the average or normal."
- Key point: These methods come with no guarantee; by definition most research cannot be truly interesting, because interesting means clearly above the average.

### E13

- Location: A Need to Consider Alternative Methodologies for Theory Development, p. 146
- Quote: "It is the unanticipated and the unexpected – the things that puzzle the researcher – that are of particular interest in the encounter." / "The ideal research process then includes two key elements: (a) to create a mystery, and (b) to solve it"
- Key point: Another route to challenging assumptions uses empirical material: pay special attention to the unexpected things that puzzle you, and ideally first create a mystery, then solve it.

### E14

- Location: Notes, note 3
- Quote: "It is of course necessary to have a reasonable knowledge of the area one is working within and do some positioning in relation to at least significant existing studies."
- Key point: Even when problematizing, you need reasonable knowledge of your area and should position your work against at least the significant existing studies.
