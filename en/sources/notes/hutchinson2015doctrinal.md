# Hutchinson 2015: The Doctrinal Method: Incorporating Interdisciplinary Methods in Reforming the Law

- Source: `hutchinson2015doctrinal`, [author's version PDF (QUT ePrints, the Queensland University of Technology repository)](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/93564/3/93564.pdf); published version DOI: 10.5553/ELR.000055
- Version: Erasmus Law Review 2015, issue 3, pp. 130-138. The author, Terry Hutchinson, then taught at the law school of Queensland University of Technology, **Australia**. The article examines Australian law doctoral theses, 2013 Australian journal articles on criminal law reform, and law reform commission reports. We read the author's version from the institutional repository, whose page numbers differ from the published version; locations give section numbers.

### E1

- Location: §2 Clarifying the Basics: What is doctrinal research?
- Quote: "a critical conceptual analysis of all relevant legislation and caselaw to reveal a statement of the law relevant to the matter under investigation"
- Key point: The defining feature of doctrinal research: a critical conceptual analysis of all relevant legislation and case law to arrive at a statement of the law relevant to the question under investigation.

### E2

- Location: §2
- Quote: "This `conceptual analysis critique' is based on an understanding of the rules of precedent between the court jurisdictions, the rules of statutory interpretation, the tacit discipline knowledges such as the difference between civil and criminal jurisdictions, and various tests of liability, along with the acknowledged reasoning methods, borrowed from philosophy and logic, such as induction and deduction."
- Key point: This analysis rests on several kinds of knowledge: the rules of precedent between courts, the rules of statutory interpretation, tacit disciplinary knowledge (such as the difference between civil and criminal jurisdiction and the various standards of liability), and reasoning methods such as induction and deduction.

### E3

- Location: §2
- Quote: "Whether the doctrinal method can ever be stated in a formulaic way is problematic. At its heart it is fluid. It is difficult to reduce to an algorithm."
- Key point: Whether the doctrinal method can be stated as a formula is doubtful; at heart it is fluid and hard to reduce to an algorithm.

### E4

- Location: §3.1 and footnote 33
- Quote: "A basic search string resulted in a retrieved list displaying 60 items. When book reviews, speeches, and government publications were omitted, then only 32 refereed journal articles remained as a relevant subset of the database." / "These were located on the AustLII database using the search term `crim* w/10 (reform* OR recommend*)' within the Australian journal titles published in 2013. The search was conducted on 16th February 2015."
- Key point: The author reports the database searched (AustLII, from the Australasian Legal Information Institute), the search string, the scope (2013 Australian journals), the search date (16 February 2015), and the screening: 60 results, of which 32 refereed articles remained after removing book reviews, speeches, and government publications.

### E5

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "The doctrinal methodology design was tacit. Not so any nondoctrinal methods. Surveys, for example, were outlined and explained using appropriate tables."
- Key point: In all 32 articles the doctrinal methodology design was tacit; nondoctrinal methods (such as surveys) were explained, with tables.

### E6

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "While only two of the articles were reporting that the authors had themselves undertaken surveys, interviews or statistical projects,34 18 used statistics published elsewhere in discussing the basis of concerns."
- Key point: Only 2 of the 32 articles reported surveys, interviews, or statistical projects by the authors themselves; 18 used statistics published elsewhere to show the basis of their concerns.

### E7

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "All the articles analysed pertinent secondary literature with only two including an explicit acknowledgement of the literature review." / "Relevant texts, journal articles and law reform publications are referenced where applicable to the discussion rather than brought together under a formal literature review heading."
- Key point: All articles analyzed the relevant secondary literature, but only 2 explicitly mentioned a literature review; relevant literature was cited throughout the discussion rather than gathered under a "literature review" heading.

### E8

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "Only two of the articles had as their main objective a comparison of the law between jurisdictions. However, 23 of the articles include a comparative review of the existing law particularly for the Australian state jurisdictions as well as pertinent examples from international jurisdictions."
- Key point: Only 2 articles had comparison between jurisdictions as their main objective, but 23 included a comparative review of existing law (mainly across Australian states, with some relevant foreign examples).

### E9

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "The current expectation in the literature is that there will be some statement of the legal jurisdictional status quo or an acknowledgement of obvious discrepancies in practice elsewhere."
- Key point: The current expectation in the literature is a statement of the status quo across jurisdictions, or at least an acknowledgment of obvious differences in practice elsewhere.

### E10

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "Many of the articles (23) include recommendations for reforming the law informed by the evidence presented." / "Five of the articles simply critiqued the existing laws."
- Key point: 23 articles made law reform recommendations based on the evidence presented; 5 simply critiqued the existing law.

### E11

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "Studies of reform in other areas of law such as tort law or corporate law or equity might well be more legalistic in approach."
- Key point: A limitation the author acknowledges: only criminal law reform was examined; reform research in areas such as tort law, corporate law, or equity may be more legalistic.

### E12

- Location: §3.2 Interdisciplinarity and the Work of the Law Reform Commissions (citing Barnett)
- Quote: "`they need to see the entire picture and identify the real problem(s) before launching into a search for policy solutions'"
- Key point: In law reform, first see the whole picture and identify the real problem, then search for policy solutions.

### E13

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "The Arthurs Report had recognised that comparative and historical research involving legal rules was likely to lead to `new' solutions."
- Key point: Canada's Arthurs Report recognized that comparative and historical research on legal rules was likely to lead to "new" solutions.

### E14

- Location: §3.1
- Quote: "All the articles used a doctrinal research methodology to some extent."
- Key point: All 32 articles examined used doctrinal research methodology to some extent.
