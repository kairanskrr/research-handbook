# American Sociological Association 2018: Code of Ethics

- Source: `asa2018code`, [original PDF](https://www.asanet.org/wp-content/uploads/asa_code_of_ethics-june2018.pdf)
- Version: Code of Ethics of the American Sociological Association (ASA), June 2018 edition. The official site returns a verification page to scripts, so the same PDF was downloaded from an Internet Archive snapshot. Locations give the section numbers of the code.

### E1

- Location: Ethical Standards 11. Informed Consent, opening
- Quote: "Informed consent is a basic ethical tenet of all research involving human subjects, including sociological research. Sociologists do not involve a human being as a participant in research without the informed consent of the participant or the participant's legally authorized representative, except as otherwise specified in these Ethical Standards."
- Key point: Informed consent is a basic ethical tenet of all research involving human subjects, including sociology; except as the code otherwise provides, no one may take part in research without their own or their legally authorized representative's informed consent.

### E2

- Location: 11. Informed Consent, (c)
- Quote: "Sociologists recognize that consent is a process. When sociologists regularly interact with research participants over a period of time (e.g., research panels and ethnographic research), they periodically remind the participants about the nature of the consent, their right to discontinue at any time, and inform the participants if the research procedures change."
- Key point: Consent is a process. In studies with repeated contact over time (e.g., research panels, ethnography), periodically remind participants what they consented to and that they may withdraw at any time, and tell them when procedures change.

### E3

- Location: 10.5 Limits of Confidentiality, (a)
- Quote: "Sociologists inform themselves fully about all laws and rules which may limit or alter assurances of confidentiality. They determine their ability to assure confidentiality and, as appropriate, inform research participants, students, colleagues, employees, clients, or others of any limitations to this assurance as soon as possible."
- Key point: First learn which laws and rules may limit assurances of confidentiality, judge how far you can assure it, and tell participants and others about its limits early on.

### E4

- Location: 10.1 Confidentiality in Research, (f)
- Quote: "The ethical requirements for confidentiality of personally identifiable information apply to the collection and analysis of large scale data sets which are generated through technology and internet activities. Sociologists do not attempt to re-identify such data."
- Key point: Confidentiality requirements for personally identifiable information also apply to large-scale data sets generated by technology and internet activity; sociologists do not attempt to re-identify such data.

### E5

- Location: 11.3 Informed Consent with Vulnerable Populations and Children, (a)
- Quote: "Sociologists respect the dignity of these populations and do not assume that the participants cannot consent for themselves."
- Key point: Respect the dignity of vulnerable populations and do not assume they cannot consent for themselves.

### E6

- Location: 11.3, (e)
- Quote: "However, waivers of parental informed consent do not imply that the assent of the child is waived; the wishes of the child regarding participation in the research must be respected."
- Key point: Waiving parental informed consent does not waive the child's own assent; the child's wishes about taking part must be respected.

### E7

- Location: 11.4 Use of Deception in Research, (d)
- Quote: "Sociologists generally disclose their identities to research participants. On rare occasions, sociologists may need to conceal their identities in order to undertake research that could not practicably be carried out were they to be known as researchers. Under such circumstances, sociologists undertake the research if it involves no more than minimal additional risk for the research participants, and if they have obtained approval from an authoritative body with expertise on the ethics of social science research such as an institutional review board."
- Key point: Researchers generally disclose their identity to participants; in rare cases where the research could not be done otherwise, they may conceal it, but only if it adds no more than minimal risk for participants and an authoritative body such as an ethics review board has approved it.

### E8

- Location: 11.1 Scope of Informed Consent, (a)(b)
- Quote: "Sociologists are aware that not all information on the internet is considered public, and must include informed consent procedures for research in restricted internet locations." / "Sociologists may conduct research without obtaining consent for research carried out in public places where privacy is not expected."
- Key point: Not all information on the internet counts as public; research in restricted online spaces needs informed consent procedures, while research in public places with no expectation of privacy may proceed without consent.

### E9

- Location: 12.5 Sharing Data, (a)
- Quote: "As a regular practice, sociologists share data and pertinent documentation as an integral part of a research plan. Sociologists generally make their data available after completion of a project or its major publications, except where proprietary agreements with employers, contractors, or clients preclude such accessibility or when it is impossible to share data and protect the confidentiality of the research participants (e.g., field notes or detailed information from ethnographic interviews)."
- Key point: Sharing data and documentation is part of the research plan; data are generally made available after the project or its main publications, except where proprietary agreements prevent it or confidentiality cannot be protected while sharing (e.g., field notes, detailed ethnographic interview material).

### E10

- Location: 12.5 Sharing Data, (f)
- Quote: "Sociologists who use data from others for further analyses explicitly acknowledge the contribution of the initial researchers."
- Key point: When using others' data for further analysis, explicitly acknowledge the researchers who first collected it.

### E11

- Location: 12.4 Reporting on Research, (d)
- Quote: "Consistent with the spirit of full disclosure of methods and analyses, once findings are publicly disseminated, sociologists permit their open assessment and verification by other responsible researchers, with appropriate safeguards to protect the confidentiality of research participants."
- Key point: Once findings are public, allow other responsible researchers to openly assess and verify them, with appropriate safeguards for participant confidentiality.

### E12

- Location: 14. Authorship, (a)
- Quote: "Although there are alternate approaches (which may be explained in a footnote or acknowledgement), the default order of authorship in Sociology is based on the relative scientific or professional contributions of the authors."
- Key point: Although other approaches exist (explained in a footnote or acknowledgement), sociology by default orders authors by relative contribution.

### E13

- Location: 14. Authorship, (b)
- Quote: "When collaborative work substantially derives from a student's dissertation or thesis, the student is usually listed as first author."
- Key point: When collaborative work derives mainly from a student's thesis or dissertation, the student is usually first author.

### E14

- Location: 10.5 Limits of Confidentiality, (d)
- Quote: "Sociologists who conduct research with participants who are engaging in illegal or potentially stigmatizing behaviors must understand the limits of confidentiality and address possible challenges to maintaining confidentiality. These limits should be reviewed by an authoritative body with expertise on the ethics of social science research such as an institutional review board."
- Key point: When participants engage in illegal or potentially stigmatizing behavior, researchers must understand the limits of confidentiality and address challenges to it, and these limits should be reviewed by an authoritative body such as an ethics review board.

### E15

- Location: 11. Informed Consent, (f)
- Quote: "Sociologists explicitly discuss confidentiality and, if applicable, the extent to which confidentiality may be limited. If sociologists plan to share the data resulting from their research, they explain the processes for ensuring confidentiality by subsequent researchers."
- Key point: When seeking consent, explicitly discuss confidentiality and its limits; if you plan to share the data, explain how later researchers will ensure confidentiality.
