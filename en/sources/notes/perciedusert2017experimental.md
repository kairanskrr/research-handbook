# Percie du Sert et al. 2017: The Experimental Design Assistant

- Source: `perciedusert2017experimental`, [original](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.2003779)
- Version: PLoS Biology 15(9): e2003779, 2017, open access, read 2026-10-06. Introduces the free online experimental design tool of the UK National Centre for the Replacement, Refinement and Reduction of Animals in Research (NC3Rs). All authors were involved in developing the tool. Locations give subheadings.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "The EDA (https://eda.nc3rs.org.uk) is a web-based application with an integrated website, which guides researchers through the process of designing animal experiments; the output includes a diagram that improves the transparency of the experimental plan. The resource is freely available"
- Key point: The Experimental Design Assistant (EDA) is a free web application that guides researchers step by step through designing animal experiments and outputs a diagram that makes the experimental plan more transparent.

### E2

- Location: The EDA improves the reliability of experimental results and analysis
- Quote: "The system generates a randomisation sequence for the experiment, which takes into account any blocking factors included in the design and provides dedicated functionalities, such as support for blinding and sample size calculation"
- Key point: It generates randomization sequences that take blocking factors into account, and supports blinding and sample size calculation.

### E3

- Location: The EDA improves the reliability of experimental results and analysis
- Quote: "For example, it helps researchers to identify variables that could confound the outcome and provides advice on how to include them in the randomisation and the statistical analysis."
- Key point: It helps researchers identify variables that could confound the outcome and advises how to include them in the randomization and statistical analysis.

### E4

- Location: The EDA improves the reliability of experimental results and analysis
- Quote: "Button, Ioannidis and colleagues estimated the average power in neuroscience animal studies to be around 20%. This constitutes a high risk of missing a genuine effect (a false negative), because only 1 in 5 experiments would have a chance of detecting an effect of the magnitude reported."
- Key point: The paper cites Button, Ioannidis, and colleagues' estimate that the average statistical power of animal studies in neuroscience is about 20%: only one experiment in five has a chance of detecting an effect of the reported size.

### E5

- Location: The EDA enables an effective assessment of the experimental plans
- Quote: "The system is not designed to replace specialist statistical advice but can facilitate it."
- Key point: The tool does not replace specialist statistical advice, but it can help you seek that advice more effectively.
