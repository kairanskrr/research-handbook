# Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010: The weirdest people in the world?

- Source: `henrich2010weirdest`, [Original PDF (Working Paper No. 139 of the German Data Forum, RatSWD)](https://www.konsortswd.de/wp-content/uploads/RatSWD_WP_139.pdf)
- Version: Working paper version (April 2010); the published version appeared in Behavioral and Brain Sciences 33(2–3):61–83. Locations are given as subsections.

### E1

- Location: Long Abstract
- Quote: "Behavioral scientists routinely publish broad claims about human psychology and behavior in the world's top journals based on samples drawn entirely from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich and Democratic (WEIRD) societies."
- Key point: Behavioral scientists routinely publish broad claims about "humans" in top journals based on samples drawn entirely from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) societies.

### E2

- Location: Long Abstract
- Quote: "WEIRD subjects are particularly unusual compared with the rest of the species—frequent outliers."
- Key point: Comparing data across populations shows that WEIRD subjects are particularly unusual compared with the rest of the species, often outliers.

### E3

- Location: §1.1 The Behavioral Sciences Database is Narrow
- Quote: "68% of subjects came from the US, and a full 96% of subjects were from Western industrialized countries" / "This means that 96% of psychological samples come from countries with only 12% of the world's population."
- Key point: Arnett (2008) analyzed top journals in six subfields of psychology from 2003 to 2007: 68% of subjects came from the US and 96% from Western industrialized countries, which hold only 12% of the world's population.

### E4

- Location: §1.1 The Behavioral Sciences Database is Narrow
- Quote: "67% of the American samples (and 80% of the samples from other countries) were composed solely of undergraduates in psychology courses"
- Key point: In top social psychology journals, 67% of American samples (and 80% of samples from other countries) consisted solely of undergraduates in psychology courses.

### E5

- Location: §1.2 Researchers Often Assume their Findings are Universal
- Quote: "In top journals such as Nature and Science researchers frequently extend their findings from undergraduates to the species—often declaring this generalization in their titles."
- Key point: In top journals such as Nature and Science, researchers often extend findings from undergraduates to the whole species, sometimes in the title.

### E6

- Location: §2.1 Visual Perception (the Müller-Lyer illusion)
- Quote: "The results show substantial differences among populations, with American undergraduates anchoring the extreme end of the distribution" / "the San foragers of the Kalahari were unaffected by the so-called "illusion""
- Key point: Example: a classic visual illusion is strongest in American undergraduates, while San foragers of the Kalahari are unaffected by it. Even visual perception varies across populations.

### E7

- Location: §6.3 Recommendations
- Quote: "Journal editors and reviewers should press authors to both explicitly discuss and defend the generalizability of their findings. Claims and confidence regarding generalizability must scale with the strength of the empirical defense."
- Key point: Editors and reviewers should press authors to discuss and defend explicitly how far their findings generalize; claims about generalizability must scale with the strength of the evidence.

### E8

- Location: §6.3 Recommendations
- Quote: "Journal editors should require explicit and detailed information on subject pool composition"
- Key point: Journals should require detailed information on the composition of the subject pool.
