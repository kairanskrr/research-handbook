# Rana et al. 2024: Standardizing Substrate Selection: A Strategy toward Unbiased Evaluation of Reaction Generality

- Source: `rana2024standardizing`, [original](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.3c01638) ([PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11046462/))
- Version: ACS Central Science 2024, 10(4), 899-906, CC BY 4.0 open access. Glorius group, University of Münster, Germany. Text taken from the Europe PMC full-text XML.

### E1

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "The underlining reason is their subjection to substantial biases, namely, selection and reporting bias (Figure 1C)." / "The former—selection bias—can be explained by the chemist's prioritization of substrates that are expected to give higher yields or are easily accessible. The latter—reporting bias—is given, as most publications do not report unsuccessful experiments or low-yielding results that constitute negative data."
- Key point: Substrate scope tables suffer from two biases: selection bias (favoring substrates expected to give high yields or that are easy to obtain) and reporting bias (most papers do not report failed or low-yield results).

### E2

- Location: Introduction

- Key point: Because of the combinatorial nature of chemistry, even the best substrate scope will always be incomplete.

### E3

- Location: Results and Discussion (last paragraph of the discussion)
- Quote: "We must state clearly that negative results are equally important in delineating the limits of applicability of a newly reported reaction and identifying potential room for further methodological improvements."
- Key point: Negative results are equally important for delineating the limits of a new reaction's applicability and identifying room for methodological improvement.

### E4

- Location: Results and Discussion (last paragraph of the discussion)
- Quote: "This originates from researchers being hesitant to share unsuccessful examples, which might negatively impact their chances of publication."
- Key point: Many failed examples go unpublished because researchers fear they will hurt their chances of publication.

### E5

- Location: Conclusions
- Quote: "To demonstrate the broad applicability of a reaction and enable quick adoption in academia and industry, a scope does not necessarily need to be enlarged but broadened in diversity and cleansed of biases."
- Key point: To show that a reaction is broadly applicable, a scope need not be larger, but more diverse and cleansed of biases.

### E6

- Location: Introduction
- Quote: "On this note, our group previously developed the robustness screen, which provides a simple way to assess the functional group tolerance of a reaction." / "This screen measures the impact of standardized additives on the reaction outcome and therefore allows an approximation of the applicability and limits of any given report."
- Key point: The group previously developed the "robustness screen": adding a set of standardized additives to the reaction and measuring their impact, to estimate which functional groups the reaction tolerates and where its limits lie.
