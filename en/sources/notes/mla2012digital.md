# Modern Language Association 2012: Guidelines for Evaluating Work in Digital Humanities and Digital Media

- Source: `mla2012digital`, [original](https://www.mla.org/About-Us/Governance/Committees/Committee-Listings/Professional-Issues/Committee-on-Information-Technology/Guidelines-for-Evaluating-Work-in-Digital-Humanities-and-Digital-Media)
- Version: revised January 2012, approved by the MLA Executive Council in February 2012

### E1

- Location: Document and Explain Your Work
- Quote: "conference proceedings are among the most prestigious publications in computer science, whereas they are generally deemed to be a lesser form of publication in the humanities"
- Key point: Conference proceedings are among the most prestigious publications in computer science but are generally deemed a lesser form of publication in the humanities.

### E2

- Location: Document and Explain Your Work
- Quote: "Faculty members who work in digital media or digital humanities should be prepared to make explicit the results, theoretical underpinnings, and intellectual rigor of their work."
- Key point: People who work in digital humanities should be prepared to make explicit the results, theoretical underpinnings, and intellectual rigor of their work.

### E3

- Location: Document and Explain Your Work
- Quote: "describe the process underlying creation of work in digital media (e.g., the creation of infrastructure as well as content) and their particular contributions"
- Key point: Describe the process behind creating digital work and your particular contributions.

### E4

- Location: Ask about Evaluation and Support
- Quote: "When candidates for faculty positions first negotiate the terms of their jobs, they should ask how credit for digital work will be considered"
- Key point: Ask how digital work will be credited in evaluation when you first negotiate the terms of a faculty position.
