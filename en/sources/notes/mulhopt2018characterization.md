# Mülhopt et al. 2018: Characterization of Nanoparticle Batch-To-Batch Variability

- Source: `mulhopt2018characterization`, [original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5977325/)
- Version: Nanomaterials 8(5):311 (2018), open access (CC BY 4.0); full text read on PubMed Central.

### E1

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "The work described here represents over three years of effort across 14 European laboratories to assess the reproducibility of nanoparticle properties produced by the same and modified synthesis routes"
- Key point: Over three years, 14 European laboratories assessed whether the properties of nanoparticles produced by the same or modified synthesis routes are reproducible.

### E2

- Location: Abstract
- Quote: "For 46 different batches of the selected NMs, all physicochemical descriptors as prioritized by the OECD have been fully characterized."
- Key point: For 46 batches of nanomaterials, they fully characterized all physicochemical descriptors prioritized by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD).

### E3

- Location: 1. Introduction
- Quote: "Current industrial and laboratory processes for synthesis of NMs give rise to quite significant variability from batch-to-batch"
- Key point: Current industrial and laboratory processes for synthesizing nanomaterials give quite significant batch-to-batch variability.

### E4

- Location: 1. Introduction
- Quote: "There is also the question of how ‘representative’ a single batch is of the NM properties across multiple batches."
- Key point: A further question is how representative a single batch is of the nanomaterial's properties across multiple batches.

### E5

- Location: 1. Introduction
- Quote: "Indeed, synthesis route is well known to affect the properties, including toxicity, of resulting NMs, as has been shown for silica, ceria, silver and many other NMs."
- Key point: Synthesis route is known to affect the properties of nanomaterials, including toxicity; this has been shown for silica, ceria, silver, and many others.

### E6

- Location: 4. Discussion (technical recommendations at the end)
- Quote: "As the NMs are frequently agglomerated or even aggregated, the particle size distributions should be measured by several methods such as DLS and/or SMPS/electrospray and TEM to minimize the variability of the determined size due to technical limitations."
- Key point: Nanomaterials are often agglomerated, so particle size distributions should be measured by several methods (e.g., dynamic light scattering (DLS), scanning mobility particle sizing (SMPS) / electrospray, transmission electron microscopy (TEM)) to reduce the bias from any single technique's limitations.

### E7

- Location: 4. Discussion (summary at the end)
- Quote: "To summarize, whereas the variability of a number of parameters suggested by the OECD WPMN is rather low that is, the values are rather stable and reproducible such as the zeta potential and size others for example, impurities are less well controlled."
- Key point: Overall, parameters such as zeta potential and particle size were fairly stable across batches, while others, such as impurities, were less well controlled.
