# Olah 2021：Research Taste Exercises

- 资料：`olah2021taste`，[原文](https://colah.github.io/notes/taste/)
- 版本：作者博客笔记，2021-01-09。作者自注为"rough note"。文末收录了推特讨论中他人的建议，引用时注明出自谁。

### E1

- 位置：开头
- 原文："One of the most important aspects of growing as a researcher is developing research taste -- roughly, the ability to chose good problems to work on."
- 要点：研究品味大致就是挑选好问题的能力，是研究者成长中最重要的方面之一。

### E2

- 位置：开头
- 原文："I think the fundamental issue is that actually testing whether a research idea you come up with is good is very expensive. Often it takes months, so you only really get a few pieces of feedback on your taste every year."
- 要点：品味难练，是因为验证一个想法好不好代价很高，常要几个月，一年只能得到几次反馈。

### E3

- 位置：开头
- 原文："I don't recommend forcing yourself to work on something you don't find exciting, even if you intellectually think it's more important. You'll be a lot less effective."
- 要点：不建议逼自己做不觉得兴奋的题目，即使理智上认为它更重要；那样效率会低很多。

### E4

- 位置：Exercises › Exercise 1
- 原文："Write down a list of research ideas. Have a mentor you respect rate each idea 1-10. Discuss ideas where you disagree with them after reflection."
- 要点：练习 1：写下研究想法清单，请尊敬的导师逐个打 1–10 分，对分歧大的想法深入讨论。

### E5

- 位置：Exercises › Exercise 2
- 原文："Pay attention when other people try ideas you’ve had. How did the results compare with your expectations?"
- 要点：练习 2：别人做了你想过的点子时，对比结果和你的预期。

### E6

- 位置：Exercises › Exercise 3
- 原文："Interview researchers around you on their taste. Why do they work on the problems they do? How do they pick problems?"
- 要点：练习 3：访谈身边的研究者，问他们为什么做这些问题、怎么选题。

### E7

- 位置：Exercises › Exercise 5
- 原文："Critically consider your research taste, and the community taste around you. Your taste is likely very influenced by your research cluster (your collaborators, advisor, etc)."
- 要点：练习 5：批判地审视自己和所在圈子的品味；你的品味很大程度上受合作者和导师影响。

### E8

- 位置：Failure Modes › Failure Mode 1
- 原文："Getting overly attached to one research direction / falling into sunk costs. [...] Set aside a week or two to step back and create a list of the other research directions you think would be most promising to work on (look at new research, talk to others, etc). Then ask what you’d want to work on if you were starting from scratch."
- 要点：失败模式 1：过度依恋一个方向，陷入沉没成本。对策是每隔一两年花一两周退一步，列出其他有前景的方向，问自己"如果从零开始，会做什么"。

### E9

- 位置：Failure Modes › Failure mode 2
- 原文："Sometimes people fall into a trap of trying to pick the perfect problem or have a brilliant insight before getting their hands dirty. You can’t have research taste in a vacuum."
- 要点：失败模式 2：还没动手就想挑出完美的问题。品味离不开知识和亲手实践。

### E10

- 位置：Failure Modes › Failure mode 3
- 原文："This generally leads to ideas optimized for compromise rather than quality."
- 要点：失败模式 3：兴趣与环境不合时，把想法扭曲成导师能接受的样子，结果是为妥协而优化，而不是为质量。

### E11

- 位置：Suggestions from other people › Rachel Prudden
- 原文："The heuristic of imagining that another group has published the paper you have in mind. Are you excited to read it?"
- 要点：Rachel Prudden 的启发式：想象另一个组发表了你脑中的论文，你会兴奋地去读吗？Olah 认为这是不错的判断方法。

### E12

- 位置：Suggestions from other people › Andy Matuschak
- 原文："running to make an idea happen as soon as it seems tractable. It's exciting to have a viable approach—makes me wanna go do it! But if I sit with an idea longer I can often evolve a deeper variant and run with that instead."
- 要点：Andy Matuschak 说的失败模式：一看到可行就马上动手；多和想法相处一段时间，常能演化出更深的版本。

### E13

- 位置：Suggestions from other people › Michael Nielsen（经 Shrey Jain）
- 原文："But a field that is a mess is really an opportunity. Chances are good that there are deep unifying and simplifying concepts still waiting to be understood and developed by someone - perhaps you."
- 要点：Olah 引用 Nielsen：一个混乱的领域其实是机会，那里可能还有待发现的统一、简化的概念。

### E14

- 位置：Suggestions from other people › Arthur Allshire
- 原文："What are the bottlenecking problems in your field?"
- 要点：Arthur Allshire 的建议：问自己领域里卡脖子的瓶颈问题是什么。

### E15

- 位置：Suggestions from other people › Christian Szegedy、Sergey Gorbunov 及 Olah 的评论
- 原文："In hindsight though, most of the errors are made in my research was when I listened to the criticisms of my respected mentors and most of the cool stuff I did were the ones those everybody called initially useless, uninteresting or "good luck!"..."
- 要点：Christian Szegedy 回顾说，他研究中的多数错误发生在听从导师批评时，而最酷的工作起初都被认为没用。

### E16

- 位置：同上，Olah 对 Szegedy 的评论
- 原文："Chris comment: I think there's a lot of truth to this, but beware survivorship bias and remember that different people may have different levels of scientific maturity / taste at different points in their careers."
- 要点：Olah 提醒：这里有幸存者偏差；不同人在不同阶段成熟度不同，很多博士生一开始就自己规划研究会很吃力。

### E17

- 位置：Failure Modes › Failure mode 2 的对策
- 原文："Help someone else with their project in a space you’re interested in. You’ll learn a lot and get your hands dirty without becoming overly committed to working on something long term."
- 要点：对策之一：在感兴趣的方向上帮别人做项目，能学到很多、亲手实践，又不必长期绑定。
