# Schulman 2020：An Opinionated Guide to ML Research

- 资料：`schulman2020opinionated`，[原文](http://joschu.net/blog/opinionated-guide-ml-research.html)
- 版本：作者博客，2020-01-24（原写于 2017 年 12 月）。针对机器学习，以下只摘跨学科成立的部分。

### E1

- 位置：开头 Exercise
- 原文："Think about what makes each one stand out—whether it's a groundbreaking result that changed your perspective on some problem; or an algorithmic idea that's reusable; or a deep insight about some recurring questions."
- 要点：让人印象深刻的成果大致有三种：改变人们看法的突破性结果、可复用的方法、对反复出现的问题的深刻洞见。

### E2

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Aim High, and Climb Incrementally Towards High Goals
- 原文："Sometimes, people who are both exceptionally smart and hard-working fail to do great research. In my view, the main reason for this failure is that they work on unimportant problems."
- 要点：聪明又勤奋的人做不出好研究，主要原因是做了不重要的问题。

### E3

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Honing Your Taste
- 原文："Your ability to choose the right problems to work on is even more important than your raw technical skill. This taste in problems is something you’ll develop over time by watching which ideas prosper and which ones are forgotten."
- 要点：选对问题的能力比纯技术能力更重要；这种品味要靠长期观察"哪些想法兴盛、哪些被遗忘"来培养。

### E4

- 位置：同上
- 原文："Seek advice from experienced researchers on what to work on. There’s no shame in working on ideas suggested by other people. Ideas are cheap, and there are lots of them in the air. Your skill comes in when you decide which one to work on, and how well you execute on it."
- 要点：可以请有经验的人建议做什么；做别人提出的想法不丢人。想法很多，本事在于挑哪个、做得多好。

### E5

- 位置：同上
- 原文："What causes some ideas to get wide uptake, whereas others are forgotten?"
- 要点：培养品味的反思题之一：为什么有些想法被广泛采用，有些被遗忘？

### E6

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Idea-Driven vs Goal-Driven Research
- 原文："Idea-driven. Follow some sector of the literature. As you read a paper showing how to do X, you have an idea of how to do X even better. Then you embark on a project to test your idea."
- 要点：想法驱动：跟着某块文献读，读到"怎么做 X"时想到怎么做得更好，然后去验证。

### E7

- 位置：同上
- 原文："One major downside of idea-driven research is that there’s a high risk of getting scooped or duplicating the work of others. Researchers around the world are reading the same literature, which leads them to similar ideas."
- 要点：想法驱动的主要缺点是容易撞车：大家读同样的文献，会想到相似的点子。

### E8

- 位置：同上
- 原文："On the other hand, with goal-driven research, your goal will give you a perspective that’s differentiated from the rest of the community. It will lead you to ask questions that no one else is asking, enabling you to make larger leaps of progress."
- 要点：目标驱动（先定一个具体目标，再解决通往目标的问题）能给你与众不同的视角，让你问出别人没问的问题。

### E9

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Case Study of Goal-Driven Research
- 原文："This example illustrates how choosing a different problem from the rest of the community can lead you to explore different ideas."
- 要点：作者没有跟随当时的热门方向，坚持自己的问题，结果做出了最有影响的工作；选和大家不同的问题，会引你走向不同的想法。

### E10

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Aim High, and Climb Incrementally Towards High Goals
- 原文："When you embark on a research project, you should ask yourself: how large is the potential upside? Will this be a 10% improvement or a 10X improvement? I often see researchers take on projects that seem sensible but could only possibly yield a small improvement to some metric."
- 要点：开题前问自己：最好的情况下收益有多大，是提升 10% 还是 10 倍？很多项目看似合理，但上限只是某个指标的小幅提升。

### E11

- 位置：同上
- 原文："Incremental work (those 10% improvements) are most useful in the context of a larger goal that you are trying to achieve."
- 要点：小幅改进在服务于一个更大目标时最有价值。

### E12

- 位置：同上
- 原文："A method that slightly improves on the baseline better be very simple, otherwise no one will bother using it—not even you."
- 要点：只比基线好一点的方法必须非常简单，否则没人会用，连你自己都不会用。

### E13

- 位置：Making Continual Progress › When to Switch Problems
- 原文："In my experience, switching problems too frequently (and giving up on promising ideas) is a more common failure mode than not switching enough."
- 要点：作者的经验是，换题太频繁（放弃有希望的想法）比不肯换题更常见。

### E14

- 位置：Choosing Problems › Idea-Driven vs Goal-Driven Research
- 原文："I personally recommend goal-driven research for most people, and I’ve mostly followed this strategy myself."
- 要点：作者向大多数人推荐目标驱动的研究。

### E15

- 位置：Idea-Driven vs Goal-Driven Research
- 原文："You can wake up every morning and imagine achieving your goal—what the result would look like and how you would feel. That makes it easier to stick to a long-running research program with ups and downs."
- 要点：有明确目标，更容易在起起落落的长期项目中坚持下去。

### E16

- 位置：Keep a Notebook, and Review It
- 原文："I strongly advise you to keep a notebook, where you record your daily ideas and experiments."
- 要点：强烈建议记研究笔记，每天记下想法和实验。

### E17

- 位置：Keep a Notebook, and Review It
- 原文："Every 1 or 2 weeks, I do a review, where I read all of my daily entries and I condense the information into a summary."
- 要点：每一两周通读日记并压缩成总结，总结分为实验发现、见解、代码进展、下一步等部分。

### E18

- 位置：Keep a Notebook, and Review It
- 原文："You also might look back at several months and realize that you’ve been jumping around between ideas too much—that you have a few half-finished projects but you didn’t follow any of these threads long enough to yield a notable result."
- 要点：笔记能帮你发现自己在想法之间跳来跳去，留下一堆半途而废的项目。

### E19

- 位置：When to Switch Problems
- 原文："Sometimes the idea has the potential to work, but if you get one detail wrong, you’ll see no signs of life. But other ideas are simply doomed to fail no matter how hard you work on them."
- 要点：难点在于：有的想法只是细节错了才没效果，有的则怎么做都注定失败，两者很难分辨。

### E20

- 位置：When to Switch Problems
- 原文："If your idea is quick to try and the potential upside is large, then go ahead and do it. But more commonly, your initial results on the new idea will be disappointing, and it’ll take a more sustained effort to yield significant results."
- 要点：新想法试起来快、潜在收益大，就去试；但更常见的是新想法的初步结果也令人失望，同样需要持续投入。

### E21

- 位置：When to Switch Problems
- 原文："One strategy, which I haven’t tried personally but makes a lot of sense upon reflection, is to devote some fixed time budget to trying out new ideas that diverge from your main line of work."
- 要点：可以给偏离主线的新想法留一份固定时间预算，例如每周一天。

- 位置：Honing Your Taste
- 原文："Seek advice from experienced researchers on what to work on. There’s no shame in working on ideas suggested by other people."
- 要点：向有经验的研究者请教该做什么；做别人建议的想法并不丢人，本事在于选哪个、做得多好。
